The recruiting steps by grade, in plain words. Every rule links to its source. Your athlete leads the conversations; your job is the calendar, the paperwork and the money.

9th grade

Get the course plan right

  • Ask the school counselor to check every class against your school's NCAA-approved course list. Not every class counts. D1 Bylaw 14.3.1.1
  • Know the two academic lines that can't be fixed late: 10 core courses (7 in English, math or science) done before senior year, and 16 core courses with at least a 2.3 core GPA by graduation. D1 Bylaws 14.3.1.1(b), 14.3.1.3

Go deeper: Eligibility: the 2 Hard Deadlines

10th grade

Start the record, not the hype

  • Keep one tracker: schools, coaches, what was said, next step. A plain spreadsheet works; several parents in our research built one after leaving a paid service.
  • Before contact opens, your athlete can email coaches and fill in questionnaires. Many sports open coach calls and texts on June 15 after sophomore year; D1 baseball waits until August 1 of junior year. D1 Bylaws 13.1.3.1, 13.1.3.1.1, 13.4.1

Go deeper: Football: Junior Year Playbook

11th grade

The real recruiting year

  • Open the NCAA Eligibility Center certification account ($110 domestic, waivers possible). You need it before any D1 official visit. D1 Bylaw 13.6.3
  • Visits: official visits open by sport (for example April 1 of junior year in football, September 1 in baseball). Check your sport's page for the exact rule. D1 Bylaws 13.6.2.1.2, 13.6.2.1.7
  • Pick camps where your target coaches will be, after you've emailed them. A camp the coach has never heard of you for is an expensive practice.

12th grade

Decide and finish

  • It isn't too late for many paths: D2, D3, NAIA and junior college programs still fill rosters in senior year.
  • Written offers and signing follow sport-specific dates; a verbal commitment binds no one. Fall enrollees can request final amateurism certification from April 1. D1 Bylaw 13.02.13.1; Eligibility Center procedure
