Basketball · NAIA · Composite story

Can she play in college? One family widens the search

A composite story built from sourced paths. Jasmine is not a real person, and this family is fictional. It shows how one family could use The Commit Book, and it promises no outcome.

Sophomore year

A steady scorer on a team nobody scouts

What was happening

Jasmine is a guard at a public high school. She scores steadily, but her team does not get much attention. Her parents have one question: can she play in college at all, and at what level? They do not know where to start.

What they did

Her parents open a Contact Log (contact-log) so every coach, date and reply goes in one grid. It is coming soon, like several tools in the Commit Book. They begin with the schools they already know by name.

How it helps

The family sees who has replied and who has not. It does not make anyone reply.

Junior year, fall

Her parents learn the academic rules first

What was happening

Before sending anything, Jasmine's mother asks the school counselor what classes count. The family learns the two hard deadlines and the 16-core, 2.3 rule apply to the NCAA path. They put them on the wall calendar.

What they did

They work through Eligibility: the 2 Hard Deadlines (eligibility-2-hard-deadlines), including the counselor meeting script and the core GPA worksheet. Jasmine's father also reads the NAIA rule in the source below.

How it helps

The family finds out early which classes count. It does not promise eligibility for any program.

Junior year, winter

A reel that fits on one page

What was happening

The family has game film but no easy way to share it. Jasmine's coach says a shorter video would be easier for staff to open. The family decides to cut the film down and attach a one-page profile.

What they did

They use the Highlight Reel & Social Playbook (highlight-reel-social) for the reel structure, the one-page profile and a quick check of Jasmine's public posts.

How it helps

A coach can see her play in two minutes. A short reel does not guarantee a response.

Junior year, spring

Widening the list on purpose

What was happening

No big offers come in. Sources describe a family that sent a highlight video to about 25 junior-college and D2 schools when no D1 offers came, and a high school coach who made calls for a player. Jasmine's family widens its list too, including NAIA and junior-college programs, and her coach agrees to make calls.

What they did

They send first emails from the First Contact Pack (coach-emails), which is coming soon, and log each send in the Contact Log (contact-log). NAIA publishes no contact-date calendar, so the family checks the rule at the source.

How it helps

The family can send many careful messages without losing track of them. It does not mean any coach will answer.

Senior year, fall

Visits, and a camp or two that earn their cost

What was happening

A few NAIA coaches reply. Jasmine's family plans visits. They also look at camps, and her father adds up travel and fees before saying yes to any of them.

What they did

They use the Camp ROI Playbook (camp-roi) for the cost budget and post-camp follow-up. Her parents write down answers after each visit and compare notes at home.

How it helps

The family spends only on events that match its list. A checklist does not predict how a program will treat her.

Camp ROI Playbook$15

Source: www.naia.org

Senior year, winter

Two offers, one kitchen table

What was happening

Two NAIA programs make offers. The aid packages look different, and the playing-time talk differs too. Jasmine's family sits down together to compare them line by line.

What they did

They use Offers & Signing Day (offer-and-signing) to understand verbal and written offers and to compare both packages side by side, with written questions for each coach.

How it helps

The family compares the same things in both offers. It does not decide which program is right for her.
