Marcus: a lineman, no D1 offers, a D3 fit

A composite story built from sourced paths. Marcus is not a real person. Here is how a family in his spot might work the calendar, with The Commit Book beside them.

Sophomore year

Good grades, a lighter frame, no big-school interest

What was happening

Marcus plays both ways on the line at a mid-size public school. He is a little light for the position. His grades are strong. No Division I program has written. His parents are not sure there is a college roster for a player his size.

What they did

They sit down at the kitchen table and read how football recruiting is timed before they spend a dollar on anything else. They use the Football: Junior Year Playbook (football-junior-year, $29) to see the calendar and the two hard dates.

How it helps

It turns a vague worry into dates on a page. It does not tell you where your son will play.

Sophomore spring

Making sure the classroom door stays open

What was happening

Coaches at every level check eligibility. Marcus's family has not looked at core courses or the deadlines that lock in. They do not want a surprise in senior year.

What they did

They meet with the school counselor and walk through the core-course list and the Eligibility Center account. They use Eligibility: the 2 Hard Deadlines (eligibility-2-hard-deadlines, $9) for the counselor meeting script and the core GPA worksheet.

How it helps

A counselor meeting goes faster when you bring the questions. It does not change anyone's eligibility decision.

Summer before junior year

Switching the question from 'who wants him' to 'who fits him'

What was happening

Marcus is not the size the big programs list. His family decides to stop waiting on Division I. The sources behind this story describe an undersized lineman with no D1 offers who chose a D3 school after it pitched its academic path. Marcus asks about both football and his intended major.

What they did

They build a target list in three tiers and draft a first note to Division III coaches. The Football: Junior Year Playbook (football-junior-year) has the tiers and the email and call scripts. The First Contact Pack (coach-emails, $9) is coming soon and will add 12 templates.

How it helps

A tiered list keeps the family from chasing one program. Coaches decide whether to answer.

January of junior year

Campus visits open, and the family keeps a record

What was happening

Under the rule this story draws on (D3 Bylaw 13.6.1.1.1), paid official visits at Division III open January 1 of junior year. Division III offers no athletic scholarships. Aid there is academic and need-based. Marcus's family starts getting invitations and does not want to lose track of who said what.

What they did

They tour campuses and keep one grid of coach names, dates and next steps. The Contact Log (contact-log) is coming soon and is built for that grid and a 5-minute Sunday review. Until then they use the trackers in the Football: Junior Year Playbook.

How it helps

One page means no one asks 'did he ever write back?' at dinner. The rule guarantees nothing. Check it at the source.

Senior fall

Reading what a school is really offering

What was happening

A Division III coach is recruiting Marcus directly. The family wants to know what a campus-visit conversation should sound like and which comments are worth a second question. Aid will be academic and need-based, so the net price matters as much as the roster spot.

What they did

They bring a short list of questions on playing time, academics and cost to each visit. Red Flags: The Checklist (red-flags) is coming soon with 40 flags in four groups. The D3 Path: Academic Aid First (d3-academic-aid-first, $19) is also coming soon and covers net price calculators.

How it helps

Written questions keep a good visit from skipping the hard topics. They do not predict how a program treats players.

Decision time

Choosing the school where he can play and study

What was happening

Marcus has more than one answer. The family picks a Division III school where he can play and study what he came for. In this composite he plays four seasons, mostly as a starter in his last two, and graduates. No draft. That is the destination.

What they did

They compare offers on one sheet and ask the same questions of every staff. The Offers & Signing Day playbook (offer-and-signing, $19) covers verbal versus written and signing periods. The Offer Comparison Toolkit (offer-comparison) is coming soon.

How it helps

A side-by-side makes a close choice easier to talk about. It does not make the choice for you.
