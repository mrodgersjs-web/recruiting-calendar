A composite story built from sourced paths. Nia is not a real person. She came to track late, with good marks but not state-meet times.

Sophomore spring

Good marks, late start

What was happening

Nia plays more than one sport at a public high school. She found track late. Her times are good, not state-meet good. Her parents wonder if any college coach will care.

What they did

They read the Track & Field playbook to see how marks, contact dates and visits fit together. They look up program standards themselves, since the playbook says you look them up.

How it helps

They stop guessing and know where her marks sit. It does not say any coach will want her.

Junior fall

The question is the major

What was happening

Nia has a major in mind and does not want to give it up. Her parents want to know if she can run and keep it. They learn that Division III offers no athletic scholarships. Aid there is academic and need-based (D3 Bylaw 13.6.1.1.1).

What they did

They read the D3 playbook on how aid works and how to use net price calculators. It is coming soon, not on sale yet.

How it helps

The family knows the money question before the first visit. The rule guarantees nothing. Check it at the source.

Junior winter

Ask about the major, then send the marks

What was happening

Nia writes to coaches. She asks about her intended major first. Then she sends her marks. Paid official visits at D3 open January 1 of junior year.

What they did

She uses the First Contact Pack for the email and call scripts. It is coming soon, not on sale yet. The family logs each reply in the Contact Log, also coming soon.

How it helps

The first email has a plain shape. The log shows which coaches wrote back and which did not.

Junior spring

Short film, tidy profile

What was happening

Coaches want to see her run. Nia has race footage from meets but nothing cut together. A coach might also look her up online.

What they did

She follows the Highlight Reel & Social playbook. She builds a short reel and a one-page profile, and checks what her accounts show.

How it helps

A coach can see her form in a minute. A clean profile removes one reason to hesitate. It does not replace times.

Compare, then sign

What was happening

Nia has more than one answer. The offers differ in aid, fees and terms. Her parents want to see them side by side before anyone signs.
