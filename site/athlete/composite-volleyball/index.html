Volleyball · D2 · Composite story

Ava picked volleyball late. Was it too late?

A composite story built from sourced paths. Ava is not a real person. Here is how a family in her spot might work the calendar, with The Commit Book beside them.

Sophomore year

Two sports, then one choice

What was happening

Ava played two sports into high school and only picked volleyball as a sophomore. Her parents worry it is too late. They also wonder whether she has to move far from home to play. The sources behind this story describe a two-sport athlete who specialized late.

What they did

They start with the calendar for her sport and what each window means. The Camp ROI Playbook (camp-roi, $15) lays out camp types and when recruiting talk opens by sport. They also plan a season of film using the Season & Film Planner (season-planner), which is coming soon.

How it helps

A late start is easier to plan once the dates are on paper. It does not tell you whether a coach will want her.

Sophomore spring

A short video, made on purpose

What was happening

Ava has good footage but it is scattered across phones. Her mom and dad want one short reel a coach can watch in a couple of minutes.

What they did

They cut a reel with a clear opening and a one-page profile, following the Highlight Reel & Social Playbook (highlight-reel-social, $15). They also tidy her public social posts before any coach looks.

How it helps

A clean reel and profile save a coach time. Coaches still decide whom to answer.

Summer after sophomore year

Contacting programs within driving distance

What was happening

Under the rule this story draws on (D2 Bylaws 13.1.1.1, 13.3.1.2), Division II calls, texts and emails are allowed at any time. In-person contact and paid official visits open June 15 after sophomore year. Staying close to home is a deliberate choice for Ava, as the sources describe for another athlete.

What they did

They list programs within a few hours' drive and email the staffs. The First Contact Pack (coach-emails, $9) is coming soon with 12 templates. The Contact Log (contact-log) is also coming soon and is built to track who answered and when to follow up.

How it helps

A tracked list means no coach goes unanswered by accident. The rule guarantees nothing. Check it at the source.

Junior year

Camps that cost money, and a budget for them

What was happening

Camps are where many staffs see players in person. They also add up fast. Ava's family has a budget and does not want to spend it on events that do not fit her goals.

What they did

They pick camps by who will be there and what the trip costs, then plan the follow-up note ahead of time. The Camp ROI Playbook (camp-roi) has a cost budget and a post-camp follow-up section.

How it helps

A budget keeps the family from chasing every event. A camp does not promise an offer.

Senior fall

A partial scholarship, read carefully

What was happening

A Division II program offers Ava a partial scholarship. A source behind this story describes a recruit with few options who took a D2 partial-scholarship offer. Partial means the family still has costs to compare, and a verbal offer is not a signed one.

What they did

They read what is verbal and what is written, and they ask the coach about fees, housing and what happens if plans change. The Offers & Signing Day playbook (offer-and-signing, $19) covers written-offer dates and signing periods. The Offer Comparison Toolkit (offer-comparison) is coming soon.

How it helps

Questions asked early are easier than questions asked after signing. This is not legal advice.

Decision time

A program a few hours from home

What was happening

Ava picks a Division II program a few hours from home with a partial scholarship. In this composite she is a rotation player, then a starter as a senior. Ordinary, and a real destination.

What they did

Before signing, they screen the program for anything that would make them walk away. Red Flags: The Checklist (red-flags) is coming soon and has 40 flags in four groups: money, playing time, academic fit and treatment.

How it helps

A checklist helps a family say what worried them out loud. It cannot predict how a program will treat a player.
