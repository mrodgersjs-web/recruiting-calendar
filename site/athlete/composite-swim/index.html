Swimming · D2 · Composite story

Maya's times were good. Nobody knew yet.

A composite story built from sourced paths. Maya is invented. The steps come from real recruiting patterns, and nothing here promises a result.

Sophomore spring

Good times, no phone calls

What was happening

Maya is a club swimmer at a mid-size public high school. Her times drop every season. They are solid, but not at the top of national lists. Her parents ask what level these times fit.

What they did

They start a Contact Log. Every coach, school, date and reply goes in one grid (offering: contact-log). It is coming soon, so they sketch the same grid on paper first.

How it helps

One grid shows the family what is actually happening. It does not make a coach reply.

Summer before junior year

A times sheet that is easy to forward

What was happening

Maya's family learns that coaches weigh steady time drops and where those times would place in their conference. D2 programs may call, text and email at any time. In-person contact and paid visits open June 15 after sophomore year (D2 Bylaws 13.1.1.1, 13.3.1.2).

What they did

They build one clean times sheet with her best swims and the dates. They use the First Contact Pack to write a short note with her key time in it (offering: coach-emails). It is coming soon.

How it helps

A coach can read the sheet in under a minute. That is the only goal of the note.

Junior fall

The club coach makes the call

What was happening

Swim recruiting often runs through the club coach, a pattern a Wabash College story describes. Maya's coach knows her training and her work ethic. Coaches trust what another coach tells them.

What they did

Her parents ask the club coach to call a short list of programs. They line up the meets worth watching with the Season & Film Planner (offering: season-planner). It is coming soon.

How it helps

The coach does the part a parent cannot. The family keeps the schedule straight.

Junior winter

Widen the list

What was happening

Maya's family does what a basketball family in the same spot did: send information to many Division II and Division III programs, not only Division I. The list is longer than they first expected.

What they did

They send her sheet to every program where her times would score in the conference. They use the Camp ROI Playbook to decide which one or two camps are worth the money (offering: camp-roi, $15).

How it helps

A budget keeps camps from turning into a pile of receipts.

Senior fall

Two interested coaches, one decision

What was happening

Replies come back. One program shows steady interest and her times would score in its conference. Another is polite but quiet. The family now has something to compare.

What they did

They lay the offers side by side with the Offer Comparison & Negotiation Toolkit (offering: offer-comparison). It is coming soon. For written-offer dates they use Offers & Signing Day (offering: offer-and-signing, $19).

How it helps

A grid shows tuition, fees and aid in the same units. The decision stays the family's.

Signing

A level where she competes right away

What was happening

In this composite, Maya picks a Division II program where her times would score and the coach keeps showing up. For her it is the destination, not a consolation. The ending is illustrative, not typical.

What they did

They write the choice down. They keep the Contact Log going so every other coach gets a thank-you (offering: contact-log). It is coming soon.
