# W26 - the carousel leads with the four written playbooks

Mike, 11 Nov 2026: "We have covered Woman's volleyball, Men's Football, and both Men and Women's
basketball. On the webage, the carousel of athletes, these 4 should be the first 4 shown - Athletes
1 thru 4. The other sports are pending based on products being built. For those athletes, grey them
out in color scheme and put a PENDING over and across the atheles. Do not allow them to be clicked
on, only the 4 main sports we just finished."

## What changed

Three files, all on the home page's athlete ring (`<story-ring>`).

`site/assets/ring.js`

- `builtInfo(a)` is now the single source of truth for what is finished. Volleyball reads
  "Women's Volleyball", football "Men's Football", basketball "Men's Basketball" or
  "Women's Basketball" by athlete id (`WOMENS_BB`), everything else is pending and keeps its plain
  sport name. A new athlete added to a written sport goes live automatically; nothing is keyed to a
  name.
- `orderBuilt(list)` puts the four written sports first: Ava (women's volleyball), Marcus (men's
  football), Derrick (men's basketball), Jasmine (women's basketball). The other athletes in those
  three sports follow as clickable, then every pending sport. No athlete was dropped.
- The opening card picks the first written sport instead of the old hardcoded Marcus lookup.
- Pending cards carry `is-pending`, the sport line reads the built label, and a `PENDING` strip is
  rendered inside the card. Ticks dim and say "Pending: <sport>". The live region, the who-line and
  the sport button all say the playbook is still being written, and the button is `disabled`.
- Every exit path is closed for pending athletes: the front card click, the in-ring card click, the
  Enter key on the stage, the hover cursor state, and keyboard focus (`tabIndex -1`). Dragging, the
  arrows and the ticks still move the ring, so a greyed athlete can still be looked at.
- Helper line under the title now reads "... Grey cards are sports whose playbooks are still being
  written."

`site/assets/ring.css` - nine rules: greyed art (`grayscale(1)` at 55% opacity), dimmed type,
`cursor:default`, the full-bleed `PENDING` strip (rotated -14 deg, wider than the card, white
letters on a 66%-black band, `pointer-events:none`), dimmed ticks, disabled button.

`site/index.html` - cache keys. `md5[:8]` was stale for five of the seven versioned assets, so
returning visitors were being served cached copies of files that had already changed. All seven now
match the bytes on disk.

## Why the ring and not the data

`site/assets/stories.js` is regenerated by `v3/W2-site/data_build.py` and the W14 scripts, so an
edit there can be overwritten. The order and the pending rule live in `ring.js`, which is only ever
edited by hand.

## Files

- `patch_ring.py` - the edit itself. Every replacement is asserted and the script is re-runnable
  (it reports "already applied"), so it can be replayed on a fresh clone.
- `check.py` - the gate: cache keys match the files, athletes 1-4 are the four written playbooks,
  pending sports sit last, the styles exist, `.card` does not clip the strip, all six exit paths are
  guarded, no "coming soon" filler. Passed 2026-11-11.
- `pixels.py` - measures the same rectangle on the front card in two screenshots.

### Derrick's figure

An image API call returned `403 Forbidden`, so no new art. The original male basketball illustration
was still in git history: `ab44c79` deleted `cut-derrick-640.webp` and `cut-derrick-1080.webp` when
the ring was redrawn, so both files came back with `git checkout ab44c79^ --`. Verified against the
house art rules rather than by eye:

- 640x1137 and 1080x1919, aspect 0.5629 against a 9:16 target; the same ratio as every other figure,
  so the card's `object-position` still frames him the same way.
- transparent cutout: the figure covers 35% of the canvas, no chroma-green residue left (0.000%).
- it is a different picture from the women's figure, not a copy of it, and on the rendered card the
  two differ by a mean channel delta of 48.5 out of 255.
- the ball reads: 15% of the opaque pixels are basketball orange.

Both sizes are back because `deck.js` swaps `-640` for `-1080` at runtime; only serving one would
break the mobile story deck.
