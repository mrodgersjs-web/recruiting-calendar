# NCAA Division I Football Recruiting — 2026-27 (and the class of 2029)

**Check date: Wednesday, November 11, 2026 (America/Denver).** All documents below were retrieved and read today; PDF footers read `11/11/26`.

**Short answer on the big structural question you asked me to watch for:** yes. As of **October 7, 2026**, the NCAA **pulled the sport-specific recruiting calendars out of the bylaws** (Bylaw 13.17 now points to NCAA.org), and football calendars are now **ratified by the football oversight committees (FBSOC / FCSOC), not by the NCAA Council**, under a "Recruiting Calendar as Policy Pilot." The bylaw framework (signing dates, contact limits, visit rules) still lives in the bylaws; the **day-by-day calendar lives on ncaa.org**. Both the Oct 2024 NLI news story *and* current rule are cited below, and I flag where2024 material is only background.

### FINDING 1 — Who sets the football recruiting calendar now (governance)
- **RULE:** Bylaw 13.17 Recruiting Calendars (Revised: 10/7/26); "Recruiting Calendar as Policy Pilot" (D1 Council, Oct 2024)
- **SOURCE:** [2026-27 NCAA Bylaws](https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008) (p. 121); [Policy Pilot PDF](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2024-25/OCT2024D1GOV_RecruitingCalendarsasPolicyResource.pdf)
- **QUOTE (bylaw):** "For access to recruiting calendars and recruiting periods for other sports, refer to the recruiting calendars published on ncaa.org."
- **QUOTE (pilot):** "Football: NCAA Division I Football Bowl Subdivision Oversight Committee (FBSOC) and NCAA Division I Football Championship Subdivision Oversight Committee (FCSOC). During the pilot program, changes to recruiting calendars will not require formal legislative action… limited to adjustments to currently legislated periods of recruiting activities."
- **Practical:** Football calendars are ratified by FBSOC/FCSOC and published on NCAA.org in **July** for football ("July: NCAA staff publish the recruiting calendars on NCAA.org following ratification"). Any new period (like a brand-new "Fall Period") would still need legislative action.

### FINDING 2 — First contact (phone, email, DM) and first off-campus contact
- **RULE:** 13.1.1.1.6 Exception -- Football [FBS/FCS]; 13.1.3.1 Time Period for Telephone Calls -- General Rule; 13.02.5.5 Dead Period
- **SOURCE:** 2026-27 NCAA Bylaws (pp. 58-59, 66)
- **QUOTE:** "In football, off-campus recruiting contacts shall not be made with an individual (or the individual's family members) **before January 1 of the individual's junior year in high school**." (13.1.1.1.6)
- **QUOTE:** "Telephone calls to an individual (or the individual's family members) may not be made **before June 15 at the conclusion of the individual's sophomore year in high school**… Thereafter, an institution may make telephone calls to the prospective student-athlete at its discretion." (13.1.3.1)
- **QUOTE:** "It remains permissible, however, for an institutional staff member to **communicate with (other than in person)** a prospective student-athlete during a dead period." (13.02.5.5)
- **Note on DM:** email/text/DM is "communication," not "contact" (a "contact" is a face-to-face encounter, 13.02.4), so a coach may text/email from **June 15 after sophomore year** and may keep doing so during dead periods. **UNVERIFIED:** I could not locate a bylaw expressly titled "observed communication" in the current manual (that label does not appear in the 2026-27 text); the nearest current provisions are 13.1.3 (telephone), 13.1.6 (contact restrictions at specified sites), 13.1.5.13 (no in-person contact on the signing day) and 13.10.1.x (social media).

### FINDING 3 — The "July rule tied to the report-day date": NOT CONFIRMED
- **STATUS:** **UNVERIFIED.** There is no current bylaw with a July date tied to a "report day." I searched the full 2026-27 Bylaws text (412 pages) for "July," "report day," "reporting date," and every football bylaw; and read the FBSOC (Jul 2026, Feb 2026), FCSOC (Jun 2026, Aug 2026) and D1 Cabinet (Apr 2026, Oct 2026) reports. No such rule exists.
- **Three nearest real items (all verified):**
  1. **Bylaw 13.16.2.1 (U.S. service academies only):** "authorized staff members may make telephonic and in-person, off-campus contacts **beginning July 1 before a prospective student-athlete's junior year** in high school… except, in football, authorized staff members may make in-person off-campus contact… during the **spring contact period** of the prospective student-athlete's junior year." (Bylaws p. 121)
  2. **The July 2026 FBSOC decision that ties the calendar to a reporting window:** "The 2027 recruiting calendar will be modified to include a **quiet period beginning January 1 through the final day of the notification of transfer period**. After the quiet period, the remainder of January will be a contact period." ([FBSOC July 23, 2026 report](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/championships/sports/football/d1/FBSOC/JULY2026D1FBSOC_Report.pdf)) **My inference (not an NCAA statement):** if "report day" means the notification-of-transfer reporting window, this is the rule you mean — the football contact period each year starts the day after the transfer-portal window closes, and the decision was taken in July.
  3. **July is also the publication month** for football calendars under the pilot process (Policy Pilot, p. 2).
- **Do not use:** any 2024-era article claiming a fixed "July 1 recruiting date" for football. Football's first-contact date is **January 1 of the recruit's junior year**.

### FINDING 5 — The "Fall Period" and September/October restrictions
- **RULE:** 13.02.5.1 (contact), 13.02.5.2 (evaluation), 13.02.5.5 (dead), 13.7.1.5, 13.1.7.4–13.1.7.4.1
- **SOURCE:** Bylaws pp. 54, 75, 93; FCSOC June 22-23, 2026 report
- **FINDING (FBS):** September and October 2026 fall in the **Evaluation Period (SEP 1 – NOV 28)**. Unofficial visits with athletics involvement **are permitted** in football "at any time" (13.7.1.5), but during an evaluation period no in-person **off-campus** contact may occur (13.02.5.2), and a coach gets **one evaluation per recruit per year** against the 33-day limit. The FBSOC "recommended maintaining the existing fall evaluation period for the 2027 recruiting calendar."
- **FINDING (FCS):** the fall period became a **contact period** for 2026-27: "amend the 2026-27 FCS recruiting calendar to **change the fall evaluation period (September 1 through the Sunday following Thanksgiving) to a contact period**." The matching legislation (effective 1/1/27) permits **one recruiting opportunity during the Fall Contact period, "provided there is no contact with the prospective student-athlete on the day of the contest."**
- **UNVERIFIED:** I found **no** bylaw imposing a special cap on unofficial visits in September/October specifically. The constraints are the period definitions plus13.7.1.5's cross-reference to "recruiting calendar restrictions (see Bylaws 13.17.2, 13.17.3 and 13.17.6)" — and those sub-bylaws **no longer exist** (they were removed when calendars moved to ncaa.org on 10/7/26). That dangling cross-reference is a real drafting gap.

### FINDING 6 — Official (paid) visits for football
- **RULE:** 13.6.2.1.7 Football; 13.6.2.1.7.1 Exception -- Home Football Contest; 13.6.2.2; 13.6.2.2.1
- **SOURCE:** 2026-27 NCAA Bylaws (p. 89); [D1 Cabinet April 14-15, 2026 report](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/committees/d1/cabinet/Apr2026D1Cabinet_Apr14Report.pdf); [FBSOC Feb 24-25, 2026 report](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/committees/d1/fbsfboc/FEB2026D1FBSOC_Report.pdf)
- **QUOTE (earliest date):** "In football, an institution may provide an individual an expense-paid visit **beginning April 1 of an individual's junior year through the Sunday before the last Wednesday in June**. However, an institution shall not provide an individual an expense-paid visit in conjunction with the individual's participation in an institutional camp or clinic. **Thereafter**, an institution may provide an expense-paid visit **beginning September 1 of the individual's senior year** in high school."
- **QUOTE (August exception):** "In August of a prospective student-athlete's senior year… an institution may provide an expense-paid visit… **48 hours before through 48 hours after** an institution's home contest."
- **Limit per school:** "An institution may finance **one visit** to its campus for a prospective student-athlete **before October 15** following completion of high school and **one visit beginning October 15**… including a visit related to a possible transfer." (13.6.2.2) Exception: one additional visit after a new head coach is hired (13.6.2.2.1). NCAA's own plain-language page: "Each school may provide only one official visit, unless a second visit is allowed after a head coaching change."
- **Annual institutional cap: ELIMINATED.** The old 70-visit institutional cap was suspended by blanket waiver and then removed: "In football, to eliminate the annual institutional limit on official visits. Previously adopted by the Division I Football Oversight Committees pending review by the Cabinet. **No action taken by the Cabinet. Adopted (Final).**" (April 14-15, 2026 Cabinet report). Blanket waivers permitting unlimited football official visits also run **through March 31, 2027**.
- **Limit during the Fall Period:** **none specific.** In September/October of a recruit's senior year, official visits are open again (Sept 1) and are bounded only by the one-per-school rule and the calendar's period definitions (a dead period bars visits entirely, 13.02.5.5).

### FINDING 7 — Signing: what replaced the National Letter of Intent
- **RULE:** 13.02.13 Athletics Aid Agreement and Settlement Related Benefits Agreement Signing Dates; 13.9 Letter-of-Intent Programs, Financial Aid Agreements and Offers; 13.9.3.2; 13.9.3.3; 13.1.1.2 / 13.1.1.2.1 (contact prohibition after signing)
- **SOURCE:** 2026-27 NCAA Bylaws (pp. 55-56, 59, 100); [D1 Council Oct 8-9, 2024 report](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/committees/d1/council/Oct2024D1Council_Report.pdf); [NCAA news, Oct 9, 2024](https://www.ncaa.org/news/2024/10/9/media-center-di-council-approves-changes-to-notification-of-transfer-windows-in-basketball-football.aspx)
- **What replaced the NLI:** the **athletics aid agreement** (and, for schools using it, a **settlement related benefits agreement**). The NLI program itself was eliminated; the *signing-date formula* was retained and applied to those written offers.
- **QUOTE (2024 Council action, background only — now codified):** "Moving forward, written offers of athletics aid will replace the NLI, and the previous formula for determining signing dates will be applied to those written offers… After a prospect signs a written offer of athletics aid, other schools that offer athletically related financial aid will be prohibited from recruiting communications with that prospect."
- **Current bylaw (13.1.1.2):** "An athletics staff member or other representative… shall not communicate or make contact with a prospective student-athlete who has signed a written offer of athletics aid with another NCAA Division I or Division II institution… A violation of this provision shall be processed pursuant to Bylaw 19.1.4."
- **Is there still a national signing day?** **Yes for football, but it is not called that and it is not a fixed date.** Bylaw 13.02.13.1 sets formulas; the NCAA publishes the resulting dates. **There is no "national signing day" event rule.** What exists: an **early football signing period** and a **regular (spring) signing period** beginning on a formula date.

### FINDING 10 — How many players a school may sign, and taking a spot away
- **RULE:** 13.02.13.1, Figure 13-2; 16.13.1.5; 17.2 Roster Limitations; 15.3.4.1; 15.3.4.2; 13.1.1.2.1
- **SOURCE:** 2026-27 NCAA Bylaws (pp. 123, 163, 193)
- **No cap on signees per period.** The NCAA fixes only the opening date; the final date is "based on institutional policy for awarding scholarships." The real limit is the roster cap: "**Football105**" (Bylaw 17.2, adopted6/6/25 effective 7/1/25) and "there is **no limit on the number of new athletics scholarships that may be awarded** consistent with the roster limit for a particular sport" (16.13.1.5). So a school may sign as many players in a period as fit under105.
- **Taking a spot away after signing is tightly limited (15.3.4.1):** aid may be reduced or canceled only if the recipient "(a) Is rendered ineligible…; (b) Fraudulently misrepresents any information on an application, letter of intent or financial aid agreement; (c) Engages in serious misconduct warranting substantial disciplinary penalty…; (d) Voluntarily… withdraws from a sport at any time for personal reasons; (e) Violates a nonathletically related condition…; or (f) Provides written notification of transfer… or is provided a release of the contact prohibition associated with signing an athletics aid agreement." A hearing is required before reduction (15.3.4.1.1).
- **NOT permitted (15.3.4.2):** "may not be reduced or canceled during the period of its award: (a) On the basis of a student-athlete's athletics ability, performance or contribution to a team's success; (b) Because of an injury, illness, or physical or mental medical condition…; or (c) For any other athletics reason."
- **Also relevant:** once a recruit signs, other schools cannot contact them (13.1.1.2) until one of five conditions ends the prohibition, including a granted release (13.1.1.2.1) — the family's release is the practical lever if a school wants to reopen recruiting.
