## FINDING 1 — What replaced the National Letter of Intent (DI and DII), when, the website, the bylaw name, and the football windows

**FINDING:** The NLI program no longer exists. Division I killed it on **October 9, 2024** (effective immediately); Division II did the same on **October 21-22, 2024** (announced October 23, 2024, also effective immediately). The document that replaced it is the **written offer of athletics aid** (plus a **settlement related benefits agreement**), and the bylaw that now controls signing dates is NCAA's own. The NLI's own website is dead: `nationalletter.org` still has Azure DNS nameservers but **no host record** (DNS check, Nov 11, 2026: "no data of the requested type was found"; HTTP HEAD failed), and the Wayback Machine's closest capture is **March 1, 2025**.

**RULE:**
- **DI Bylaw 13.9** heading is now: *"Letter-of-Intent Programs, Financial Aid Agreements and Offers."*
- **DI Bylaw 13.9.3 Letter of Intent Restriction:** *"An institution may not participate in an institutional or conference athletics letter-of-intent program or issue an institutional or conference financial aid agreement or settlement related benefit agreement that involves a signing date other than those set forth in Bylaw 13.02.13."*
- **DI Bylaw 13.02.13** *"Athletics Aid Agreement and Settlement Related Benefits Agreement Signing Dates"* (Revised: 10/9/24, 10/8/25). **DI 13.02.13.1:** (a) Basketball Early Signing Period — the second Wednesday (7 a.m.) in November through the following Wednesday; (b) Basketball Regular Signing — the Wednesday (7 a.m.) in April one week after the NCAA Men's and Women's Basketball Championships; (c) **Football Early Signing Period** — the Wednesday (7 a.m.) before the first Saturday in December through the immediately following Friday; (d) Football Midyear Two-Year College Transfers — the Wednesday (7 a.m.) before the first Saturday in December; (e) **Football Regular Signing** — the first Wednesday (7 a.m.) in February; (f) All Other Sports — the second Wednesday (7 a.m.) in November.
- **DII Bylaw 13.02.10** *"Institutional Athletics Aid Agreement Signing Dates"* (Revised: 10/22/24), with **13.02.10.1(a)-(c)** (football midyear 2YC transfers; football regular = first Wednesday in February; all other sports = second Wednesday in November).

**2026-27 dates (NCAA.org recruiting page, Nov 11, 2026):** DI basketball early **Nov 11-18, 2026**; DI basketball regular **April 14, 2027**; DI football early **Dec 2-4, 2026**; DI/II football regular **Feb 3, 2027**; DI/II midyear 2YC transfers **Dec 2, 2026**; all other DI/II sports **Nov 11, 2026**.

**Football difference:** Yes — **both an early period and a spring window still exist for football**, and **the NCAA now sets those dates by bylaw formula** (DI 13.02.13.1(c),(e)) rather than the NLI program (which was administered by the Conference Commissioners Association). **DII has no football early period** — only the midyear two-year-college date (Wed before the first Saturday in December) and the regular period (first Wednesday in February).

**WHAT IT BINDS:** The athletics aid agreement itself. Nothing a family signs in 2026-27 is called an NLI. **Caution:** the early period is a *signing window*, not a date for the school to write the offer — DI 13.9.3.3 forbids any written football offer before **November 15** of senior year, and DI 13.9.3.2 forbids issuing an agreement "to be signed before the early football signing period."

## FINDING 2 — DI Bylaw 13.9.3.1: when a school may put aid in writing, by sport

**FINDING:** 13.9.3.1 governs **sports other than football**; football was split out in2025. The three-way split a family must track is **offer date** vs **signing window**, and it differs by sport.

**RULE:**
- **DI 13.9.3.1 Written Offer of Aid or Settlement Related Benefits Agreement Before Signing Date -- Sports Other Than Football:** *"In sports other than football, before August 1 of an individual's senior year in high school, an institution shall not, directly or indirectly, provide a written offer of athletically related financial aid or settlement-related benefits agreement (see Bylaw 16.13) or indicate in writing that such aid or benefits will be offered... On or after August 1... the institution may indicate in writing that an athletically related grant-in-aid... will be offered...; however, the institution may not permit the prospective student-athlete to sign a form indicating acceptance of such an award... other than during the early signing period or on or after the regular signing date in the applicable sport (see Bylaw 13.02.13)."* (Revised: 8/5/04, 4/29/10, 3/3/11, 10/9/24, 10/8/25)
- **DI 13.9.3.2 Early Signing Period Prohibition -- Football:** *"An institution shall not issue an athletics aid agreement... that is to be signed before the early football signing period to a prospective student-athlete who will participate in football."* (Adopted: 10/9/24, Revised: 10/8/25)
- **DI 13.9.3.3 ... -- Football:** *"In football, before November 15 of an individual's senior year in high school, an institution shall not, directly or indirectly, provide a written offer of athletically related financial aid... On or after November 15... may indicate in writing... however, the institution may not permit the prospective student-athlete to sign... other than during the early signing period or on or after the regular signing date in football."* (Adopted: 10/8/25)
- **DI 13.9.2 Requirements for Written Offer:** Eligibility Center registration + institutional request list + completion of the athletics eligibility certification questionnaire before any written offer.
- **DI 13.9.3.1.1 Nontraditional Academic Calendars** (offer may be indicated on/after opening day of classes of senior year; signing still barred before the sport's initial signing date).

**How it differs by sport:** **Football** — offer on/after **Nov 15**, sign in the Dec 2-4, 2026 early period or Feb 3, 2027 regular period. **Basketball** — offer on/after **Aug 1** (same as every other sport; basketball is *not* in the football carve-out), but sign in the **Nov 11-18, 2026 early period** or the **April 14, 2027 regular period** (DI 13.02.13.1(a),(b)). **All other sports** — offer on/after **Aug 1**, sign on/after **Nov 11, 2026**.

**SOURCE:** 2026-27 NCAA Division I Bylaws, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008 — Bylaws 13.9, 13.9.2, 13.9.3, 13.9.3.1, 13.9.3.1.1, 13.9.3.2, 13.9.3.3, 13.02.13, 13.02.13.1

**WHAT IT BINDS:** An August 1 (or November 15) letter is a *promise of a future offer*, not a scholarship commitment. The binding act is the signature on the athletics aid agreement inside that sport's window. Nothing else in the process is enforceable.

## FINDING 3 — Division II award letters: when issued, what they guarantee, and the multi-year rules

**FINDING:** DII's binding document is the **institutional athletics aid agreement** (the "financial aid award letter"). It cannot be signed before the sport's initial signing date; the school may indicate in writing earlier that a grant-in-aid will be offered. Once signed, it guarantees **one academic year** of aid, the terms of which cannot be cut mid-period — and **renewal is never automatic**.

**RULE:**
- **DII 13.6.4 Offer of Aid Before Signing Date:** *"An institution may indicate in writing to a prospective student-athlete that an athletically related grant-in-aid will be offered by the institution; however, the institution may not permit the prospective student-athlete to sign a form indicating their acceptance of such an award before the initial-signing date in that sport."* (Revised: 10/22/24)
- **DII 13.6.1** (Eligibility Center registration + institutional request list) and **13.6.2** (transcript required before a written offer); **13.6.5** no in-person, off-campus contact on the day of signing.
- **DII 15.5.2.3 Written Statement Requirement:** the awarding agency *"shall give the recipient a written statement of the amount, duration, conditions and terms of the award,"* signed/electronically authorized by the regular financial aid authority — *"The signature of the athletics director, attesting to the committee's award, does not satisfy this requirement."*
- **DII 15.5.2.2:** *"the institution shall be committed for the term of the original award, even if the student-athlete's physical condition prevents them from participating."*
- **DII 15.5.4.3.3 Decrease Not Permitted:** no decrease from the moment the student signs the financial aid award letter until the conclusion of the agreement period.
- **DII 15.5.4.3.2:** no athletically-related condition (e.g., aid contingent on specified performance or position).
- **DII 15.5.4.1 / 15.5.4.1.1 / 15.5.4.1.2:** the only grounds for reduction/cancellation (ineligibility, fraud, misconduct, voluntary withdrawal, written transfer notification); non-athletically-related conditions (e.g., academic or team standards) are permitted.
- **DII 15.5.2.4:** hearing opportunity, heard by an entity outside athletics, on30 days' notice.
- **Multi-year:** **DII 15.5.3.1 One-Year Period:** *"Athletics aid shall neither be awarded in excess of one academic year nor for a period less than one academic year."* **DII 15.5.3.1.2 Financial Aid Authority Precedent:** a staff member may say the athletics department will recommend renewal each year for four years, *"however, the prospective student-athlete must be informed that the renewal will not be automatic."* **DII 15.5.5.1:** renewal made on or before July 1, notified in writing by the institution's regular financial aid authority, **not** by athletics.
- **CORRECTION TO THE PREMISE — DII has no head-count sports.** All DII sports are equivalency-limited: **DII 15.4.2.1.1** (men: Football 36.0, Basketball 10.0, Baseball 9.0, Ice Hockey 13.5, Volleyball 4.5, Gymnastics 5.4, ...) and **DII 15.4.2.1.2** (women: Basketball 10.0, Softball 7.2, Ice Hockey 18.0, Volleyball 8.0, Gymnastics 6.0, ...), with an overall ceiling of **60 awards across all men's sports other than football and basketball** (**DII 15.4.2.1.1.1**). Head-count sports and multi-year grant-in-aid agreements are a **Division I** structure (**DI 15.3.2.2**: *"a written statement of the amount, duration, conditions and terms of the award"* for *"a regular academic year or multiple regular academic years"*; **DI 15.3.2.3.1** Reduction of a Multiyear Award).

**SOURCE:** 2026-27 NCAA Division II Bylaws, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90010 — Bylaws 13.6.1-13.6.5, 13.02.10.1, 15.4.2.1.1, 15.4.2.1.2, 15.4.2.1.1.1, 15.5.2.2, 15.5.2.3, 15.5.2.4, 15.5.3.1, 15.5.3.1.2, 15.5.4.1, 15.5.4.1.2, 15.5.4.3.2, 15.5.4.3.3, 15.5.5.1; and DI Bylaws 15.3.2.2, 15.3.2.3.1 at https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008

**WHAT IT BINDS:** The school is bound to pay the stated amount for one academic year on the stated terms; the athlete is bound to enroll and report to the team, with the recruiting contact prohibition attaching. **Nothing is guaranteed beyond year one**, and a coach who says "four years" is permitted only if the athlete is told renewal is not automatic.

## FINDING 4 — Division III: the aid letter, the admissions-deposit reality, and the ban on letters of intent

**FINDING:** DIII **bans letters of intent outright** and has **no athletic scholarship and no athletic aid agreement at all**. The "aid letter" is the ordinary institutional financial aid letter, and the real commitment is the **admission offer plus enrollment deposit**.

**RULE:**
- **DIII 13.9.1 Letter-of-Intent Prohibition:** *"An institution shall not use any form of a letter of intent or similar form of commitment in the recruitment of a prospective student-athlete."* (Revised: 1/10/05, 12/23/09). **DIII 13.9.1.1** permits only the NCAA national office's **standard, nonbinding athletics celebratory signing form**, used after acceptance for enrollment; **13.9.1.1.1** it may not be signed on campus and no institutional staff may be present. **DIII 20.11.5** repeats the prohibition for membership purposes.
- **DIII 15.01.3 Criteria for Institutional Financial Aid:** *"A member institution shall not award financial aid to any student on the basis of athletics leadership, ability, participation or performance."* **DIII 15.03.1(a)** the package may not consider athletics ability; **15.03.1(b)** procedures must be the same as all students; **15.03.1(c)** the package cannot be *"clearly distinguishable from the general pattern of all financial aid for all recipients."* **DIII 15.02.1** *"Administered By"* — aid must be awarded by the institution's regular financial aid committee or agency.
- **The admissions-deposit reality:** **DIII 13.02.10.5 Exception -- After Receipt of Financial Deposit:** *"On or after January 1 of a prospective student-athlete's senior year in high school, there shall be no limit on the forms of electronically transmitted correspondence that may be sent by an institution to a prospective student-athlete, provided the institution has received a financial deposit in response to the institution's offer of admission or for institutions that do not require a paid acceptance or financial deposit, the institution has received a commitment from the prospective student-athlete that is required of all incoming students."* (Adopted: 10/19/10, Revised: 1/17/23, 2/19/24) — the same deposit trigger appears at **DIII 13.10.3.1**.

**SOURCE:** 2026-27 NCAA Division III Bylaws, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90011 — Bylaws 13.9.1, 13.9.1.1, 13.9.1.1.1, 13.02.10.5, 13.10.3.1, 15.01.1-15.01.8, 15.02.1, 15.02.2, 15.03.1, 20.11.5

**WHAT IT BINDS:** The admission offer and the deposit, and a financial aid package governed by the same rules as every other student. There is no athletic commitment to breach and no athletic penalty for leaving.

## FINDING 5 — NAIA: are letters of intent enforced, and what to check in the school's own document

**FINDING:** **No.** NAIA has no letter-of-intent program and will not enforce one against either party. Some NAIA conferences impose their own intra-conference recognition rules.

**RULE / NAIA's own words:**
- NAIA Legislative Services, *Implications of Letters of Intent:* *"The NAIA as a national office does not recognize these documents and will not enforce them against either party. That is, the NAIA will not make a student stay enrolled at an institution if they signed an intent document, nor will the NAIA force a school to honor a financial aid agreement... There is no document the NAIA recognizes that will identify a student with a school. The only way a student can identify with an institution is by enrolling in 12 institutional credit hours or representing an NAIA institution in competition."* Also: *"Can a student sign a letter of intent and still participate in the recruiting process? Yes... The NAIA's bylaws do not prohibit a high school athlete from being recruited or pursuing other schools until they are identified on campus."* And: *"if a student signs a letter of intent but failed to meet the academics requirements... you would need to refer to the document and to your school to see if this would nullify your contract."*
- NAIA, *Parents* page: *"As an association, the NAIA does not have a letter of intent program. Student-athletes may sign letters of intent with an individual NAIA school, however, they aren't obligated to attend that institution. Be aware that some NAIA conferences require their member schools to recognize letters of intent that are signed with other institutions within the conference. Check with the prospective school to see if any conference programs apply."*
- NAIA, *Guide for the College-Bound Student-Athlete* (May 2026): same language; and *"Student-athletes may sign letters of intent with an individual NAIA school, however, they aren't obligated to attend that institution."*

**WHAT A FAMILY MUST CHECK IN THE SCHOOL'S OWN DOCUMENT** (NAIA Guide's own checklist): *"What types of financial-aid agreements does your school have? Which campus representatives have to sign off on the agreement for it to be valid? What will the financial-aid agreement cover? Housing? Food? Books? Tuition? How long will the agreement last? A quarter/semester? A full academic year?"* Plus: any **conference** transfer-recognition rule, and whether the school will honor aid if the athlete changes schools or loses eligibility. NAIA also notes member institutions set aid themselves: *"Decisions regarding the amount of aid awarded—and any adjustments to that aid—are made solely by the institution through its established financial aid processes."*

**SOURCE:** https://interpretations.naia.org/implications-of-letters-of-intent/ (NAIA Legislative Services Interpretations Repository); https://www.naia.org/findyourpath/parents/index; https://www.naia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/naia_guide_college_bound_student-1.pdf; https://www.naia.org/student-athletes/prospective/high-school-students

**WHAT IT BINDS:** At the NAIA level, **nothing**. Any binding force comes from the institution's or conference's own document — which is exactly why the four checklist questions above matter.

## FINDING 6 — NJCAA: letter-of-intent rules, signing date, one-year binding, and release

**FINDING:** NJCAA **kept** the letter of intent, and it is the *only* valid athletic-scholarship document. It binds for **one academic year (Aug 1 - Jul 31)**, and no other NJCAA school may recruit the athlete until a **release agreement** is executed and filed.

**RULE (2026-27 NJCAA Handbook - Bylaws):**
- **Article VI, Section 1, A.1.a:** *"The scholarship agreement must take the form of the NJCAA Letter of Intent."* **A.1.b Filing:** *"An NJCAA Letter of Intent must be electronically submitted in the NJCAA online Letter of Intent system for all each athletic scholarship recipients, prior to the expenditure of any funds or conveyance of benefits."*
- **Section 3, A.2 Signing Dates:** *"A student must have completed their junior year of high school to sign an NJCAA Letter of Intent for the subsequent year."* **A.2.a:** earliest dates set by sport on the NJCAA Sports Procedures Chart. **A.2.b:** *"Under no circumstance may a member institution enter into a scholarship agreement or contract of any type with a recruit prior to the official NJCAA Letter of Intent signing date for the applicable sport."*
- **A.3 Term:** *"The NJCAA Letter of Intent shall be awarded for a maximum of one (1) academic year (August 1 to July 31)."*
- **A.4** addenda must be attached to the LOI and concurrently signed; **A.5** the NJCAA LOI takes precedence over region/conference LOIs; **A.6** no funds may be expended before signing (except an official recruiting visit); **A.7** club teams may not use it; **A.8 Recruiting Prohibition** (staff may not meet or contact a recruit who signed with another institution); **A.9** signing a second LOI before the first release = *"immediately become ineligible to compete for one academic year."*
- **B.1 Signature Requirements:** the LOI is valid on the student's signature *"and parent's/legal guardian's signature if the student-athlete is under the age of 18"*; **B.1.a** the Athletic Director and President (or designee) must sign and date before it goes to the student; **B.1.b** the student (and parent if under 18) must sign **within 21 calendar days** of the LOI creation date.
- **2026-27 signing dates (2026-27 Sports Procedures Chart):** **Football (Fall) Men — LOI signing date Feb 3; 85 LOI signees/scholarships; supplemental eligibility roster deadline Oct 30.** **Basketball (Men/Women) — LOI signing date Nov 1; 15 LOI signees/scholarships.** Baseball (Spring) Men — Nov 1, 30. Most sports — Nov 1.
- **Release, Section 3 C:** *"A release agreement is required in order for a student-athlete who has previously signed an NJCAA Letter of Intent to contact other member institutions' athletic departments, including representatives of an institution's athletic interests."* **C.1:** a student may request release on (a) transfer to another NJCAA member institution, or (b) other non-transfer rationale (check "Other," explain). **C.2:** release must be signed by the student (and parent if under 18), the Athletic Director and the President. **C.2.a:** the student's signature is **not** required if the student fails to enroll, loses academic eligibility, graduates, withdraws, chooses not to participate during the academic year, signs an LOI with a non-member institution/university, or is disciplined for reasons unrelated to athletics (C.2.a(1)-(7)). **C.3:** *"The NJCAA Release Agreement must be electronically submitted to the NJCAA Letter of Intent system for a student-athlete to become immediately recruitable."* **C.4/C.4.a/C.4.b:** aid cancellation after release must be shown on the form and pro-rated; a released student who keeps aid must have it noted and the aid may not be reassigned to another athlete. **C.5 Unconditional Release:** *"The member institution may not add conditions or stipulations to the release agreement."* (C.5 continues onto the following page — **remainder of C.5 UNVERIFIED**, I stopped at the page break.)

**SOURCE:** NJCAA Handbook - Bylaws, 2026-27 (updated 10/27/26), https://ardor-prod-media.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/files/404933495157163011/cd9ce55b904f4165bc9d471570b0b763-NJCAA-Handbook---Bylaws---2026-27_10-27-26.pdf — Article VI Section 1, Section 3 A/B/C; 2026-2027 Sports Procedures Chart (9-9-26), https://media.ardorsportshub.com/files/404933495157163011/3fb3dae3297443a688333ba7d0cbef68-2026-2027-Sports-Procedures-Chart-9-9-26.pdf; NJCAA LOI portal page, https://www.njcaa.org/eligibility/loi (note: manual LOIs are not accepted beginning with the 2027-28 signing date)

**WHAT IT BINDS:** The school to a stated scholarship for **one academic year (Aug 1 - Jul 31)**; the athlete to that school for that year; and the athlete's recruiting status to the executed-and-filed release. A second signature without a release costs a season (A.9).

## FINDING 7 — What a "verbal commitment" is, and what a "preferred walk-on" offer is and is not

**FINDING:** Both are **NCAA recruiting terms, not NCAA bylaws.** The NCAA defines them in its own recruiting glossary; no bylaw defines, guarantees, or enforces either.

**RULE (NCAA's own words), *Guide for the College-Bounded Student-Athlete*, 2026-27 glossary "Recruiting Terms to Know":**
- **Verbal Commitment:** *"When you verbally agree to play sports for a college before you sign or are eligible to sign an athletics aid agreement. The commitment is not binding on you or the school."*
- **Preferred Walk-On:** *"'Preferred walk-on' status means a college coach wants you on the team but doesn't have a scholarship for you. You are guaranteed a spot on the roster."*
- **Walk-On:** *"Someone who is not typically recruited by a school to participate in sports and does not receive a scholarship from the school but who becomes a member of one of the school's athletics teams."*
- **Official Commitment:** *"When you sign an athletics aid agreement for a scholarship with a Division I or II school."*
- Cover warning: *"Walk-on and 'preferred' walk-on recruits must register with the Eligibility Center and meet initial-eligibility standards."*

**NCAA.org Recruiting FAQ:** *"A verbal commitment happens when you verbally agree to play sports for a college before signing or being eligible to sign. It is not binding for you or the school."*

**What a preferred walk-on offer IS:** a coach's commitment to a roster spot without athletic aid, plus initial-eligibility certification. **What it IS NOT:** a scholarship, a signed agreement, a financial commitment, or an NCAA-guaranteed right. I searched the full 2026-27 Division I manual (412 pages): the string **"preferred walk-on" appears zero times**, and **"walk-on" appears exactly once**, in an unrelated rule (Bylaw 17.13.3.4, seven-day acclimatization). So no bylaw backs the roster guarantee; it is the school's word, worth getting in writing.

**What the real signature does bind (DI):** **DI 13.1.1.2** — once a prospect signs a written offer of athletics aid with another DI or DII institution, athletics staff may not communicate with the prospect or associated persons; **DI 13.1.1.2.1** the prohibition lifts when aid is reduced/canceled, eligibility is lost, the student is disenrolled, the head coach departs (30 days), or the institution grants a release; **DI 13.1.1.2.1.1** the institution must update the NCAA database within two business days of a release request. Also **DII 13.6.5** (no in-person off-campus contact on signing day) and the NCAA explainer: *"Can a prospect continue to be recruited after signing? No... This release only lifts the contact prohibition and does not automatically cancel the athletic scholarship signed by the prospective student-athlete."*

**SOURCE:** *Guide for the College-Bound Student-Athlete*, 2026-27, http://fs.ncaa.org/Docs/eligibility_center/Student_Resources/CBSA.pdf (glossary, p. 27-28 of35); NCAA Recruiting, https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/recruiting; NCAA, *Signing An Athletic Scholarship* education resource, https://athleticsaid.s3.amazonaws.com/PSA+Education.pdf; 2026-27 DI Bylaws 13.1.1.2, 13.1.1.2.1, 13.1.1.2.1.1, 17.13.3.4, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008; DII Bylaw 13.6.5, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90010

**WHAT IT BINDS:** A verbal commitment binds **nothing** on either side; it functions as a signal that stops other coaches. A preferred walk-on offer binds the school's roster spot and nothing else — no money, no aid, and no NCAA enforcement behind it.

## FINDING 8 — Name, image and likeness in WISCONSIN: what a high-school athlete may and may not do, registration, and minors' contracts

**FINDING:** Wisconsin **permits** high-school NIL, tightly constrained. **No deal registration or disclosure is required** by WIAA or by statute. A 14-to-17-year-old **may** sign a NIL contract, but under Wisconsin common law that contract is **voidable at the minor's option**, which is a real risk for a brand.

**RULE (WIAA Senior High Handbook, 2026-27, Rules of Eligibility, Article IV - Amateur Status, Section 1 - Loss of Eligibility):**
- **1-C:** *"An athlete forfeits amateur status in a sport by capitalizing on athletic fame by receiving money, compensation, endorsements or gifts of monetary value in affiliation or connection with activities involving the student's school team, school, Conference or WIAA (scholarships to institutions of higher learning are specifically exempted)."*
- **1-C-1:** *"This provision is not intended to restrict the right of any student to participate in a Name, Image and Likeness (NIL) commercial endorsement provided there is no school team, school, Conference or WIAA affiliation."*
- **1-C-2 Prohibited NIL Activities:** (a) may not appear in the uniform of the student's school or use the marks/logos of the school team, school, Conference or WIAA in any endorsement; (b) may not promote gaming/gambling, alcoholic beverages, tobacco, cannabis, banned/illegal substances, adult entertainment products or services, or weapons; (c) *"The compensation is not contingent on specific athletic performance or achievement"*; (d) *"The compensation is not provided by the school or persons associated with school as real or perceived inducement to attend a particular school ('recruiting') or to remain enrolled at a particular school"* (the Note defines "persons associated with school" to include parents/guardians of current or former athletes **except one's own parent**, booster club members, alumni, spouses/relatives of coaches, teachers and employees, active coaching applicants, and employees of donors); (e) NIL must not interfere with academic obligations; (f) the student must not miss practice, competition, travel or other team obligations to participate in NIL; (g) *"A student can not utilize representation or contract an agent. High schools or its employees are not allowed to help facilitate deals for student-athletes."*
- **1-B-2-b** (contracting is permitted): *"This rule shall not prevent a student from signing 1) an agreement which binds him/her to play only for a particular team, 2) an athletic tender with a university or college or 3) a name, image or likeness opportunity as outlined in Article IV Section 1-C."*
- **Registration:** **NONE.** I searched the full 62-page 2026-27 WIAA Senior High Handbook: the word **"disclose" occurs zero times**, and there is no NIL registration or disclosure requirement anywhere in the rules. The only acknowledgment requirement is annual and is not deal registration — WIAA's own NIL-vs-NCAA document: *"Student-athletes and their parents/guardians are annually required to sign the WIAA High School Athletic Eligibility Information Bulletin, which states that they have read, understand, and agree to abide by all of the information contained in the bulletin, including NIL guidelines."* And because 1-C-2(g) bars schools and their employees from facilitating deals, **a school cannot demand a copy of the deal.** *Individual schools may adopt local policies; I did not verify any specific school policy — UNVERIFIED.*
- **Adoption/effective date:** WIAA members voted **293-108** to approve the NIL language (WIAA Bulletin, May 20, 2025), with NIL permissible after that cycle. I verified the **rule text** in the 2026-27 handbook (downloaded and extracted) but **not** the adoption memo or the precise effective date against a WIAA primary document — **effective date partially UNVERIFIED** (see Sources/Flags).

**May a 14-to-18-year-old sign a contract?** Yes as a matter of WIAA rules (1-B-2-b). As a matter of Wisconsin law:
- **Wis. Stat. § 990.01(3):** *"'Adult' means a person who has attained the age of 18 years."* **§ 990.01(20):** *"'Minor' means a person who has not attained the age of 18 years."*
- **Wisconsin common law, Kiefer v. Fred Howe Motors, Inc., 39 Wis. 2d 20, 158 N.W.2d 288 (Wis. 1968):** *"the contract of a minor, other than for necessaries, is either void or voidable at his option"*; *"The general rule is not affected by the minor's status as emancipated or unemanorcipated"* (sic, court); the minor may ratify after majority; misrepresenting age is a separate tort.
- **Payroll / employment side (Wisconsin):** minimum age generally 14;14- and 15-year-olds need a **work permit** before working in most jobs — Wisconsin DWD, *Employment of Minors*: *"If you are the parent or guardian of a 14- or 15-year-old, you must apply and pay for a work permit before your child is allowed to work in most jobs"*; minors under 14 may not be employed in any gainful occupation except narrow exceptions (Wis. Admin. Code **DWD 270.10(2)**); hours limits (**DWD 270.11**), and 14-17 year olds are barred from the most hazardous occupations (**DWD 270.12, 270.13**).
- **18+ side:** (a) *Payroll* - if the brand treats the payment as compensation for services, the brand is the employer: Form W-2 withholding via Form W-4, worker's compensation and unemployment insurance, Wisconsin wage reporting; at 18+ no work permit is needed. (b) *Business* - an LLC contracts with the brand and pays the athlete a wage or guaranteed payment (then a W-2 to the athlete, and the LLC files its own return with an EIN from Form SS-4); the specific Wisconsin DOR entity-registration steps are **UNVERIFIED** (confirm with a CPA and DOR). (c) *Paid to a parent* - the payment becomes the parent's income (or a gift to the minor), with gift-tax annual-exclusion consequences; the allocation depends on facts, so this route needs a CPA. **The IRS citation for "a minor's income is reported on the parent's return" is UNVERIFIED**: I confirmed IRS Publication 505 (2026) is now titled **"Tax Withholding"** and therefore is *not* the right source for that point, and I could not locate the operative sentence in the Instructions for Form 1040 PDF I extracted (2025 instructions, 126 pp.) — treat the minor-income reporting rule as **UNVERIFIED pending a CPA or the "Qualifying Child of Another Person" section of the current Instructions for Form 1040**.

**WHAT IT BINDS:** WIAA's rules bind the athlete's **eligibility** (violating 1-C ends amateur status for that sport); the 1-C-2 list defines what is prohibited rather than granting a right. A NIL contract with a minor is **not void** but is **voidable by the 14-17-year-old** until majority and can be ratified after 18 — so a brand wanting enforceability should wait until 18 or structure the deal with the parent's involvement and a ratifying clause.
