## 1. Participation: how many US high-school players there are

| Figure | Boys | Girls |
|---|---|---|

| Schools with a basketball team, 2025-26 | **18,799** | **18,259** |
| Rank among all sports by participants | 3rd (behind football 1,043,716 and outdoor track 648,723) | **1st** (girls outdoor track 2nd at 509,070) |
| Rank among all sports by number of schools | **1st** | **1st** |

- Total high-school sports participation 2025-26: 8,415,684 (boys 4,761,095; girls 3,654,589) — record highs for both, fifth straight year of growth. Source: NFHS release cited above.
- Girls' basketball participation **rose 6,142** in 2025-26 (from 356,240 in 2024-25), "halting a downward trend" of roughly **100,000 over the prior 20 years**. Source: same NFHS release.
- The NCAA's own probability model uses the 2024-25 NFHS figures as its high-school denominator: **boys 540,704; girls 356,240**. Source: NCAA, *Estimated Probability of Competing in College Athletics*, last updated March 2026 (ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/research/pro_beyond/RES_ProbabilityBeyondHSFiguresMethod.pdf).

**SO WHAT FOR A FAMILY:** There are about **one-and-a-half times as many boys playing high-school basketball as girls** (540k vs 362k), but almost the **same number of schools** (18,799 vs 18,259). That gap is absorbed by multi-sport boys (football overlaps heavily with basketball) and by girls who play other sports too. Practically: a girl in a small town can often find a girls' team, but she is competing for a much thinner talent pool.

**SO WHAT FOR A FAMILY:** For a son and a daughter the *number of roster slots* is now genuinely identical at D1 (15 each) and identical at D2 (10 equivalencies each) and identical at NAIA (8 each). What is **not** identical is the money behind the slot: a top men's program has roughly three times the school-paid pool of a top women's program, and men capture the overwhelming majority of it.

"Boys have more talent" is not what the numbers say — there are just far more boys in the pipeline, so there are far more boys in the room at every level.

## 4. Contact and evaluation rules that differ by sex

All bylaw text below was read directly from the NCAA's Legislative Services Database (LSDBi), 2026-27 legislation, at web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi. Calendars from the official NCAA recruiting-calendar PDFs (ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/compliance/recruiting/calendar/2026-27/2026-27D1Rec_MBBRecruitingCalendar.pdf and ...WBBRecruitingCalendar.pdf, both updated 30 July 2026).

**The one rule that matters most to a family — and it runs opposite to what most people assume:**

| Rule | Boys' basketball | Girls' basketball |
|---|---|---|
| **Bylaw 13.1.1.1.3 / 13.1.1.1.5 — first off-campus (in-person) contact** | **Not before the opening day of the individual's junior year in high school.** During junior year, contacts outside April recruiting periods may occur **only at the prospect's school**; during the April recruiting periods of junior year, at **school or residence**. | **Not before September 1 at the beginning of the individual's senior year in high school.** Contacts may occur **only at school or residence**. |
| **Bylaw 13.1.3.1.6 / 13.1.3.1.5 — first telephone/email contact** | Not before **June 15** at the conclusion of the sophomore year | Not before **June 1** at the conclusion of the sophomore year |
| **Bylaw 13.02.5.3 / 13.17.2 vs 13.17.3 — calendar structure** | Has a **"recruiting period"** (in-person off-campus contacts *and* evaluations permitted): **Sept 9, 2026 – April 30, 2027** | No recruiting period; **contact periods are class-specific**: **Sept 1–30, 2026** and **March 1–31, 2027** — and **for seniors and two-year-college prospects only**; all other classes get evaluation-only periods. **Oct 1, 2026 – Feb 28, 2027 = evaluation period, scholastic activities only.** |
| **Bylaw 13.1.5.5 — recruiting opportunities** | **No cap** | **Seven recruiting opportunities (contacts + evaluations combined) per prospect per year**, and women's basketball staff may not exceed **65 recruiting-person days** per year (Aug 1 – July 31) |
| **Bylaw 13.1.5.5.1 — contact with a family member who coaches the player's club team** | Not applicable | Counts as a recruiting contact (women's only) |
| **Bylaw 13.1.5.5.2 and 13.1.3.1.5.1 — July evaluation periods** | July is an **evaluation period** (8–11 and 15–18 July 2027); coaches may watch | **All communication** with the prospect, the prospect's family, the prospect's coach, or anyone associated with the prospect's basketball participation, **directly or indirectly, is prohibited** during July evaluation periods. Also: a prospect **may not make an official or unofficial visit during the July evaluation periods unless she has signed** the institution's written offer and/or received a financial deposit (calendar note citing Bylaws 13.1.5.5.2 and 13.7.2.2) |
| **Tryouts** | "Only Division I men's basketball programs may hold tryouts for certain prospective student-athletes during an official or unofficial campus visit" (ncaa.org/eligibility-center/recruiting) | No equivalent provision |
| **Dead periods / shutdowns, 2026-27** | Dead Aug 1–19; quiet Aug 20 – Sept 8; recruiting period Sept 9 – Apr 30, with dead Nov 9–12, Dec 24–26, Apr 1–8, May 9, May 26 – June 6; quiet May 1 – June 30; dead July 1–31 with quiet July 19–25 | **Recruiting shutdown Aug 10–16, 2026**; quiet Aug 1–31; dead Dec 24–26; dead Apr 1–5 (plus single-day dead Apr 22 and Apr 26 for HS/JUCO); quiet Apr 6 – July 31; **recruiting shutdown May 3–9, 2027**; dead May 13, May 17, June 16, June 20, July 8, 13, 22, 27 |

**Grassroots certification window:**
- **Bylaw 13.18 Basketball Event Certification**: "In basketball, in order for an event (e.g., camp, league, tournament or festival) to be certified, it must be reviewed and conducted in accordance with certification policies and procedures established and maintained by the Men's and Women's Basketball Oversight Committees." (Adopted 11/1/01, last revised 1/24/19.) Certification is administered by the NCAA's Enforcement Certification and Approvals Group. Source: web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/bylaw?ruleId=101227&refDate=20261026.
- Both sexes now need NCAA-certified nonscholastic events for their April/May/June/July evaluation windows. **Permissible event times differ by sex** (NCAA Basketball Certification, ncaa.org/eligibility-center/recruiting/basketball-certification): Spring — Men 8 a.m. first day to 4 p.m. last day; **Women 8 a.m.–10 p.m. daily**. June — Men Noon Friday to 6 p.m. Sunday; **Women Noon Thursday to 6 p.m. Saturday**. July — both 8 a.m.–10 p.m. daily. 2026 June scholastic events: **Men June 12–14 and June 26–28; Women June 18–20** (ncaa.org/eligibility-center/recruiting/basketball-certification/june-scholastic-basketball-events).
- **The specific 2026-27 certification deadline date for prospective student-athletes: UNVERIFIED.** The NCAA maintains a "Current Certification Dates" knowledge base article (KB-3893) that I did not retrieve.
- **Recent housekeeping in these rules (adopted 24 June 2026):** 13.1.1.1.1 baseball (Sept 1 junior year), 13.1.1.1.2 softball (1 p.m. Central, Sept 1 junior year), 13.1.1.1.4 women's bowling (July 5 at conclusion of sophomore year), 13.1.5.6 women's ice hockey (adopted 7 Oct 2026). Men's basketball (13.1.1.1.3) and women's basketball (13.1.1.1.5) were not changed in that cycle.

**SO WHAT FOR A FAMILY:** Boys get contacted **in person a full year earlier than girls**. A boy's class of 2029 can be met off campus from the opening day of his junior year (autumn 2027) and can have coaches at his house in April 2028; a girl's class of 2029 cannot be contacted in person off campus until **1 September 2028** — after she has chosen a school. Girls also get a *smaller* menu of coaching attention (seven opportunities per school per year, a 65-day staff cap, and no July contact at all), which means a girl has to get noticed a different way.

## 5. What fills the roster before a US high-school player

**International players:**
- FIBA / CIES Sports Observatory, *International Basketball Migration Report 2026* (published 8 Oct 2026), 2025-26 season: **NCAA Division I men's basketball — 1,055 international players** (up from 888 in 2024-25); **NCAA Division I women's basketball — 1,042 international players** (up from 951). Both set records and passed 1,000 for the first time. **European players were 52.2%** of international players in both. Source: assets.fiba.basketball/image/upload/documents-corporate-studies-and-facts-international-basketball-migration-report-2026.pdf. (2025 edition for comparison: assets.fiba.basketball/.../international-basketball-migration-report-2025.pdf.)
- NCAA's own figure (2024-25 season): **1,838 international student-athletes** in D1 men's and women's basketball combined, **+175% since 2009-10**. At the 2025 women's tournament, **130 international players made the 68-team field (15%)**; at the men's tournament, **134 (about 14% of the bracket)**. Source: NCAA, *March Madness across the world*, 18 March 2025 (ncaa.org/news/2025/3/18/media-center-march-madness-across-the-world-ncaa-basketballs-global-impact.aspx).
- **Approximate share (my calculation across two sources — FIBA numerator for 2025-26, NCAA denominator for 2023-24):** women's D1 ≈ 1,042 / 5,048 ≈ **21%**; men's D1 ≈ 1,055 / 5,607 ≈ **19%**. Corroborated by Her Hoop Stats (22 Dec 2025, using the Sports Roster Data Project at the University of Maryland), which reports the share of D1 women's basketball players who are international **rising from 15% to 20% over four seasons**. Source: herhoopstats.substack.com/p/a-new-high-for-international-players. **Exact same-year percentages: UNVERIFIED** — FIBA does not publish the roster denominator.

**Transfers:**
- Pathley, *College Transfer Study: 2026-27 Transfers by Sport and Division* (updated Oct 2026; built from rosters published on each program's official athletics website for 2025-26 and 2026-27): **D1 men's basketball — 29.4% of the 2026-27 roster are new transfers from another four-year program (115 transfers per 100 freshmen)**; **D1 women's basketball — 20.8% (83 per 100 freshmen)**. Across all levels: men's 16.7%, women's 11.7%. Source: pathley.ai/college-transfer-study.
- NCAA's own (2022 portal cohort): **1,649 men's D1 basketball players entered the portal** (84% on athletics aid) and **1,276 women's** (93% on aid); 1,123 men's and 939 women's transferred to an NCAA school with aid. Source: NCAA 2022 transfer trends (link in section 3).
- For context on churn: in the April 2026 portal alone, **more than 2,000 D1 men's players entered** (USA Today Sports research, 10 Apr 2026) — roughly 39% of a 15-spot men's roster; about **25% of women's D1 players entered the2025 portal** (~1,300 of 5,048; On3 reporting via Front Office Sports, 10 Apr 2025).

**SO WHAT FOR A FAMILY:** On a D1 **men's** roster, roughly **3 in 10 spots go to transfers and about 1 in 5 to international players**. On a D1 **women's** roster, roughly **2 in 10 go to transfers and about 1 in 5 to international players** — women's rosters have actually been internationalising *faster* than men's, at exactly the moment women's transfers have also surged. For a US high-school recruit, the realistic competition at D1 is not "the other freshmen": it is transfers plus internationals, roughly 40 to 50% of the roster.

## 6. Money: NIL and revenue sharing

**Market size:**
- Total collegiate NIL market **$4.5 billion** (Opendorse *2026 Annual NIL Report*, published 22 June 2026 — a 50% upward revision from Opendorse's own prior projection). Source: biz.opendorse.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/AnnualNILReport_06222026.pdf.
- **$932.5 million** spent on NIL products and services for **men's and women's basketball combined** in 2025-26, versus **$314.4 million** in 2021-22 (Opendorse, reported by On3, 12 March 2026 — on3.com/nil/news/college-basketball-nil-spending-surges-932-5-million-2025-26-season).
- School revenue sharing: **up to $20.5 million per school per year** for 2025-26 (House settlement).
- Non-D1 market: **more than $25 million** across DII, DIII, NAIA and NJCAA in 2026-27 — about **0.5%** of the D1 market (Opendorse; Student-Athlete Advisors, 28 Sept 2026).

**What a mid-major women's player can realistically earn (published differentials):**
- Opendorse, *NIL at Four*, July 2025, pp. 13–14 — earnings elasticity per extra unit of production:
  - **High Major MBB:** +**$125K+** per +1.0 PPG; +**$23K+** per additional minute played.
  - **Mid-Major MBB:** +**$47K+** per +1.0 PPG; +**$7K+** per additional minute.
  - **High Major WBB:** +**$21K+** per +1.0 PPG; +**$3K+** per additional minute.
  - **Mid-Major WBB:** +**$3K+** per +1.0 PPG; +**$500+** per additional minute.
  - **So one extra minute of playing time is worth about $23,000 to a high-major man and about $500 to a mid-major woman — roughly 1/46th.**
 - AAV bands by performance output tier: D1 **women's** basketball $50K+ / $150K+ / $250K+ / $350K+ (low to high output); D1 **men's** basketball $500K+ / $1M+ / $1.5M+ / $2M+.
- Moving up or down levels (Opendorse, 2026): players moving from a mid-major to a high-major saw earnings **rise more than 540%**; players moving from a high-major to a mid-major saw earnings **fall 35%**.
- The floor: NCAA's own **NIL Assist Data Dashboard** — the **median total annual NIL earnings per athlete is $1,031**, **75% of NIL money flows to the top 5%** of athletes, and **68% of deals are worth under $1,000** (as reported by Christine Brown & Partners, 3 July 2026, citing the NCAA dashboard).
- Small college: average earnings of **$50 per activity** (Opendorse, reported28 Sept 2026).
- The average NIL deal in 2026: **$6,300 season-long / $4,800 one-off** (Opendorse 2026 Annual NIL Report).

**SO WHAT FOR A FAMILY:** NIL money in basketball is real but it is not a salary. The gap between a high-major man and a mid-major woman on the same circuit is roughly **forty-fold**, and the median D1 athlete earns about **$1,000 a year**. If a daughter plays at a mid-major, treat any NIL income as a part-time side business, not a scholarship replacement, and choose the school on coaching, playing time, and academics first.

## 7. Path after college: WNBA, overseas, and why the degree matters more for girls

**WNBA (all from the WNBA/WNBPA's new seven-year CBA, 20 March 2026, ratified 24 March 2026 — wnba.com/news/wnba-wnbpa-tentative-cba-deal-2026, pr.nba.com/wnba-and-wnbpa-reach-tentative-deal-on-historic-collective-bargaining-agreement):**
- **15 teams** in 2026 (Portland Fire and Toronto Tempo joined as the 14th and 15th franchises;2026 Expansion Draft, 3 April 2026). Source: wnba.com/news/2026-expansion-draft-faq; the league's stated goal is 16 teams (wnba.com/faq).
- **Roster:** minimum **12 players plus 2 developmental spots** per team under the new CBA.
- **Draft:** **3 rounds × 15 teams = 45 picks** in 2026 (wnba.com/draft/2026/faq). Domestic entrants must be **22** in the draft year with no remaining college eligibility.
- **Pay (2026):** salary cap **$7.0 million** (up from $1.5 million in 2025); **maximum $1.4 million** (to >$2.4M by 2032); **average $583,000** (to >$1M by 2032); **minimum $270,000–$300,000** (to $340K–$380K by 2032); more than **$1 billion** in salaries and benefits over the deal.

**The overseas route:**
- Entry-level women's import contracts (Euro-Grades, 2026): Israel **€3,000–€20,000/month**; Turkey **€6,000–€50,000**; Italy **€3,000–€20,000**; Poland **€2,500–€12,000**; Portugal **€1,200–€5,000**; Romania **€1,000–€4,500**; Iceland **€1,500–€4,000**. Source: euro-grades.com/overseas-basketball-salaries. (EuroLeague Women's maximum salary: **UNVERIFIED** — no official published figure found.)
- Australia (WNBL, official CBA signed April 2025): minimum wage **A$23,000 (2024-25) → A$29,465 (2025-26) → A$46,952 (2028-29)**; salary cap **A$574,348 (2025-26)** rising 8% a year to A$723,513 (2028-29). Source: ABC News (Australia), 26 April 2025; wnbl.com.au.
- **The NCAA constraint families must know:** a player keeps college eligibility while playing abroad only if she is not paid by a professional league and receives no more than **actual and necessary expenses** (Bylaw 12.2.2.2.2 and Bylaw 12.02.1). Women's basketball has its own draft exception allowing a student-athlete to enter a professional league's draft once during college without jeopardising eligibility (Bylaw 12.2.4.2.3; a women's-specific exception adopted 4/15/26 at 12.2.3.2.4). In May 2026 the NCAA reiterated that athletes paid above actual and necessary expenses are ineligible, and the status of many overseas professionals seeking NCAA eligibility is unresolved (The Athletic, 30 July 2026).
- **The post-college payoff is shorter and flatter for women than the W numbers suggest:** among 1,000+ athletes who cleared $100,000 in NIL, Opendorse finds **69% are still current college athletes, 19% compete professionally (US or overseas), 2% played professionally and have retired, and 10% went straight into the workforce**. Opendorse states plainly: **"For many athletes — especially Women's Basketball and Olympic sports — peak earning potential still sits on campus, not in rookie pay scales."** Source: Opendorse *NIL at Four*, July 2025, p. 5. The same report notes the pattern of a pay cut on the jump to the pros (MiLB players earn $20K–$36K a year; Athletes Unlimited Softball average $45K).

**Why the degree matters more for girls — the arithmetic:**
- **45 WNBA draft picks a year** against roughly **5,048 D1 women's basketball players produced per year** (NCAA). Across the whole league there are only about **180 roster spots** (15 teams × 12).
- The NCAA's own published pro rates: **0.9% of NCAA women's basketball players are drafted**; only **33 were drafted in 2023** (versus **46** NCAA men's basketball players) — NCAA *Recruiting Facts*.
- For comparison, boys' professional basketball absorbs a far larger share of NCAA output: NBA plus G League plus long overseas careers.

**SO WHAT FOR A FAMILY:** A son has a realistic shot at a multi-year, well-paid professional career; a daughter has a **less-than-1% shot at a WNBA career** and an overseas route that is typically a short, modestly paid stint. For a daughter, the degree is not a backup plan for a basketball career that failed — it is very likely the plan. For a son, college basketball can be a genuine professional pipeline, so the family should be willing to treat an elite-but-not-D1 offer as a stepping stone rather than the end.

## 8. Program cuts and additions in the last five years that change girls' opportunities

**Structural changes affecting girls (2024–2026):**
| Date | Change | Effect on girls |
|---|---|---|
| **10 May 2024 / 6 June 2025 / 23 June 2025, effective 1 July 2025** | House v. NCAA settlement adopted: D1 scholarship limits removed for opt-in schools; **roster limits set at 15 for women's basketball** (and 15 for men's); revenue sharing up to $20.5M/school/yr | **Not a cut.** It equalised D1 basketball roster spots between the sexes and removed the old 13-vs-15 scholarship asymmetry. **Additions:**
- **Marquette (D1)** added **women's swimming** (2025). **St. Bonaventure** added **women's golf** (2025-26 and 2026-27). **UT Arlington** added **women's golf** (2025-26). **Radford** added **women's flag football** (club, 2025-26) and men's indoor/outdoor track (fall 2025). **Merrimack College (D1)** announced four new varsity sports including **women's flag football** (first in New England) and women's acrobatics and tumbling (November 2026). **Georgia Gwinnett College** began a new **women's basketball** programme in 2025-26 (inaugural head coach appointed July 2024). **Bridgeport University** reinstated **women's basketball** in 2024.
- **Net effect on basketball specifically:** women's basketball programs **grew** from 350 (2024-25) to **363 projected for D1 in 2026-27**, while men's grew from 356 to **365**. Across all NCAA divisions women now sponsor **1,077** basketball programs to men's **1,070** — and women's program count is growing faster (NCAA membership/sponsorship page; 2024-25 team counts via ncsasports.org). NCAA projected2026-27 sponsorship totals as of 29 July 2026.
- New women's roster spots outside basketball: women's flag football gets a **25-player roster limit** under Bylaw 17.2, effective beginning2027-28 (NCAA LSDBi, revised 25 Aug 2026).

**SO WHAT FOR A FAMILY:** The five-year trend for girls is **not fewer basketball spots — more of them**. Women's basketball now has more NCAA programs than men's (1,077 vs 1,070) and more programs at every division except D1, where the two are nearly identical. The losses have been concentrated in **Olympic sports other than basketball** (tennis, swimming, golf, gymnastics) and at small institutions — which is exactly why a family should check a specific school's sport-sponsorship record, not just whether it fields a basketball team.
