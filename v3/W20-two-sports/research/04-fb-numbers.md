# Football Roster Spots & Scholarships by Division — Primary-Source Findings

**Check date: November 11, 2026.** All "as of" dates below are the publication/revision dates of the source itself.

---

## 1. FBS — the 85-scholarship rule and how it splits

**NUMBER:** 85 (FBS football, annual limit on counters) — still the codified rule for Division I football that has *not* opted into the House settlement; superseded by a 105-player roster limit at settlement schools.
**RULE:** "**15.5.6.1 Bowl Subdivision Football. [FBS]** There shall be an annual limit of 85 on the total number of counters in football at each institution. (Revised: 1/10/91 effective 8/1/92, 12/15/06, 10/4/23)"
**SOURCE:** NCAA Division I Bylaw 15 (General Manual, Sports Sponsorship and Participation Rates), file `2024-25D1GMS_Bylaw15.pdf`, version mark 3/13/26, in the NCAA's 2026-27 D1 legislation folder — https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2024-25D1GMS_Bylaw15.pdf (Bylaw 15.5.6, printed p. 20–21)

**NUMBER:** Headcount, not equivalency — football is a "head-count sport" under Bylaw 15.5.1.5; once a player is a counter, they remain one for the rest of the academic year unless they withdraw before classes/first contest.
**RULE:** "**15.5.1.5 Cancellation of Aid.** Once an individual becomes a counter in a head-count sport (see Bylaws 15.5.2, 15.5.4, 15.5.5, **15.5.6**, 15.5.7 and 15.5.8), the individual normally continues as a counter for the remainder of the academic year."
**SOURCE:** Same PDF, Bylaw 15.5.1.5 (printed p. 14)

**NUMBER:** 85 full grants at a non-opted-in FBS school (85 counters × full grant-in-aid). At a settlement/opted-in FBS school the same 85 becomes **105 roster spots with aid in any amount to any of the 105** (full or partial), because all sports become equivalency-based inside the roster cap.
**RULE:** NCAA: "Moving forward, NCAA rules for Division I programs will no longer include sport-specific scholarship limits. Instead, schools in the defendant conferences and others that opt in to the settlement rules and associated benefits to student-athletes will have roster limits, and schools will have the option to offer scholarships to any and all of those student-athletes." College Sports Commission roster-limit table: "Football (M) | Old Scholarship Limit 85 | New Roster Limit 105 | Potential Increase in Scholarships 20."
**SOURCE:** NCAA, "DI Board of Directors formally adopts changes to roster limits," June 23, 2025 — https://www.ncaa.org/news/2025/6/23/media-center-di-board-of-directors-formally-adopts-changes-to-roster-limits.aspx ; College Sports Commission, "Roster Limits" — https://www.collegesportscommission.org/roster-limits

**NUMBER:** Individual ceiling — aid may never exceed the cost of attendance.
**RULE:** "**15.1 Maximum Limit on Financial Aid -- Individual.** A student-athlete shall not be eligible to participate in intercollegiate athletics if the student-athlete receives financial aid that exceeds the value of the cost of attendance... (Revised: 4/29/04 effective 8/1/04... 6/6/25 effective 7/1/25)"
**SOURCE:** NCAA Division I Bylaw 15 (2026-27 D1 Manual, via LSDBi) — https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/bylaw?refDate=20261007&ruleId=3848

**FOR A FAMILY:** At an FBS school on the new rules, "85 scholarships" is no longer the ceiling — it is 105 players, and in theory every one of them can be on some aid. But the money is finite, so the real competition is the dollars, not the headcount.

## 2. May aid be reduced or cancelled mid-year?

**NUMBER:** 6 enumerated grounds only; a hearing is required before reduction during the award.
**RULE:** "**15.3.4.1 Reduction, Cancellation or Nonrenewal Permitted.** Institutional financial aid based in any degree on athletics ability awarded to an individual other than an undergraduate four-year transfer... may be reduced or canceled during the period of the award... if the recipient: (a) Is rendered ineligible...; (b) Fraudulently misrepresents any information...; (c) Engages in serious misconduct...; (d) Voluntarily (on the recipient's own initiative) withdraws from a sport at any time for personal reasons; however, the recipient's financial aid may not be awarded to another student-athlete in the academic term in which the aid is reduced or canceled; (e) Violates a nonathletically related condition outlined in the financial aid agreement or violates a documented institutional rule or policy...; or (f) Provides written notification of transfer... however, the student-athlete's financial aid may not be reduced or canceled until the end of the regular academic term in which written notification of transfer is received."
**RULE:** "**15.3.4.1.1 Timing of Reduction or Cancellation.** Any reduction or cancellation of aid during the period of the award may occur only after the student-athlete has been provided an opportunity for a hearing per Bylaw 15.3.2.3."
**SOURCE:** NCAA Division I Bylaw 15 (3/13/26), Bylaws 15.3.4.1 and 15.3.4.1.1 (printed p. 11–12) — https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2024-25D1GMS_Bylaw15.pdf

**NUMBER:** Roster management, performance and injury are *not* grounds for pulling aid.
**RULE:** "The division's core guarantees -- which include scholarship protections -- are not impacted by the rules changes. **If a student-athlete receiving athletics aid loses a roster spot for roster management, athletics performance reasons, or due to injury, that student-athlete's scholarship cannot be revoked unless and until the student chooses to transfer.**"
**SOURCE:** NCAA, "DI Board of Directors formally adopts changes to roster limits," June 23, 2025 — https://www.ncaa.org/news/2025/6/23/media-center-di-board-of-directors-formally-adopts-changes-to-roster-limits.aspx

**NUMBER:** Transfers (only) can lose aid mid-year, and only in specific ways.
**RULE:** "**15.3.4.1.2 Undergraduate Four-Year College Transfers.** Institutional financial aid... awarded to an undergraduate four-year college transfer student in the academic year of initial full-time enrollment at the certifying institution may only be reduced or canceled during the period of the award if the recipient: (a) Transfers to another institution (see Bylaw 14.5.2);" (plus medical disqualification, loss of amateur status, cancellation under 15.3.5.1.2(c), or voluntary withdrawal for nonathletically related reasons).
**RULE:** The transfer-specific aid guarantee is now gone: "**Question No. B6: Do the financial aid protections associated with the undergraduate four-year transfer legislation remain in place? Answer: No.**"
**SOURCE:** Bylaw 15.3.4.1.2, NCAA D1 Bylaw 15 (3/13/26); NCAA, "Q&A: Implementation of the House Settlement," updated May 29, 2026 — https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/May2026D1Gov_PhaseEightSetQuestionandAnswer.pdf

**FOR A FAMILY:** Once a kid is on aid, the school cannot take it away mid-season for "we found someone better." It can, however, decline to renew for a following year for the six listed reasons, and it can cut aid if the athlete transfers or quits.

## 3. FCS — 63 equivalencies, how it splits, and roster management

**NUMBER:** 63 equivalencies of aid + 85 total counters (roster ceiling) at FCS institutions that have not opted into the settlement.
**RULE:** "**15.5.6.2 Championship Subdivision Football. [FCSD]** There shall be an annual limit of 63 on the value of financial aid awards (equivalencies) to counters and an annual limit of 85 on the total number of counters in football at each Football Championship Subdivision institution. (Revised: 1/10/91 effective 8/1/92, 12/15/06, 10/4/23)"
**SOURCE:** NCAA Division I Bylaw 15 (3/13/26), Bylaw 15.5.6.2 (printed p. 21) — https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2024-25D1GMS_Bylaw15.pdf

**NUMBER:** How the 63 may be split — as *equivalencies*, i.e., partial grants across many more than 63 players; the 85 is the hard roster ceiling (no aid required for the other 22).
**RULE:** Bylaw 15.5.6.2 (above) read with Bylaw 15.5.3.2 (equivalency computations, including the 0.25 minimum and the $800 books denominator) and Bylaw 15.5.3.2.3's "An institution may use either the actual cost or average cost... of any or all the elements (other than books) of the equivalency calculation."
**SOURCE:** NCAA Division I Bylaw 15 (3/13/26), Bylaws 15.5.3.2 / 15.5.3.2.3 (printed p. 18–19)
**UNVERIFIED:** The commonly repeated claim that FCS football may award at most **26 initial full grants per year** does not appear anywhere in the current football provisions of Bylaw 15 (15.5.6.1 through 15.5.6.3.7) that I read in full. I could not confirm it from any primary NCAA document. Treat as unverified.

**NUMBER:** FCS roster-management exceptions (pre-settlement, non-opted-in schools): midyear replacement, preseason voluntary withdrawal, voluntary withdrawal from the institution, replacement of an ineligible athlete, study abroad, internship, sprint football.
**RULE:** "**15.5.6.3.1 Midyear Replacement. [FBS/FCS]** The financial aid of a counter who graduates at midyear... may be provided to another student-athlete and the replaced student-athlete shall not be a counter for the remainder of that academic year. In bowl subdivision football, an institution may use the midyear replacement exception only if it previously has provided financial aid during that academic year to the maximum number of overall counters (85 total counters). In championship subdivision football, an institution may use the midyear replacement exception only if it previously has provided financial aid during that academic year that equals the maximum number of overall equivalencies or overall counters."
**RULE:** "**15.5.6.3.7 Sprint Football. [FBS/FCS]** Participants in sprint football programs who do not participate in the institution's regular varsity intercollegiate program shall not be counted in the institution's financial aid limitations."
**RULE:** "**15.5.6.2.1 Exception -- Championship Subdivision. [FCS]** Championship subdivision football programs that meet the following criteria are exempt from the championship subdivision football counter requirements... (a) In football, the institution awards financial aid only to student-athletes who demonstrate financial need..."
**SOURCE:** NCAA Division I Bylaw 15 (3/13/26), Bylaws 15.5.6.2.1 and 15.5.6.3.1–.7 (printed p. 21–22)

**NUMBER:** Multisport athletes are counted in football first.
**RULE:** "**15.5.9.1 Football. [FBS/FCS]** In football, a counter who was recruited... and/or offered financial aid to participate in football and who participates (practices or competes) in football and one or more sports (including basketball) shall be counted in football."
**SOURCE:** NCAA Division I Bylaw 15 (3/13/26), Bylaw 15.5.9.1 (printed p. 22)

**FOR A FAMILY:** At an FCS school that stayed out of the settlement, 63 is the aid pool and 85 is the headcount — so 22 players are unpaid walk-ons, which is the traditional FCS model.

## 4. The settlement roster caps: 2025, 2026, and "later"

**NUMBER:** 105 for football, starting 2025-26. The cap is **flat** — it does not rise.
**RULE:** "**Section 1. Initial Roster Limits.** The NCAA has currently determined that during the first Academic Year following Final Approval, the NCAA Division I roster limits for Member Institutions that choose to provide or facilitate payments or benefits to student-athletes as permitted by the Injunctive Relief Settlement including but not limited to incremental scholarships permitted by Article 3, Section 3(b) shall be as follows:" — table includes **"Football | 105"** (with Baseball 34, Men's Basketball 15, Women's Basketball 15, Men's Soccer 28, Women's Golf 9, Women's Rowing 68, etc.).
**SOURCE:** *Fourth Amended Stipulation and Settlement Agreement*, In re: College Athlete NIL Litigation, No. 4:20-cv-03919-CW (N.D. Cal.), Document 958-1, filed 05/07/25, **Appendix B, Article I, Section 1** (document p. 133–135 of 137) — https://www.collegeathletecompensation.com/media/5808226/ncco_fourth_amended_stipulation_and_settlement_agreement.pdf

**NUMBER:** Football = 105 for 2025-26 *and* 2026-27, with an explicit NCAA statement that it will never exceed 105.
**RULE:** "**17.2 Roster Limitations. [S]** Each sport of a Division I institution that is a member of a defendant conference or opts in to the House settlement must comply with the roster limits set forth below..." — table row "**Football | 105**" (Adopted: 6/6/25 effective 7/1/25, Revised: 8/25/26 women's flag football limit effective beginning with the 2027-28 academic year).
**RULE:** "For example, a school's roster limit for football will never be more than 105. If the school has one or more Designated Student-Athletes in football, those Designated Student-Athletes will be exempt from the 105-person limit and those Designated Student-Athletes can participate without counting toward the 105-person limit."
**SOURCE:** NCAA Bylaw 17.2 (2026-27 D1 Manual via LSDBi, refDate 2026-10-07) — https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/bylaw?refDate=20261007&ruleId=106414 ; NCAA, "Q&A: Implementation of the House Settlement," updated May 29, 2026 — https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/May2026D1Gov_PhaseEightSetQuestionandAnswer.pdf

**NUMBER:** The settlement itself lets the NCAA adjust the numbers later — upward or downward — but the NCAA's own rule set says football stays at 105.
**RULE:** "**Section 3. Changes to Roster Limits.** During the Term of the Injunctive Relief Settlement, Defendants may increase or decrease the roster limits in each or any of the sports identified in Section 1 or subsequently added by Section 2 of this Article, provided that any such changes must comply with the Injunctive Relief Settlement and the SSA. In accordance with Article 4, Section 1 of the Injunctive Relief Settlement, individual Member Institutions each maintain the right to unilaterally reduce the number of sports, the roster size, and/or the number of athletic scholarships available to student-athletes of any sport. Individual Conferences each maintain the right to unilaterally reduce the number of sports Member Institutions within their respective conferences are required to offer, the number of sports sponsored by the conference, any change in roster limits shall not result in a reduction in the number of athletic scholarships permissible under the current NCAA Division I rules in any sport." "**Section 4. Effect on Non-NCAA-sponsored Sports.** Nothing in this Appendix B should be read to limit, in any way, the right of individual Member Institutions... from unilaterally increasing or decreasing the roster sizes of sports not identified in Section 1..."
**SOURCE:** *Fourth Amended Stipulation and Settlement Agreement*, Appendix B, Article I, Sections 3–4 (document p. 135–136 of 137) — same URL as above
**NOTE (important correction to the premise in the request):** There is **no escalation schedule for football** in the settlement. The table has one limit per sport for "the first Academic Year following Final Approval," and NCAA guidance states football "will never be more than 105." Where a sport limit did change later, it was NCAA legislation (e.g., women's flag football, revised 8/25/26, effective 2027-28) — and downward, not upward, for football.

**NUMBER:** How "counters" work now — they don't. There is no counter at a settlement school; there is a roster with a headcount cap.
**RULE:** "**17.02.8 Designated Student-Athlete.** A designated student-athlete is an individual who a member institution attests was or would have been removed from the institution's 2025-26 roster due to the implementation of the roster limits specified in Bylaw 17.2 and was either: (a) Certified as eligible for practice or competition by the institution or otherwise placed on the institution's squad list form for the 2024-25 academic year, prior to April 7, 2025; or (b) Recruited prior to April 7, 2025, to be, or was assured by an institutional staff member the individual would be, on the institution's roster for the 2025-26 academic year."
**RULE:** "**17.2.1 Roster Submission Deadline.** An institution must submit its roster for each sport in the designated software system of record not later than the calendar day before the first contest or date of competition in the sport that is used for championship selections or December 1, whichever is earlier. **17.2.1.1 Roster Changes.** Once an institution submits its roster, it may add student-athletes to the roster only if the institution has not reached the roster limit before the addition."
**RULE:** "**17.2.5 Roster Limit Replacements.** ... (b) Midyear Withdrawal – Fall Sports. In fall sports, a student-athlete who was on a submitted roster and withdraws from the institution for any reason (e.g., transfer, professional draft) between regular academic terms may be replaced on the roster. A student-athlete who withdraws from the team but remains enrolled at the institution may not be replaced. (c) **Football Midyear Replacement – Competition in Subsequent Academic Term.** In football, a student-athlete who will be replaced at midyear may continue to practice and compete during the second term of the academic year... A replacement student-athlete may not participate in team athletically related activities or receive competition-related travel expenses until after the end of the sport's playing season."
**SOURCE:** NCAA Division I Proposal 2025-17, "PLAYING AND PRACTICE SEASONS -- IMPLEMENTATION OF HOUSE V. NCAA SETTLEMENT -- ROSTER LIMITATION PROVISIONS," effective July 1, 2025 ("Passed Roster Limit Rules, June 23, 2025") — https://assets.tina.io/29b83311-e587-42b1-861e-87ebde9aa253/Passed%20Roster%20Limit%20Rules,%20June%2023,%202025.pdf

**NUMBER:** Walk-ons now count. Before July 1, 2025 an FBS roster could run 120+ because walk-ons sat outside the 85. Now a walk-on uses one of the 105.
**RULE:** "**17.2.2 Roster Limit Inclusion.** Beginning the day after an institution's deadline for roster submission, an individual must be on the sport's submitted roster to participate in athletically related activities (countable, required, or voluntary) for the remainder of academic year or the end of the sport's playing season, whichever is later."
**SOURCE:** NCAA Division I Proposal 2025-17 (June 23, 2025) — same URL as above

**FOR A FAMILY:** The number that matters is 105, and it is not going up. But the real story is that D1 football rosters used to average about 146; squeezing them into 105 is what pushed coaches to shed dozens of walk-ons and rebuild rosters through the portal.

## 5. Division II and Division III

**NUMBER:** D2 football = 36.0 equivalencies (a team-wide pool, split as partial grants across a much larger roster).
**RULE:** "In Division II, each sport has a maximum number of athletics scholarships that may be awarded across an entire team. These are called equivalency limits." Table: "Football | 36.0".
**SOURCE:** NCAA, "Division II Athletics Scholarship" (ncaa.org, updated May 20, 2026) — https://www.ncaa.org/division-ii/governance/membership-and-institutional-resources/athletics-scholarships ; see also NCAA, "Division II partial-scholarship model" — https://www.ncaa.org/sports/2014/9/25/division-ii-partial-scholarship-model.aspx ("schools are allowed to award up to 36 'equivalencies' or full grants, but of course the rosters in football are much larger than 36 players")

**NUMBER:** D2 has no roster cap and no settlement.
**RULE:** The settlement applies to Division I: "Each sport of a Division I institution that is a member of a defendant conference or opts in to the House settlement must comply with the roster limits set forth below..."
**SOURCE:** NCAA Bylaw 17.2 (LSDBi, 2026-10-07) — https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/bylaw?refDate=20261007&ruleId=106414
**UNVERIFIED:** The D2 "60 total equivalencies across men's sports other than football and basketball" cap, and the pending proposal to remove it from August 2027, appear only in secondary sources (NextCommit, Oct 12, 2026) — I did not confirm them in a D2 manual or NCAA release.

**NUMBER:** D3 = zero athletic aid.
**RULE:** "**ATHLETICS SCHOLARSHIPS** ... Division III: **No athletics scholarships.** ... **80%** of athletes receive nonathletics aid."
**RULE:** "Most, but not all Division I or II schools offer athletics scholarships; **Division III schools do not offer athletics scholarships.**"
**SOURCE:** NCAA, *2026-27 Divisional Facts and Figures* (Sept 29, 2026) — https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/ncaa/common/2026-27_NCAADivisionalFactsandFigures.pdf ; NCAA, "NCAA Recruiting" (ncaa.org, updated May 15, 2026) — https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/recruiting

**FOR A FAMILY:** D2 football is 36 full-ride-equivalents spread over roughly 100+ players, so most D2 players are on partial aid. D3 is 0 — the family pays, and the trade is academics and campus life.

## 6. NAIA and NJCAA

**NUMBER:** NAIA football = 24 full-scholarship equivalencies per team, dividable; no NAIA roster-size limit.
**RULE:** "In the NAIA, each sport has an upper limit for the amount of institutional aid allowed per school. The school may choose to divide the scholarships among student-athletes, so long as the total does not exceed the limit for the sport." Table: "**Football | 24**". "The total amount of scholarship money a team has available is equal to (the average cost of attendance at the school) multiplied by (the above limit for scholarships)."
**SOURCE:** NAIA, "NAIA Financial Aid" — https://www.naia.org/why-naia/naia-financial-aid (consistent with NAIA Official Handbook 2026-27, Policy XIII, per NAIA's "Guide for the College-Bound Student-Athlete," May 10, 2026 — https://www.naia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/naia_guide_college_bound_student.pdf)

**NUMBER:** NAIA aid ceiling = actual cost of attendance; no letter-of-intent program.
**RULE:** "Financial aid to student-athletes is limited to the actual cost of: [tuition, mandatory fees, required books and supplies, room and board]." "As an association, the NAIA does not have a letter of intent program in which students sign a binding agreement to participate in athletics at a particular institution."
**SOURCE:** NAIA, "Guide for the College-Bound Student-Athlete," May 10, 2026 — https://www.naia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/naia_guide_college_bound_student.pdf

**NUMBER:** NJCAA football = 85 Letters of Intent / scholarships per team; NJCAA LOI signing date for fall football = **February 3** for the 2026-27 academic year (February 4 for 2025-26).
**RULE:** Sports Procedures Chart, Football (Fall), Men: "Start Date Aug 1 | Game Date Aug 20 | End Date Nov 21 | 11 Games | 2 [scrimmages] | Supplemental Eligibility Roster Deadline Oct 30 | **85** [Letters of Intent Signees/Scholarships] | **Feb 3** [Letter of Intent Signing Date]." Chart notes: "The numbers listed above are the same for each Division (I, II & III). Division I may grant the number of scholarships listed above. Division II may grant the number of scholarships listed above, but are limited to tuition, course related fees, course related books and up to $250 in course required supplies. Division III may not offer athletic scholarships." And: "A signee of an NJCAA Letter of Intent counts toward the overall maximum number of individuals in that sport regardless of the amount of athletic scholarship."
**SOURCE:** NJCAA, *2026-27 Sports Procedures Chart* (file dated 9/9/26) — copy at https://media.ardorsportshub.com/files/404933495157163011/3fb3dae3297443a688333ba7d0cbef68-2026-2027-Sports-Procedures-Chart-9-9-26.pdf ; NJCAA compliance/LOI page — https://www.njcaa.org/eligibility/loi ; NJCAA Handbook Bylaws 2026-27, Art. VI, Sec. 1 — https://media.ardorsportshub.com/files/404933495157163011/6b815aea7cf842719e5deeefd7dc9310-NJCAA-Handbook---Bylaws---2026-27-9-9-26.pdf
**NOTE:** The NJCAA chart carries the footnote "As of December 16, 2025, this document is tentative and may have changes after BOR and SG Meetings are conducted..." Confirm the date with the NJCAA compliance office before relying on Feb 3.

**NUMBER:** NJCAA junior-college eligibility to sign: a high-schooler must have completed junior year.
**RULE:** "**A.2. Signing Dates:** A student must have completed their junior year of high school to sign an NJCAA Letter of Intent for the subsequent year."
**SOURCE:** NJCAA Handbook Bylaws 2026-27, Article VI, Section 1.A.2

**FOR A FAMILY:** NAIA's 24 is a genuine money constraint (24 full rides' worth of dollars spread over ~100 players). NJCAA's 85 is headcount, but each junior-college "scholarship" is usually tuition-plus only, and the paper LOI lands in early February — earlier than any NCAA signing date.

## 7. Preferred walk-ons

**NUMBER:** A "preferred walk-on" is a recruited athlete with a guaranteed roster spot and no scholarship.
**RULE:** "**Preferred Walk-On:** 'Preferred walk-on' status means a college coach wants you on the team but doesn't have a scholarship for you. **You are guaranteed a spot on the roster.**" And: "**Walk-on and 'preferred' walk-on recruits must register with the Eligibility Center and meet initial-eligibility standards.**"
**SOURCE:** NCAA, *Guide for the College-Bound Student-Athlete*, 2026-27 (Sept 5, 2026) — http://fs.ncaa.org/Docs/eligibility_center/Student_Resources/CBSA.pdf ; see also NCAA, "NCAA Recruiting" (updated May 15, 2026) — https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/recruiting

**NUMBER:** The protections are thin, and they are thinner than they used to be.
**RULE (protections that exist):** the athlete is recruited, so they must be NCAA-certified eligible (initial-eligibility standards) before they can practice or compete; a tryout has a 14-day grace period to get on the squad list, and any tryout after the roster deadline must be added to the roster if the team is under the cap: "**17.2.6.3.1 Exception -- 14-Day Grace Period.** ... For an institution that is subject to the roster limitations in Bylaw 17.2, an individual who participates in a tryout after the roster submission deadline must be added to the sport's submitted roster per Bylaw 17.2.1.1."
**RULE (protections that do not exist):** no scholarship means no scholarship protection. The core-guarantee shield ("cannot be revoked unless and until the student chooses to transfer") applies only to "a student-athlete receiving athletics aid." A preferred walk-on receiving no aid has none of it. And the walk-on's spot is inside the 105 (Bylaw 17.2), so a coach can simply not renew the offer.
**SOURCE:** NCAA Division I Proposal 2025-17 (June 23, 2025), Bylaw 17.2.6.3.1 — https://assets.tina.io/29b83311-e587-42b1-861e-87ebde9aa253/Passed%20Roster%20Limit%20Rules,%20June%2023,%202025.pdf ; NCAA, "DI Board of Directors formally adopts changes to roster limits," June 23, 2025 — https://www.ncaa.org/news/2025/6/23/media-center-di-board-of-directors-formally-adopts-changes-to-roster-limits.aspx

**NUMBER:** Tryout rights differ sharply by division: D1 tryouts are "very limited"; D2 may hold one on-campus tryout; D3 may not hold practice tryouts with college-bound athletes.
**RULE:** "**Division I** tryouts are very limited. ... **Division II** schools may conduct one on-campus tryout. **Division III** schools cannot hold practice tryouts with college-bound student-athletes."
**SOURCE:** NCAA, "NCAA Recruiting" (ncaa.org, updated May 15, 2026) — https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/recruiting

**FOR A FAMILY:** "Preferred walk-on" is a coach's promise of a *spot*, not a promise of money, and it is not contractually protected. It is worth a lot in a sport where the program is cutting from 146 to 105 — but the kid should treat it as a scholarship offer that pays $0.

## 8. Program counts and the odds

**NUMBER:** Football programs, 2026-27 NCAA projected sponsorship: **Division I 266, Division II 161, Division III 243, total 670.**
**RULE:** "Football | **266** (DI) | **161** (DII) | **243** (DIII) | **670** (Total)" — 2026-27 NCAA Projected Sport Sponsorship, finalized with data as of July 29, 2026.
**SOURCE:** NCAA, "NCAA Membership Composition and Sport Sponsorship" (ncaa.org, updated May 21, 2026) — https://www.ncaa.org/about-us/membership-directory/membership-composition-and-sport-sponsorship

**NUMBER:** FBS/FCS split of those 266: **138 FBS / 128 FCS** (derived).
**RULE:** DI membership as of September 2026: "Active | FBS/Autonomy **68** | FBS/Non-Autonomy **68** | FCS **124** | DI **99** | DI Total **361**"; reclassifying "From FCS to FBS **2**" and "From DIII to DI-FCS **4**." So 68+68+2 = **138** FBS football programs and 124+4 = **128** FCS football programs, which sums to the NCAA's own **266** DI football total.
**SOURCE:** Same NCAA page as above (membership breakdown, data final as of Sept 14, 2026). The individual FBS/FCS program counts are my derivation from NCAA membership data, not a separately published NCAA football-team table.

**NUMBER:** NAIA football = **96** programs. NJCAA football = **59** teams.
**RULE:** "**250** NAIA Members | **96** NAIA Football Members | **20** NAIA Conferences | **31** National Championship Sports." And NJCAA football: "# Football Teams 2026-27 · **59** teams".

## 9. Transfer rules that change how many spots are left for a high-school player

**NUMBER:** There is **no NCAA limit on the share of a football roster that may come in as transfers.** What the NCAA used to cap was the number of *new high-school signees*, and that cap was removed.

**NUMBER:** The gateway rule — a four-year transfer must sit out one year of championship-counting competition if he played in the same sport at a school where his performance counted for an NCAA championship.
**RULE:** "**14.5.5 Four-Year College Transfers.** An undergraduate transfer student from a four-year institution is eligible for competition if at the time of transfer to the certifying institution (see Bylaw 14.5.2), the student would have been academically eligible to compete had the student remained at the institution where the student most recently attended class during a regular term as a full-time student, except that the student is not required to have fulfilled percentage-of-degree requirements at the previous institution. (Revised: 4/18/24)"
**RULE:** "**14.5.5.2 Competition in Year of Transfer.** A transfer student from a four-year institution who satisfies the applicable four-year college transfer requirements (see Bylaw 14.5.5) shall not be eligible for competition in which the student-athlete's performance could be used for NCAA championship qualification or consideration if the student-athlete participated in competition at the previous four-year institution in the same sport in which the student-athlete's performance could have been used for NCAA championship qualification or consideration. (Adopted: 1/10/95 effective 8/1/95... Revised: 4/18/24)" Also "**14.5.5.1 General Rule.** A transfer student from a four-year institution shall not be eligible for intercollegiate competition at a member institution until the student has fulfilled a residence requirement of one full academic year..." with exceptions in 14.5.5.2.
