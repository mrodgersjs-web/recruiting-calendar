## Football + Girls' Basketball: What Coaches Say They Want to See

**Check date: November 11, 2026.** Every finding below is sourced. Items with anonymous coaches/blogs are marked UNVERIFIED. Where I could not find a named coach on a sub-question, I say so rather than fill it.

---

### 1. Football — the highlight video itself

**Finding 1 — Length and the 45-second hook**
WHO SAID IT: Ryan Gunderson, Director of Player Personnel, Nebraska; Chris Fore, recruiting coordinator.
WHAT: "Always put your best stuff first... You may only get 30 seconds or a minute of somebody's time and if that doesn't impress them right away, they're not going to turn your film back on." Fore: 7-on-7 film and stats "mean nothing to college coaches."
SOURCE: FootballScoop, republication of Hudl (https://footballscoop.com/news/5-things-college-coaches-actually-look-recruiting-highlight); MaxPreps "Coaches Corner" (https://www.maxpreps.com/news/QQsLGVUHkkiINham7c6PGw/coaches-corner-a-recruiters-look-at-7-on-7-football-and-how-relevant-it-can-be.htm)

**Finding 2 — Target length and what NOT to do**
WHO SAID IT: NCSA recruiting coaches (staff-authored guidance).
WHAT: Best 25 plays, roughly 3-5 minutes, no more than five. No music, sound effects, crazy transitions, or slowed/sped-up film. Start with best plays. File: https://www.ncsasports.org/football/recruiting-video

**Finding 3 — Camera angle rules (the part parents get wrong)**
WHO SAID IT: NCSA recruiting coaches.
WHAT: Game film should be shot from the press box with a wide angle capturing all 22 players. Some coaches arrange an end-zone tight shot showing the 10 linemen to display alignment and footwork. Start each clip a couple seconds before the snap; use a drop shadow or arrow before every play.
SOURCE: NCSA, https://www.ncsasports.org/football/recruiting-video

**Finding 4 — QB: what must be in the tape**
WHO SAID IT: NCSA Senior Recruiting Coaches (position skills video).
WHAT: 2-3 reps per drill, skills video 2-4 minutes max; consistency and accuracy primary; drills showing pocket movement, throwing on platform, throwing to multiple route trees, variety of spot throws.
SOURCE: NCSA, https://www.ncsasports.org/football/recruiting-video

**Finding 5 — Skill players: WR and RB**
WHO SAID IT: Jordan Wells, former WR player and coach, for NCSA; NCSA recruiting coaches for RB.
WHAT: WR drills: Vertical Tracking (2-3 reps), 30-degree tracking break (3 each side), Gauntlet (1 rep), plus 2-3 routes on air in your system (post, post-corner, 15-yard dig). RB film should show blocking, catching and running the ball, not just carries.
SOURCE: NCSA, https://www.ncsasports.org/football/recruiting-video

**Finding 6 — Line and linebacker**
WHO SAID IT: Bill Perkins, NCSA recruiting coach, 25+ years as a defensive line coach; AJ Foster, NCSA recruiting coach and high school coach; Brett Morris, former OL player and coach.
WHAT: DL: use of hands, change of direction, burst; drills Bag Slap, 45 Break, Punch and Slide. LB: stay low and explosive, great stance, don't cross your feet; drills Box, Attack the Bag, Run/Pass Read, Shuffle. OL: good feet, stance, power; Long Pull, Short Pull, Drive Block, Kick Slide (called the most important OL drill).
SOURCE: NCSA, https://www.ncsasports.org/football/recruiting-video

**Finding 7 — Full games, all-conference footage, and skills tape**
WHO SAID IT: NCSA recruiting coaches (football and women's basketball).
WHAT: Football: "College coaches might ask for full game footage, but that will also be on a case-by-case basis"; 7-on-7 film only as a supplement unless a coach asks. Women's basketball: send the highlight video "as well as one unedited full game video"; clips from two or three games is enough.
SOURCE: NCSA football and women's basketball pages (https://www.ncsasports.org/football/recruiting-video ; https://www.ncsasports.org/womens-basketball/highlight-video)

**Finding 8 — Do coaches want full games? (girls' basketball, named D1)**
WHO SAID IT: Gayle Fulks, head coach, Davidson women's basketball.
WHAT: "I look for a diverse skill set in a limited amount of games. I want to see everything you do." A D1 women's coaches' panel said: "The elevator pitch is the highlight video, then comes the full game." (Panel coaches partly unnamed — UNVERIFIED as to identity.)
SOURCE: 2aDays (https://www.2adays.com/blog/missing-the-madness-look-ahead-to-next-season-with-recruiting-advice-from-davidson-womens-basketball-coach-gayle-fulks); BCANY panel recap PDF (https://www.bcany.org/_files/ugd/b7ad0b_2cf3e0e85c804e3894d0f18a629f3de2.pdf) (panel identities partly UNVERIFIED)

**Finding 9 — What makes a coach stop watching**
WHO SAID IT: NCSA; Coach McCabe (Brennan? not named — UNVERIFIED); recruiting-service guidance.
WHAT: NCSA: "Don't give them any reason to stop watching." If a coach spends five seconds hunting for you on each play, that is a coach who stops watching. If coaches can't find you within three seconds, they stop. Show the athlete before every snap; spot-shadow, don't rely on jersey number alone.
SOURCE: NCSA (https://www.ncsasports.org/college-recruiting-video); VarsityDraft (https://varsitydraft.com/recruiting/highlight-videos); Knup (https://knup.com/blog/how-to-make-a-recruiting-video) — the last two are recruiting services, not coaches.

**Finding 10 — Do not edit to hide mistakes**
WHO SAID IT: D3sports editorial guidance for college recruits.
WHAT: A college coach roughly gauges a recruit within the first two minutes. Send half and complete game footage through the regular season plus an end-of-season "blow by blow" highlight. Skills video roughly 1-2 minutes. "Avoid editing video to camouflage mistakes."
SOURCE: D3sports, https://www.d3sports.com/columns/on-recruiting/2017-2018/video-tips-to-showcase-athlete

### 2. Football — the first email, subject line, one-sheet, parent emails, contact rules

**Finding 11 — Subject line**
WHO SAID IT: NCSA; SportsRecruits; Scout Connect coaching panel (coaches anonymous — UNVERIFIED).
WHAT: NCSA: keep subject 50-75 characters; include name, graduation year, position(s), city/state, one standout detail. SportsRecruits: "Make it something short & eye-catching, and add your graduation year." Panel: "Name, Grad Year, Position"; "Grad Year | Name | Position | GPA."
SOURCE: NCSA (https://www.ncsasports.org/recruiting/contacting-college-coaches/email); SportsRecruits help center (https://help.sportsrecruits.com/hc/en-us/articles/213531786-What-Should-I-Say-To-A-College-Coach); Scout Connect (https://scout-connect.org/2026/01/13/recruiting-tips-emailing-a-college-coach)

**Finding 12 — What the email body must contain**
WHO SAID IT: NCSA; SportsRecruits; FieldLevel (recruiting service).
WHAT: NCSA checklist: good subject line; introduce yourself and purpose; make it personal; notable athletic and academic stats; give an action item; state your follow-up plan; proofread. SportsRecruits: address each coach by name, express interest specifically, include notable achievements, end with "Please let me know if there is anything you need from me." Don't send blast emails; coaches recognize them.
SOURCE: NCSA (https://www.ncsasports.org/recruiting/contacting-college-coaches/email); SportsRecruits (https://sportsrecruits.com/resources/contacting-college-coaches/emailing-coaches)

**Finding 13 — The position profile / one-sheet must list**
WHO SAID IT: NCSA recruiting coaches (football video info slide); Verified Athletics survey of college coaches.
WHAT: Slide with name, height, weight, position(s), phone, email, cumulative GPA, ACT/SAT, and your head coach's name, phone and email. Survey: D1 coaches were 50% more likely to watch film if it went to the right coach and included transcripts; only 1 in 3 D1 coaches watch all film sent.
SOURCE: NCSA (https://www.ncsasports.org/football/recruiting-video); Verified Athletics (https://www.verifiedathletics.com/athlete-resources/how-can-high-school-coaches-get-college-coaches-to-watch-recruiting-film/)

**Finding 14 — What coaches say about parent-written emails**
WHO SAID IT: Scout Connect coaching panel, Coach #3 and Coach #5 (identities not published — UNVERIFIED); NCSA.
WHAT: Panel Coach #3: BCC-ing 50 coaches, using the wrong school or coach name, and "email from a parent" are no-go items. Coach #5: "If your mom or dad is reaching out on your behalf, that is a huge red flag. I want to get to know the kid first." NCSA: avoid calling coaches early to ask about scholarships; coaches are recruiting student-athletes, not parents.
SOURCE: Scout Connect (https://scout-connect.org/2026/01/13/recruiting-tips-emailing-a-college-coach); NCSA (https://www.ncsasports.org/blog/parents-talk-college-coaches)

**Finding 15 — Texting after the coach gives a number**
WHO SAID IT: NCSA; Next Level Sports; CaptainU; PROACTIVEVOLLEY.
WHAT: NCSA: exchange numbers and explicitly ask "is it okay to text?"; texting usually starts after a first call. Next Level: don't text without permission, sign your full name, never text between 11 p.m. and 6 a.m. in the coach's time zone. CaptainU: "Persistent is good. Pushy isn't" — if no answer, don't send five more texts.
SOURCE: NCSA (https://www.ncsasports.org/recruiting/contacting-college-coaches/text-messaging); Next Level Sports (https://nlevelsports.net/blogs/articles/texting-coaches); CaptainU (https://captainu.com/texting-college-coaches-what-to-say-and-when-to-send-it)

**Finding 16 — Calling, and how to ask for a number**
WHO SAID IT: SportsRecruits; NCSA; a Head D2 college football coach, "Coach Williams" (first name not published — UNVERIFIED as to identity).
WHAT: SportsRecruits: there are no rules restricting a recruit from calling a coach; coaches asking for your number means real interest. NCSA: wait one to three days after the email to call; leave a voicemail with name, grad year, school, position and a callback number; never ask for a scholarship on an early call. Coach Williams: "It is on the website. Every number in his office is in the staff directory... Call the main office number. Ask who is responsible for recruiting your son's position and his geographic area."
SOURCE: SportsRecruits (https://sportsrecruits.com/resources/contacting-college-coaches/calling-coaches); NCSA (https://www.ncsasports.org/recruiting/contacting-college-coaches/phone-calls); GoNextPlay podcast (https://gonextplay.com/post/head-d2-coach-shares-how-to-get-an-offer-with-1-phone-call)

### 3. Football — 7-on-7, camps, combines, all-star games, wasted money

**Finding 17 — 7-on-7: the sharpest quote in the set**
WHO SAID IT: David Shaw, head coach, Stanford.
WHAT: 7-on-7 "means absolutely nothing to me as an evaluator. I will also never ever, ever have a recruiting conversation with a 7-on-7 coach." He has never watched 7-on-7 film.
SOURCE: MaxPreps quoting Shaw (https://www.maxpreps.com/news/QQsLGVUHkkiINham7c6PGw/coaches-corner-a-recruiters-look-at-7-on-7-football-and-how-relevant-it-can-be.htm); Tacoma News Tribune (https://www.thenewstribune.com/sports/high-school/article161493228.html)

**Finding 18 — 7-on-7: the FCS view**
WHO SAID IT: Brian Strandley, assistant coach, Eastern Washington (FCS).
WHAT: Asked how beneficial 7-on-7 is in college recruiting: "It's not. It's not at all." Parents chase "this recruiting service, this camp, this combine, this 7-on-7 league"; the benefit ends at meeting new kids. A survey in the same piece: 80% of parents said coaches told them 7-on-7 isn't useful.
SOURCE: Tacoma News Tribune, https://www.thenewstribune.com/sports/high-school/article161493228.html

**Finding 19 — What 7-on-7 IS good for**
WHO SAID IT: Chris Fore, recruiting coordinator (MaxPreps Coaches Corner).
WHAT: "I feel it's another evaluation tool for us. Ultimately, we don't look at 7-on-7 film and stats; those mean nothing to college coaches. We need to see how athletes execute in a true 11-on-11 game environment." But hidden character traits (fit in a new group, not a "me" guy) show up.
SOURCE: MaxPreps, https://www.maxpreps.com/news/QQsLGVUHkkiINham7c6PGw/coaches-corner-a-recruiters-look-at-7-on-7-football-and-how-relevant-it-can-be.htm

**Finding 20 — 7-on-7 vs game film vs combines**
WHO SAID IT: Gary Beck, football coordinator of support services, Oregon State.
WHAT: 7-on-7 tape "isn't as good as game film, but it is certainly better than combine numbers." Separately, Bleacher Report noted coaches are barred from attending 7-on-7 events and colleges from hosting them (2012 NCAA legislation), though recruits may send tape.
SOURCE: Bleacher Report, https://bleacherreport.com/articles/1661050-inside-the-world-of-7-on-7-college-footballs-explosive-new-recruiting-trend

**Finding 21 — Combines**
WHO SAID IT: Coach McCabe, football coach quoted in Bleacher Report (name partly UNVERIFIED); Go Big Recruiting (named recruiting service).
WHAT: Combines usually take place without college coaches in attendance; McCabe: "If you think college coaches pay attention to anything that happens at these combines or where the recruits are rated, you are delusional." Go Big: combine results let coaches cross-check a verified 40 time or height/weight; don't expect to be recruited solely by attending.
SOURCE: Bleacher Report (https://bleacherreport.com/articles/2366666-how-much-do-college-coaches-care-about-recruiting-camps-and-the-star-system); Go Big Recruiting (https://www.gobigrecruiting.com/recruiting101/football/scholarships_faq/faq_question_combines)

**Finding 22 — Camps**
WHO SAID IT: Dan Mullen, head coach, Mississippi State (2015).
WHAT: "The satellite camps, they're recruiting camps... The only purpose for it is recruiting. I don't think it's the right purpose for camps." "We've probably had 1,000 kids on campus in camps and there's probably a couple guys who will be SEC players out of that group."
SOURCE: FootballScoop, https://www.footballscoop.com/2025/03/05/dan-mullen-recruiting-shouldnt-be-the-purpose-of-camps

**Finding 23 — Paid recruiting services: the money coaches say to keep**
WHO SAID IT: James Franklin, head coach, Penn State; Chris Creighton, head coach, Eastern Michigan.
WHAT: Franklin on paid recruiting services: "They go right in the trash." Creighton: "I guarantee we are all watching film... you don't have to go through an outside agency for us to see the film." USA Football notes coaches only trust services they vet and pay for.
SOURCE: USA Football blog, https://blogs.usafootball.com/blog/1105/college-football-coaches-say-parents-shouldn-t-pay-for-recruiting-services

**Finding 24 — How coaches evaluate camps and what parents waste money on**
WHO SAID IT: NCSA (parent guidance).
WHAT: "Too many parents waste money and time sending their kids to college camps that won't help them get recruited... most college coaches attend these events to evaluate recruits who are already on their list." Advice: confirm coaches from your target schools will be in attendance before paying.
SOURCE: NCSA, https://www.ncsasports.org/parents-recruiting-tips/stress-reducers

**Finding 25 — All-star games: GAP**
WHO SAID IT: no named coach found in this pass.
WHAT: I could not find a named D1/FCS coach or athletics-department statement on what high school all-star games are actually for. Do not state anything as coach consensus here. Two adjacent facts only: coaches may attend a school's own camp on campus, and Dan Mullen's point in Finding 22 (recruiting camps attract bodies, not talent).
SOURCE: Gap — flagged as UNVERIFIED/unconfirmed in this research pass.

### 4. Girls' basketball — same questions, plus AAU and the certification rule

**Finding 26 — The rule: evaluation only at certified events**
WHO SAID IT: NCAA Enforcement Certification and Approvals Group (ECAG).
WHAT: "NCAA certification permits Division I basketball coaches to attend nonscholastic events... to evaluate participating prospective student athletes." Bylaws: 13.1.7.6 (Women's Basketball Evaluations), 13.17.3 (women's calendar). "The purpose of Division I coach attendance at an event is for evaluation only." Certification requires athletic trainer on site, background checks, online rosters.
SOURCE: NCAA ECAG PDF, https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/enforcement/ecag/junschbb/ECAG_BasketballCertificationRequirementsandProcedures.pdf

**Finding 27 — The same rule stated for coaches explicitly**
WHO SAID IT: National Federation of State High School Associations (NFCA) compliance deck.
WHAT: "Div I coaches cannot attend non-certified events." "College coaches are at the events to EVALUATE not 'recruit'." "College coaches can always attend scholastic events — scholastic events don't need NCAA certification."
SOURCE: NFCA, https://nfca.org/web_docs/CertificationofEvents.pdf

**Finding 28 — When women's coaches may evaluate**
WHO SAID IT: NCAA official site.
WHAT: Permissible certified-event times: Spring 8 a.m.-10 p.m. daily (women), June noon Thursday-6 p.m. Saturday (women), July 8 a.m.-10 p.m. daily. June scholastic events for women in 2026: June 18-20.
SOURCE: NCAA.org, https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/recruiting/basketball-certification and https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/recruiting/basketball-certification/june-scholastic-basketball-events

**Finding 29 — What D1 women's programs say about AAU**
WHO SAID IT: Gayle Fulks, head coach, Davidson.
WHAT: "With the current basketball landscape the way it is, playing well in AAU tournaments during our NCAA sanctioned evaluation periods is probably the best way to get on our staff's radar." Her staff looks for high basketball IQ, defensive motor, good shooting, smart passing.
SOURCE: 2aDays, https://www.2adays.com/blog/missing-the-madness-look-ahead-to-next-season-with-recruiting-advice-from-davidson-womens-basketball-coach-gayle-fulks

**Finding 30 — Coach on whether AAU is still the best evaluation tool**
WHO SAID IT: an NCAA/NAIA coaching panel in Athletic Director magazine (names in excerpt: Ridder, Bethune-Cookman; Martin; "Martin" quoted on AAU).
WHAT: "Without a doubt there are flaws, but AAU basketball is still the best way to evaluate student-athletes in basketball." Concedes problems: shoe companies control the economics, AAU coaches expect returns, and event organizers charge "$500 for 'recruiting packets' that are full of wrong information."
SOURCE: Athletic Director magazine, https://athleticdirectoru.com/articles/experts-roundtable-a-new-era-of-basketball-recruiting

**Finding 31 — AAU: the coaches who think it's optional**
WHO SAID IT: Andy Sears, club coach, quoted in Hudl's Basketball Recruiting blog.
WHAT: "Unless they're playing Division I level high school basketball, we'd prefer seeing your club film on them." And: "Coaches don't have time to watch you go out and destroy a terrible team. They want to see you play one of the top-ranked teams in New England."
SOURCE: Hudl, https://www.hudl.com/blog/build-the-brand-how-to-get-hype-for-your-club-and-your-players

**Finding 32 — AAU does not guarantee evaluation; it costs money**
WHO SAID IT: Level Up Basketball (named recruiting service); Scorability (named service).
WHAT: "Certified events can allow Division I coaches to evaluate prospects during permitted periods, but certification is not an endorsement and attendance does not guarantee recruitment." Scorability: the shoe circuits concentrate high-major D1 evaluation, but "regional grassroots circuits serve a much wider share of college recruiting than the shoe circuits."
SOURCE: Level Up Basketball (https://www.levelupbasket.com/blog/college-basketball-recruiting-guide-for-high-school-players); Scorability (https://scorability.com/recruiting/basketball/aau-and-grassroots)

**Finding 33 — Girls' basketball video: what to include and what to cut**
WHO SAID IT: a panel of six collegiate women's basketball head coaches (BCANY meeting recap; panel coaches partly unnamed — UNVERIFIED).
WHAT: "Keep it to 2 minutes but show the range. Pazzazz but no gimmicks. Less is more. Attach a good game film too." "We do not want to see slo-mo or special effects. Also we do not need to see your free-throw attempts." Circle the player in the first 1-3 plays.
SOURCE: BCANY PDF, https://www.bcany.org/_files/ugd/b7ad0b_2cf3e0e85c804e3894d0f18a629f3de2.pdf

**Finding 34 — Girls' basketball: a D1 head coach who doesn't want highlights**
WHO SAID IT: Ron Dambrot, then head coach, Duquesne.
WHAT: On finding a team: it "doesn't necessarily have to be the biggest program, but there has to be some other recruitable guys on that team." On film: "I can't speak for everybody, but I don't watch them" — don't send highlights. Warns parents about programs promising playing time.
SOURCE: Andscape, https://andscape.com/features/real-talk-a-college-basketball-coach-explains-how-to-go-d-free-series/

**Finding 35 — Girls' basketball: coaches want multiple in-person looks**
WHO SAID IT: Tammi Reiss, head coach, URI women's basketball; BCANY panel.
WHAT: Reiss: "I must see the player in person multiple times to evaluate them properly and all the intangibles we are looking for." BCANY panel: "We want to see kids play in person, multiple times... We would rather have coaches reach out to her rather than the player sometimes."
SOURCE: 2aDays (https://www.2adays.com/blog/13-recruiting-questions-with-uri-head-womens-basketball-coach-tammi-reiss); BCANY PDF (as above)

### 5. Both sports — "we like you but can't write it down yet," asking for a number, real interest vs. marketing

**Finding 36 — What the soft phrases actually mean**
WHO SAID IT: Pathley (recruiting service, published 2026); FullRideU (recruiting service, Nov 10 2026); NCAA via NCSA.
WHAT: "We love you"/"you're a priority"/"we'll work something out" = real interest, nothing about money or a spot yet. "We are working on what we can do financially" = the number is not approved and may be small or zero. A verbal offer is not binding for either side. Written offer of athletics aid: August 1 of senior year in most sports, November 15 in football.
SOURCE: Pathley (https://www.pathley.ai/blog-posts/scholarship-offer-vs-roster-spot); FullRideU (https://fullrideu.com/post/football-scholarship-offer-vs-coach-interest); NCSA (https://www.ncsasports.org/recruiting/contacting-college-coaches/recruiting-letters)

**Finding 37 — The "cannot write it down yet" reason, from the coach's side**
WHO SAID IT: a Texas high school coaches' association guide (College Recruiting Information for HS Coaches).
WHAT: An offer may be non-committable because the school filled up at that position or is no longer recruiting the prospect. Advice to athletes: ask whether the offer is committable and what the coach's ideal commitment timeline looks like. Written offer of aid cannot come before August 1 of senior year (written before the new NLI).
SOURCE: Thrillshare-hosted PDF, https://files-backend.assets.thrillshare.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/4731/Yisd/7a92ce91-d4ac-4e8e-87ac-d0ec9da7e90d/College-Recruiting-Information-for-HS-Coaches.pdf

**Finding 38 — The cleanest "real interest vs. noise" ladder**
WHO SAID IT: First Step Athletics (named recruiting service, July 2026); Shownxt (recruiting service).
WHAT: "A recruiting questionnaire is not an offer. A camp invite is not an offer. A handwritten letter is not an offer." Strongest non-offer signal: the coach traveling to your house or school. Shownxt: "A generic camp invite, a questionnaire link or a form letter is marketing, not interest. Count the actions, not the warmth of the words."
SOURCE: First Step Athletics (https://fsathletics.site/blog/reading-a-college-coachs-actual-interest-level); Shownxt (https://www.shownxt.com/answers/how-to-know-if-a-college-coach-is-interested)

**Finding 39 — Camp email vs. real interest**
WHO SAID IT: National Scouting Report (named service); 1001 Recruit Tips (blog, UNVERIFIED).
WHAT: NSR: "Most of the time the response you're getting is an automatic circuit of emails"; most contact is generic because the recruit reached out, not because the coach is interested. 1001 Recruit Tips: a camp invite is a good sign, a personal call or visit to camp is a great sign; if the coach invites you to camp but has no other contact, they may be filling the camp.
SOURCE: NSR (https://www.nsr-inc.com/scouting-news/how-to-know-if-a-coach-is-interested-in-you); 1001recruittips.com (https://1001recruittips.com/q-if-a-coach-invites-me-to-their-camp-are-they-going-to-offer-me-or-are-they-just-interested-in-making-money-filling-up-the-camp) — blog, UNVERIFIED as to coach identity.

**Finding 40 — The family question to ask**
WHO SAID IT: NCSA; Shownxt.
WHAT: NCSA's "3 questions to avoid": don't ask "Why haven't you offered me yet?" — "Don't back the coach into a corner." Instead ask where you stand on the board and what they still need to see. Shownxt: "If you are unsure, ask the coach directly where you stand and what they still need to see."
SOURCE: NCSA (https://www.ncsasports.org/blog/questions-to-ask-a-college-coach); Shownxt (as above)

### Gaps to disclose to the parent

- **All-star games:** no named coach or athletics-department statement found (Finding 25).
- **"All-conference footage":** I found only case-by-case full-game requests (Finding 7). No coach specifically asks for all-conference tape as a separate category; the practical answer is: send the full game of your best/highest-profile games, including conference games.
- **NFCA advice** is field hockey, not football (I cited it only for the certified-events rule, which it states directly).
- Several strong sources are recruiting services rather than coaches (VarsityDraft, Knup, Pathley, First Step Athletics, Shownxt, Go Big, Level Up, Scorability). Coach statements themselves are attributed where available; anonymous coaches are flagged UNVERIFIED.
