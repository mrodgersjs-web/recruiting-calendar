## 1. DIVISION I — roster limit under the House settlement

**1. NUMBER:** 15 players max on a D1 women's basketball roster (settlement schools only).
**RULE:** NCAA Bylaw **17.2 Roster Limitations** [S]: "Each sport of a Division I institution that is a member of a defendant conference or opts in to the House settlement must comply with the roster limits set forth below…" — table row: "Women's Basketball | 15".
**SOURCE:** *NCAA Division I Manual, 2026-27*, Bylaw 17.2, NCAA Legislative Services Database, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008 (same text at https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/bylaw?refDate=20261108&ruleId=106414). Bylaw 17.2 footnote: "(Adopted: 6/6/25 effective 7/1/25, Revised: 8/25/26 women's flag football limit effective beginning with the 2027-28 academic year)".
**PARENT MEANING:** At a D1 school in the settlement, a women's basketball roster can legally hold 15 players — no more.

**2. NUMBER:** Effective **July 1, 2025** (2025-26 academic year); roster mechanics adopted June 23, 2025, also effective July 1, 2025.
**RULE:** Bylaw 17.2 "(Adopted: 6/6/25 effective 7/1/25)"; Bylaw 17.2.1–17.2.3 "(Adopted: 6/23/25 effective 7/1/25)". The DI Board of Directors adopted the implementing changes June 23, 2025.
**SOURCE:** NCAA Manual 2026-27, Bylaw 17.2 (above); "DI Board of Directors formally adopts changes to roster limits," NCAA.org, June 23, 2025, https://www.ncaa.org/news/media-center-di-board-of-directors-formally-adopts-changes-to-roster-limits ("The changes … take effect July 1").
**PARENT MEANING:** These rules are live right now, for the 2026-27 season.

**3. NUMBER:** What it replaced: an old cap of **15 counters (headcount) per year** in women's basketball. The number 15 did not change; the mechanism did.
**RULE:** Pre-settlement *NCAA Division I Manual 2024-25*, **Bylaw 15.5.5.2 Women's Basketball**: "There shall be an annual limit of 15 on the total number of counters in women's basketball at each institution." (Men's, for contrast — Bylaw 15.5.5.1: "There shall be a limit of 13 on the total number of counters in men's basketball at each institution.") The old rule was a *scholarship headcount* limit; the new rule is a *roster* limit, and schools may now give full **or partial** aid to every one of the 15.
**SOURCE:** NCAA, *NCAA Division I Bylaw 15 (2024-25)*, archived at https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2026-27/2024-25D1GMS_Bylaw15.pdf (linked from NCAA Division I Legislation and Policy, https://www.ncaa.org/division-i/governance/legislation-and-policy/). Cross-check of the old-vs-new table by the settlement enforcement body (College Sports Commission): Basketball (W) | Old Scholarship Limit 15 | New Roster Limit 15 | Potential Increase in Scholarships 0, https://www.collegesportscommission.org/roster-limits.
**PARENT MEANING:** Before July 1, 2025 a D1 team could hand out 15 scholarships and no more; now it can have 15 players and choose to aid any or all of them, including with partial scholarships.

**4. NUMBER:** Non-settlement D1 schools have **no national roster limit**; they keep the old scholarship caps.
**RULE:** Bylaw 17.2 applies only to institutions "that is a member of a defendant conference or opts in to the House settlement." NCAA settlement Q&A: "Any Division I institution that is a member of a defendant conference (i.e., the Atlantic Coast Conference, the Big Ten Conference, the Big 12 Conference, the Pac-12 Conference, and the Southeastern Conference) **or is providing revenue share payments or benefits** is a Participating Institution." And: "…new incremental athletically related financial aid above the Division I institutional financial aid limits **in the 2024-25 Division I Manual** will count against the benefits cap." So a school that did not opt in is still governed by the 2024-25 aid limits (15 counters for women's basketball), not by a roster law.
**SOURCE:** NCAA, *Q&A: Implementation of the House Settlement*, updated May 29, 2026, https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/May2026D1Gov_PhaseEightSetQuestionandAnswer.pdf (Questions on Participating Institution and D9); also updated Feb 11, 2026, https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/Feb2026D1Gov_PhaseSevenSetQuestionandAnswer.pdf. NCAA Manual 2026-27, Bylaw 16.13.1.5 "Incremental Athletics Scholarships" (references "the 2024-25 academic year team scholarship limit for a particular sport").
**PARENT MEANING:** Whether a D1 team is on the new 15-spot roster cap depends on that specific school, not on the sport. Ask the school directly which system it operates under.

## 2. ROSTER SUBMISSION DEADLINE AND WHAT HAPPENS AFTER

**5. NUMBER:** Deadline = the **calendar day before the first contest or date of competition used for championship selections, or December 1, whichever is earlier.**
**RULE:** NCAA Bylaw **17.2.1 Roster Submission Deadline** [S]: "An institution must submit its roster for each sport in the designated software system of record no later than the calendar day before the first contest or date of competition in the sport that is used for championship selections or December 1, whichever is earlier. (Adopted: 6/23/25 effective 7/1/25)"
**SOURCE:** NCAA Division I Manual 2026-27, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008.
**PARENT MEANING:** For women's basketball (a winter sport), the trigger is the day before the team's first game, not a fixed calendar date. December 1 is a backstop, not the norm.

**6. NUMBER:** The system of record for rosters is **CAPS**, developed by LBi under College Sports Commission oversight. Certification must be done before the competitive season starts or by December 1, whichever comes first.
**RULE:** NCAA Q&A, Question C5: "**CAPS**, developed by LBi under the oversight of the College Sports Commission, is the system of record for an institution's rosters." College Sports Commission: "All participating institutions are required to submit their rosters through the CAPS platform and formally certify that each team's roster complies with all applicable rules. This certification must be completed prior to the start of the competitive season — or by December 1, whichever comes first."
**SOURCE:** NCAA Q&A, updated May 29, 2026, https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/May2026D1Gov_PhaseEightSetQuestionandAnswer.pdf; College Sports Commission, "Roster Limits," https://www.collegesportscommission.org/roster-limits. Note: CAPS in the NCAA's own compliance system is the Compliance Application Processing System; the Q&A's use of "CAPS" for the roster system of record is the same LBi platform.
**PARENT MEANING:** Once the roster is filed in the NCAA's system, that list is the team's legal roster for the season.

**7. NUMBER:** After the deadline, **only players on the submitted roster may take part in any athletically related activity** (countable, required, or voluntary) for the rest of the academic year or the end of the playing season, whichever is later.
**RULE:** Bylaw **17.2.2 Roster Limit Inclusion** [S]: "Beginning the day after an institution's deadline for roster submission, an individual must be on the sport's submitted roster to participate in athletically related activities (countable, required, or voluntary) for the remainder of academic year or the end of the sport's playing season, whichever is later." Bylaw **17.2.1.1 Roster Changes** [S]: "Once an institution submits its roster, it may add a student-athlete to the roster only if the institution has not reached the roster limit before the addition. A change to a sport's roster following the roster submission date must be identified in the designated software system of record before the sport's next contest." Exceptions: 17.2.2.2 (designated student-athlete carried over from 2025-26) and 17.2.2.3 (medically documented season-ending injury before the submission date).
**SOURCE:** NCAA Division I Manual 2026-27, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008.
**PARENT MEANING:** A spot that is not on the filed roster is not a spot. Once you're on the list, the team is at 15 and no one else can be added.

## 3. DIVISION II

**8. NUMBER:** D2 women's basketball: **10.0 athletic-scholarship equivalencies** per year (same as D2 men's basketball, 10.0).
**RULE:** NCAA Division II Bylaw **15.4.2.1.2 Women's Sports** [♦]: "There shall be a limit on the value (equivalency) of financial aid awards (per Bylaw 15.2.1) that an institution may provide in any academic year to counters in each women's sport, as follows:" — table row "Basketball | 10.0" (table revision noted "Revised: 1/16/26"). Men's table, Bylaw 15.4.2.1.1: "Basketball | 10.0".
**SOURCE:** *NCAA Division II Manual, 2026-27*, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90010 (footer date 11/11/26).
**PARENT MEANING:** A D2 team can give out the equivalent of ten full scholarships' worth of aid for women's basketball.

**9. NUMBER:** D2 has **no NCAA squad-size limit** for basketball; the binding roster document is the **Eligibility List Form**, compiled on the first day of competition.
**RULE:** No squad cap appears in the D2 Manual for women's basketball. Bylaw **14.1.2.2 Eligibility List Form**: "The institution's athletics director shall compile on a form approved by the Management Council a list of the squad members in each sport on the first day of competition and shall indicate thereon the status of each member in the designated categories. A student-athlete's name must be on the official institutional form in order for the student to be eligible to represent the institution in intercollegiate competition." (Searched the full D2 Manual text for "squad": the only basketball-adjacent hits are this eligibility-list rule and multi-sport definitions.)
**SOURCE:** *NCAA Division II Manual, 2026-27*, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90010.
**PARENT MEANING:** A D2 women's basketball roster can be bigger than 10 people — the extra players simply are not on aid.

**10. NUMBER:** How partial aid works in D2: every athlete's aid is converted to a **fraction of a full grant-in-aid**, and the fractions plus full awards must not exceed the sport's 10.0.
**RULE:** Bylaw **15.4.2.2 Equivalency Computations**: "(a) For each counter, the institution shall count all athletics aid (per Bylaw 15.02.2) received during that academic year for food and housing and tuition and fees. (b) A fraction shall be created, with the amount received by the student-athlete as the numerator and the full grant-in-aid value for that student-athlete as the denominator based upon the actual cost or average cost of a full grant for all students at that institution… (c) The sum of all fractional and maximum awards received by counters shall not exceed the total limit for the sport in question for the academic year as a whole." Multisport aid is prorated if not specified on the athletics aid agreement (Bylaw 15.4.3 / 15.4.3.1).
**SOURCE:** *NCAA Division II Manual, 2026-27*, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90010.
**PARENT MEANING:** In D2, "10.0" is a pot of money, not 10 people. A coach can hand out 10 full rides, or 20 half rides, or any mix that adds to 10.0.

**11. NUMBER:** D2 escape hatch: a school may exceed the D2 grant-in-aid limit in a sport "in which only one NCAA championship is conducted," and a school reclassifying a sport to D1 may exceed the D2 limits.
**RULE:** Bylaw **15.4.2.1.3 Exception** [♦]: "In accordance with the provisions of Bylaw 7.9.2.2, an institution may exceed the Division II institutional grant-in-aid limitations in a sport in which only one NCAA championship is conducted. An institution that is reclassifying a sport to Division I may exceed the Division II institutional grant-in-aid limitations."
**SOURCE:** *NCAA Division II Manual, 2026-27*, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90010.
**PARENT MEANING:** A D2 school adding or moving a sport can temporarily offer more than 10.0.

## 4. DIVISION III — no athletic aid

**12. NUMBER:** D3 offers **no athletic aid at all**. There is no equivalent of a D1 or D2 scholarship, and no scholarship number to count.
**RULE:** Bylaw **15.01.3 Criteria for Institutional Financial Aid**: "A member institution shall not award financial aid to any student on the basis of athletics leadership, ability, participation or performance." Bylaw **15.01.7**: "No part of an institution's financial aid budget shall be set aside either for particular sports or for athletics in general, nor may an institution establish athletically related quotas of financial aid recipients." Bylaw **15.01.8 Athletics Fund**: "A member may not establish any fund for student-athletes." Bylaw 15.01.1 permits aid "that does not conflict with the governing legislation of Division III"; Bylaw 15.01.4 requires the aid package to be "consistent with the established policy of the institution's financial aid office… for all students."
**SOURCE:** *NCAA Division III Manual, 2026-27*, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90011. Corroborated by NCAA's own recruiting fact sheet: "Division III… No athletics scholarships. 80% of athletes receive nonathletics aid."
**PARENT MEANING:** A D3 offer is a tryout spot and a financial-aid-office decision, not a scholarship. If a D3 coach says "we offer a scholarship," that is not a D3 thing.

**13. NUMBER:** What a D3 "offer" actually is: **admission + a roster/team spot + need- or merit-based institutional aid**, with no binding athletics document.
**RULE:** Bylaw **13.9.1 Letter-of-Intent Prohibition** [D]: "An institution shall not use any form of letter of intent or similar form of commitment in the recruitment of a prospective student-athlete. However, it shall be permissible for the institution to use in the recruitment of a prospective student-athlete its pre-enrollment forms executed by prospective students in general at that institution." Exception13.9.1.1 allows the NCAA's standard **nonbinding** athletics celebratory form only *after* acceptance for enrollment, and 13.9.1.1.1 forbids signing it on campus or with staff present. There is no national D3 roster cap; the operational roster document is the institutional eligibility list (D3 Bylaw 14.1.2.2, same structure as D2).
**SOURCE:** *NCAA Division III Manual, 2026-27*, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90011.
**PARENT MEANING:** D3's offer is a team roster spot and the school's normal financial aid package. Nothing in athletics can promise you money, and nothing binds you either.

**15. NUMBER:** NAIA has **no limit on the number of student-athletes on a varsity roster** (varsity or JV combined).

**16. NUMBER:** NAIA **does not** enforce national letters of intent and has **no national signing day/date**.
**RULE:** I extracted the full text of the NAIA Handbook 2025-2026 and searched it: the string "Letter of Intent" appears **once**, in an unrelated context ("prior to signing a letter of intent, or prior to participation for those not contracted before"), and there is no national LOI form, no national clearinghouse, and no national signing date anywhere in the Handbook. Aid commitments are institutional (school-to-athlete), not filed with a national association.
**SOURCE:** *NAIA Handbook 2025-2026*, https://www.naia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/2025_Official_Handbook.pdf (Article II, Section D, and full-text searches for "Letter of Intent" / "aid agreement").
**PARENT MEANING:** In the NAIA there is no national signing day, so a verbal offer or a school letter is not the same kind of commitment as an NCAA signing-period letter of intent. Read the actual document.

**24. NUMBER — DERIVED, not an NCAA publication:** At a D1 settlement school in women's basketball, the 15-spot roster cap is **larger than the average roster (14.6)**, so a compliant school can offer aid to **every single player on the roster** — the number of aided players is now capped by roster size, not by a scholarship line.

**27. NUMBER:** **950 international student-athletes on D1 women's basketball rosters in 2024-25** (up from 262 in 2009-10); **130 international players in the 2025 women's tournament = 15% of the field.**
**FIGURE:** NCAA, "March Madness across the world: NCAA basketball's global impact" (March 18, 2025; "This data is pulled from stat files for the 2024-25 season"): "The number of international student-athletes in Division I men's and women's basketball has more than doubled since the 2009-10 season, going from 668 to 1,838 in 2024-25. Europe: 374 D1 women's players (from 112); Africa: 79 (from 15); in the tournament Spain led with 21, Canada 16, Australia 13.
**SOURCE:** https://www.ncaa.org/news/2025/3/18/media-center-march-madness-across-the-world-ncaa-basketballs-global-impact.aspx (read directly). NCAA also maintains ISA participation data: https://www.ncaa.org/what-we-do/research/international-student-athlete-participation.
**PARENT MEANING:** Roughly one in six spots on a top D1 women's basketball roster can be filled by a player from abroad, and that share has been growing for15 years.

**28. NUMBER — DERIVED:** About **18% of all D1 women's basketball players (950 of 5,153) were international in 2024-25.**
**BASIS:** 950 international (NCAA article above) ÷ 5,153 D1 women's basketball participants (NCAA 2025RES participation report) = 18.4%. This is my arithmetic across two NCAA sources, not an NCAA-published percentage.
**PARENT MEANING:** For a U.S. high school recruit, roughly one in six D1 women's basketball roster spots is effectively pre-filled by the international market.
