## 1. NCAA Division I men's soccer: contact and communication

Men's soccer has no sport-specific exception in the three timing bylaws below. The exception headings read in full were: 13.1.1.1.1-.11 (baseball, softball, men's basketball, women's bowling, women's basketball, football, women's lacrosse, men's lacrosse, cross country/track, women's ice hockey, men's wrestling); 13.1.3.1.1-.9 (baseball, women's lacrosse, men's lacrosse, softball, women's basketball, men's basketball, men's ice hockey, men's wrestling, women's ice hockey); 13.4.1.1-.9 (same family of sports). Soccer therefore sits under each general rule.

- [OFFICIAL] Phone calls: "Telephone calls to an individual (or the individual's family members) may not be made before June 15 at the conclusion of the individual's sophomore year in high school ... Thereafter, an institution may make telephone calls to the prospective student-athlete at its discretion." Bylaw 13.1.3.1. Source: D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Email, texts and other electronic messages, plus mailed recruiting materials: an institution "shall not provide recruiting materials, including general correspondence related to athletics, or send electronic correspondence to an individual (or the individual's family members) until June 15 at the conclusion of the individual's sophomore year in high school." Bylaw 13.4.1. Source: D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [UNCONFIRMED] Whether "electronic correspondence" in Bylaw 13.4.1 names direct messages on social media word for word. Looked for: the phrase "direct message" in the D1 MANUAL (zero hits). The CBSA recruiting table uses the row labels "Calls / Texts / Emails / Messages via Social Media", but that table was not transcribed. Source: D1 MANUAL; CBSA. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Headings exist for narrower exceptions that were not read in full: 13.1.3.1.10 (calls to a prospect, heading truncated), 13.1.3.1.13 (calls regarding an institutional camp), 13.4.1.10-.12 (electronic correspondence exceptions, including one tied to institutional camps), 13.4.1.13 ("Responding to Prospective Student-Athlete's Request"). Source: D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] In-person, off-campus contact: "Off-campus recruiting contacts shall not be made with an individual (or the individual's family members) before August 1 at the beginning of the individual's junior year in high school." Bylaw 13.1.1.1. Source: D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] How many times: in sports other than baseball, basketball, football, women's ice hockey, lacrosse, softball and men's wrestling, "each institution is limited to seven recruiting opportunities (contacts and evaluations combined) per prospective student-athlete per year ... Beginning August 1 at the start of a prospective student-athlete's junior year of high school, not more than three of the seven opportunities per year may be off-campus contacts". Contacts during an official visit or an unofficial visit under 13.7.5 are not counted. Bylaw 13.1.5.1. Source: D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] For a class-of-2029 player both D1 triggers fall in the summer between sophomore and junior year: June 15 at the end of sophomore year (calls, email, recruiting materials) and the August 1 that starts junior year (off-campus contact, official visits, athletics-involved unofficial visits). The NCAA prints the rule, not the calendar year, so no year is printed here. Source: Bylaws 13.1.3.1, 13.4.1, 13.1.1.1, 13.6.2.1.1, 13.7.1.1, D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.

### 1b. The "no contact during his high school season" claim

- [UNCONFIRMED] No NCAA bylaw was found that stops a college coach from contacting a soccer player for the length of the player's own high school season. Looked for: every heading under Bylaw 13.1 in the D1, D2 and D3 manuals. What exists is a day-of-competition rule in each division (next three lines). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] D1: "In sports other than basketball, in-person contact may not be made with a prospective student-athlete at any site prior to any athletics competition (including a noninstitutional, private camp or clinic, but not an institutional camp or clinic) in which the prospective student-athlete is a participant on a day of competition, even if the prospective student-athlete is on an official or unofficial visit. In-person contact may occur after the prospective student-athlete's competition concludes for the day and the prospective student-athlete has been released by the appropriate authority (e.g., coach)." Bylaw 13.1.6.2.1. Source: D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] D2: same idea, and "Contact includes the passing of notes or orally relaying information to a prospective student-athlete by a third party on behalf of an institutional staff member." Bylaw 13.1.4.1. Source: D2 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] D3: no recruiting contact before competition that day until the player is released by the coach; in addition "any electronic communications ... may not be made with a prospective student-athlete at the site of organized competition involving the prospective student-athlete by athletics personnel who are attending the competition or who are aware of the competition." Bylaw 13.1.3.1. Source: D3 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Whether a boy may play for his club during the high school season is a high school rule, not an NCAA rule: your state association. No state dates are given here by house rule.

## 2. NCAA Division II men's soccer: contact and communication

- [OFFICIAL] In-person contact: "Contact shall not be made with a prospective student-athlete or a prospective student-athlete's relatives or legal guardians before June 15 immediately preceding the prospective student-athlete's junior year in high school." Bylaw 13.1.1.1. Source: D2 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Calls, email, written and electronic correspondence: "Communication with a prospective student-athlete or their relative or legal guardian(s) shall be made at any time." Bylaw 13.1.3.1 (Revised 7/23/24 effective 8/1/24). "Communication" is defined as "All electronically transmitted human voice exchange (including videoconferencing and videophones), electronically transmitted correspondence (e.g., electronic mail, facsimiles), recruiting materials and written correspondence". Bylaw 13.02.2. Source: D2 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] D2 has no sport calendar section for soccer in the manual (no "Recruiting Calendars" heading was found in D2 Article 13). The only dead period that applies to soccer is the signing-date one: "The period 48 hours before 7 a.m. on the date for signing in the applicable sport." Bylaws 13.02.10, 13.02.10.1.1, 13.02.10.1.2 (Revised 1/16/26 effective 8/1/26). Source: D2 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] D2 tryouts are allowed: a college "may conduct a tryout of a prospective student-athlete only on its campus or at a site at which it normally conducts practice or competition beginning June 15 immediately preceding the prospective student-athlete's junior year in high school"; "Not more than one tryout per prospective student-athlete per institution per sport", counted separately for high school and after high school; one more after a new head coach is hired. Bylaw 13.8.2.1. Source: D2 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] D1 by contrast: a college "shall not conduct ... any physical activity (e.g., practice session or test/tryout) at which one or more prospective student-athletes ... reveal, demonstrate or display their athletics abilities" except as 13.11.2 and 13.11.3 allow. Bylaw 13.11.1. Source: D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.

## 3. NCAA Division III men's soccer: contact and communication

- [OFFICIAL] In-person, off-campus contact: not "until the prospective student-athlete has completed their sophomore year in high school." Bylaw 13.1.1.1. Source: D3 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Calls, texts, email: "There are no restrictions on the timing for electronic communication (e.g., telephone call, electronic mail, Instant Messenger, text messages or facsimiles) to prospective student-athletes." Bylaw 13.02.10.1 (Adopted 8/4/21). Source: D3 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Recruiting materials: "There are no restrictions on the timing for providing recruiting materials". Bylaw 13.4.1.1. Evaluations: "There are no restrictions on the number or timing of evaluations". Bylaw 13.1.4. Source: D3 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Tryouts: "It is impermissible for an athletics department coaching staff member to observe or conduct physical workouts or other recreational activities designed to test the athletics abilities of a prospective student-athlete." Bylaw 13.11.2.1. Camps, clinics and competition-only events are handled under 13.11.3. Source: D3 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] D3 has no recruiting calendar section in its Article 13 table of contents (13.1 Contacts and Evaluations through 13.15). Source: D3 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.

## 4. NAIA and NJCAA: contact, tryouts, national rule and where a conference can differ

### NAIA
- [OFFICIAL] "The NAIA has no recruiting calendar. Students and coaches can communicate year-round. There is also no limit to the number of communications or how the communication takes place." Source: https://www.naia.org/why-naia/recruiting . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] The one national contact restriction is about students already at an NAIA school: once enrolled or practicing at an NAIA school, another NAIA school "cannot initiate contact"; if the student contacts the coach, the coach may respond but must notify the current school (in writing within 10 days per the interpretations page). Source: NAIA Guide for the College-Bound Student-Athlete (May 2026) https://www.naia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/naia_guide_college_bound_student-1.pdf ; https://interpretations.naia.org/basics-of-recruiting-for-naia-coaches (search snippet). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] TWO NAIA PAGES DISAGREE on tryouts. (a) "Students are permitted two days of try-outs at each NAIA member school. The two days do not have to be consecutive" - https://www.naia.org/why-naia/recruiting (no date shown). (b) "You have a maximum of two days of tryouts throughout your entire athletic career" - https://www.naia.org/student-athletes/prospective/high-school-students (page dated 2026-05-11) and the May 2026 guide PDF above. Both printed as found. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] On a tryout visit the school may provide lodging and meals only "if the same would be provided to non-athletes trying out for other programs at the school" / "If a school has a policy for reimbursing all students for such visits, the same can be applied to you". Source: the two naia.org pages above. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Conference difference: the NAIA has no national letter of intent, and some NAIA conferences require their members to honor each other's letters. Carried from the house volleyball packet (same check date): https://www.naia.org/student-athletes/prospective/parents ; https://interpretations.naia.org/implications-of-letters-of-intent . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] NAIA recruiting and tryout rules, with article and section numbers, are in the NAIA Official Handbook 2026-2027, 41st edition. The 2025-2026 copy read earlier says the same thing on the two tryout days. Source: https://www.naia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026-07-2026_Official_Handbook.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.

## 5. Official and unofficial visits

### Division I
- [OFFICIAL] Earliest paid visit: in sports other than baseball, basketball, football, women's ice hockey, lacrosse, softball and men's wrestling, "an individual may not be provided an expense-paid visit earlier than August 1 of the individual's junior year in high school." Bylaw 13.6.2.1.1 (last revised 10/7/26). Source: D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Per-school limit: "An institution may finance one visit to its campus for a prospective student-athlete before October 15 following completion of high school and one visit beginning October 15 following completion of high school". One extra visit is allowed after a new head coach is hired. Bylaws 13.6.2.2, 13.6.2.2.1. Source: D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Per-athlete total: no heading in Bylaw 13.6.2 (13.6.2.1 through 13.6.2.5) sets a total number of schools a prospect may visit; the only numeric limit read is per institution. Source: D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Length: "An institution shall not provide more than two consecutive nights of lodging to a prospective student-athlete in conjunction with an official visit." Bylaw 13.6.4. Source: D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Who pays what: actual transportation costs for the prospect (13.5.2.1; no limousine or helicopter); lodging and meals for the prospect "and up to four family members" within a 30-mile radius (13.6.6); "actual meals, not to exceed three per day" for the prospect and up to four family members (13.6.7.7); entertainment "up to $60 per person" (13.6.7.4, heading text); "a maximum of five complimentary admissions to a home athletics event" (13.6.7.2). Source: D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Before a D1 official visit the prospect must (a) give the school a transcript, (b) "register with the NCAA Eligibility Center" and (c) be placed on the school's institutional request list. Bylaw 13.6.3. Source: D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Unofficial visits: Bylaw 13.7.2 "Number Permitted" begins "A prospective student-athlete may visit a member institution's campus at the prospect..." (extract cut there; the only listed exceptions are men's and women's basketball, 13.7.2.1 and 13.7.2.2). The D2 and D3 versions read "at their own expense an unlimited number of times". But "an unofficial visit with athletics department involvement (e.g., contact with athletics department staff, athletics-specific tour, complimentary admission) shall not occur ... before August 1 at the beginning of the individual's junior year in high school." Bylaw 13.7.1.1. Source: D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] On an unofficial visit the school "may not pay any expenses or provide any entertainment except a maximum of three complimentary admissions (issued through digital ticketing or a pass list) to a home athletics event". Bylaw 13.7.3.1. Source: D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] No visits of either kind during a dead period (definition on the D1 CALENDAR; 2026-27 men's soccer dead periods Nov 9-12 and Dec 11-14). Source: D1 CALENDAR. Checked 2026-11-11.

## 7. Amateurism rules that bite in soccer

### Division I (Article 12 was rewritten: most lines carry "Revised: 6/6/25 effective 7/1/25")
- [OFFICIAL] What a professional team is: "any organized team that: (a) Provides any of its players more than actual and necessary expenses for participation on the team, except as otherwise permitted by NCAA legislation; or (b) Declares itself to be professional." Bylaw 12.02.7. Source: D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Trials before college: "Before initial full-time collegiate enrollment, an individual may participate in a tryout with a professional team or league, provided not more than actual and necessary expenses are received to participate." Bylaw 12.2.1.1 (Revised 11/7/24 effective 8/1/25, 6/6/25 effective 7/1/25). No hour limit appears in this sentence. Source: D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Trials after enrolling: allowed "at any time, provided the individual does not miss class"; expenses may be received "in conjunction with one 48-hour tryout per professional team"; a longer tryout must be self-financed. Bylaw 12.2.1.2. Source: D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Playing on a professional team before college: "Before initial full-time collegiate enrollment, an individual may compete on a professional team (per Bylaw 12.02.4), provided the individual does not receive more than actual and necessary expenses to participate on the team." Bylaw 12.2.2.2.1. After enrollment the general rule applies: "An individual shall not compete on a professional team". Bylaw 12.2.2.2. Source: D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Contracts: "An individual shall not enter into any kind of agreement to compete in professional athletics, either orally or in writing". Exception before enrollment: an agreement to compete on a professional team is allowed "provided the agreement does not guarantee or promise payment (at any time) in excess of actual and necessary expenses". Bylaws 12.2.4, 12.2.4.1. Source: D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Money from a professional club or academy before college: "an individual may accept up to actual and necessary expenses for competition and practice held in preparation for such competition from a professional sports organization that sponsors the event." Bylaw 12.1.2.2.3 (Revised 4/15/26 for those enrolling on or after 8/1/26). Prize money based on place finish from the event sponsor is allowed before enrollment. Bylaw 12.1.2.4.1. Source: D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Draft: in sports other than basketball and football "an individual may enter a professional league's draft one time before initial full-time collegiate enrollment and one time after initial full-time collegiate enrollment, provided the individual is not drafted"; after an in-college entry he must declare within 72 hours that he will return. Bylaw 12.2.3.2.6. Source: D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Soccer-only exception: "In men's soccer, an individual may receive actual and necessary expenses from Major League Soccer to attend the MLS Showcase regardless of the duration of the event and without such activity being considered a tryout with a professional team." Bylaw 12.2.1.2.5 (Adopted 6/26/24 effective 8/1/24). Source: D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] The clock: Bylaw 12.6 is now a five-year period tied to age and first enrollment (12.6.1 "Timing of 19th Birthday", Adopted 6/24/26 effective 8/1/26; 12.6.2 Adopted 9/9/26).
