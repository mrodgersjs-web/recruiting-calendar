Source: D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.

## 8. Where the domestic and international pathways meet

### FIFA status and transfer rules
- [OFFICIAL] Edition: "These regulations were approved by the Bureau of the Council on 10 June 2026 and come into force on 1 January 2027." (Article 29.) The edition in force on the check date is the prior one; the wording quoted below for Articles 19 and 20 matches the January 2025 and July 2025 editions seen in search results. Source: RSTP. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Status: "Players participating in organised football are either amateurs or professionals. No other status shall be recognised. A professional is a player who has a written contract with a club and is paid more for his footballing activity than the expenses he effectively incurs. All other players are considered to be amateurs." Article 2. Source: RSTP. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Going back: "A player registered as a professional may not re-register as an amateur until at least 30 days after his last match as a professional." Article 3. Source: RSTP. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Under 18: "International transfers of players are only permitted if the player is over the age of 18." Five exceptions follow; the first three read: (a) "The player's parents move to the country in which the new club is located for reasons not linked to football"; (b) the player is 16 to 18 and the transfer is inside the EU/EEA or between two associations in the same country, with the new club obliged to provide football education, guarantee "an academic and/or school and/or vocational education ... which will allow the player to pursue a career other than football", and arrange living standards; (c) the player lives within 50km of a border and the club is within 50km of it. Article 19. Source: RSTP. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Trials have their own article (19ter "Trials", with a FIFA Trial Form and sanctions for failing to upload it). The operative paragraphs (length of a trial, age limits) were not extracted. Source: RSTP table of contents and Article 19ter paragraph 11. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Training compensation: "shall be paid to a player's training club(s): (1) when a player is registered for the first time as a professional, and (2) each time a professional is transferred until the end of the calendar year of his 23rd birthday." "The principles of training compensation shall not apply to women's football." Article 20. Source: RSTP. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] "A player's training and education takes place between the ages of 12 and 23. Training compensation shall be payable, as a general rule, up to the age of 23 for training incurred up to the age of 21". Not due if the former club ends the contract without just cause, if the player moves to a category 4 club, or if a professional reacquires amateur status on transfer. Annexe 4, articles 1 and 2. Source: RSTP. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Solidarity mechanism: "If a professional is transferred before the expiry of his contract, any club that has contributed to his education ..." (extract cut there; the percentages in Annexe 5 were not read). Article 21. Source: RSTP. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] FIFA's own help page: training compensation is due "when a player is registered for the first time as a professional; and each time a professional player is transferred internationally until the end of the calendar year of their 23rd birthday." Source: https://support.fifatms.com/en/support/solutions/articles/7000097496-training-compensation (dated 2026-10-09, search snippet). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [UNCONFIRMED] How this lands on a US family in practice: whether a US youth club or college can claim training compensation or solidarity for a player who signs abroad, and the U.S. Soccer policy on it. Looked for: nothing on ussoccer.com or mlsplayers.org was opened in this pass. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Where the two rule books touch: FIFA calls a player professional when he has a written contract and is paid more than his expenses (RSTP Article 2). NCAA D1 uses the same line: a professional team "provides any of its players more than actual and necessary expenses" (Bylaw 12.02.7), and before enrollment a player may sign with and play for such a team only if he gets no more than actual and necessary expenses (Bylaws 12.2.2.2.1, 12.2.4.1). Sources: RSTP; D1 MANUAL. Checked 2026-11-11.
