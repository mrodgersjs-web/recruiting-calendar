## 0. How to read this file (plain words)

- [OFFICIAL] = I read it in a rule book, calendar or page published by the NCAA, NAIA, NJCAA, NFHS or the College Sports Commission.
- [SERVICE] = a recruiting company or event page said it. Useful, but not a rule.
- [DERIVED] = arithmetic or a plain reading I did from an official text. The source text is named so it can be checked.
- [UNCONFIRMED] = I looked and could not verify it. What I looked for is written next to it.
- "Junior year" in softball rules means the school year that starts in the fall of grade 11. For the class of 2029 that is the 2027-28 school year. The 2027-28 calendars are not published yet, so every calendar date below is from the 2026-27 documents.

## 1. NCAA Division I softball: calls, texts, email, in-person contact

Softball has its own exception in almost every Division I recruiting bylaw. All of the softball exceptions below carry the note "Adopted: 6/24/26".

- [OFFICIAL] Coach calls OUT: "In softball, telephone calls to an individual (or the individual's family members) may not be made before 1 p.m. Central time September 1 at the beginning of the individual's junior year in high school." After that, a school "may make telephone calls to the prospective student-athlete at its discretion." Source: D1M Bylaw 13.1.3.1.4, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008 . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Calls IN from the family are also blocked: "an institutional staff member may not receive telephone calls from an individual (or the individual's family members) before 1 p.m. Central time September 1 at the beginning of the individual's junior year in high school." So a ninth or tenth grader phoning a Division I softball coach cannot have the call taken. Source: D1M Bylaw 13.1.3.2.8, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008 . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Email, letters and other written or electronic messages from the school: a school "shall not provide recruiting materials, including general correspondence related to athletics, or send electronic correspondence" to the player or family "until 1 p.m. Central time September 1 at the beginning of the individual's junior year in high school." Source: D1M Bylaw 13.4.1.4, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008 . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [UNCONFIRMED] Texts and direct messages (DMs): the bylaw's term is "electronic correspondence". I did not find and read the Division I definition list for that term, so "texts and social DMs are covered by 13.4.1.4" is my reading, not a quote. Looked for: a Division I definition of electronic correspondence (the Division III manual lists text messages and private social media messages in its own definition, Bylaw 13.02.10). Source: D1M, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008 ; D3M, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90011 . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] In-person contact away from campus: "In softball, off-campus recruiting contacts shall not be made with an individual (or the individual's family members) before 1 p.m. Central time September 1 at the beginning of the individual's junior year in high school." Source: D1M Bylaw 13.1.1.1.2, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008 . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] How many times: each school gets seven "recruiting opportunities (contacts and evaluations combined)" per player per year. From 1 p.m. Central September 1 of junior year, "not more than three of the seven opportunities may be off-campus contacts each year", family contacts included. A contact made during an official visit or an unofficial visit "does not count". Source: D1M Bylaw 13.1.5.9, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008 . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Counting detail: evaluations from June 1 through August 31 do not count against the seven; evaluations September 1 through May 31 do (unless on the same day as a contact). Any number of contacts in one day counts as one. Source: D1M Bylaws 13.1.5.11 and 13.1.5.12, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008 . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Camps: at a college softball camp or clinic, "recruiting conversations ... are not permitted between an institution's coach or off-campus recruiter and a participating individual before 1 p.m. Central time September 1 at the beginning of the individual's junior year in high school." This applies even to a camp for kids who are not yet prospects. Source: D1M Bylaw 13.12.1.5.4, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008 . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Where college softball staff may work camps: at a private (non-college) camp only "when evaluation at nonscholastic practice or competition activities is permissible"; at other times only at college camps on a college campus or regular practice or game facility. Source: D1M Bylaws 13.12.1.2.4 and 13.12.2.2.8 (the calendar PDF cites the same rule as "13.12.2.3.6"; the numbering differs between the two documents), https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008 and https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/compliance/recruiting/calendar/2026-27/2026-27D1Rec_WSBRecruitingCalendar.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Who may recruit off campus: softball schools must designate "off-campus recruiters", and "only four off-campus recruiters may engage in off-campus recruiting activities during any single day." Source: D1M Bylaws 11.7.3.3 and 11.7.6.3 (Adopted 6/24/26, Revised 10/7/26), https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008 . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] For contrast, the general Division I rule for sports with no exception is "June 15 at the conclusion of the individual's sophomore year" for recruiting materials and electronic correspondence, and "August 1 at the beginning of the individual's junior year" for off-campus contact. Softball is later than both. Source: D1M Bylaws 13.4.1 and 13.1.1.1, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008 . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] After she signs: once a prospect signs a written offer of athletics aid, other schools that give athletics aid may not have recruiting communication with her. Source: NCAA news release 9 Oct 2024, https://www.ncaa.org/news/media-center-di-council-approves-changes-to-notification-of-transfer-windows-in-basketball-football . Checked 2026-11-11.

## 2. NCAA Division I softball: the 2026-27 recruiting calendar (dates as printed)

The Division I Manual no longer prints sport calendars; Bylaw 13.17 (Revised 10/7/26) sends readers to ncaa.org. The softball calendar below is the NCAA PDF "Division I Softball, 2026-27 NCAA Recruiting Calendar, Updated: July 30, 2026". I read the dates from the rendered page image.

- [OFFICIAL] Contact period: AUG 1-9 (2026). Source: WSB-CAL, https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/compliance/recruiting/calendar/2026-27/2026-27D1Rec_WSBRecruitingCalendar.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Evaluation period, "for scholastic practice and competition activities only": AUG 10 - NOV 22. Inside it: Dead period AUG 28 - SEP 3; Evaluation period "for scholastic and nonscholastic practice or competition activities" on OCT 17-18, 24-25, OCT 31 - NOV 1, NOV 7-8, 14-15, 21-22; Dead period NOV 9-12. Source: WSB-CAL (same URL). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] The same calendar block, written out day by day instead of in a compressed list: contact 1-9 August; dead period 28 August - 3 September; evaluation dates for scholastic and nonscholastic practice or competition 17-18 October, 24-25 October, 31 October - 1 November, 7-8 November, 14-15 November and 21-22 November 2026. Restated in full by the W28 editor from the block above, which compresses the list to "OCT 17-18, 24-25, OCT 31 - NOV 1, NOV 7-8, 14-15, 21-22". Source: WSB-CAL (same URL). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Quiet period: NOV 23 - JAN 2, 2027. Inside it: Recruiting shutdown NOV 25-29; Dead period DEC 9-12; Recruiting shutdown DEC 22-26; Recruiting shutdown DEC 31 - JAN 2, 2027. Source: WSB-CAL (same URL). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Evaluation period, "for scholastic practice and competition activities only": JAN 3 - MAY 31. Source: WSB-CAL (same URL). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Dead period: JUN 1-11*. Contact period: JUN 12* - JUL 31. The asterisk: "Dates are based on the Women's College World Series. If the WCWS dates are delayed, the dead period continues through the day of the final championship game. If the WCWS ends after two games, the dead period concludes on the day of the final game and the contact period begins the next day." Source: WSB-CAL (same URL). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Extra evaluation period: "During high school regional and state championship competition that does not occur during a dead period." Source: WSB-CAL (same URL). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] What the words mean (as printed on the calendar). Contact period: coaches may make in-person, off-campus contacts and evaluations. Evaluation period: coaches may watch off campus, but "No in-person, off-campus recruiting contacts shall be made". Quiet period: in-person contact only on the college's campus. Dead period: no in-person contact or evaluation on or off campus and no official or unofficial visits. Recruiting shutdown: "no form of recruiting (e.g., contacts, evaluations, official or unofficial visits, correspondence or making or receiving telephone calls) is permissible." Source: WSB-CAL (same URL). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [DERIVED] Two things follow from the printed 2026-27 calendar. (a) September 1 sits inside the AUG 28 - SEP 3 dead period, so on the day calls and email open, campus visits and in-person contact are still closed. (b) The calendar marks travel-ball (nonscholastic) evaluation only on the six fall weekends listed; the two contact periods (AUG 1-9 and JUN 12 - JUL 31) allow contacts and evaluations; the rest of the school year is high-school-only evaluation. (c) In an evaluation period a coach may not make in-person, off-campus contact, so in 2026-27 off-campus face-to-face contact is limited to the two contact periods. Source: my reading of WSB-CAL (same URL). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [UNCONFIRMED] The 2027-28 and 2028-29 softball calendars (the class of 2029's junior and senior years) are not published. Looked for: any softball calendar past 2026-27 on https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/recruiting/ (only 2026-27 files are linked). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Division I transfer windows for softball, 2026-27 (Figure 13-1): fall window for all spring sports December 1, 2026 to December 15, 2026; softball spring window June 14, 2027 to June 28, 2027. Rule text: December 1-15, or "A 15 consecutive-day period beginning the Monday immediately following the conclusion of the Division I Softball Championship game." Source: D1M Figure 13-1 and the notification-of-transfer bylaw, item (k) Softball, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008 . Checked 2026-11-11.

## 3. NCAA Division II softball: contact, communication, calendar

Division II has no softball-specific recruiting bylaws; softball follows the all-sports rules.

- [OFFICIAL] Calls, texts, email, written mail: "Communication with a prospective student-athlete or their relative or legal guardian(s) shall be made at any time." (Revised 7/23/24 effective 8/1/24.) "Communication" is defined to include voice calls, video calls, electronic mail, recruiting materials and written correspondence. Source: D2M Bylaws 13.1.3.1 and 13.02.2, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90010 . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] The NCAA's own Division II guide says the same thing in plain words: "Athletically related recruiting materials may be sent at anytime" (freshman/sophomore row) and "Communication (e.g., email, text messaging, instant messages, telephone calls)". Source: D2-GUIDE, https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/compliance/recruiting/calendar/2026-27/2026-27D2REC_RecGuide.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] In-person contact: "Contact shall not be made with a prospective student-athlete or a prospective student-athlete's relatives or legal guardians before June 15 immediately preceding the prospective student-athlete's junior year in high school." After that date the guide lists "no limits" on in-person, off-campus contacts. Source: D2M Bylaw 13.1.1.1; D2-GUIDE (URLs above). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Boosters may not communicate with a prospect before June 15 immediately preceding junior year. Source: D2M Bylaw 13.1.3.1.1, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90010 . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] At a game or practice site, contact waits until the player's competition is over for the day and she has been released by the appropriate authority. Source: D2-GUIDE (URL above). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Division II 2026-27 calendar, "ALL OTHER SPORTS" (softball's group): Signing Date Dead Period "NOV 9 @ 7 AM - NOV 11 @ 7 AM", "For all other high school prospective student-athletes." The other dead periods on that page (DEC 22 - JAN 1, 2027; FEB 15-17; MAY 29-31; and the football signing dead periods) sit under the heading "DII FOOTBALL" in the side panel, and the manual lists them as football dead periods. The month grid colors those football dates as well, so the grid alone can mislead; the remaining days are colored as contact period. Source: D2-CAL, https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/compliance/recruiting/calendar/2026-27/2026-27D2Rec_RecruitingCalendar_AllSports.pdf ; D2M Bylaw 13.02.10.2 "Football Dead Period". Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] During a Division II dead period a coach may still communicate with prospects; what stops is in-person contact, evaluation and campus visits. Source: D2M Bylaw 13.02.10, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90010 . Checked 2026-11-11.

## 4. NCAA Division III softball: contact, communication, calendar

- [OFFICIAL] Calls, texts, email, private social messages: "There are no restrictions on the timing for electronic communication (e.g., telephone call, electronic mail, Instant Messenger, text messages or facsimiles) to prospective student-athletes." The definition also names "private communication through social media platforms", and requires the message to be private between sender and recipient. Source: D3M Bylaws 13.02.10 and 13.02.10.1, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90011 . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Printed recruiting materials: "There are no restrictions on the timing for providing recruiting materials to prospective student-athletes." Source: D3M Bylaw 13.4.1.1 (same URL). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] In-person contact away from campus: not "until the prospective student-athlete has completed their sophomore year in high school." Source: D3M Bylaw 13.1.1.1 (same URL). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Evaluations: "There are no restrictions on the number or timing of evaluations". Source: D3M Bylaw 13.1.4 (same URL). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] No Division III recruiting calendar: the NCAA calendars page links Division I and Division II files only, and the words "dead period" do not appear in the Division III Manual text I searched. Source: https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/recruiting/ ; D3M (URL above). Checked 2026-11-11.

## 5. Amateur status and professional teams (NCAA, NAIA, NJCAA, high school)

- [OFFICIAL] NCAA Division I, before college: a prospect "may participate in a tryout with a professional team or league, provided not more than actual and necessary expenses are received" (12.2.1.1); may compete on a professional team before first full-time college enrollment if she "does not receive more than actual and necessary expenses" (12.2.2.2.1); and may sign an agreement to play for a professional team before enrollment if it "does not guarantee or promise payment (at any time) in excess of actual and necessary expenses" (12.2.4.1). Source: D1M Bylaws 12.2.1.1, 12.2.2.2.1, 12.2.4.1, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008 . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] NCAA Division I, general rule: "An individual shall not enter into any kind of agreement to compete in professional athletics, either orally or in writing, regardless of the legal enforceability of that agreement." A "professional team" is one that pays any player more than actual and necessary expenses, or "Declares itself to be professional." Source: D1M Bylaws 12.2.4 and 12.02.7 (same URL). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] NCAA Division I draft rule: "An individual shall not request to be placed on the draft list or supplemental draft list of a professional league." (Exceptions are written for men's basketball.) Source: D1M Bylaw 12.2.3.2 (same URL). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] NAIA is stricter on paper: "Signing a contract with any professional team" and "Entering into an agreement of any kind to compete in professional athletics with a professional sports organization" each cause loss of amateur standing in that sport. The casebook example rules that signing with a team that calls itself "semi-professional" counts even if no player is paid above expenses. Source: NAIA-HB Bylaws Article VII, Section D, https://www.naia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026_Official_Handbook.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] NJCAA: "Only amateur athletes are permitted to participate"; a professional team is one that gives players more than actual and necessary expenses "or Declares itself to be professional"; a player or her representative "may negotiate directly with a professional sports organization provided no agent is involved, and no contract is agreed upon, either orally or in writing." Source: NJCAA-BL Article V, Section 4 (A.5, B.1, F.3.a), https://media.ardorsportshub.com/files/404933495157163011/6b815aea7cf842719e5deeefd7dc9310-NJCAA-Handbook---Bylaws---2026-27-9-9-26.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] High school (NFHS): the only national rule about signing with a professional team that I found in the NFHS Handbook is the "Agreement with Professional Baseball" (in effect since 1944), which quotes Major-Minor League Rule 3(h): "No student of a high school shall be signed to a contract by a Major or Minor League club during the period the student is eligible for participation in high school athletics." It is a baseball agreement. The handbook prints no softball equivalent. Source: NFHS-HB pp. 24-25, https://assets.nfhs.org/umbraco/media/7213201/2024-25-nfhs-handbook_final.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] NFHS states the reason for amateur rules but leaves the rule itself to its members: "An amateur/awards limitation promotes amateurism; stimulates participation for the sake of the game itself; prevents exploitation of students". Voting members are the state high school associations, which write eligibility rules. For softball, the rule on a player who signs with a professional team is therefore your state association's amateur rule. Source: NFHS-HB "Rationale for Eligibility Rules" p. 23 and Article 2.1 (same URL). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [UNCONFIRMED] A 2025-26 or 2026-27 NFHS Handbook: not found; the 2024-25 edition is the newest I could open. Looked for: newer handbook PDF on nfhs.org via search. Checked 2026-11-11.

## 6. NAIA softball: contact, visits, tryouts

- [OFFICIAL] "The NAIA has no recruiting calendar. Students and coaches can communicate year-round. There is also no limit to the number of communications or how the communication takes place." Source: https://www.naia.org/why-naia/recruiting (read through the search tool's page excerpt; on 2026-11-11 the older /why-naia/ address redirected to https://www.naia.org/explore/ in the browser). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] The words "dead period", "quiet period" and "contact period" do not appear anywhere in the 2026-27 NAIA handbook text I searched. The recruiting section (Article II, Section D) regulates contact with athletes already enrolled at another NAIA school (written notice to that school within 10 days of first contact), bars contacting an athlete who "has drawn equipment and/ or is engaged in organized pre-school practice at an NAIA institution", and covers graduate students. Source: NAIA-HB Bylaws Article II, Section D, https://www.naia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026_Official_Handbook.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Tryouts are allowed (the NCAA Division I has no equivalent for softball prospects): "Tryouts, where permitted, shall be limited to no more than two days for a specific student per member institution." Source: NAIA-HB Bylaws Article II, Section C, Item 2 (same URL). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Two official NAIA pages word the tryout limit differently. Handbook 2026-27: two days "per member institution". NAIA parent and high school pages (page dates 2026-05-11): "a maximum of two days of tryouts throughout your entire athletic career." NAIA recruiting page: "two days of try-outs at each NAIA member school". Both versions are printed here because they conflict. Source: NAIA-HB (URL above); https://www.naia.org/student-athletes/prospective/high-school-students/ ; https://www.naia.org/why-naia/recruiting (page excerpts via search). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Coaches may watch high school or junior college games and practices; it stays "observation" as long as the coach does not ask the player to show a skill or direct the workout. Source: NAIA-HB Bylaws Article II, Section C casebook "Coach Observation" (URL above). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Who pays for a visit: "No part of the travel expense, meals, and lodging of prospective students making visitations to an institution shall be paid by the institution unless such practice is a part of the general institutional policy in procurement of other students with special talents". There is no NAIA dollar limit and no NAIA limit on the number of visits; the school must treat athletes like other special-talent recruits. Source: NAIA-HB Bylaws Article II, Section C, Item 3 and casebook "Expenses Paid by Institution" (URL above). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Junior college players: "Effective April 7, 2025" an NAIA coach may initiate contact with a junior college student at any time. Source: NAIA-HB note to Article II, Section D, Item 1b (URL above). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [UNCONFIRMED] Conference differences in the NAIA: the handbook leaves visit spending to each school's policy and I found no softball-specific conference recruiting rule in it. Whether a given NAIA conference adds its own letter-of-intent or recruiting rule was not checked conference by conference. Looked for: conference recruiting rules inside NAIA-HB. Checked 2026-11-11.

## 7. NJCAA (junior college) softball: contact, visits, letters

- [OFFICIAL] In-person recruiting away from campus: "Athletic department staff may visit a prospective student-athlete at any location for recruiting purposes." Staff may spend money only on their own recruiting expenses, not on the prospect or family. Source: NJCAA-BL Article VI, Section 4.C, https://media.ardorsportshub.com/files/404933495157163011/6b815aea7cf842719e5deeefd7dc9310-NJCAA-Handbook---Bylaws---2026-27-9-9-26.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Calls, texts, email: the NJCAA recruitment section sets no start date, calendar or count limit for communicating with high school prospects (the words "dead period", "quiet period" and "contact period" do not appear in the 2026-27 NJCAA bylaws text I searched). What it bans is contact with a player who "has signed an NJCAA Letter of Intent" with another member college or who is in season at another NJCAA college. Source: NJCAA-BL Article VI, Section 4.D and Section 3.A.8 (same URL). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Official (paid) visit: allowed "starting on June 1 prior to their junior year in high school"; a college "may pay for one official visit for a prospective student-athlete per recruiting year"; "may not exceed 48 hours"; limited to campus and the local community; costs may be paid for the prospect "plus two guests"; transportation, meals and lodging per the college's policy. Source: NJCAA-BL Article VI, Section 4.B.1 (same URL). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Tickets on an NJCAA official visit: a college "may not provide a prospective student-athlete with admission to institution's athletic events in a manner other than that of the general public", and may not arrange admission to professional or outside college games. Source: NJCAA-BL Article VI, Section 4.B.1.f.(4) (same URL). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Unofficial visit: "No expense or benefit is provided". A prospect "may take up to two unofficial visits that include athletic participation with team or current student-athletes. There is no limit on the number of unofficial visits that do not include athletic participation." Source: NJCAA-BL Article VI, Section 4.B.2 (same URL). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Where a region or conference differs: "The NJCAA Letter of Intent shall take precedence over all region or conference Letters of Intent; however, regions and/or conferences may impose more stringent regulations than current NJCAA regulations." ## 8. Official and unofficial visits, side by side

## 9. Scholarships and roster rules for softball

- [OFFICIAL] Division I, old rule: softball's scholarship limit was 12 (an equivalency sport, so the 12 could be split). Division I, rule now: at schools in the five defendant conferences or that opt in to the House settlement, "NCAA scholarship limits have been removed" and softball has a ROSTER limit of 25. The table reads "Softball (W): old scholarship limit 12, new roster limit 25, potential increase in scholarships 13." Source: College Sports Commission, https://www.collegesportscommission.org/roster-limits . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] The 25 is in the manual: "Each sport of a Division I institution that is a member of a defendant conference or opts in to the House settlement must comply with the roster limits ... Softball 25." Source: D1M Bylaw 17.2, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008 . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] "The Division I Manual no longer includes institutional financial aid limits for any sport." Schools that do not opt in "should refer to the financial aid legislation in the 2024-25 Division I Manual" (for softball that is the old 12). No school is required to give a full scholarship: aid "may be provided in any amount (full or partial) up to the individual limit in any sport." Source: HOUSE-QA questions B2 and B3, https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/May2026D1Gov_PhaseEightSetQuestionandAnswer.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] How the roster rule works: a roster is submitted "no later than the calendar day before the first contest or date of competition used for championship selection or December 1, whichever is earlier"; after that only listed players may take part in team activities; anyone getting an athletics scholarship or settlement payments "must be included on the sport's submitted roster"; players named Designated Student-Athletes in summer 2025 do not count against the limit. Source: https://www.collegesportscommission.org/roster-limits . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [DERIVED] What changed "for 2026 or later" in softball: (1) the 25-player roster limit and removal of the scholarship cap took effect 1 July 2025 and stay in force for 2026-27; (2) the softball contact rules were rewritten with the "1 p.m. Central time September 1" wording, adopted 6/24/26; (3) softball coaching-staff rules moved to an "off-campus recruiter" model, adopted 6/24/26; (4) the five-year eligibility period in Bylaw 12.1 was revised "6/24/26 effective 8/1/26". Source: D1M Bylaws 17.2, 13.1.1.1.2, 11.7.3, 12.1 (URL above); effective date of roster limits from the HOUSE-QA text "became effective July 1, 2025". Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Division II softball: 7.2 equivalencies per team ("limit on the value (equivalency) of financial aid awards ... to counters"), table revised 1/16/26. NCAA's plain-words page: coaches "often divide available scholarship funds among multiple players on the roster"; a partial scholarship covers "a portion of college expenses". Source: D2M Bylaw 15.4.2.1.2, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90010 ; https://www.ncaa.org/division-ii/governance/membership-and-institutional-resources/athletics-scholarships . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Division III: "A member institution shall not award financial aid to any student on the basis of athletics leadership, ability, participation or performance." The NCAA guide's summary line is "No athletics scholarships. 80% of athletes receive nonathletics aid." Source: D3M Bylaw 15.01.3, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90011 ; CBSA, https://s3.amazonaws.com/fs.ncaa.org/Docs/eligibility_center/Student_Resources/CBSA.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] NAIA softball: upper limit for institutional aid = 10 (full-grant equivalents). Source: NAIA-HB Council of Presidents Financial Aid Policy, item H, https://www.naia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026_Official_Handbook.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] NAIA, what one player may get: no more than "the actual cost of: Tuition; Mandatory fees, books, and supplies required for courses ...; and Board and room for the student-athlete only". Source: NAIA-HB Bylaws Article II, Section B, Item 1a (same URL). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] NAIA, how a partial award counts: the player's share of the team limit is "the total amount of countable financial aid awarded to the student divided by the summation of the tuition cost plus the standard cost of room and board, books, and fees." Her aid counts only if she plays in more than 20% of the maximum varsity contests (softball: 11 contests) or in postseason; junior-varsity-only players do not count; "No limit will be placed on the number of student-athletes on any varsity roster". Source: NAIA-HB Financial Aid Policy items D and F (same URL). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] NAIA, what counts and what does not: countable aid includes athletic grants, academic scholarships, leadership awards, room or meal credits, institutional loans "and institutional work study ... that is funded by the institution". NOT countable: Pell and other federal or state grants, federal or state work study, loans the school does not control, and "campus employment". Source: NAIA-HB Financial Aid Policy items B and C (same URL). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] NAIA, work rule: the limit on benefits "does not include or restrict any financial benefits earned on the basis of employment or payment for actual services rendered." The NAIA handbook sets no hours cap or hourly-rate test that I could find. Source: NAIA-HB Bylaws Article II, Section B, Item 3 (same URL). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] The "paid only for hours worked" wording is an NJCAA rule, not an NAIA one: pay may go to an NJCAA athlete "Only for work actually performed" and "At a rate commensurate with the going rate in that locality for similar services". Source: NJCAA-BL Article V, Section 4.E.1.a, https://media.ardorsportshub.com/files/404933495157163011/6b815aea7cf842719e5deeefd7dc9310-NJCAA-Handbook---Bylaws---2026-27-9-9-26.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.

Source: NAIA-HB Financial Aid Policy item G (same URL). Checked 2026-11-11.

Division I colleges may cover tuition and course fees, room and board, books, up to $250 in required supplies and one round trip home per year; Division II colleges tuition, course fees, books and up to $250 in supplies; "Division III programs may not provide any athletic scholarship of any kind."
