## 1. NCAA Division I women's soccer: when a coach may reach your daughter

Women's soccer has NO sport-specific exception in the Division I contact bylaws. It falls under the "general rule" or "sports other than ..." wording in each one. Method: read the full list of exceptions under each bylaw; soccer is not named in any.

### 1a. Calls, texts, email, direct messages
- [OFFICIAL] Calls from a coach: "Telephone calls to an individual (or the individual's family members) may not be made before June 15 at the conclusion of the individual's sophomore year in high school ... Thereafter, an institution may make telephone calls to the prospective student-athlete at its discretion." Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 13.1.3.1. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Calls TO a coach are also blocked before that date: "an institutional staff member may not receive telephone calls from an individual (or the individual's family members) before June 15 at the conclusion of the individual's sophomore year." Soccer is inside this bylaw (it covers sports other than baseball, basketball, football, lacrosse, ice hockey, softball and men's wrestling). Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 13.1.3.2.5 (Adopted 5/1/19, Revised 4/15/26, 10/7/26). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Email, text and direct messages: a school "shall not provide recruiting materials, including general correspondence related to athletics, or send electronic correspondence to an individual (or the individual's family members) until June 15 at the conclusion of the individual's sophomore year." Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 13.4.1. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Exceptions listed under 13.1.3.1 and 13.4.1 are baseball, women's and men's lacrosse, softball, basketball, men's and women's ice hockey and men's wrestling. No soccer line. Source: D1 Manual Bylaws 13.1.3.1.1 to 13.1.3.1.9, 13.4.1.1, 13.4.1.2. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] After 15 June there is no NCAA limit on how often a Division I soccer coach may call ("at its discretion"). Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 13.1.3.1. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [UNCONFIRMED] A bylaw that names "verbal offers." A text search of D1 Manual Bylaw 13 for "verbal offer" found nothing; the timing limits that exist are on calls, correspondence, contact and written offers.

Men versus women (calls, texts, email): no difference. Both men's and women's soccer use the 15 June general rule. Source: D1 Manual Bylaws 13.1.3.1, 13.1.3.2.5, 13.4.1. Checked 2026-11-11.

### 1b. In-person contact away from campus
- [OFFICIAL] "Off-campus recruiting contacts shall not be made with an individual (or the individual's family members) before August 1 at the beginning of the individual's junior year in high school." Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 13.1.1.1. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Each school gets seven recruiting opportunities (contacts and evaluations combined) per prospect per year; from 1 August of junior year not more than three of the seven may be off-campus contacts, and contact with parents counts. Contacts during an official or unofficial visit do not count. Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 13.1.5.1 (Revised 10/7/26). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] At a tournament or showcase: "in-person contact may not be made with a prospective student-athlete at any site prior to any athletics competition ... in which the prospective student-athlete is a participant on a day of competition, even if the prospective student-athlete is on an official or unofficial visit. In-person contact may occur after the prospective student-athlete's competition concludes for the day and the prospective student-athlete has been released by the appropriate authority (e.g., coach)." Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 13.1.6.2.1. Checked 2026-11-11.

Men versus women (off-campus contact): no difference in the bylaw. Source: D1 Manual Bylaws 13.1.1.1, 13.1.5.1. Checked 2026-11-11.

### 1c. The four period words, as the NCAA defines them
- [OFFICIAL] Contact period: staff may "make in-person, off-campus recruiting contacts and evaluations." Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 13.02.5.1. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Evaluation period: staff may be off campus to assess academics and playing ability; "no in-person, off-campus recruiting contacts shall be made." Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 13.02.5.2. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Quiet period: in-person recruiting contacts "only on the institution's campus"; no off-campus contacts or evaluations. Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 13.02.5.4. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Dead period: no in-person contacts or evaluations on or off campus and no official or unofficial visits. "It remains permissible, however, for an institutional staff member to communicate with (other than in person) a prospective student-athlete during a dead period." Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 13.02.5.5. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Recruiting shutdown: "no form of recruiting (e.g., contacts, evaluations, official or unofficial visits, correspondence or making or receiving telephone calls) is permissible." Women's soccer has none in 2026-27. Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 13.02.5.6; D1 Calendar. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] A dead period stops applying to a recruit once she "signs the institution's written offer of admission and/or financial aid" or the school receives her deposit. Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 13.02.5.5.1. Checked 2026-11-11.

### 1d. The Division I women's soccer recruiting calendar, 2026-27
- [OFFICIAL] The manual no longer prints sport calendars: "For access to recruiting calendars and recruiting periods for other sports, refer to the recruiting calendars published on ncaa.org." Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 13.17 (Revised 10/7/26). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] WOMEN'S SOCCER, four dead periods and nothing else: (1) "AUG 1-11"; (2) "NOV 9-12" ("Monday through Thursday of the initial week for the fall signing date for athletics aid agreements"); (3) "DEC 16 - JAN 6, 2027"; (4) "JUL 28-31". The calendar prints no explanation line for (1), (3) or (4). Source: D1 Calendar, "Women's Soccer" block. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] The calendar lists no quiet period, no evaluation period and no recruiting shutdown for women's soccer. Outside the four dead periods, contacts and evaluations are governed only by the general bylaws in 1a and 1b. Source: D1 Calendar. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [DATA] DERIVED, not printed by the NCAA: the summer dead period lines up with women's preseason. Preseason may start 14 days before the first permissible contest date (Bylaw 17.22.4); Figure 17-2 gives that date as 12 August 2026 and 11 August 2027. Fourteen days before 11 August 2027 is 28 July 2027, the first day of dead period (4); dead period (1) ended the day before the 12 August 2026 first contest date. Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 17.22.4 and Figure 17-2; D1 Calendar. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [UNCONFIRMED] The 2027-28 and 2028-29 women's soccer calendars (the two that cover a class of 2029 player's junior and senior years). Not published; only 2026-27 is on ncaa.org today.

Men versus women (Division I calendar): the calendars differ. MEN'S SOCCER 2026-27 has a dead period "NOV 9-12", a dead period "DEC 11-14" ("Friday through Monday of the NCAA Division I Men's Soccer Championship. A coaching staff member may attend an event conducted in conjunction with and in the host city of the championship") and a quiet period "DEC 23-25". Men have no August or late-July dead period and no three-week December-January dead period. Source: D1 Calendar, "Men's Soccer" blocks. Checked 2026-11-11.

### 1e. Transfer windows (they shape when roster spots open)
- [OFFICIAL] Division I women's soccer notification-of-transfer window: 23 November to 22 December 2026; spring window for fall sports other than football: 1 May to 15 May 2027. Source: D1 Manual Figure 13-1. Checked 2026-11-11.

Men versus women (transfer window): identical dates for men's and women's soccer in 2026-27. Source: D1 Manual Figure 13-1. Checked 2026-11-11.

## 2. NCAA Division II and Division III

### 2a. Division II
- [OFFICIAL] Calls, texts, email: "Communication with a prospective student-athlete or their relative or legal guardian(s) shall be made at any time." Source: D2 Manual Bylaw 13.1.3.1 (Revised 7/23/24 effective 8/1/24). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Boosters are the exception: no booster communication "before June 15 immediately preceding the prospective student-athlete's junior year." Source: D2 Manual Bylaw 13.1.3.1.1. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] In-person contact: "Contact shall not be made with a prospective student-athlete or a prospective student-athlete's relatives or legal guardians before June 15 immediately preceding the prospective student-athlete's junior year in high school." A contact is "any face-to-face encounter ... during which any dialogue occurs in excess of an exchange of a greeting." Source: D2 Manual Bylaws 13.1.1.1 and 13.02.5. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Dead period: for sports other than football the only one in the manual is the signing-date dead period, "the period 48 hours before 7 a.m. on the date for signing in the applicable sport." Staff may still communicate during it. Source: D2 Manual Bylaws 13.02.10, 13.02.10.1.1, 13.02.10.1.2 (Revised 1/16/26 effective 8/1/26). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Tryouts are allowed in Division II: a school "may conduct a tryout of a prospective student-athlete only on its campus or at a site at which it normally conducts practice or competition beginning June 15 immediately preceding the prospective student-athlete's junior year," one tryout per prospect per school per sport. Source: D2 Manual Bylaw 13.8.2.1. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [UNCONFIRMED] A Division II recruiting calendar specific to soccer. A search of D2 Manual Bylaw 13 found no Bylaw 13.17 and no soccer line; the D2 football dead periods (13.02.10.2) are football only.

### 2b. Division III
- [OFFICIAL] Calls, texts, email, private direct messages: "There are no restrictions on the timing for electronic communication (e.g., telephone call, electronic mail, Instant Messenger, text messages or facsimiles) to prospective student-athletes." Messages must be private (no public social media posts to the recruit). Source: D3 Manual Bylaws 13.02.10 and 13.02.10.1 (Adopted 8/4/21). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Printed material: "There are no restrictions on the timing for providing recruiting materials." Source: D3 Manual Bylaw 13.4.1.1. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] In-person contact away from campus: not "until the prospective student-athlete has completed their sophomore year in high school." Source: D3 Manual Bylaw 13.1.1.1. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] At a game: no recruiting contact before her competition that day "until the prospective student-athlete is released for that day"; coaches attending may not send her electronic messages at the competition site. Source: D3 Manual Bylaw 13.1.3.1. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] The words "dead period" do not appear in D3 Manual Bylaw 13. Method: text search of the article. Source: D3 Manual. Checked 2026-11-11.

Men versus women (Divisions II and III): no difference found. None of the D2 or D3 bylaws above names men's or women's soccer. Source: D2 and D3 Manuals. Checked 2026-11-11.

## 3. NAIA and NJCAA

### 3a. NAIA
- [OFFICIAL] "The NAIA has no recruiting calendar. Students and coaches can communicate year-round. There is also no limit to the number of communications or how the communication takes place." Source: https://www.naia.org/why-naia/recruiting . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] "For high school students, there are no recruiting calendar restrictions"; student-athletes "are free to initiate contact with NAIA coaches at any time." Source: https://www.naia.org/student-athletes/prospective/parents/ . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Tryouts: "Students are permitted two days of try-outs at each NAIA member school. The two days do not have to be consecutive, and the student may be provided lodging, meals, etc." Source: https://www.naia.org/why-naia/recruiting . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] The one NAIA no-contact rule: once a student is identified with an NAIA school, "representatives from another NAIA school cannot initiate contact with you," even before classes start. Source: NAIA Guide for the College-Bound Student-Athlete (excerpt), https://play.mynaia.org/media/pzaf3rnz/naia_guide_college_bound_student.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Where a conference differs: "the NAIA does not have a letter of intent program. Student-athletes may sign letters of intent with an individual NAIA school, however, they aren't obligated to attend ... some NAIA conferences require their member schools to recognize letters of intent that are signed with other institutions within the conference." Source: https://www.naia.org/student-athletes/prospective/parents/ . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Eligibility basics: four seasons of competition in the first 10 semesters; 12 hours per term; every athlete registers with the NAIA Eligibility Center (PlayNAIA.org). Source: same NAIA parents page. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] TWO NAIA PAGES DISAGREE on the tryout limit (found by the men's soccer lane today; not re-opened here): "two days of try-outs at each NAIA member school" (naia.org/why-naia/recruiting, no date) vs "a maximum of two days of tryouts throughout your entire athletic career" (naia.org high-school-students page dated 2026-05-11 and the May 2026 NAIA guide). Print both. Source: soccer-mens-01-rules.md section 4; https://www.naia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/naia_guide_college_bound_student-1.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] NAIA Eligibility Center registration fee: "US/Canadian high school: $110; International: $170" (found by the men's soccer lane today in the May 2026 NAIA guide; not re-opened here). Source: https://www.naia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/naia_guide_college_bound_student-1.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [UNCONFIRMED] NAIA rules on who pays for a campus visit outside a tryout. Looked on the NAIA recruiting, parents and financial-aid pages; not stated there.

### 3b. NJCAA (junior college)
- [OFFICIAL] Paid visit: "Prospective student-athletes may receive an official recruiting visit to an NJCAA member institution starting on June 1 prior to their junior year in high school." "A member institution may pay for one official visit for a prospective student-athlete per recruiting year." "The official visit may not exceed 48 hours from prospective student-athlete's arrival to campus," and is limited to the campus and local community. Source: NJCAA Bylaws Article VI, Section 4.B.1.a to B.1.d. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] A visit is official "if any benefit is provided or funds are expended by the institution ... to facilitate the visit of the prospective student-athlete plus two guests"; housing and meals may be provided per school policy; game admission only by normal public admission. Source: NJCAA Official Visit Form (April 2025), https://media.ardorsportshub.com/media/404933495157163011/imported/njcaa/eligibility/docs/Official_Visit_Form_-_Updated_April_2025.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Signing: soccer Letter of Intent signing date is "Nov 1"; 24 signees per team; "Under no circumstance may a member institution enter into a scholarship agreement or contract of any type with a recruit prior to the official NJCAA Letter of Intent signing date"; the letter lasts one academic year (1 August to 31 July). Source: NJCAA Chart; NJCAA Bylaws Article VI, Section 3. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Signing is moving online: "Beginning with the 2027-28 LOI signing date, manual LOIs will no longer be accepted." Source: https://www.njcaa.org/compliance . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Where a region differs: "A region has the authority to implement a rule or rules which are stricter than the NJCAA rule." Source: NJCAA Bylaws (general responsibilities, item B). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] NJCAA soccer season frame, men and women: fall practice start 1 August; first game date 20 August 2026 ("third Thursday in Aug"); 22 games or scrimmages; eligibility roster due 9 October (Division III) or 16 October (Divisions I and II); spring segment starts 5 February with 6 scrimmage dates and ends 1 May. Source: NJCAA Chart. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [SERVICE] Secondary guides state the NJCAA has no recruiting calendar and that a recruit must finish junior year before signing an NJCAA letter. The matching bylaw text was not in the excerpts read. Source: https://readthefield.com/recruiting-calendars/womens-soccer ; https://www.aasoccerrecruiting.com/insights/ncaa-soccer-recruiting-rules . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [UNCONFIRMED] An NJCAA rule on the first date for calls, texts or email. Not found in the bylaw excerpts read.

Men versus women (NAIA and NJCAA): no difference found. The NJCAA Chart prints one soccer row for "Men/Women"; NAIA rules are not sport-specific. Source: NJCAA Chart; NAIA pages above. Checked 2026-11-11.

## 4. Official and unofficial visits

### 4a. Division I
- [OFFICIAL] Earliest paid visit: "an individual may not be provided an expense-paid visit earlier than August 1 of the individual's junior year in high school." Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 13.6.2.1.1 (Revised 10/7/26). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Per-school limit: a school "may finance one visit to its campus for a prospective student-athlete before October 15 following completion of high school"; one more is allowed after a new head coach is hired. Source: D1 Manual Bylaws 13.6.2.2 and 13.6.2.2.1. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Per-athlete limit: none found. The 2026-27 manual has no bylaw capping the total number of official visits a recruit may take (13.6.2.2.1 still cross-refers to a "13.6.2.2.2" that no longer exists as a heading). Method: listed every heading under Bylaw 13.6. Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 13.6. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Length: "not more than two consecutive nights of lodging." Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 13.6.4 (Revised 4/26/23 effective 7/1/23). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Who pays what: the school may pay the recruit's actual transportation (13.5.2.1) and "the actual round-trip cost for up to two family members" (13.5.2.6); lodging and meals for the recruit "and up to four family members," within 30 miles of campus (13.6.6); up to three meals a day (13.6.7.7); "up to $60 per person" entertainment for the recruit, the student host and up to four family members, with no cash and no souvenirs (13.6.7.4). Source: D1 Manual, bylaws as listed. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Free tickets on an official visit: "a maximum of five complimentary admissions to a home athletics event," general seating only. Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 13.6.7.2. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Before the visit she must give the school a transcript, be registered with the NCAA Eligibility Center and be on the school's institutional request list. Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 13.6.3. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Unofficial visits: unlimited in number at the family's expense (13.7.2), but one "with athletics department involvement (e.g., contact with athletics department staff, athletics-specific tour, complimentary admission) shall not occur ... before August 1 at the beginning of the individual's junior year" (13.7.1.1). Source: D1 Manual Bylaws 13.7.1.1 and 13.7.2. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Unofficial visit benefits: "a maximum of three complimentary admissions" to a home event and nothing else; none during a dead period. She pays for her own meals. She may sleep in a player's dorm room "only if the prospective student-athlete pays the regular institutional rate." Local rides to see facilities are allowed with a staff member. Source: D1 Manual Bylaws 13.7.3.1, 13.7.3.1.2, 13.7.3.1.3, 13.5.3. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] One free meal exists: at an admissions open-house event (sports other than football and basketball) a recruit "may be provided with one meal in the institution's on-campus student dining facility" if the school does that for all prospective students. Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 13.7.3.1.2.2. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] No visits of either kind may happen during the four women's soccer dead periods in 1d. Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 13.02.5.5; D1 Calendar. Checked 2026-11-11.

Men versus women (Division I visits): the visit bylaws are the same, but the women's 1-11 August dead period means the "1 August of junior year" opening date fell inside a dead period for women's soccer in 2026; the men's calendar has no August dead period. Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 13.6.2.1.1; D1 Calendar. Checked 2026-11-11.

### 4b. Division II
- [OFFICIAL] Earliest paid visit: not "earlier than June 15 immediately preceding the prospective student-athlete's junior year." One financed visit per school (a second after a head-coach change). No per-athlete total found. Length: "shall not exceed 48 hours." Source: D2 Manual Bylaws 13.3.1.1, 13.3.1.1.1, 13.3.1.2, 13.3.3. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Who pays what: the recruit's "actual round-trip transportation costs" (13.3.4.1); lodging "similar to the accommodations provided to enrolled student-athletes" (13.3.5); meals for the recruit and "their relatives, legal guardian(s) or those accompanying" (13.3.7); lodging for those accompanying (13.3.8); complimentary admissions for the recruit and those accompanying (13.3.6.2). Source: D2 Manual, bylaws as listed. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Before the visit: transcript, Eligibility Center registration, and placement on the school's institutional request list. Source: D2 Manual Bylaw 13.3.2.1. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Unofficial visits: unlimited. The school may give complimentary admissions and, unlike Division I, "may provide the actual cost of meals on an unofficial visit" for the recruit and those with her. Dorm stay only at the regular rate. Source: D2 Manual Bylaws 13.4.1, 13.4.2.1.1, 13.4.2.1.2, 13.4.2.1.3. Checked 2026-11-11.

### 4c. Division III
- [OFFICIAL] Earliest paid visit: not "earlier than January 1 of the prospective student-athlete's junior year." One financed visit per school. Length: 48 hours. Complimentary admissions for the recruit and those with her. Source: D3 Manual Bylaws 13.6.1.1, 13.6.1.1.1, 13.6.2, 13.6.5.2. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Unofficial visits: unlimited; complimentary admissions; "a meal in the institution's on-campus dining facility" (Revised 1/16/26 effective 8/1/26); housing if it "is generally available to all visiting prospective students." Source: D3 Manual Bylaws 13.7.1, 13.7.2.1, 13.7.2.1.1, 13.7.2.1.2. Checked 2026-11-11.

### 4d. NAIA and NJCAA
- See section 3: NAIA two tryout days with lodging and meals allowed; NJCAA one paid 48-hour visit per college per recruiting year from 1 June before junior year, recruit plus two guests.

### 5c. Amateurism rules that bite in soccer (Division I text)
- [OFFICIAL] A professional team is one that "provides any of its players more than actual and necessary expenses" or "declares itself to be professional." Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 12.02.7. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Before college she MAY play on a professional team "provided the individual does not receive more than actual and necessary expenses." After enrolling she may not compete on a professional team. Source: D1 Manual Bylaws 12.2.2.2 and 12.2.2.2.1. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Contracts: "An individual shall not enter into any kind of agreement to compete in professional athletics, either orally or in writing." Before college an agreement is allowed only if it "does not guarantee or promise payment (at any time) in excess of actual and necessary expenses." Source: D1 Manual Bylaws 12.2.4 and 12.2.4.1. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] A contract she signs first still counts even if the club never signs it. Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 12.2.4.2. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Trials: before college she "may participate in a tryout with a professional team or league, provided not more than actual and necessary expenses are received." Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 12.2.1.1. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] The NCAA's own recruiting page words the trial rule more tightly: "A professional team may pay for one tryout before college enrollment," "The tryout cannot exceed 48 hours." The Division I bylaw text read today has no 48-hour line. Both are printed; ask the school's compliance office which applies. Source: https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/recruiting (excerpt) vs D1 Manual Bylaw 12.2.1.1. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Prize money before college: allowed "based on place finish or performance in an athletics event," paid "only by the sponsor of the event." Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 12.1.2.4.1 (Revised 4/15/26). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Agents before college: new in 2026, "a prospective student-athlete may be represented by an agent for the purposes of marketing the prospective student-athlete's athletics ability or reputation in a sport to secure an opportunity as a professional athlete." ## 6. Where the women's calendar really differs from the men's, and what it means

- [OFFICIAL] Season model. Since 1 July 2026 Division I women's soccer has its own preseason rules: a "transition period" that may begin 14 days before preseason (maximum ten hours a week, one on-field session a day), then preseason starting "14 calendar days before the first permissible contest date," and a first contest no earlier than "the Wednesday before the 14th weekend before the start of the NCAA Division I Women's Soccer Championship." Men: practice starts "16 calendar days before the first scheduled regular-season intercollegiate contest," first contest "the Thursday before the 12th weekend before the start of the NCAA Division I Men's Soccer Championship." Source: D1 Manual Bylaws 17.22.2 to 17.22.6. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] The printed first dates: women's soccer 12 August 2026, 11 August 2027, 9 August 2028; men's soccer 27 August 2026, 26 August 2027, 24 August 2028. Source: D1 Manual Figure 17-2. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] The transition period does not count against the 132-day season in women's soccer. Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 17.22.1. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Recruiting calendar. Women: dead periods 1-11 August, 9-12 November, 16 December to 6 January, 28-31 July. Men: dead 9-12 November and 11-14 December, quiet 23-25 December. Source: D1 Calendar. Checked 2026-11-11.

Plain-language consequences (each follows from the cited lines above; none is advice):
1. Women's college soccer starts about two weeks earlier than men's (12 August vs 27 August in 2026). With a 14-day preseason and an optional 14-day transition period before it, a Division I women's player can be due on campus in mid to late July. The summer before freshman year is short.
2. In the women's game, the bylaw date "1 August of junior year" for first visits and first off-campus contact sat inside a dead period in 2026 (1-11 August). Phone, text and email were still allowed (Bylaw 13.02.5.5). A boys' family could visit on 1 August; a girls' family could not until 12 August.
3. Women's soccer coaches cannot watch or host recruits from 16 December to 6 January. Holiday-week showcases in that window cannot be attended in person by Division I women's coaches under the 2026-27 calendar; the men's calendar has only a three-day quiet period (23-25 December) plus the College Cup weekend.
4. The transfer window (23 November to 22 December 2026) closes six days after the women's December dead period begins. Roster movement and high-school recruiting overlap in late November and early December.
5. Everything outside those four dead periods is open: no quiet or evaluation-only periods are printed for women's soccer. In the 2026-27 calendar that means 12 August to 15 December (except 9-12 November) and 7 January to 27 July, subject to the seven-opportunities rule.
6. Signing is the same day for both (second Wednesday of November of senior year). The old idea that soccer signs in February no longer matches the printed sheet.

## 7. Rules that contradict what recruiting websites commonly say

- [OFFICIAL] vs [FOLKLORE] "A coach can't call you, but you can call the coach any time and they can pick up." Not in Division I soccer: staff "may not receive telephone calls" before 15 June after sophomore year. Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 13.1.3.2.5. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] vs [FOLKLORE] "You get five official visits." The 2026-27 Division I manual has no per-athlete total; the limit is one paid visit per school. Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 13.6.2.2. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] vs [SERVICE] "The school can pay for ... three tickets to a home sports event" on an official visit (NCSA page). The Division I bylaw says a maximum of five on an official visit; three is the unofficial-visit number. Source: https://www.ncsasports.org/ncaa-eligibility-center/recruiting-rules/official-visits vs D1 Manual Bylaws 13.6.7.2 and 13.7.3.1. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] vs [SERVICE] "You can tour campus at any age" (a soccer recruiting site). True only without the athletics department: in Division I an unofficial visit "with athletics department involvement" may not happen before 1 August of junior year. Source: https://www.aasoccerrecruiting.com/insights/ncaa-soccer-recruiting-rules vs D1 Manual Bylaw 13.7.1.1. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] vs [FOLKLORE] "Visits open 1 August before junior year." For women's soccer that day was in a dead period in 2026. Source: D1 Calendar. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] vs [FOLKLORE] "Division II coaches can start calling 15 June." Since 1 August 2024 Division II communication "shall be made at any time"; 15 June before junior year is the date for in-person contact, paid visits and tryouts. Checked 2026-11-11.
