## 1. Scholarships and roster limits, division by division

### 1a. NCAA Division I
- [OFFICIAL] Before 1 July 2025 the Division I women's soccer team limit was 14 scholarship equivalencies (the CSC table lists "Soccer (W): old scholarship limit 14, new roster limit 28, potential increase 14"). Source: CSC table. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Roster limit now: 28 for women's soccer at any Division I school that "is a member of a defendant conference or opts in to the House settlement." Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 17.2 (Roster Limitations table: "Women's Soccer 28"); CSC table. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] At those participating schools the NCAA scholarship limit is removed: schools have "the option to offer full or partial scholarships to every student-athlete on a team's roster, so long as the total number ... does not exceed the sport-specific roster limit." Source: CSC table page text. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] The 2026-27 D1 Manual Bylaw 15 (Financial Aid) contains no per-sport team limit and no "counter" cap; its sections run 15.01, 15.02, 15.1 (individual limit = cost of attendance), 15.2 (elements of aid), 15.3 (terms of the award). Method: listed every Bylaw 15 heading in the manual text. Source: D1 Manual Bylaw 15. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] How a partial scholarship counts in D1 now: it does not count against any NCAA team total at a participating school. What counts is the roster spot: "Any student-athlete receiving an athletics scholarship or payments, benefits, or expenses must be included on the sport's submitted roster for the entirety of the academic year." Source: CSC table page text. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Roster deadline: the roster is submitted "no later than the calendar day before the first contest or date of competition used for championship selection or December 1, whichever is earlier"; after that only rostered players may take part in team activities. Designated Student-Athletes named in summer 2025 do not count against the limit. Source: CSC table page text. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] An individual athlete's aid ceiling is the school's cost of attendance ("A student-athlete shall not be eligible to participate ... if the student-athlete receives financial aid that exceeds the value of the cost of attendance," Bylaw 15.1), and aid "may be increased for any reason at any time" (Bylaw 15.3.5). Source: D1 Manual Bylaws 15.1, 15.3.5. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Division I schools that did NOT opt in: roster limits do not apply and they stay under the 2024-25 aid ceiling (for women's soccer that ceiling was 14 equivalencies). This was read in the NCAA House-settlement Q&A (updated 29 May 2026) by the W24 volleyball lane today and was not re-opened in this lane. Source: https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/May2026D1Gov_PhaseEightSetQuestionandAnswer.pdf (Q&A A11, A16, C3 as recorded in W24-volleyball/research/01-numbers-and-rules.md). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [UNCONFIRMED] Which non-defendant-conference Division I women's soccer programs opted in for 2026-27. Looked for a CSC or NCAA list of participating institutions; none found.
- [SERVICE] A recruiting service states "D1 women's soccer programs are now allowed to offer 28 scholarships, up from the previous limit of 14." The official wording is a roster limit of 28 with no scholarship requirement; no school is required to fund any spot. Source: https://www.ncsasports.org/womens-soccer/scholarships vs CSC table. Checked 2026-11-11.

Men versus women (Division I): the old limit was 9.9 for men's soccer and 14 for women's soccer; the new roster limit is 28 for both. Source: CSC table; D1 Manual Bylaw 17.2. Checked 2026-11-11.

### 1b. NCAA Division II
- [OFFICIAL] Women's soccer: 9.9 equivalencies per team per academic year. Source: D2 Manual Bylaw 15.4.2.1.2; same figure on https://www.ncaa.org/division-ii/governance/membership-and-institutional-resources/athletics-scholarships . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Counters: "A student-athlete who is receiving athletics aid shall be a counter and included in the maximum awards limitations." No cap on the NUMBER of counters in soccer was found in Bylaw 15.4; the cap is on the summed value. Source: D2 Manual Bylaw 15.4.1. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] How a partial counts: for each counter the school counts athletics aid for food, housing, tuition and fees; it makes a fraction with the amount received on top and the full grant-in-aid value on the bottom; "the sum of all fractional and maximum awards received by counters shall not exceed the total limit for the sport." So a half scholarship counts 0.5 of the 9.9. Source: D2 Manual Bylaw 15.4.2.2 (item (a) revised 1/13/26 effective 8/1/26). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] NCAA plain-language version: Division II uses "a partial-scholarship model, allowing coaches to divide scholarship funds among multiple student-athletes," and athletics aid "can often be combined with academic scholarships and grants." Source: https://www.ncaa.org/division-ii/governance/membership-and-institutional-resources/athletics-scholarships . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] No national roster limit in Division II or Division III: the phrase "roster limit" appears 0 times in the full text of the D2 Manual and 0 times in the D3 Manual (22 times in the D1 Manual). Method: text search of each manual. Source: D2 Manual; D3 Manual. Checked 2026-11-11.

Men versus women (Division II): men's soccer 9.0, women's soccer 9.9. Source: D2 Manual Bylaws 15.4.2.1.1 and 15.4.2.1.2. Checked 2026-11-11.

### 1c. NCAA Division III
- [OFFICIAL] No athletics scholarships: "A member institution shall not award financial aid to any student on the basis of athletics leadership, ability, participation or performance." The aid package must be "consistent with the established policy of the institution's financial aid office ... for all students." Source: D3 Manual Bylaws 15.01.3 and 15.01.4. Checked 2026-11-11.

### 1d. NAIA
- [OFFICIAL] NAIA is equivalency: "Each sport has an overall limit on the amount of financial aid it can award to students who will compete in varsity contests in that sport." The page's worked example is baseball at 12 full-grant equivalencies ("1 full grant equivalency, 10 half grant equivalencies and 24 quarter grant equivalencies"). Source: https://www.naia.org/student-athletes/current/financial-aid/ . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Soccer limit = 12, per the NAIA Guide for the College-Bound Student-Athlete sport table (copy indexed with a 2019 date). Source: https://play.mynaia.org/media/1091/naia_guide_college_bound_student.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] "H. Upper Limits for Institutional Aid ... Soccer 12." This is a ceiling on a team's school aid, not a roster limit: printed page 121 of the same handbook says "No limit will be placed on the number of student-athletes on any varsity roster". The table prints no unit. Source: NAIA Official Handbook 2026-2027, printed page 123, which is page 124 of the PDF file. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Two NAIA exemptions that stretch the 12: aid to "academically gifted students can be exempted from these limits if the student meets academic criteria," and "aid to students who play at the junior varsity level or who do not compete in varsity contests does not count." Source: https://www.naia.org/student-athletes/current/financial-aid/ . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] An individual's aid is capped at cost of attendance (tuition, mandatory fees, required books and supplies, room and board). Source: same NAIA page. Checked 2026-11-11.

### 1e. NJCAA (junior college)
- [OFFICIAL] Soccer, men and women: 24 "Letter of Intent Signees/Scholarships" per team; the women's 24 is separate from the men's 24; "the numbers listed above are the same for each Division (I, II & III)." Source: NJCAA 2026-2027 Sports Procedures Chart (dated 4-6-26), https://media.ardorsportshub.com/media/404933495157163011/imported/njcaa/governance/handbook/2025-26_Documents/2026-2027_Sports_Procedures_Chart_-_4-6-26.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] What the money may cover: NJCAA Division I "full athletic scholarships (tuition, books, fees, room & board), up to $250 in course required supplies, and transportation costs one time per academic year"; Division II "limited to tuition, books, fees, and up to $250 in course required supplies"; Division III "not permitted to offer any athletic scholarships." Source: https://www.njcaa.org/member_colleges/Divisional_Structure ; same wording in the chart footnotes. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] An NJCAA athletic scholarship is "any financial assistance awarded to the student-athlete, from any source, because of his/her athletic capabilities," and the Letter of Intent runs one academic year (1 August to 31 July). Source: Sports Procedures Chart footnotes. Checked 2026-11-11.

## 4. The odds (NCAA Research)

- [OFFICIAL] Women's soccer, 2024-25 data (table "Last Updated: March 2026"): 393,048 high-school participants; 31,200 NCAA participants; 7.9% overall high school to NCAA; 2.8% to Division I; 2.2% to Division II; 3.0% to Division III. Source: https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/research/pro_beyond/RES_ProbabilityBeyondHSFiguresMethod.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Men's soccer for comparison: 484,908 high-school; 28,746 NCAA; 5.9% overall; 1.4% D1; 1.7% D2; 2.8% D3. Source: same PDF. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Method and limits: NCAA participants divided by NFHS high-school participants in the same year; "data from club teams not included"; NCAA calls the figures "approximations and not exact accounting"; NCAA schools only, so NAIA and NJCAA spots are not counted. Source: same PDF, page 2; https://www.ncaa.org/student-athletes/probability-of-competing-beyond-high-school . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] NCAA no longer prints an NCAA-to-pro percentage for women's soccer: "NWSL and WPF were previously included, but the leagues have since discontinued their drafts." Source: https://www.ncaa.org/student-athletes/probability-of-competing-beyond-high-school . Checked 2026-11-11.

## 7. Professional paths after (or instead of) college

### 7a. NWSL
- [OFFICIAL] The current collective bargaining agreement runs through 2030 ("The new CBA will be in place through 2030"). The league's own manual gives the term as 30 July 2024 through 31 December 2030. Source: https://www.nwslplayers.com/cba ; NWSL Competition Manual 2025 definitions, https://images.nwslsoccer.com/image/private/t_q-good/prd/bqso2kywvrxygd3ppsru.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Minimum annual base salary (CBA Section 8.1): 2024 $37,856; 2025 $48,500; 2026 $50,500; 2027 $65,000 ("auto stipend is eliminated & Locality COLA and Supplemental Stipend are implemented"); 2028 $67,600; 2029 $70,500; 2030 $82,500. Source: NWSL CBA Section 8.1 (PDF page 22). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] No maximum: "There is no limit for an individual player's maximum annual salary." Source: https://www.nwslsoccer.com/news/nwsl-and-nwslpa-agree-to-historic-collective-bargaining-agreement (22 August 2024). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Team base salary cap by year: 2025 $3.3 million; 2026 $3.5 million; 2027 $4.4 million; 2028 $4.7 million; 2029 $4.9 million; 2030 $5.1 million, plus a revenue-share add-on of at least $200,000 a year. For 2026 the league set "a $3.5 million base salary cap to be used against a 22-26 player roster ... with additional monies ... bringing the cap to $3.7 million." Source: https://www.nwslplayers.com/cba ; https://www.nwslsoccer.com/news/nwsl-releases-competition-rules-updates-for-2026-season (11 March 2026). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Roster size: minimum 22, maximum 26 players. Source: NWSL Competition Manual 2025 ("Team Roster sizes must include a minimum of twenty-two (22) players and a maximum of twenty-six (26) players"); 11 March 2026 league release ("22-26 player roster"). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [SERVICE] A contract-tracking site's summary of the CBA says the minimum roster rises from 22 to 23 beginning in 2027. A text search of the public CBA PDF for "Roster Size" returned nothing in this pass, so the clause number is not confirmed. Source: https://www.spotrac.com/news/_/id/2758/nwsl-cba-released-in-full (8 April 2025). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] The draft is gone: "The NWSL and the NWSLPA agreed that the NWSL would eliminate the Entry Draft no later than the 2026 League Season, and the NWSL has eliminated the Entry Draft prior to the 2025 League Season." "The Expansion Draft is abolished." Source: NWSL CBA Sections 13.8 and 13.9. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] What replaced it: free agency for all players when contracts expire, guaranteed contracts, and no trade without the player's consent. A college player now chooses her club and negotiates directly. Source: https://www.nwslplayers.com/cba . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Under-18 signings: a contract signed by a player under 18 may run at most three years including options (five years for players 18 and older). For any trialist or amateur under 18, teams "must provide a safeguarding plan that addresses housing and living arrangements, transportation, education, supervision, health and wellness, and player development." Source: NWSL CBA Section 13.7 and Article 7A (PDF page 21). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Education money for under-18 signings: "tuition or tutoring costs for high school education, and college tuition costs up to $10,000 per year, through the end of the calendar year in which the Player turns 22" may be paid outside the salary cap if the player was under 18 when she signed. Source: NWSL CBA side letter, item 5 "Education Benefits for U18 Players" (PDF page 123). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] U18 Entry Mechanism document (league PDF indexed January 2024): players 17 or younger enter through this mechanism; "the Player and Parent/Guardian must consent"; each team is limited to four U18 players between its entry list and roster; a guaranteed contract must be signed within 30 days of listing; a U18 player may not be waived or traded before 18 without player and parent consent. The 2023 league release gave the team limit as two. Source: https://images.nwslsoccer.com/image/private/t_q-good/prd/zgv2blabos3ojcz5rekn.pdf ; https://www.nwslsoccer.com/news/national-womens-soccer-league-releases-full-2023-competition-guidelines (20 March 2023). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [UNCONFIRMED] Whether the U18 Entry Mechanism limits above are unchanged in the 2026 competition rules. The 2026 NWSL competition manual was not opened in this pass.
- [OFFICIAL] The NCAA consequence: an NWSL contract pays a salary, so it is "an agreement to compete in professional athletics," which ends NCAA eligibility in soccer. Source: D1 Manual Bylaws 12.2.4 and 12.02.7. Checked 2026-11-11.
