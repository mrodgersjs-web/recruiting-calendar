Scope: NCAA women's softball, class of 2029, US parent reader. Facts and sources only, no advice.
Checked 2026-11-11 (every claim below). Tags: [OFFICIAL] [COACH] [SERVICE] [DATA] [FOLKLORE] [UNCONFIRMED] [DERIVED].
Claim format: "- [TAG] fact. Source: URL. Checked 2026-11-11."
Rule: three kinds of number are kept apart: an association's own count [OFFICIAL], a count I made from an official page [DATA], and a recruiting company's or event's number [SERVICE].
Companion file: softball-01-rules.md (contact rules, calendars, visits, scholarships, paperwork).

## 0. How to read this file (plain words)

- [OFFICIAL] = the NCAA, NAIA, NJCAA, NFHS, a pro league or a national governing body published it.
- [DATA] = I counted rows on an official page. The counting method is written out so it can be repeated.
- [SERVICE] = a recruiting company, club, camp or tournament page said it. Prices and "targets" on those pages are not rules and can change.
- [DERIVED] = simple arithmetic on official numbers. The sum is shown.
- No college and no association publishes a required pitch speed, pop time or sprint time. Every measurable in section 7 is a service target.

## 2. The odds (NCAA Research)

- [OFFICIAL] Softball, table "Last Updated: March 2026": 338,315 high school participants; 21,916 NCAA participants; 6.5% of high school players go on to NCAA softball; 2.1% to Division I, 1.9% to Division II, 2.5% to Division III. Source: https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/research/pro_beyond/RES_ProbabilityBeyondHSFiguresMethod.pdf (page: https://www.ncaa.org/student-athletes/probability-of-competing-beyond-high-school/ ). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Method and limits: high school numbers are from the 2024-25 NFHS High School Athletics Participation Survey; "data from club teams not included"; college numbers are from the NCAA 2024-25 Sports Sponsorship and Participation Rates Report; NCAA schools only, so NAIA and junior college players are not in the 6.5%. Source: same PDF. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [SERVICE] A different pair of numbers is in wide use: "Only about 7.8% of high school players continue their careers in college, with just 1.6% making it to NCAA D1" (the 7.8% is said to include NCAA, NAIA and NJCAA; no year given). The Division I figure (1.6%) does not match the NCAA's current 2.1%. Source: https://www.ncsasports.org/softball/scholarships . Checked 2026-11-11.

## 7. Measurables by position (all SERVICE targets, not rules)

No authoritative body publishes position standards. The fullest single table is NCSA's, below. NCSA does not say how many players, which years, or how each number was measured, so treat every figure as a rough marketing guide.

### 7a. How the numbers are supposed to be measured
- [SERVICE] NCSA says the National Fastpitch Coaches Association (NFCA) "has developed a series of testing measurables", and describes them: fastball and changeup velocity (three throws on a radar gun); pop time (stopwatch from first move to the ball reaching second base, best three of six); overhand throw velocity (shuffle and throw 60 feet, radar from behind, best three of six); ball exit speed off a tee (best three of five); pro agility shuttle; 20-yard sprint ("Run 60 feet to measure speed from home plate to first base"); vertical jump. Source: https://www.ncsasports.org/softball/recruiting-guidelines . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [UNCONFIRMED] The NFCA's own published test protocol: not opened. Looked for: nothing beyond the NCSA description above. Checked 2026-11-11.

### 7b. NCSA table, pitcher
- [SERVICE] Division I: pitch velocity 63+ MPH; command of at least 4 pitches; ERA below 2.50; 1 or more strikeouts per inning; 1-2 walks per game or fewer. Source: https://www.ncsasports.org/softball/recruiting-guidelines . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [SERVICE] Division II: 58+ MPH; at least 3 pitches; ERA below 3.00; 1 strikeout per inning; 1 walk per 2 innings or fewer. Source: same URL. Checked 2026-11-11.

## 8. After high school: the roads that exist

### 8a. Is there a draft?
- [OFFICIAL] Yes, since 2025 there is a professional college draft in softball. The Athletes Unlimited Softball League (AUSL) College Draft aired "Monday night" May 4, 2026 on ESPN2: "17 college softball superstars" who had received the league's "Golden Tickets" were assigned to the six teams in "a two-plus-round order". Source: https://www.mlb.com/news/athletes-unlimited-softball-league-airs-college-draft-monday (dated May 4th, 2026). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [DERIVED] Scale: 17 draft picks in 2026 against 21,916 NCAA softball players (2024-25) and 6,956 in Division I. The NCAA's own "probability of competing in professional athletics" table has no softball row; its note says "NWSL and WPF were previously included, but the leagues have since discontinued their drafts." Source: mlb.com page above; https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/research/pro_beyond/RES_ProbabilityBeyondHSFiguresMethod.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] College rule that sits beside the draft: a Division I athlete "shall not request to be placed on the draft list or supplemental draft list of a professional league", and may not make any agreement to compete in professional athletics while eligible. Source: NCAA Division I Manual 2026-27 Bylaws 12.2.3.2 and 12.2.4, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008 . Checked 2026-11-11.

### 8b. Professional leagues in the United States
- [OFFICIAL] AUSL "launched in June 2025. It is a professional women's softball league featuring six teams playing a 25-game season in a traditional format", under Commissioner Kim Ng. ### 8d. Junior college (JUCO) as a road
- [OFFICIAL] NJCAA softball is split into Division I (may cover tuition, fees, room and board, books, up to $250 in supplies, one round trip home), Division II (tuition, fees, books, up to $250 in supplies) and Division III (no athletic scholarship). Up to 24 Letter of Intent signees per team. The letter lasts one academic year, August 1 to July 31. Source: NJCAA Bylaws 2026-27 Article VI, Sections 2 and 3, https://media.ardorsportshub.com/files/404933495157163011/6b815aea7cf842719e5deeefd7dc9310-NJCAA-Handbook---Bylaws---2026-27-9-9-26.pdf ; NJCAA Sports Procedures Chart, https://media.ardorsportshub.com/files/404933495157163011/3fb3dae3297443a688333ba7d0cbef68-2026-2027-Sports-Procedures-Chart-9-9-26.pdf . - [OFFICIAL] A junior college player who is not re-signed by June 15 "automatically becomes recruitable and can sign a NJCAA Letter of Intent for the subsequent year with another NJCAA member institution on June 16." Source: NJCAA Bylaws Article VI, Section 2.A.5.c.(1) (URL above). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Softball-specific catch when moving from a two-year college to NCAA Division I at midyear: "In baseball, basketball and softball, a qualifier who ... initially enrolls at the certifying institution as a full-time student after the conclusion of the institution's first term of the academic year, shall not be eligible for competition until the ensuing academic year."
