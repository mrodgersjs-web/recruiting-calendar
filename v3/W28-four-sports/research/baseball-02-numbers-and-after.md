## 1. Scholarships

### What "equivalency" means
- [OFFICIAL] Division II definition: "each sport has a maximum number of athletics scholarships that may be awarded across an entire team. These are called equivalency limits. Instead of awarding full scholarships to every student-athlete, coaches often divide available scholarship funds among multiple players on the roster." Source: https://www.ncaa.org/division-ii/governance/membership-and-institutional-resources/athletics-scholarships . Checked 2026-11-11.

### Division I
- [OFFICIAL] OLD rule (2024-25 manual, no longer in the manual): "There shall be an annual limit of 11.7 on the value of financial aid awards (equivalencies) to counters and an annual limit of 27 on the total number of counters in baseball." Each counter had to get at least 25 percent of an equivalency. Bylaws 15.5.4 and 15.5.4.1 (2024-25). Source: NCAA Division I Manual 2024-25, Bylaw 15 (local copy D1_Bylaw15_2024-25, download URL not recorded). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] RULE IN FORCE: the College Sports Commission table lists "Baseball (M): Old Scholarship Limit 11.7, New Roster Limit 34, Potential Increase in Scholarships 22.3". For participating schools "NCAA scholarship limits have been removed" and schools "have the option to offer full or partial scholarships to every student-athlete on a team's roster". Source: https://www.collegesportscommission.org/roster-limits . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] RULE IN FORCE, bylaw: "17.2 Roster Limitations. Each sport of a Division I institution that is a member of a defendant conference or opts in to the House settlement must comply with the roster limits set forth below ... Baseball 34." Source: D1 Manual, Bylaw 17.2. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Roster deadline: the roster is due "no later than the calendar day before the first contest or date of competition in the sport that is used for championship selections or December 1, whichever is earlier." Bylaw 17.2.1 (adopted 6/23/25 effective 7/1/25). From the next day, only players on the submitted roster may take part (Bylaw 17.2.2). Source: D1 Manual. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Anyone getting athletics aid "must be included on the sport's submitted roster for the entirety of the academic year". So at a participating D1 school a partial scholarship of any size uses one of the 34 spots. Source: https://www.collegesportscommission.org/roster-limits . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] The current D1 Manual's Bylaw 15 has no baseball equivalency limit (a search of Bylaw 15 headings for "Baseball" and "Equivalency" returned none). Source: D1 Manual. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Carried from the W24 packet (not re-read today): non-participating D1 schools are not bound by roster limits and stay under the 2024-25 aid ceiling; aid may be "in any amount (full or partial)"; designated student-athletes do not count against the limit. Source: NCAA House settlement Q&A updated 29 May 2026, https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/May2026D1Gov_PhaseEightSetQuestionandAnswer.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11 (by the W24 lane).
- [UNCONFIRMED] The exact baseball wording for non-participating D1 schools (whether the old 27-counter cap and 25 percent minimum still apply to them) was not read.
- [UNCONFIRMED] No proposal to change the baseball roster limit of 34 was found, and none was searched for specifically.

### Division II
- [OFFICIAL] RULE IN FORCE: baseball 9.0 equivalencies. Bylaw 15.4.2.1.1 ("Baseball 9.0"). Source: D2 Manual; same number on https://www.ncaa.org/division-ii/governance/membership-and-institutional-resources/athletics-scholarships . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] There is also an overall cap: "An institution shall not provide more than an equivalent of 60 total awards in all men's sports other than football and basketball in any academic year." Bylaw 15.4.2.1.1.1. Source: D2 Manual. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] How a partial is counted in D2: for each counter the school counts athletics aid for food, housing, tuition and fees; "A fraction shall be created, with the amount received by the student-athlete as the numerator and the full grant-in-aid value for that student-athlete as the denominator". The fractions are added up to the 9.0. Bylaw 15.4.2.2. Source: D2 Manual. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [SERVICE] No national D2 roster limit (carried from the W24 packet, a secondary source). Not re-checked today.

### Division III
- [OFFICIAL] "A member institution shall not award financial aid to any student on the basis of athletics leadership, ability, participation or performance." Bylaw 15.01.3. Source: D3 Manual. Checked 2026-11-11.

### NAIA
- [OFFICIAL] "H. Upper Limits for Institutional Aid ... Baseball 12." This is a ceiling on a team's school aid, not a roster limit: printed page 121 of the same handbook says "No limit will be placed on the number of student-athletes on any varsity roster". The table prints no unit. Source: NAIA Official Handbook 2026-2027, printed page 123, which is page 124 of the PDF file. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Carried from the W24 packet: NAIA limits are full-grant equivalents that a school may divide among athletes. Source: https://www.naia.org/why-naia/naia-financial-aid (this page returned HTTP 403 to my fetch today; read by the W24 lane). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [UNCONFIRMED] NAIA counting details (academic exemptions that keep some aid out of the 12) appear in the same Handbook section but were not read in full.

### NJCAA (junior college)
- [OFFICIAL] Baseball: 30 in the column "# of Letter of Intent Signees/Scholarships"; earliest Letter of Intent signing date November 1. "The numbers listed above are the same for each Division (I, II & III)." Source: NJCAA Chart (10-27-26). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] The same chart carries the line "As of December 16, 2025, this document is tentative and may have changes after BOR and SG Meetings are conducted". Source: NJCAA Chart (10-27-26). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] How a partial is counted: "A signee of an NJCAA Letter of Intent counts towards the overall maximum number of individuals in that sport regardless of the amount of athletic scholarship." Source: NJCAA Chart (10-27-26). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] What each NJCAA division may pay: Division I tuition and course fees, room and board, course-related books, up to $250 in required supplies, and transportation home once per academic year. Division II tuition, course-related fees, books, up to $250 in supplies, no transportation. "Division III programs may not provide any athletic scholarship of any kind." "Divisional play is designated by sport, not by school". Source: NJCAA Bylaws (10-27-26), Permissible Athletic Scholarships, B.1.b-B.1.d, p. 44. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [FOLKLORE] 24 is the NJCAA baseball number often repeated. The 2026-27 chart prints 30. I did not capture a named page printing 24 in this pass.

## 2. Paperwork and eligibility

### NCAA Eligibility Center
- [OFFICIAL] Three account types: Academic and Athletics Certification account (Division I or II), Athletics Certification account, and a free Profile Page account. Source: https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/register . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Fee, two NCAA sources that do not match:
  - Live registration page: "$120 Domestic | $180 International". Source: https://web3.ncaa.org/ecwr3/register/CERTIFICATION . Read 2026-11-11.
  - NCAA Registration Checklist: "Effective September 1, the fee for an Academic and Athletics Certification account is $110 for domestic students and $170 for international students. The fee for an Athletics Certification account is $75. Profile Page accounts are free." No year is printed next to "September 1" in the excerpt. Source: http://fs.ncaa.org/Docs/eligibility_center/Student_Resources/Registration_Checklist.pdf (read as a search excerpt, 2026-11-11).
- [SERVICE] A third-party help page dated 2026-09-23 prints $120 / $180 and $85 for a D3 international athletics certification account. Source: https://help.sportsrecruits.com/hc/en-us/articles/360025480672-NCAA-Eligibility-Number . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] "All fees are nonrefundable after 30 days"; a fee waiver exists. Source: Registration Checklist (URL above). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] What the certification account is needed for: "to go on Division I official visits, sign an athletics aid agreement". Source: Registration Checklist (URL above). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] The same requirement in the bylaws: before a D1 official visit a high school prospect "must register with the NCAA Eligibility Center" and be on the school's institutional request list. Bylaw 13.6.3. Source: D1 Manual. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] When to register: the NCAA's student presentation says "Best Practice: Register Before Freshman Year" and that the Profile Page account is "The right place to start for most students", to be moved to a certification account when a D1 or D2 school is actively recruiting. Source: https://s3.amazonaws.com/fs.ncaa.org/Docs/eligibility_center/Updated%20Presentations/Initial_Eligibility_PSA_FULL.pdf (search excerpt). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] "We will not begin your certification until it is requested by an NCAA school." The athletics certification request is "Available during your senior year of high school." Source: https://web3.ncaa.org/ecwr3/register/CERTIFICATION . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] What it issues: a final certification. "Before engaging in practice or competition, a student-athlete shall receive a final certification of athletics eligibility status". Without it a player may practise, not compete, for at most 45 days. Bylaws 12.1.1.1.3 and 12.1.1.1.3.1 (revised 6/24/26 effective 8/1/26). Source: D1 Manual. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [UNCONFIRMED] The current amateurism rules themselves (what a baseball player may accept from an adviser, a pro tryout or NIL before college) were not read. Bylaw 12 was revised on 6/24/26.

### Core courses
- [OFFICIAL] Division I: 16 core courses, with "Completion of 10 of the required 16 core courses before the start of the seventh semester (or the equivalent) of high school. Seven of the 10 core courses must include English, mathematics and natural or physical science." Those 10 grades are locked. Bylaw 14.3.1.1(b). Source: D1 Manual. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Division II qualifier: "16 core-course units in the required subject areas; At least a 2.200 core-course GPA; and Proof of graduation." Source: D2 Manual, Bylaw 14 initial-eligibility figure (page footer 150). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] The NCAA guide: "Not all high school courses are NCAA-approved core courses and may not count toward your 16 core-course requirement" and the 16 must be completed "in four consecutive academic years (eight semesters)". Source: NCAA Guide for the College-Bound Student-Athlete 2026-27, http://fs.ncaa.org/Docs/eligibility_center/Student_Resources/CBSA.pdf (search excerpt). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [UNCONFIRMED] The Division I minimum core GPA was not read today.
- [SERVICE] Division III: a third-party page says all D3 enrollees need an NCAA ID number starting 2026-27. Not confirmed on ncaa.org. Source: https://help.sportsrecruits.com/hc/en-us/articles/360025480672-NCAA-Eligibility-Number . Checked 2026-11-11.

- [OFFICIAL] Division II: "First Date for All Other Sports. The second Wednesday in November." Bylaw 13.02.10.1(c). Source: D2 Manual. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] 2026-27 date as printed by the NCAA: "All Other Division I and II Sports. Initial signing date: November 11, 2026. Final signing date: Based on institutional policy for awarding scholarships." Source: https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/recruiting . Checked 2026-11-11.

- [OFFICIAL] A midyear high school graduate who will enrol midyear may sign from the first day of the signing period. Bylaw 13.02.13.1.1. Source: D1 Manual. Checked 2026-11-11.

## 8. Paths after high school that exist

- [OFFICIAL] MLB Draft straight from high school: 127 high school seniors were drafted in 2026 (section 5). Source: https://statsapi.mlb.com/api/v1/draft/2026 . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] NCAA Division I: 309 baseball programs; roster limit 34 at participating schools; no NCAA aid cap at those schools. Sources: sections 1 and 3. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] NCAA Division II: 251 programs; 9.0 equivalencies; tryouts allowed. Sources: sections 1 and 3; baseball-01-rules.md section 5. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] NCAA Division III: 380 programs, the largest NCAA group; no athletics aid. Sources: sections 1 and 3. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] NAIA: aid limit 12; no recruiting calendar; no association letter of intent; its own Eligibility Center. Sources: sections 1 and 2. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Junior college (NJCAA): three divisions with different aid (Division I up to tuition, fees, room, board, books; Division II tuition, fees, books; Division III none); up to 30 signees per baseball team; one-year letters. Sources: section 1. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Junior college players are drafted: 12 picks in 2026 carried a junior-college class code. Source: https://statsapi.mlb.com/api/v1/draft/2026 . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Carried from the W24 packet: under the NCAA age-based eligibility model the five-year clock starts at the earlier of first full-time enrolment "(incl. a two-year college)" or the academic year after the 19th birthday, for students first enrolling in fall 2027 or later. A junior college year therefore uses NCAA clock time. Source: https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/division-i-and-division-ii-age-based-eligibility-rules (read by the W24 lane). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] MLB Draft League: "a six-team baseball league featuring a split-season amateur-professional format". First half: "Amateur/Draft-eligible players from the Divisions I-III, NAIA, Junior College and High School (graduating seniors) levels." Second half: paid players who have used up amateur eligibility. 58 Draft League players were picked in the 2026 draft. Source: https://www.mlb.com/mlb-draft-league/about/faq ; https://www.mlbdraftleague.com/news/58-mlb-draft-league-players-selected-in-2026-mlb-draft (search excerpts). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Independent professional leagues: "The American Association, Atlantic League, Frontier League and Pioneer League are professional baseball leagues that have been designated as Partner Leagues of Major League Baseball." Source: https://www.mlb.com/glossary/miscellaneous/partner-leagues (search excerpt). Checked 2026-11-11.
