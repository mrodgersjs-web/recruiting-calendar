5. Club, region, camps, and where she is actually seen

One number should settle the argument at the dinner table. In the NCAA's 2025 participation study, 94 percent of college volleyball players played both high school and club, 3 percent played high school only, and 2 percent played club only. USA Volleyball repeats the same finding as "more than 90%." The club season is the showcase. The high school season is where she gets better and stays healthy.

The shape of the year, in any state

In one state the 2026 season ran from the earliest practice on 17 August to the showpiece tournament on 5 to 7 November, played as a bracket in a rented arena. Every state association sets its own calendar: most run a fall season between late August and early November, some split schools into as many as eleven classes, and some run one open class where a small school can win it all. Then club tryouts. One club posted tryouts for 14 November 2026 and a second chance on 19 November, with fees from $25 to $70. Club play then runs into the spring. Two years of dates ahead are rarely printed, so treat her last two fall calendars as PROJECTED and ask her own association for its published schedule.

One of the largest club regions in the country reported 18,137 members and 148 clubs at the end of 2024-25, and listed 14,628 youth girls in its May 2026 newsletter. Coaches know numbers like this. A girl from a small club is not unseen because of her club; she is unseen because nobody sent her film.

The ladder, in the order it actually happens

  1. Club tryout in the fall. The team decides which tournaments she is seen at. Ask, before paying, which qualifiers the team enters.
  2. A regional league weekend in January. One region's own league event is 9 to 10 January 2027, all ages. Your region publishes dates like this one in the fall.
  3. Her region's qualifier, 27 to 28 February 2027, in a rented convention center. Only that region's teams play. At one region the 16s age group had four bids on offer for the July national: one National, two American, one Freedom. Extra bids trickle down from other teams and can arrive as late as the first week of May. Team entry there is $925. Ask what a bid is worth in your own region before the family pays.
  4. Region Championships, 6 to 21 March 2027, over three weekends. This is where 17s and 18s earn their way to Junior Nationals.
  5. A national qualifier if the club goes: Northern Lights in Minneapolis, 29 to 31 January and 12 to 14 March 2027, or the Windy City Invitational in Chicago, 27 to 29 March 2027.
  6. Junior Nationals. USA Volleyball's Girls 14-17s event in Las Vegas, 2 to 5 July 2027 for 16s and 17s, with registration that opened 9 December 2026 at 11 a.m. Eastern. AAU's 16s and 17s event in Orlando runs 25 to 28 June 2027. Staffs work both.

Where a staff says it looks

A Texas State assistant coach and recruiting coordinator wrote for the coaches' association that her staff faced "over 150 club tournaments between Jan. 1 and Aug. 1 alone, plus you have to factor in camps and clinics." That is the market. A club coach's advice, repeated by several staffs: build a free Otto Prep profile, the platform formerly called University Athlete, because "this is how coaches at the tournaments locate you." The same page shows her schedule, her club team and her grades without another email. A Stanford head coach says on that company's own site that it is "the first place we look to find athletes," and the company gives every athlete a free profile.

A camp invite is not interest, and this is not an opinion

USA Volleyball, in its own recruiting Q&A: "an invitation to a camp may or may not indicate interest, and you should follow up with an email or phone call for more information." The Junior Volleyball Association puts it harder: if she has "not already received a lot of interest from this college program prior to receiving the camp brochure, do not assume this school is recruiting you." Every camp site checked for this book says the camp is open to any and all entrants, limited only by number and age group. One region's own summer camp page adds a warning about its own listings: "All listings are paid advertisements."

What is real activity, per USA Volleyball: a coach watching her profile, replying to an email, or asking for film or her schedule. That is "at least a moderate level of interest." A mail-merged brochure is not.

What camps cost, so the math is plain

A private university's college prep camp in July was $310 commuting and $380 resident, open to grades 9 to 12 with high-level club or varsity experience, and the page says each camper "will be evaluated." Its one-day passers camp was $60 and its overnight satellite camp was $200. A public university's prospect camp was $160 for 40 players, lunch included. A Division III school's camp was $154 for one session and $318 for two. A Division I position camp ran $375 to $485. A flagship campus filled a week of June and July 2026 with its own camps, and the June packet says the camp is "recommended only" for players who compete at the club level and in high-level national events. One campus camp was not open to everyone at all: $342 and $447, by invitation. Prices for 2027 were not posted at any of these schools when this book was checked, so read them as the shape of the market, not as a quote.

Ask these four things on any visit or call

  • How many players are you signing in my class, at my position, and who is already ahead of me?
  • What does the aid look like in year one, and how is it renewed?
  • What happens to the promise if you leave, and who do I talk to then?
  • Which dates in 2028 can I come back on an official visit, and does the school pay?
