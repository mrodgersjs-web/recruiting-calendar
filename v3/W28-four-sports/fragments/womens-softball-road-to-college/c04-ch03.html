The calendar's own asterisk

The two June dates carry an asterisk on the printed page. WCWS in it is short for the Women's College World Series. So the June dates are set from that event's schedule. They move every year. The asterisk reads: "Dates are based on the Women's College World Series. If the WCWS dates are delayed, the dead period continues through the day of the final championship game. If the WCWS ends after two games, the dead period concludes on the day of the final game and the contact period begins the next day."

The day the phone opens, the door is still shut

Coaches may first call at 1 p.m. Central time on 1 September of junior year. On the printed 2026-27 calendar, 28 August to 3 September is a dead period. So 1 September sits inside a dead period. The first day a softball coach may talk to a girl is a day the coach still may not meet her. No meeting in person. No visit, paid or unpaid. Calls and email are open. This is this book's reading of the rule and the calendar side by side. The NCAA does not print it as one line.

For your daughter the day is 1 September 2027. The 2027-28 calendar is not printed. So nobody can say yet where that dead period will fall. If it sits in the same week again, plan the first visit for after it ends.

Why her dates say PROJECTED

A class-of-2029 family is planning against dates that do not exist yet. The 2027-28 and 2028-29 softball calendars are not published. On 11 November 2026 the NCAA recruiting page linked only 2026-27 files. That is why the PROJECTED tag is on her dates. A rule can also change before she gets there. Check each date again when the new calendar is printed.
