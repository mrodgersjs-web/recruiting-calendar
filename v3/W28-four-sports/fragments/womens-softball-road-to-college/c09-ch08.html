Club membership fees, and club dues

One travel-ball membership body charges a team fee each year. For one of its membership levels the fee is $100 for a team of players 12 and under (12U) and $200 for a 14U, 16U or 18U team. The page says: "This is an annual fee good through August 16, 2027." This is a fee for the team, not for each girl. SERVICE

Now the number families ask for most: what a travel team costs for a season. This book found no figure it could cite. Clubs set their own dues. No association prints a national number. So ask the club for one page that shows the dues, what they cover, and what each event adds.

The membership fee was read on 11 November 2026 from the body's help page, through a search excerpt. The fee text sits in a folded part of that page.

Two more numbers to keep in view

  • The Eligibility Center fee. $120 on the live page, as Chapter 7 explains.
  • What a scholarship is worth. One recruiting company prints an average for softball. It says about $20,715 a year in Division I. It says about $7,281 a year in Division II. It took both numbers from a second site. It shows no year and no method. SERVICE
  • Why most awards are partial. Division II has 7.2 scholarships to spread over a squad that averages 23.1 players. Split evenly, that is less than a third of a scholarship each. That last line is this book's arithmetic.

What is wasted

  • A camp bought as a meeting. Before 1 p.m. Central on 1 September of junior year, a Division I softball coach may not hold a recruiting conversation at camp. A camp at $100 to $325 buys teaching and a look. It does not buy a conversation. Bylaw 13.12.1.5.4.
  • A showcase on the wrong weekend. In 2026-27 Division I coaches may watch travel ball in the two contact periods and on six fall weekends. Before the team pays an entry fee, check the date against Chapter 3. Then ask the event which college coaches came last year.
  • A trip planned on last year's June dates. The June dead period and the summer contact period move with the Women's College World Series.
  • A number bought as a goal. See the table below.

Recruiting services, not scouts

Look at the last column. Company A puts junior college pitchers below its Division III line. Company C puts them above. Company D also asks a Division I pitcher for an earned run average of 1.0 or less. Company A says below 2.50. None of the four says how many pitchers it measured. These are recruiting services, not scouts. No college and no association prints a pitch speed she must reach. Do not pay for a target. Measure her the same way each time, and send the number with its date.
