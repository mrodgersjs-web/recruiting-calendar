7. What the paperwork really means

There are three papers to know. The first is her account with the NCAA Eligibility Center. The second is the offer she signs. The third is the junior college letter, which works in a different way.

The Eligibility Center account

  • When. The NCAA guide says to register "before ninth grade." She is in 10th grade, so do it now.
  • Which account. A Profile Page is for a student who is not being recruited yet, is not sure of her level, or is headed to Division III. It moves up to a certification account once a Division I or II school recruits her.
  • Why it matters. She needs the paid certification account, or a fee waiver, to take a Division I official visit. She needs it to sign for a scholarship. She needs it to play at a Division I or II school.
  • What it costs. On 11 November 2026 the live sign-up page showed two prices, $120 and $180. They were read as the price for US students and the price for students from other countries. "All fees are nonrefundable after 30 days." A fee waiver exists for students who qualify.

Proof: NCAA 2026-27 Guide for the College-Bound Student-Athlete, pages 6 and 7, and the NCAA certification sign-up page. Read 11 November 2026.

A core course is a class the NCAA has approved. The guide warns that not every high school class counts toward the 16. So ask her counselor which of her classes are NCAA-approved core courses. The guide's wording is on page 9.

The paper she signs in Division I or II

  • The old letter is gone. Division I ended the National Letter of Intent on 9 October 2024. Division II did the same at its meeting of 21 to 22 October 2024.
  • What took its place. A written offer of athletics aid. Softball sits in the group "All Other Division I and II Sports." It has no early period and no late period of its own.
  • The date. The rule is the second Wednesday in November, at 7 a.m. in Division I. For 2026-27 the NCAA printed 11 November 2026. The last day to sign is set by each school's own policy.
  • Her date. She signs in the fall of 2028. The NCAA has printed no date for that year. Chapter 3 works it out as Wednesday 8 November 2028 and tags it PROJECTED.
  • The quiet week. In 2026 a Division I softball dead period ran from 9 to 12 November. Division II had one from 7 a.m. on 9 November to 7 a.m. on 11 November.
  • What signing does. Once she signs, other schools that give athletics aid must stop recruiting her.
  • How big the aid is. At a settlement school, aid may be any amount, full or partial. No school must give a full scholarship. Ask how much, for what costs, and for how many years.
  • The second paper. A school may also give her a written offer about payments for her name, image and likeness, or a share of school revenue. It may come on or after 1 August of senior year. She may not sign it before the signing date.
  • One note on the roster count. The commission's page names a group called Designated Student-Athletes. They were named in summer 2025, when the settlement rules began. They do not count against the 25-player limit. This book's sources do not say what the title is for. Ask the coach if any current players hold it.

The junior college letter

  • The NJCAA still uses a Letter of Intent. The softball signing date on its chart is "Nov 1."
  • She "must have completed their junior year of high school to sign."
  • The letter lasts one school year, from 1 August to 31 July.
  • The college must tell her by 15 June if it will sign her again for the next year.
  • If she is not re-signed by 15 June, she "automatically becomes recruitable." She can sign with a different NJCAA college on 16 June.
  • If she signs with a second NJCAA college before the first one lets her go, she cannot play for one school year.
  • A region or a conference may set tighter rules than the national ones.

Proof: NJCAA Sports Procedures Chart for 2026-27. NJCAA Bylaws 2026-27, Article VI, Sections 2.A.5 and 3.A. Read 11 November 2026.

The NAIA: grades can make room for her

The NAIA handbook for 2026-2027 has no bylaw on a letter of intent. What it does hold is a money rule worth knowing. A team has 10 full grants to share. One player may get no more than the real cost of tuition, required fees, books and supplies, and her own room and board. But aid to a new freshman with strong grades counts less against the team's 10, or not at all.

  • Her aid counts against the team's 10 only if she plays in more than 20% of the varsity games, or in the postseason. For softball the handbook puts that at 11 games.
  • Federal and state grants, federal or state work study and campus jobs do not count against the 10. Athletic grants, academic scholarships and school-funded work study do.
  • Pay for real work does not count as a benefit. The handbook sets no cap on hours or pay rate that this book could find.

Proof: the same financial aid policy, items B, C, D and F, and Bylaws Article II, Section B, Item 3. Read 11 November 2026.

If a professional team calls

The rules differ by level. Division I lets a girl who is not yet in college try out with a professional team, as long as she gets no more than her real and needed costs. The NAIA is stricter on paper: signing with any professional team ends her amateur standing in that sport. For high school play, the national handbook leaves the amateur rule to its members. So the rule that covers her school team is the one written by your state association.

Proof: Division I Manual, Bylaw 12.2.1.1. NAIA Official Handbook 2026-2027, Bylaws Article VII, Section D. NFHS Handbook 2024-25, pages 23 to 25, the newest edition this book could open. Read 11 November 2026.
