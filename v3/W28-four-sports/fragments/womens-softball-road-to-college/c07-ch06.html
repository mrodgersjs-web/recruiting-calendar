6. What to send, and what good video looks like

One email, from her, with two links. It is short. Every number in it says how it was measured, and when. Then one video that lets a coach check those numbers.

The player, the school, the club and the coach are invented. This book's rule is that a sample email names no real high school, club, camp or service. The numbers only show the form, and the two links are placeholders.

Why it is written this way

  • She writes it, from her own mailbox. The rules below are about what a coach may send to her. So the thread should be hers.
  • Class year, position and school sit in the subject. A coach can sort it without opening it.
  • Every number has a tool and a date. Say which tool was used. Say who held it. A coach cannot weigh a number that has no method.
  • Two questions a coach can answer in one line. The first is the roster question from Chapter 2. The second is the time zone, so her first call lands at a good hour.
  • It names no place. She asks for the coach's time zone. She does not guess at it.

When a coach may answer

Nothing in the rule text this book read stops her from sending an email. The limit is on the coach. A Division I softball school may not send her "electronic correspondence" before 1 p.m. Central on 1 September of junior year. So an email to a Division I softball coach in 10th grade gets no recruiting answer. The coach may send a short reply that explains the rule or points her to admissions. The reply may say nothing about the athletics program. Camp information may be sent at any time. That is the rule working. It is not a verdict on her. Bylaws 13.4.1.4, 13.4.1.13 and 13.4.1.14(b).

A phone call is tighter still. Before that date the staff may not even take her call. One recruiting company tells players they "can - and should - proactively contact college coaches at any time." For a Division I softball phone call, the rule says no. Bylaw 13.1.3.2.8.

Division II, Division III, NAIA and junior college coaches may answer her at any time. So in 10th grade, send this email to those levels first. Send the Division I round in the week of 1 September 2027.

Proof: Division I Manual 2026-27. Division II Manual, Bylaw 13.1.3.1. Division III Manual, Bylaw 13.02.10. Read 11 November 2026. Chapter 11 explains one open point about texts and direct messages.

What good video looks like

No association prints a rule for recruiting video. This book found no softball coach on the record about how long a video should be. So it prints no number of minutes. What its sources do carry is how the common tests are run. A good video lets a coach see each test done the same way.

TestHow one recruiting company says it is run
Pitch speedFastball and changeup, three throws each, on a radar gun.
Pop timeFor catchers. A stopwatch runs from her first move to the ball reaching second base. Best three of six.
Overhand throwShuffle and throw 60 feet. The radar gun reads from behind. Best three of six.
Exit speedBall speed off the bat, hit from a tee. Best three of five.
Home to firstA sprint of 60 feet, which the company also calls the 20-yard sprint.
OthersA pro agility shuttle and a vertical jump.

The company says a national coaches' group built these tests. This book did not open that group's own test sheet. Read 11 November 2026. SERVICE

So film the tests she lists in her email. Show the radar gun or the stopwatch in the shot. Put the date, her name, her class year and her position on the first frame. Keep the link open, with no login. These four lines are this book's own advice. They are not a rule, and no coach this book read said them.
