10. Ten ways families get softball wrong

  1. They trust "June 15." That is the general Division I date for email and mail. Softball's date is 1 p.m. Central on 1 September of junior year. For the class of 2029 that is 1 September 2027.
  2. They call a Division I coach in 9th or 10th grade. The staff may not take the call. The family hears nothing and decides the school is not interested. The silence was the rule.
  3. They book a visit for the first week of September. On the printed 2026-27 calendar, 28 August to 3 September is a dead period. The phone opens on 1 September. The door does not.
  4. They plan June from last year's dates. The June dead period and the summer contact period are set from the Women's College World Series. They move every year.
  5. They count 25 scholarships. The 25 is a roster limit at settlement schools. It is a cap on people. Other Division I schools still work under the old 12 scholarships, and no public list shows which is which.
  6. They pay for a camp to talk to the coach. Before 1 p.m. Central on 1 September of junior year, a Division I softball coach may not hold a recruiting conversation at camp.
  7. They pay for a showcase without checking the date. In 2026-27, Division I coaches may watch travel ball in two contact periods and on six fall weekends.
  8. They chase a pitch speed from a chart. Four companies print four different Division I numbers. None is a rule, and none says how it was measured.
  9. They skip Division III, the NAIA and junior college. The NCAA counts more softball teams in Division III than in Division I. And the 6.5 percent odds count NCAA players only.
  10. They plan on a professional career. The next section says what this book could and could not confirm.

Proof for each line is in Chapters 2 to 8. All read 11 November 2026.

After college: what is known, and what is not

A professional softball league began play in the United States in June 2025. It has six teams and a 25-game season. In May 2026 it held a college draft, and 17 players were picked. Set that beside 21,916 NCAA softball players, 6,956 of them in Division I.

This book could not confirm what that league pays its players. It could not confirm any other professional softball league in the United States in 2026. It could not confirm softball at the 2028 Olympic Games. The NCAA's own table of professional odds has no softball row. So this book makes no promise about a professional road. Plan on the degree.

One rule matters while she is in college. A Division I athlete "shall not request to be placed on the draft list or supplemental draft list of a professional league." That is Bylaw 12.2.3.2.

One piece of money is certain. The NCAA Postgraduate Scholarship is "a one-time $7,000 scholarship" for graduate study. Up to 100 are given each year, split evenly between men and women across the fall, winter and spring seasons.

Proof: the league's own pages and two league news pages dated 4 May 2026 and 18 July 2026. NCAA Research probability table, last updated March 2026. NCAA Postgraduate Scholarship page. Read 11 November 2026.

  • 24 is a count of signed players. It is not 24 full scholarships. A signed player counts as one of the 24 "regardless of the amount of athletic scholarship."
  • The letter lasts one school year. If she is not re-signed by 15 June, she may sign with a different NJCAA college on 16 June.
  • A move in the middle of the year has a catch. The Division I rule names softball. A player who first enrolls at the Division I school after its first term may not play until the next school year. The same rule is written for transfers from four-year schools.
  • Other levels may recruit her freely. Since 7 April 2025 an NAIA coach may start contact with a junior college student at any time. Division II puts no limits on mail, calls or meetings with two-year college transfers.
  • Not every two-year college is in the NJCAA. Some play under other rule books. This book did not open them.

The five-year clock

Division I Bylaw 12.1 says a player's time to play "shall be limited to five years." It was revised on 24 June 2026 and took effect on 1 August 2026. This book did not read how junior college seasons count against the five years. So do not assume a junior college year is free. Before she enrolls, ask the Division I school's compliance office in writing how many seasons she would have left.
