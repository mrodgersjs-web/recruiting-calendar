The 25-player limit, and why it is not 25 scholarships

One recruiting site says Division I softball teams are allowed "up to 25 scholarships per year." The rule does not say that. The 25 is a cap on people. The cap on scholarships was taken away at settlement schools. The commission's own table reads: "old scholarship limit 12, new roster limit 25, potential increase in scholarships 13." The key word is potential. A school may fund 25 players. It may fund far fewer.

Which schools are settlement schools? Every school in the five defendant conferences is one. Any other Division I school may opt in. This book found no public list of the schools that did. So ask each school in writing: "Are you under the 25-player roster limit, or the old 12 scholarships?"

How the 25-player limit works

  • The school turns in its roster by the day before its first game that counts toward the championship, or by 1 December. The earlier date wins.
  • After that, only listed players may take part in team activities.
  • Anyone who gets an athletics scholarship or settlement payments must be on the list.
  • Players named Designated Student-Athletes in summer 2025 do not count against the 25-player limit.

Proof: College Sports Commission roster limits page. Read 11 November 2026.

Now the part nobody prints. Take the average squad and divide it by four class years. A team can add about five or six new players a year. That is a ceiling, and it is arithmetic. It assumes four equal classes and no transfers. Each transfer a coach takes is one fewer place for a high school player. So the real number for high school girls is lower. No association publishes how many players a team recruits each year. The NCAA, NAIA and NJCAA pages this book read were all checked for it.

The odds, from the NCAA's own table

The NCAA's table was last updated in March 2026. It says 338,315 girls play high school softball. It says 21,916 play NCAA softball. That is 6.5 percent. By level it is 2.1 percent to Division I, 1.9 percent to Division II and 2.5 percent to Division III.

The NCAA prints its method too. It divides its own players by the high school count. The high school count is the 2024-25 NFHS High School Athletics Participation Survey. Club teams are not included. The NCAA count is from its own 2024-25 participation report. Only NCAA schools are counted. So a girl who plays NAIA or junior college softball is not in the 6.5 percent.

One recruiting company prints other odds: 7.8 percent to any college and 1.6 percent to Division I. It gives no year. Its Division I figure does not match the NCAA's 2.1 percent. SERVICE

Proof: NCAA Research, estimated probability of competing beyond high school, figures and method. Read 11 November 2026.

What this means for your daughter

Ask each coach three things. How many players are on the roster now? How many leave before fall 2029? How many of the open places go to transfers? The answers tell you more than any national number in this chapter.
