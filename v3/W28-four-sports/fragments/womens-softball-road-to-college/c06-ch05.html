5. Club, camps, and where she is seen

A Division I coach may not watch just any game on just any day. The calendar says which kind of softball may be watched, and when. Learn that first. Then pick the events.

Two kinds of softball, two sets of days

The calendar uses two words. Scholastic means her high school team. Nonscholastic means the rest, which for most girls is travel ball. Here is what the printed 2026-27 calendar lets a Division I coach do.

Read the table once more. Outside the two contact periods, the calendar marks travel ball for watching on six fall weekends only. The rest of the school year is high school softball only. And in an evaluation period a coach may watch but may not meet her off campus. So in 2026-27 a face-to-face meeting away from campus can happen only in the two contact periods. This is this book's reading of the printed calendar. The NCAA does not print it as one line.

These days bind Division I coaches only. Division II, Division III, NAIA and junior college coaches do not follow this calendar. Chapter 4 gives their rules.

The extra evaluation period

The calendar adds one more time when Division I coaches may watch. It reads: "During high school regional and state championship competition that does not occur during a dead period." The calendar prints no dates for it. Those dates come from your state association.

Camps

  • No recruiting talk before the date. Before 1 p.m. Central on 1 September of junior year, a Division I softball coach may not hold a recruiting conversation at camp. The rule covers college camps and clinics, even one for younger girls. Bylaw 13.12.1.5.4.
  • What a camp is before that date. It is teaching, and a chance to be watched. It is not a meeting about her future. A coach who stays quiet about recruiting is following the rule.
  • Where college staff may work. Division I softball staff may work a private camp only on days when they may watch travel ball. The private camp must also be open to any player and advertised at least 14 days ahead. At other times they may work only college camps, on a college campus or at the college's own field. Bylaws 13.12.1.2.4 and 13.12.2.2.8. The calendar cites the same rule as 13.12.2.3.6, so the two papers number it differently.
  • Who else is at camp. College players may be paid to work camps and clinics. The rule sets no limit on how many. Bylaw 17.23.7.

Proof: Division I Manual 2026-27 and the 2026-27 softball calendar. Read 11 November 2026. Camp prices are in Chapter 8.

Club and travel ball

More than one body runs travel softball. The sport's national governing body covers adults and youth, slow pitch and fast pitch. Its own pages print two sets of numbers. They do not agree, and neither one carries a date.

Travel teams also join membership bodies. One of them places teams in "one of our nine (9) Member Leagues" by where they are, for age groups from 8 and under (8U) to 18 and under (18U). That text is from its 2024-2025 season page. From the 2027 season, its membership no longer includes membership in the national governing body. Ask the club which bodies it has joined, and which events each one opens. SERVICE

How much travel ball does she need? One recruiting company prints an answer. It says four to five years of "high-level travel ball" for Division I. It says three to four years for Division II. It says two to three years for Division III and NAIA. No association prints such a rule. Treat it as one company's view. SERVICE

How the other levels watch

  • Division II. At a game or a practice, a coach waits until she is done for the day and has been released by the proper authority. Division II coaches' recruiting guide, updated 30 July 2026.
  • Division III. No limit on how often or when a coach may watch. Bylaw 13.1.4.
  • NAIA. A coach may watch her high school games and practices. It stays watching as long as the coach does not ask her to show a skill or run the workout. NAIA Official Handbook 2026-2027, Article II, Section C.
  • Junior college. Staff may visit her at any place to recruit. NJCAA Bylaws, Article VI, Section 4.C.

How to build her list

This book has no counted sample of softball rosters. It will not guess at one. Here is how a family builds its own. Start with schools that have what she wants to study. Open each team's roster page. Count the players at her position by class year. Mark the ones who will be gone before fall 2029. Do it for schools at all five levels. Chapter 2 gives the team counts by level. Division III alone has more teams than Division I. The junior college count is the weak one. The only number in hand is one company's 364. It covers all junior colleges, not only the NJCAA, and this book could read no split by NJCAA division. Treat 364 as unsettled.
