4. What a coach may and may not do

Each level has its own rules. Division I softball is the strict one, and it has its own dates. Read the first table slowly. It is the one a recruiting site can get wrong.

Division I softball has its own clock

One time and date runs through the Division I softball rules: 1 p.m. Central time on 1 September of junior year. Junior year means the school year that starts in the fall of 11th grade. For the class of 2029 that day is 1 September 2027. The softball lines in the rule book carry the note "Adopted: 6/24/26," which is 24 June 2026.

Why "June 15" is wrong for softball

One softball recruiting site, on a page marked "Updated August 2026," prints this:

"D1 and D2: Coaches cannot call, text, or email you until June 15 after your sophomore year. You can contact them any time."

June 15 is real. It is the general Division I rule, for sports that have no rule of their own. Softball has its own, and it is later. The table shows both, so you can see the gap.

The site is wrong about Division II as well. Division II coaches may call and write at any time. And "contact them any time" misses a rule. Before 1 September of junior year, a Division I softball coach may not take her call.

How many times a Division I coach may see her

  • Each school gets seven recruiting chances per player per year. A meeting counts as one. A trip to watch her counts as one. The rule's word is "opportunities."
  • From 1 September of junior year, the seven may hold no more than three off-campus meetings a year. Meeting her family counts too.
  • A meeting during an official or unofficial visit does not count.
  • Trips to watch her from 1 June through 31 August do not count against the seven. Trips from 1 September through 31 May do, unless a meeting happens the same day.
  • Any number of meetings in one day counts as one.
  • A school may send only four off-campus recruiters out on any one day.

Proof: Division I Manual 2026-27, Bylaws 13.1.5.9, 13.1.5.11, 13.1.5.12, 11.7.3.3 and 11.7.6.3. Read 11 November 2026.

Before a Division I paid visit

Three things must be done first. This is Bylaw 13.6.3.

  1. She sends the school a transcript.
  2. She is registered with the NCAA Eligibility Center.
  3. She is on the school's request list.

The rule book calls it the institutional request list. It is the school's own list of the players it is recruiting. Ask the coach to add her. Being on the list is not an offer, and it does not commit her to that school.

Four more rules about visits

  • Unpaid visits in Division I have no limit on number. The family pays. The school may give up to three free tickets to a home game, and none in a dead period. Bylaws 13.7.2 and 13.7.3.1.
  • "The family pays for everything" is not quite true. On an unpaid visit a Division II school may pay the real cost of meals. A Division III school may give one meal in its dining hall. Division II Bylaw 13.4.2.1.2 and Division III Bylaw 13.7.2.1.1.
  • Junior college unpaid visits come with no expense or benefit. She may take up to two that include playing with the team. NJCAA Bylaws, Article VI, Section 4.B.2.
  • After she signs a written offer of athletics aid, other schools that give athletics aid must stop recruiting her. NCAA news release, 9 October 2024.
