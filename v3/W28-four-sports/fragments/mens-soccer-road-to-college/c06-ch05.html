5. Club, camps, and where he is seen

This is the thinnest chapter in the book, and it says so. The sources behind this edition hold rules and counts. They do not hold a study of where college coaches find boys. So this chapter prints what is on paper, names what is missing, and shows how to find the rest in ten minutes.

What is on paper

  • The NCAA's odds leave club out. Its high school number counts school teams only. Its own note reads "data from club teams not included." A boy who plays only club is not in the count at all.
  • One youth league prints its size. Major League Soccer's youth league calls 2026-27 its seventh season and its largest. It lists more than 53,000 players, 2,743 teams and 310 clubs, with all 30 of the pro league's academies. A second source counts 318 clubs. Chapter 11 prints both.
  • The Olympic Development Program says who watches. Its page says players "compete against the strongest players in their age group from other states and regions in front of college and professional scouts." Its steps are a state team, summer camps in four regions, and games between regions.
  • The rule book says when a coach may watch. Division III has "no restrictions on the number or timing of evaluations" (Division III Bylaw 13.1.4). Division I gives each school seven recruiting chances a year with each player (Division I Bylaw 13.1.5.1). And no coach may speak to him before he plays that day. Chapter 4 has that rule.

Club and high school at the same time

No NCAA rule this book read limits a high school boy's amateur club soccer before he enrolls in college. Whether he may play club during the high school season is a high school rule. Ask your state association. Whether his club league lets him play for his school is the league's rule. This book did not read any league's rule on that. Ask the club director in writing.

The picture changes once he is on a Division I college team. In season, a college player who plays for an outside team loses his eligibility until it is given back (Bylaw 17.22.11.1.1). Out of season, a college may let no more than five of its players practice or play on an outside amateur team (Bylaw 17.22.11.1.2).

Camps: what he is buying

A camp is a paid event. In Division I a tryout is not allowed, so a camp is not a tryout, whatever the flyer says. The camp rules sit in Bylaw 13.12, and this book did not read their text. Chapter 4 says what that means. This book sampled four price pages for one-day camps and showcase days. Chapter 8 prints them, tagged SERVICE.

What this book does not know

  • Which events college staffs go to. No source in this edition counts it.
  • What a Division I overnight camp costs, or what a national showcase team charges. No page was sampled.
  • What a club season costs. No club fee sheet was sampled, and no travel cost.
  • How many NAIA and junior college men's teams there are. No official count was found.
  • The rules of the other boys club leagues. No official page was read.

This book will not fill those holes with a guess. Chapter 11 lists what the next edition will add.

How to find it yourself in ten minutes

  1. Open five roster pages. Pick five colleges on his list. If a page lists each player's last club or school, write it down and tally it. That is where those five staffs found their players.
  2. Send the coach one question. "Which events will your staff watch this year?" A coach can answer that in one line. A Division I coach may answer from 15 June after 10th grade.
  3. Open the camp's own sign-up page. Write down the price, the hours, the ages, the coaches it names and the refund rule. If it names no coach, ask who will be there.
  4. Ask the club for its full-year fee sheet. Ask for it in writing, with travel and event fees on it.
  5. Count the teams near you. Go to the NAIA's own site and the junior college body's own site, and look for men's soccer. This book did not reach those pages, so it cannot tell you what they show.

Sources: NCAA estimated probability figures, last updated March 2026; the youth league's own schedule page, dated October 2026; the Olympic Development Program page; NCAA Division I and Division III Manuals 2026-27. All read 11 November 2026. Links and names are in Chapter 11.
