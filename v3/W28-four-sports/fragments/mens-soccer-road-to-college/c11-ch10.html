10. Ten ways families get soccer wrong

Each one costs money or a year. Each one has a rule or a number behind it, in the chapter named at the end of the line.

  1. Paying for a Division I "tryout." There is no tryout in Division I (Bylaw 13.11.1). It is a camp, or it is nothing. (Chapter 4)
  2. Quoting the wrong Division I number. 9.9 is the old aid limit. 28 is the new roster limit at schools that opted in. They are not the same kind of number, and only one of them binds a given school. (Chapter 2)
  3. Reading 18.1 as money. It is room on paper: 28 minus 9.9. No school has promised to fill it. (Chapter 2)
  4. Reading silence as a no. Before 15 June after 10th grade, a Division I coach may not send him recruiting messages. If he writes first, the school may send a short reply that explains the rule or points him at admissions. (Chapters 1 and 3)
  5. Feeling snubbed on match day. No NCAA coach may talk to him in person before he plays that day. (Chapter 4)
  6. Believing in a season-long ban. This book found no NCAA rule that stops contact for his whole high school season. Club play in that season is a matter for your state association. (Chapter 4)
  7. Copying dates from the wrong sheet. The women's soccer dates sit on the same NCAA sheet and are not the same. Last year's dates are not this year's. The sheets for 2027-28 and 2028-29 do not exist yet. (Chapter 3)
  8. Signing with a pro club without reading the pay line. Real costs keep him an amateur. A promise of pay above them can end his college soccer before it starts. (Chapter 7)
  9. Looking only at Division I. Division I is 213 of the NCAA's 823 projected men's teams. The NCAA puts the odds of reaching it at 1.4 percent. For all three divisions it is 5.9 percent. (Chapter 2)
  10. Waiting for a "letter of intent." Division I ended it in 2024. The paper is a written offer of athletics aid. If there is no paper, there is no offer. (Chapter 7)

The question that heads off most of these

"Is your school under the 28-player roster limit this year, and how many players do you plan to add at my position for 2029?" A coach can answer it in one line. The answer tells you which rule the school lives under, and if there is a seat.
