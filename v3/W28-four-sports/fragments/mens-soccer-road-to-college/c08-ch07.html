7. What the paperwork really means

Four things decide this: an account, his grades, an offer, and his amateur status. Soccer puts the most weight on the last one, because a pro club may come to him before a college does.

The account

Eligibility Center accountWho it is forPrice
Academic and Athletics CertificationA boy who plans to play in Division I or Division II. He needs it before a paid visit at those levels.$120 on the live sign-up screen. $110 in the NCAA's own checklist. For students from other countries, $180 and $170.
Athletics CertificationA student from another country who is going to Division III, or a transfer who needs only the athletics check.$75 in the NCAA's checklist.
Profile PageA boy headed to Division III, or not yet in high school, or not sure. It can be changed to a paid account later.Free.

Two NCAA sources disagree on the price, so both are printed. The live screen is the later one, read on 11 November 2026. Fees are not paid back after 30 days. A student who qualifies may ask for a fee waiver. A walk-on is a player who joins with no athletics aid. He must register too.

The NAIA has its own Eligibility Center. Its May 2026 guide prints a one-time fee of $110 for students from US and Canadian high schools, and $170 for others.

The grades

  • Division I. 16 core courses, with a core-course GPA of at least 2.300 on a 4.000 scale. That includes four years of English and three of math at Algebra I or higher (Bylaw 14.3.1.1, revised 6/24/26). Ten of the 16 must be done before his seventh semester starts. Seven of those 10 must be in English, math and science.
  • Division II. 16 core courses, a core-course GPA of at least 2.200, and proof that he graduated. An early path lists 14 core courses and a 2.500 (Division II Manual, Figure 14-1).
  • Division III. The NCAA sets no core-course count. Each school sets its own bar.

The offer

In Division I the paper is a written offer of athletics aid. The rule book's name for it is an athletics aid agreement (Bylaw 13.02.13). The National Letter of Intent ended for Division I on 9 October 2024. A guide that still says "letter of intent" about a Division I school is out of date.

  • When. The fall signing date is the second Wednesday in November, from 7 a.m. This year the NCAA printed it as 11 November 2026. The last day to sign is set by each school's own policy. These dates do not apply to a player who transfers from a four-year college.
  • Division II. The paper is an institutional athletics aid agreement. The first date is the same second Wednesday (Division II Bylaw 13.02.10.1).
  • Division III. There is no athletics aid, so there is no aid agreement (Division III Bylaw 15.01.3).
  • NAIA. There is no national letter. Some NAIA conferences make their members honor each other's letters. Ask the coach what his conference does.
  • Junior college. The paper is still a Letter of Intent. He commits to the college, and the college commits to an amount of athletics aid. It covers one school year. A letter he has not signed goes dead 21 days after it is issued, and it may be issued again. Once he signs, staff at other NJCAA colleges may not contact him.

Five questions to ask before he signs

  1. Is your school under the 28-player roster limit this year, or under the old 9.9 aid limit?
  2. How many players are on the roster today, and how many will be back?
  3. What is the amount in dollars, and what does it cover?
  4. Is this a written offer of athletics aid, and what is the first day he may sign it?
  5. Has your school asked the Eligibility Center to certify him?

Staying an amateur

FIFA and the NCAA draw almost the same line. FIFA says a professional has a written contract and "is paid more for his footballing activity than the expenses he effectively incurs." All other players are amateurs (FIFA transfer rules, Article 2). The NCAA says a team is professional if it gives any player more than "actual and necessary expenses," or if it calls itself professional (Division I Bylaw 12.02.7). This book calls those expenses his real costs.

Before he enrolls in collegeWhat the Division I rule says
A trial with a pro teamAllowed, if he takes no more than his real costs (Bylaw 12.2.1.1). The sentence sets no limit in hours. Once he is enrolled, each pro team may pay his costs for one 48-hour trial (12.2.1.2).
Playing on a pro teamAllowed, if he takes no more than his real costs (Bylaw 12.2.2.2.1). Once he is enrolled in college it is not allowed (12.2.2.2).
Signing somethingHe may not make "any kind of agreement to compete in professional athletics, either orally or in writing" (12.2.4). There is one exception before college. A deal to play on a pro team is allowed if it does not "guarantee or promise payment (at any time)" above his real costs (12.2.4.1).
Money from a pro club or academyHe may take up to his real costs for games, and for practice held to get ready for them, from a pro body that sponsors the event (12.1.2.2.3, revised 4/15/26). Prize money for where he finishes is allowed from the event's sponsor (12.1.2.4.1).
A pro draftHe may enter one once before college and once after he enrolls, as long as he is not drafted. After he enters in college, he has 72 hours to say he will return (12.2.3.2.6).
The men's soccer ruleHe may take his real costs from Major League Soccer to go to its showcase, however long it runs. It does not count as a trial with a pro team (12.2.1.2.5, effective 8/1/24).

Who decides? The Eligibility Center does, one player at a time (Bylaw 12.1.1.1). A club's word that a deal is "college safe" is not a ruling. Before he signs anything with a pro club or its academy, get the pay line in writing and read it against the rows above.

  • Division III. Before college he may take his real costs from a pro team for practice and games. He may not play for pay or take a promise of pay. A boy who enrolls on or after 1 August 2027 may be drafted (Division III Bylaws 12.1.4.1 and 12.1.4.2).
  • NAIA. Its guide says he loses amateur standing by "signing a contract with any professional team."
  • Division II and junior college. This book could not find the Division II sentence on pay and contracts before college. It did not open the junior college rule. Ask the school's rules office before he signs anything.
  • The clock. Division I gives five years. They start at the earlier of two things: his first full-time term in college, or the school year after his 19th birthday (Bylaw 12.6). A gap year at an academy or abroad runs on the same clock.
  • Abroad. FIFA bars a move to a club in another country before age 18, with a short list of exceptions (Article 19). The first is that his parents move there "for reasons not linked to football."

If a pro club calls

The pro road is real in this sport, and it is narrow. Major League Soccer's 2026 draft had three rounds and a pool of 436 players from 150 schools. Its 2026 pay floor was $113,400 for a senior roster player and $88,025 for a reserve player, who must be 24 or younger. The league's page says there is no limit on the number of homegrown players a club may sign.
