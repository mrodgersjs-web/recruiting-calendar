Invented player, invented school, invented club and invented coach. Every number in the sample is made up to show the form, and the links are placeholders. Sample emails in this book name no real high school, club, league, camp or service.

Why it is written this way

  • He writes it. It comes from his mailbox, in his words. A parent may check it. A parent should not send it.
  • Class, position and school in the subject. A coach should know who this is before he opens it.
  • Numbers with a count behind them. Two goals mean one thing in three matches and another in 11. Give matches, starts and minutes.
  • A measure with a date. Say who measured him, in what month, and with shoes on or off.
  • His NCAA number. A Division I school needs his Eligibility Center sign-up and his transcript before it may pay for a visit (Bylaw 13.6.3). Putting the number in the email saves a week.
  • The roster question. The answer tells you if the school lives under the 28 or under the old 9.9. Chapter 2 says why that matters.
  • A time zone, not a town. A Division I coach may call him from 15 June after 10th grade (Bylaw 13.1.3.1). To set a call, the two of them need the hour, not the address.
  • Sent on the right day. Before 15 June after 10th grade, a Division I coach may not send him recruiting messages. If he writes first, the school may send a short reply that explains the rule or points him at admissions. Coaches in Division II, Division III and the NAIA may write back today.
  • Links that open. Test each link on a phone that is signed out. If it asks for a login, fix it before he sends.

What good video looks like

No rule sets a standard for a soccer reel. No official body in this book's sources prints one, and this edition holds no coach's statement on video. So this part is this book's guidance, not a rule, and no document stands behind it. The best source is the coach. Ask him what he wants to see.

  • Use match play, not drills.
  • Use a wide shot, so the coach can see the players around him.
  • Mark him for a second before each clip starts. Then let the play run.
  • Start each clip a few seconds before he gets the ball, and end it a few seconds after he lets it go.
  • Put his name, class, position, shirt number and shirt color on the first frame.
  • Put his best clips first, and keep the reel short.
  • Show both halves of his job: with the ball and without it.
  • Keep one full match ready, and say which half he plays where.
PositionWhat to show, as guidance only
GoalkeeperSaves, high balls, one-on-ones, and passing with feet and hands.
Center backOne-on-one defending, headers, and passing out from the back under pressure.
Outside backOne-on-one defending, runs to get back, and crosses.
MidfielderTaking the ball under pressure, passing short and long, and winning the ball back.
Winger or forwardRuns off the ball, finishing with both feet, and taking on a defender.

What this chapter cannot tell you

How long a college coach watches a reel. How many emails a staff gets. Which stats a staff trusts. This edition has no source for any of them, so it prints none. A line in his first email does more than any guide: "What would you like to see from me next?"
