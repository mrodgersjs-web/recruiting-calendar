8. What it costs, and what is wasted

This chapter has fewer prices than it wants, and it says which ones are missing. What it does have is a list of things families pay for that the rules make pointless. Start there.

What is wasted

  • A paid "tryout" with a Division I staff. Division I may not hold one (Bylaw 13.11.1). You are buying a camp, or you are buying nothing.
  • A trip to meet a Division I coach before 1 August of 11th grade. Before that day the soccer staff may not take part in his visit. That means no meeting, no team tour and no free ticket (Bylaw 13.7.1.1).
  • A trip inside a dead period. No visits of either kind are allowed. This year's dead periods are 9 to 12 November and 11 to 14 December.
  • A camp bought as a way to "get recruited." A flyer is not an offer. Chapter 4 says what this book could and could not read about camps.
  • A paid account bought too soon. The Profile Page is free and can be changed later. The paid fee is not paid back after 30 days.
  • NAIA tryout days spent early. One NAIA page says he gets two days for his whole career. Do not spend one on a school he would never go to.
  • A plan built on a full scholarship. Division II has 9.0 to share, and its average squad was 39.0. Division III gives no athletics aid. A Division I school that opted in may give aid to all 28, and no rule says it must.

The prices this book does not have

  • A Division I overnight camp on campus.
  • The fee for a national showcase team or an academy team.
  • A full club season, with travel.
  • What two travel weekends cost a family.

No page was sampled for any of the four, so no number is printed. Do not let anyone hand you a "typical" one. Use the ten-minute steps in Chapter 5, and write down the real prices for his own club and his own list. Chapter 11 says what the next edition will add.

Junior college money

The junior college body has three divisions, and a college picks one for each sport for four years. In Division I a full award may cover tuition, books, fees, room and board, and up to $250 in course supplies. It may also cover one trip a school year to the college and back. Division II is held to tuition, fees, books and up to $250 in supplies. Division III gives no athletics aid. Ask a junior college coach which division his soccer team is in before you talk about money.

What is free

The rule text. Every roster page. The Profile Page. The email in Chapter 6. The five questions in Chapter 7. A family that uses all five knows more than most families who pay for help.
