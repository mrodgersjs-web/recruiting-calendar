7. What the paperwork really means

Three kinds of paper decide whether he plays: an account, a list of classes, and a signed offer. Each level uses its own paper. None of it is hard, and all of it has a date.

The NCAA Eligibility Center account

The Center has three kinds of account. The first is the Academic and Athletics Certification account, for Division I or Division II. The second is the Athletics Certification account. The third is the Profile Page account, and it is free. The NCAA's own register page lists all three.

The Center's own page wins. Two NCAA sources print two fees. Pay what the live registration page shows on the day he registers.

  • The Center waits for a school. Its page says, "We will not begin your certification until it is requested by an NCAA school." The athletics request opens "during your senior year of high school."
  • A paid Division I visit needs it. Before the visit he must register with the Center and send the school a transcript. He must also be on that school's request list. That is Bylaw 13.6.3.
  • The last step is a final certification. He must have it "before engaging in practice or competition." Without it he may practice for up to 45 days, and he may not play in games. Those are Bylaws 12.1.1.1.3 and 12.1.1.1.3.1, in effect from 1 August 2026.

The seventh semester is the first half of 12th grade. So the 10 are counted before senior year starts, and those 10 grades are then locked. Ask his counselor which of his classes the NCAA counts. Division I asks for a core-course Grade Point Average of at least 2.300. That is Bylaw 14.3.1.1(a).

The signed offer

There is no National Letter of Intent. The Division I Council ended that program on 9 October 2024. The Division I manual now prints signing dates for an "Athletics Aid Agreement." In plain words, it is a written offer of athletics aid. At an opted-in school he may also get a second paper, a settlement related benefits agreement, and the same first day applies.

Baseball has one opening day. The rule is the second Wednesday in November, in Bylaw 13.02.13.1(f). Division II uses the same Wednesday, in its Bylaw 13.02.10.1(c). Baseball has no early period and no regular period. For 2026-27 the NCAA printed the day as 11 November 2026. For the class of 2029 the day is PROJECTED as Wednesday 8 November 2028. This book worked that out from the rule, and the NCAA has not printed it. The NCAA says the last day to sign is "based on institutional policy," so each school sets its own.

A player who graduates at midyear, and will enroll at midyear, may sign from the first day too. That is Bylaw 13.02.13.1.1.

A junior college year and the clock

The NCAA gives a player a five-year clock. The clock starts at the earlier of two things. One is the first time he enrolls full time at any college, and the NCAA says that includes a two-year college. The other is the school year after his 19th birthday. The rule covers students who first enroll in fall 2027 or later, so it covers him. So a junior college year uses NCAA clock time. This rule was read for the volleyball edition of this book and carried forward.

Before he signs, get five answers in writing

  1. How much athletics aid is it, and which costs does it cover?
  2. Did the school opt in to the new Division I rules?
  3. Will he be on the roster the school turns in by 1 December?
  4. What is the school's last day to sign?
  5. Who has to sign it: him alone, or a parent too? Chapter 11 says why this book cannot answer that.
