8. What it costs, and what is wasted

This sport has two kinds of price. A fee is set by a body that makes the rules, and it is the same for every family. A SERVICE price is set by a seller, and it can change. This chapter keeps the two apart.

The survey calls baseball "the most expensive in 2024 among the three most-played sports." Baseball parents spent more than soccer or basketball parents on four things. The four were sign-up fees, travel and hotels, lessons, and camps. The survey covers every age from 6 to 18. It is not a budget for a recruit. This book found no official figure for what a recruit costs a family.

SERVICE prices: camps, events and streaming

This book found prices for camps, team events and game streaming. Each one is a SERVICE price, set by the seller. Each one was read from a search result and not from the seller's full page. So this chapter does not print them as settled. They sit in Chapter 11, each with its SERVICE label and the day it was read. This book names no seller, so ask a seller for its own current price before you budget.

What is wasted

  • A Division I camp bought to start a talk. Before 1 September of junior year the coach may not talk recruiting with him at that camp. Pay for it if he wants the teaching. Do not pay for it to be recruited.
  • A winter event sold on Division I coaches. From 12 October 2026 to 28 February 2027 those coaches may not watch off campus.
  • A summer event on a dead day. No Division I coach may watch in person from 31 May to 7 June 2027, 19 to 21 June 2027, or 3 to 5 July 2027.
  • A trip to meet a Division I staff too early. An unofficial visit with the baseball staff is not allowed before 1 September of junior year.
  • The paid Center account, bought years ahead. The Profile Page is free. The Center will not start his file until an NCAA school asks.
  • Any plan built on 11.7. That number is superseded. Chapter 2 has the one in force.
  • A chart of speeds by position. No bylaw this book read sets one. A chart from a seller is a SERVICE number.

What is free

  • Every rule and date in this book, at the pages listed in Chapter 11.
  • The NCAA Profile Page account.
  • An email. Division II, Division III and NAIA coaches may answer it at any time.
  • A paid visit, when a college offers one. Chapter 4 lists what each level may cover.

Five questions before any check is written

  1. What is the full price, with every add-on?
  2. Which college staffs have said in writing that they will be there?
  3. Is the date in a contact period on the Division I calendar?
  4. May those coaches talk recruiting with a player his age?
  5. What will he hold in his hand when it is over?
