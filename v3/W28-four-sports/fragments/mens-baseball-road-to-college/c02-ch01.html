1. Read this first

Read Chapter 2 before you spend any money. Then put the plan in Chapter 3 on one page and cross items off. The rest is for reference: what the rules allow, what to send, what the paper means, and what it costs.

The main idea

The rules are free. The dates are free. Most families never read them. This book read them on 11 November 2026 and wrote down where each one is printed. A family that knows three things is ahead of a family that spends far more. Those three things are the roster number, the dates a coach may answer, and the paper that counts.

This book is written to the parent of a boy in the class of 2029. He is in 10th grade this school year. He is a junior in 2027-28 and a senior in 2028-29. Every date here is his date. If your son is in another class, the rules are the same and the dates are not. Work each date out again from the rule printed next to it.

What makes baseball different

Three things move the calendar and the money.

First, baseball waits longer. The general Division I rule for other sports lets a coach call or write from 15 June after 10th grade. In baseball the date is 1 August of junior year. A coach may not meet him off campus, or host him on a visit, until 1 September of junior year.

Second, the number that matters is a roster number, and it is 34. A roster is the list of players on a team. The number many recruiting pages still print is 11.7 scholarships. That number is not in the current rule book. Chapter 2 prints both, with the document for each.

Third, pro ball sits next to college ball. The draft is the yearly event where Major League Baseball teams pick new players. Teams picked 613 players in the 2026 draft. Of those, 127 were high school seniors, 474 came from four-year colleges and 12 came from junior colleges. So college is also the main road to the draft.

Three rules that surprise families

  • He may not phone a Division I coach early. The rule stops the coach from making a call, and from taking one, before 1 August of junior year. For the class of 2029 that day is 1 August 2027, worked out from the rule. Email and texts from the coach wait for the same day.
  • Division II may write to him at any time. The date families quote is 15 June before junior year. That date is for meeting in person, paid visits and tryouts. It is not for calls or texts.
  • There is no National Letter of Intent any more. Division I ended it in October 2024. The paper is now a written offer of athletics aid. He may sign from the second Wednesday in November of 12th grade. For him that day is Wednesday 8 November 2028, worked out from the rule.

Words this book uses

WordWhat it means here
NCAAThe body that sets the rules for most four-year college sports. It has three levels: Division I, Division II and Division III.
NAIAA second body for four-year colleges. It has its own rules and its own Eligibility Center.
Junior collegeA two-year college. The NJCAA is the national body this book read. It has three divisions of its own.
BylawOne numbered rule in a rule book. This book prints the number so you can look it up.
Roster limitA cap on people, not money. In Division I baseball it is 34 at a school that opted in.
Opted-in schoolHouse is a court case that changed Division I roster and scholarship rules. An opted-in school is in one of the five conferences named in that case, or it chose the same rules.
EquivalencyA cap on money that a coach may cut into pieces. One equivalency is worth one full scholarship. Division II baseball has 9.0.
CounterA player who gets athletics aid, so he counts against a limit.
ContactA coach meeting your son or your family in person, away from the college.
EvaluationA coach watching him play in person, away from the college.
Official visitA campus visit the college pays for. This book also calls it a paid visit.
Unofficial visitA campus visit your family pays for.
TryoutA workout a college runs to test one player. Division II and NAIA rules allow it, with limits.
TranscriptHis school's own record of his classes and grades.
Eligibility CenterThe NCAA office that decides whether he may play. Its last answer is called a final certification.
Request listThe rule book calls it the institutional request list. He must be on a school's list before a paid Division I visit. Ask the coach to add him.
Core courseA high school class the NCAA counts. Division I asks for 16 core courses.
Letter of intentA paper a player signs with one college. The NCAA no longer has one. Junior colleges still do.
SigneeA player who has signed a junior college letter of intent.
Aid agreementThe written offer that took the place of the letter of intent. The rule book calls it an athletics aid agreement.
ClubAny team that is not his school team: a travel team, a summer team or a fall team.
SERVICEA price or number from a seller's own page, not from a rule book.
HARDA date that a document prints. You can look it up today.
PROJECTEDA date this book worked out from rule text. The NCAA has not printed it yet. Chapter 3 tags every one.
