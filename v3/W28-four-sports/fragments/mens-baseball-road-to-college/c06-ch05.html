5. Club, camps, and where he is seen

A coach can only recruit a player he is allowed to watch. So this chapter starts with where the rules let a coach be. Then it shows you how to build your own list of schools.

What this book will not tell you

In this book, "club" means any team that is not his school team. That can be a travel team, a summer team or a fall team. This book has no count of where college players were first seen. No source it read splits school ball from club ball. So it will not say that one matters more than the other. It will say what the rules allow, and that part is firm.

Read it by season. Fall has one open month. Winter is closed. Spring and summer are open from 1 March to 31 July. His school season runs on dates your state association sets, so lay those dates over this table.

Summer has one more edge. A Division I school gets seven recruiting chances with a player each year. A look at him from 1 June to 31 August does not use one up. So a staff can watch him all summer and keep its seven for the school year.

Camps, showcases and tryouts

A showcase is a paid event where players work out in front of people who grade them. Chapter 4 has the rules on what a camp or a showcase can do for him.

  • A Division I school's own camp can teach him. It cannot recruit him before 1 September of junior year.
  • A winter showcase needs a second look. From 12 October 2026 to 28 February 2027, Division I staff may not watch off campus. They may work only their own camp, on their own campus or regular field.
  • A private camp in the open months must be open to all. A Division I coach may work it only if any player may sign up and it was advertised 14 days ahead.
  • Division II may try him out. One tryout per school, from 15 June before junior year.
  • NAIA may try him out and may watch his games. NAIA has no recruiting calendar.
  • A junior college may bring him in to play. He may make up to two unofficial visits that include playing with the team.

Build your own list

  1. Write five headings: Division I, Division II, Division III, NAIA and junior college. Put schools under every one.
  2. Open each team's own roster page. Count the players. Count the players at his position. Count the seniors at his position.
  3. Write down the date you counted. Rosters change.
  4. For each Division I school, add one more box: opted in, yes or no. Ask the staff.
  5. A roster page may show more than 34. Some older players do not count toward the limit. Ask the staff how many count. That is Bylaw 17.2.2.2.
  6. For each junior college, add its NJCAA division. That sets what it may pay.
  7. Cross off any school he would not attend if baseball ended.

Ask before you pay for any event

  • Which college staffs have said in writing that they will be there?
  • Is the date in a contact period, a quiet period, a dead period or a shutdown?
  • May those coaches talk recruiting with a player his age yet?
