What we could not settle

Each item below is UNCONFIRMED. This book has no document that settles it.

  • His own Division I calendars. The 2027-28 and 2028-29 baseball calendars are not printed. Every period date in this book is for 2026-27 only.
  • His own signing date. The 2028-29 signing sheet does not exist yet. Wednesday 8 November 2028 is arithmetic from the second-Wednesday rule, and it can move.
  • Which Division I schools opted in. This book found no list. Ask each school.
  • Any plan to change the 34. None was found, and none was searched for.
  • Watching a 10th grader. This book did not read whether a Division I coach may watch a sophomore in person in a contact period. It read only the junior-year dates.
  • Direct messages on social media. The manual says "electronic correspondence." The rule that names direct messages was not found.
  • Older wording. Bylaws 13.1.1.1.1, 13.6.2.1.2 and 13.7.1.2 were all revised on 24 June 2026. Pages written before then may show another baseball date.
  • Division I visit limits. The total number of paid visits across all schools was not found. Nor was the rule on paying for family travel.
  • Division II tickets. The count of free tickets on a Division II visit was not printed in the part read.
  • Roster limits in Division II and Division III. None was read. One other source says Division II has no national limit.
  • Junior college contact. The recruiting section prints no start date for calls or texts. The rest of the bylaws were not read, so "no rule" is not proven. Region rules were not collected.
  • Junior college counts. The NJCAA Division III page could not be read. Two-year colleges outside the NJCAA are not in the 397. The 397 itself came from a search result. Its split leaves only 17 teams for NJCAA Division III, which looks too low.
  • The junior college number 24. It is often repeated. The 2026-27 chart prints 30, and no page printing 24 was captured.
  • NAIA counts and aid. No official count of NAIA baseball programs was found. The Handbook lists some aid that may sit outside the 12, and that part was not read in full. Its table prints no unit for the 12. The unit in Chapter 2 comes from the NAIA's own aid page.
  • NAIA tryouts. The NAIA's own pages disagree: two days at each school, or two days in a whole career.
  • The NAIA Eligibility Center fee. A search result of the NAIA student guide prints $110 for a high school student in this country or Canada, and $170 from abroad. The full guide was not opened.
  • The NCAA fee. The live page says $120 and $180. The checklist says $110 and $170, with no year. The checklist also prints $75 for the Athletics Certification account.
  • Who signs. This book did not read who must sign an aid agreement.
  • Amateur rules. Bylaw 12 was revised on 24 June 2026. The rules on advisers, pro tryouts and pay for name and image were not read.
  • The draft. This book did not count how many of the 127 high school picks signed. It did not read the rules on who may be drafted. It did not read which teams get the six extra rounds, or why.
  • Size and speed by position. No official standard was found. Commercial tables were not collected.
  • Other paths. The summer draft league and the independent pro leagues were read only as search results. So this book does not describe them.
  • What a recruit costs. No rule body prints a typical family spend for a baseball recruit. The survey in Chapter 8 covers every age from 6 to 18.
