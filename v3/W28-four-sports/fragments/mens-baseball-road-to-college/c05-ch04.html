4. What a coach may and may not do

A quiet coach is often a coach following a rule. This chapter says what each level allows, and when. Every date is for the class of 2029, worked out from the rule next to it.

Division I: the fine print that matters

  • His call does not get around the rule. Before 1 August 2027 a Division I coach may not call him. The coach also may not take a call from him or from you. That is Bylaw 13.1.3.2.3.
  • Email, texts and mail wait for the same day. Bylaw 13.4.1.1 says a school may not send recruiting materials or electronic messages before then.
  • A school gets seven recruiting chances with him a year. A chance is a contact or an evaluation. From 1 September of junior year, no more than three off-campus contacts may be among the seven. A meeting with you counts the same as a meeting with him. That is Bylaw 13.1.5.2, revised 15 April 2026.
  • Summer looks are not counted. Watching him from 1 September to 31 May uses a chance. Watching him from 1 June to 31 August does not. A contact uses a chance in any month. A talk during a campus visit does not. That is Bylaw 13.1.5.11.
  • No calls during the college's own game. Staff may not call from the time their team reports until it is let go. That is Bylaw 13.1.3.2.1.

Camps and tryouts

  • A Division I school's own camp. A coach may not have a recruiting talk with a camper before 1 September of the camper's junior year. That is Bylaw 13.12.1.5.2, in effect from 1 August 2026.
  • A Division I coach at a private camp. In a quiet period he may not work one. Outside a quiet period he may, if the camp is open to any and all players and was advertised at least 14 days ahead. Those are Bylaws 13.12.1.2.1 and 13.12.2.2.2.
  • A Division II tryout. A school may hold one tryout with him, from 15 June before junior year. It must be on campus or at the team's normal practice site. That is Bylaw 13.8.2.1.
  • An NAIA tryout. Two NAIA sources do not match. The NAIA Official Handbook 2026-2027 and the recruiting page say two days of tryouts at each NAIA school. The parents page and the student guide say "a maximum of two days of tryouts throughout their entire athletic career." Both were read on 11 November 2026. Ask the school which one it follows.
  • An NAIA coach at his game. The coach may come to watch a game or a practice. He may not ask your son to show a skill, or run the workout. That is the NAIA Handbook 2025-2026, page 50.
  • A Division III coach at his game. On a game day his own high school or club coach lets him go first. Only then may a Division III college coach speak to him. That is Division III Bylaw 13.1.3.1.

Four lines to check against a rule

  • "Have him call me." A Division I coach may not take that call before 1 August of junior year.
  • "Stop by and meet the staff." Not at a Division I school before 1 September of junior year.
  • "Come to camp so we can talk." A Division I camp may teach him. It may not recruit him before that same day.
  • "We have 11.7." Ask if the school opted in. If it did, the number is 34 roster spots.
