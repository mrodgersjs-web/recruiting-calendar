5. Club, camps, and where she is seen

A coach can only rate a player the coach has watched. So the first question is not which event is biggest. It is which days a coach is allowed to stand at the field.

The days a Division I coach may watch

In 2026-27 a Division I women's soccer coach may watch and meet recruits from 12 August to 15 December, except 9 to 12 November. Then again from 7 January to 27 July. The four blocks below are closed. Division I recruiting calendar for 2026-27, updated 4 August 2026.

Dead periodWhat it does to a club schedule
1 to 11 AugustEarly August events are not watched in person by Division I staff. The first open day was 12 August in 2026.
9 to 12 NovemberFour days, Monday to Thursday of signing week.
16 December to 6 JanuaryHoliday showcases fall here. Division I coaches cannot watch them or host her on campus.
28 to 31 JulyThe last four days of July are closed too.

Check every event on her club list against those four blocks before you pay for a trip. The other levels work on other rules. Division II has only its 48-hour signing dead period. The Division III recruiting rules have no dead period. The NAIA has no recruiting calendar. So an event that Division I staff must skip is not closed to those coaches by any calendar this book found.

The high school season

Her high school season is set by your state association. This book prints none of those dates. Lay her school dates and her club dates next to the four blocks above. Then you can see which games a Division I coach could attend.

Camps and tryouts

  • A Division I camp must be open to all. The school may limit it only by number, age, grade or gender. So a camp flyer in her inbox is not an offer. Division I Manual, Bylaw 13.12.1.3.
  • What a camp costs. One-day college ID camps and clinics ran about $125 to $250. SERVICE An ID camp is one where college coaches come to watch players.
  • What a private showcase costs. Events with several college staffs ran about $169 to $299. SERVICE One company says it caps its events at six players per coach.
  • Division II tryouts. A Division II school may hold one tryout with her, on its own campus or field, from 15 June before junior year. Division II Manual, Bylaw 13.8.2.1.
  • NAIA tryouts. Allowed, with a two-day limit. Chapter 4 explains why that limit has two readings.

Camp prices were printed on listing and sign-up pages on 11 November 2026. They are examples, not a full survey.

Summer teams with older players

Before college she may play on a professional team, as long as she takes no more than her actual and necessary costs. She may also try out with one. Division I Manual, Bylaws 12.2.2.2.1 and 12.2.1.1. One summer league for women says all of its players are amateurs, to protect college eligibility. Chapter 7 draws the line she must not cross.

What this chapter does not count

No club fee or team tournament price is printed here. Tournament entry fees are billed to clubs, and this book found no official public page with a cost per family. It did not collect overnight camp prices. It found no official NAIA program count. And it holds no official figure for how many college players came up through a club.

How to build her list

  1. Write down schools at all five levels, not only Division I.
  2. For each Division I school, ask the coach if the school opted in to the settlement for the year she would enroll. Schools choose each year.
  3. Open each team's roster page. Count the players, then count the ones at her position by class year.
  4. Mark which of her events fall on days a coach may watch.
  5. Send the email in Chapter 6, with her schedule, before each of those events.
