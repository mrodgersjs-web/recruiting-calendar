1. Read this first

Read Chapter 2 before you spend any money. Then put the plan in Chapter 3 on one page and cross items off. The rest is for looking things up: what a coach may do, what to send, what the papers mean, and what it all costs.

The main idea

Three things shape this sport, and all three are free to know. The team limit changed on 1 July 2025. The NCAA prints four dead periods, and they set when a Division I coach may watch your daughter. And the rule book sets the first day a coach may write to her, meet her, or host her. A family that knows these dates buys less and wastes less.

Every rule in this book says where it is written. Every number names its document. All of it was read on 11 November 2026, unless a line gives another date. Where this book could not settle a point, Chapter 11 says so.

Four rules that surprise families

  • A Division I coach may not take her call yet. Before 15 June after her sophomore year, a Division I coach may not call, text or email her. The coach may not pick up when she calls, either. The one thing allowed is a short reply that does not recruit her. For the class of 2029 that day is 15 June 2027. This book worked that date out from the rule. Division I Manual, Bylaws 13.1.3.1, 13.1.3.2.5, 13.4.1 and 13.4.1.13.
  • "28 scholarships" is not the rule. Since 1 July 2025 the number 28 is a roster limit at Division I schools that are part of a court settlement. It caps players. It does not promise money to any of them. The old limit was 14 scholarship equivalencies. Chapter 2 tells both stories. The 28 is in Division I Manual, Bylaw 17.2. The old 14 is in the College Sports Commission table.
  • The first visit day can land in a dead period. The rule opens Division I visits on 1 August of junior year. In 2026 that day sat inside a dead period for women's soccer. The first day a coach could meet a girl in person was 12 August. Phone and text stayed open. Division I Manual, Bylaw 13.6.2.1.1, and the printed 2026-27 calendar.
  • There is no National Letter of Intent now. The paper is the school's own offer of aid, in writing. Soccer signs from the second Wednesday in November of senior year, and there is no February date. For her that day works out to 8 November 2028. Division I Manual, Bylaws 13.02.13 and 13.9.3.

If you also own the men's soccer book

Four things are the same in both books: the contact dates, the visit rules, the 28-player limit and the signing date. Six things differ. The calendars differ: women have four dead periods, and men have two dead periods and one quiet period. The old Division I limit was 14 for women and 9.9 for men. Division II is 9.9 for women and 9.0 for men. The odds differ: 7.9 percent of high school girls reach an NCAA team, and 5.9 percent of boys do. The women's first game date is earlier: 12 August 2026, against 27 August 2026. And after college, the top women's league has no draft.

Read 11 November 2026. The Division I recruiting calendar for 2026-27. The College Sports Commission table of old and new limits. Division II Manual, Bylaws 15.4.2.1.1 and 15.4.2.1.2. The NCAA Research odds table, updated March 2026. Division I Manual, Figure 17-2. The women's league labor contract, Section 13.8.

Words this book uses

WordWhat it means here
EquivalencyOne full scholarship's worth of money. A coach may cut it into pieces. Division II has 9.9 of them per team. Half a scholarship counts as 0.5.
Roster limitA cap on players, not on money. It is 28 in Division I women's soccer at a settlement school.
Settlement schoolA Division I school in a defendant conference, or one that opted in to the House settlement. House is the name of a court case. A defendant conference is one that was sued in it. This book uses the short name for both kinds.
Dead periodDays when a Division I coach may not meet her or watch her, on campus or off. No visit of either kind may happen. Calls and texts are still allowed. Bylaw 13.02.5.5.
Quiet periodDays when a coach may meet her only on the college campus. No watching or meeting away from campus. Bylaw 13.02.5.4.
Evaluation periodDays when a coach may watch her away from campus but may not talk with her there. Bylaw 13.02.5.2.
Recruiting shutdownDays when no recruiting of any kind is allowed, not even a call. Bylaw 13.02.5.6.
ContactA face-to-face talk that goes past hello.
EvaluationA coach watching her play, or checking her grades, away from campus.
Official visitA campus visit the school pays for.
Unofficial visitA campus visit the family pays for.
Eligibility CenterThe NCAA office that checks her school record and clears her to play. The NAIA has its own.
Core courseA high school class the NCAA has approved. Division I and Division II each ask for 16 of them.
Aid agreementThe school's offer of athletics aid, in writing. It took the place of the National Letter of Intent.
Cost of attendanceThe school's own figure for one year there. One Division I player's aid may not go above it. Bylaw 15.1.
HARDA date that a named document prints.
PROJECTEDA date this book worked out from rule text. Nobody has printed it yet, so it can move. Chapter 3 tags every one.
SERVICEA price or number from a company's own page, not from a rule book.
