What a school may pay, so you do not pay twice

  • Division I official visit. Her travel, and the round trip for up to two family members. Lodging and meals for her and up to four family members. Five tickets to a home game. Up to $60 a person for fun.
  • Division I unofficial visit. Three tickets to a home game, and nothing else. One more thing is allowed: a free meal at an open house for all future students, if the school gives it to everyone.
  • Division II unofficial visit. The school may pay the cost of meals for her and those with her.
  • NAIA tryout. The school may give lodging and meals.
  • Junior college visit. If the school spends any money on the visit, it counts as her one paid visit there that year.

Six ways the money gets wasted

  1. A holiday showcase, bought for Division I eyes. From 16 December to 6 January a Division I coach may not watch. Those events go largely unwatched by Division I staff.
  2. Flights for 1 August. In 2026 that day sat in a dead period. The first open day was 12 August. Wait for the new calendar before you book.
  3. A trip in the last days of July. The calendar closes 28 to 31 July as well.
  4. A camp at a school that is not on her list. A Division I camp must be open to all. An invite does not show that the staff wants her.
  5. A service that sells "28 scholarships." The 28 is a roster limit. No school has to fund one spot of it.
  6. A fee the family could have skipped. Ask about the fee waiver first. And the Profile Page is free.

What this book could not price

No club fee or team tournament price is printed in this book. Tournament entry fees are billed to clubs, not to families. This book found no official public page that gives a cost per family. It did not collect overnight camp prices either. Ask her club for last year's full bill, with travel, before the season starts. That number is the one to plan around.

What an offer is worth

No rule sets a dollar figure. In Division I one player's aid may reach the school's cost of attendance and no higher. In Division II it is a slice of 9.9 for the whole team. In the NAIA the team's aid ceiling for soccer is 12. That is a ceiling on aid, not on players. The same handbook says: "No limit will be placed on the number of student-athletes on any varsity roster." NAIA Official Handbook 2026-2027, printed pages 123 and 121. Division III gives none for sport. So ask every school for the amount in dollars, on paper.
