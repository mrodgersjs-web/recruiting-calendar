10. Ten ways families get soccer wrong

Each line below is something families repeat. Each answer names the rule or the document that says otherwise. The last one has a real answer that most other sports do not have.

  1. "She can call the coach any time, and the coach can pick up." Not in Division I. Staff may not take her call before 15 June after sophomore year. Before 15 June a Division I coach may only send a short reply that does not recruit her. It can explain the rules or point her to admissions. Division I Manual, Bylaws 13.1.3.2.5 and 13.4.1.13.
  2. "She gets five official visits, with three tickets each." Both numbers are off. The 2026-27 manual has no cap on her total visits, only one paid visit per school. And an official visit comes with up to five tickets. Division I Manual, Bylaws 13.6.2.2 and 13.6.7.2.
  3. "Visits open on 1 August before junior year." By rule, yes. But in 2026 that day sat in a dead period for women's soccer. The first open day was 12 August. Division I recruiting calendar for 2026-27.
  4. "The holiday showcase is where Division I coaches shop." From 16 December to 6 January they may not watch or host at all. The same calendar.
  5. "Division I has 28 scholarships now." It has a roster limit of 28 at settlement schools, with no aid cap and no duty to fund a spot. Before 1 July 2025 the limit was 14 equivalencies. The 28 is in Division I Manual, Bylaw 17.2. The old 14 is in the College Sports Commission table.
  6. "Soccer signs the National Letter of Intent in February." That letter is gone. Soccer signs an aid agreement from the second Wednesday in November. NCAA signing dates sheet for 2026-27.
  7. "Division II coaches can start calling on 15 June." They may call, text or email at any time, and have since 1 August 2024. The 15 June date is for meeting in person, paid visits and tryouts. Division II Manual, Bylaws 13.1.3.1 and 13.1.1.1.
  8. "She can tour campus at any age." Only without the athletics staff. In Division I a visit with the athletics staff may not happen before 1 August of junior year. Division I Manual, Bylaw 13.7.1.1.
  9. "Playing with pros ends her eligibility." Not by itself. Before college she may play on a professional team if she takes no more than actual and necessary costs. Signing to play for pay is what ends it. Division I Manual, Bylaws 12.2.2.2.1 and 12.2.4.
  10. "There is no real job in soccer after college." There is one, and its pay is in print. See below.

The pro answer: the National Women's Soccer League

The league and its players signed a labor contract that runs through 2030. It was announced on 22 August 2024. Its cover page and Article 2 give its term as 30 July 2024 to 31 December 2030. Section 8.1 sets the lowest base salary a team may pay, year by year.

  • Rosters hold 22 to 26 players in 2026. From the 2027 season the low end is 23. Labor contract, Article 15, Section 15.1.
  • The draft was abolished. A draft is teams taking turns to pick new players. The league ended its entry draft before the 2025 season. The contract ends the expansion draft too. A college player now picks her club and deals with it herself. Sections 13.8 and 13.9.
  • A contract signed before she turns 18 runs at most three years. That count includes option years, which are extra years written into the deal. For players 18 and older the limit is five. Labor contract, Section 13.7.
  • A team that brings in a girl under 18 as a trialist or an amateur player must give a safety plan. It covers housing, rides, school, adult care, health and her growth as a player. A trialist is a player on a tryout. Article 7A.
  • School costs for a player who signed before 18. The team may pay college tuition of up to $10,000 a year, through the year she turns 22. Side letter on school costs.
  • A girl of 17 or younger comes in by her own route. She and a parent must both agree. One league paper limits a team to four such players. The league's 2023 release said two. Both are printed here, and Chapter 11 says what was not read.
  • There is no top pay for one player. The league said so in its release of 22 August 2024.
  • The cost in college terms. A league contract pays a salary. So it ends her NCAA eligibility in soccer. Division I Manual, Bylaws 12.2.4 and 12.02.7.

Other roads after high school

A second top-level women's league began play in August 2024. It lists eight clubs for its fall 2026 season. This book found no printed pay floor or roster size for it. Moving abroad young is hard: world soccer's rules allow a move between countries only for players over 18, with five listed exceptions. Those rules also cap a contract for a player under 18 at three years. And the NCAA no longer prints the odds of going pro in this sport, because the leagues ended their drafts.
