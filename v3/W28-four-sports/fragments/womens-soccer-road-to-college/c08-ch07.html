7. What the paperwork really means

Four papers matter: the Eligibility Center account, the list of core courses, the offer of aid, and any contract a club puts in front of her. Each one has a date and a price.

She needs the NCAA certification account for three things: a Division I official visit, signing for athletics aid, and playing at a Division I or Division II school. Fee waivers exist for families who qualify. The fee cannot be refunded after 30 days. The NCAA does not start her file until a school asks for it. Most Division III players are cleared by their own campus instead.

The classes

Division I and Division II each ask for 16 core courses. Division I adds two timing rules. She must finish all 16 in eight semesters, counted from the start of ninth grade. And ten of them must be done before her seventh semester, with seven of the ten in English, math or science. Division I also asks for a core-course grade average of at least 2.3. NCAA initial-eligibility brochure, and the NCAA slide deck of March 2026.

Division II asks for a core-course grade average of at least 2.2. NCAA Guide for the College-Bound Student-Athlete 2026-27, page 16. Ask her counselor to check her classes against the NCAA's list each spring.

The offer, and the day she signs

  • The paper. The National Letter of Intent is gone from the NCAA rules. What she signs is the school's written offer of athletics aid. The NCAA also calls it an athletics aid agreement. A school may not use any other letter or signing date. Division I Manual, Bylaws 13.02.13 and 13.9.3.
  • The first day. Soccer signs from 7 a.m. on the second Wednesday in November. This year the NCAA printed 11 November 2026. There is one signing period, and no February date for soccer. Each school sets its own last day. NCAA signing dates sheet for 2026-27.
  • The offer cannot come early. Before 1 August of senior year a Division I school may not send the offer. It may not even say in writing that one is coming. Division I Manual, Bylaw 13.9.3.1.
  • Division III. There is no aid agreement. A school may give her a signing form to celebrate once she is accepted. It does not bind her, and staff may not be there when she signs. Division III Manual, Bylaw 13.9.1.1.
  • NAIA. There is no national letter and no signing date. She may sign a letter with one school, but it does not make her attend. Some NAIA conferences honor letters signed inside the conference. NAIA parents page.
  • Junior college. The letter of intent may be signed from 1 November. It lasts one school year, 1 August to 31 July. From the 2027-28 signing date, paper letters are no longer taken. NJCAA chart and NJCAA compliance page.

All read 11 November 2026. Signing dates do not apply to players who transfer from another four-year school.

What the offer can hold

In Division I her aid may not go above the school's cost of attendance. It may go up for any reason at any time. At a settlement school the offer may come with a second paper about settlement benefits. In Division II her aid is one slice of the team's 9.9. In the NAIA one player's aid stops at cost of attendance too. Division I Manual, Bylaws 15.1, 15.3.5 and 13.02.13.

The line she must not cross with a club

A professional team is one that pays any player more than actual and necessary costs, or that calls itself professional. Before college she may play on such a team and try out for it. She must take no more than those costs. Division I Manual, Bylaws 12.02.7, 12.2.2.2.1 and 12.2.1.1.

What ends it is a deal to play for pay. That holds if it is only spoken. It holds if she signs and the club never does. Division I Manual, Bylaws 12.2.4 and 12.2.4.2.

Two official texts differ on tryouts. The bylaw sets no time limit. The NCAA's own recruiting page says a team may pay for one tryout, and that it cannot run past 48 hours. Both are printed here. Ask the school's compliance office, the staff who check the rules, which one it applies.

Three newer rules

  • The clock. Division I gives five years to play. The clock starts when she first enrolls full time at any college, here or abroad. If she waits, it starts with the school year after her 19th birthday. A gap year spent playing does not stop it. Division I Manual, Bylaws 12.6 and 12.6.1.
  • Agents. New in 2026: a recruit may have an agent to market her to professional teams. This covers players who first enroll on or after 1 August 2026. Division I Manual, Bylaw 12.3.1.
  • Prize money. Before college she may take prize money based on how she placed. Only the event's sponsor may pay it. Division I Manual, Bylaw 12.1.2.4.1.

Ask these in writing

  • Did your school opt in to the settlement for the year I would enroll? Schools choose each year.
  • Is the team under the 28-player limit? How many names will be on the roster you turn in?
  • How much athletics aid is in the offer, in dollars, and for which years?
  • Am I on your school's request list?
  • What is the last day your school will take a signed offer?
