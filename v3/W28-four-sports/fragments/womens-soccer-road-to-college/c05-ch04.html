4. What a coach may and may not do

The rules are written about the coach, not about her. Each level sets its own first days. Women's soccer has no special line in the Division I contact rules. It follows the general rule in each one.

Division I: seven chances a year

Each Division I school gets seven recruiting chances with her each year. A chance is a contact or an evaluation. A contact is a face-to-face talk. An evaluation is a coach watching her play or checking her grades. From 1 August of junior year, no more than three off-campus contacts may be among the seven. A talk with a parent counts as a contact. Talks during a campus visit do not count. Division I Manual, Bylaw 13.1.5.1.

At a tournament

A Division I coach may not talk with her in person before she plays on a game day. The coach may talk with her once her games are done for the day and her own coach lets her go. Division I Manual, Bylaw 13.1.6.2.1. Division III works the same way, and its coaches may not message her at the field. Division III Manual, Bylaw 13.1.3.1.

What a dead period stops, and what it does not

In a dead period a Division I coach may not meet her or watch her, on campus or off. No visit of either kind may happen. The coach may still call, text and email. The four dead periods for women's soccer in 2026-27 are 1 to 11 August, 9 to 12 November, 16 December to 6 January, and 28 to 31 July. A dead period stops for her at one school in two cases. She has signed that school's offer of a place or of aid. Or she has paid her deposit there. Division I Manual, Bylaws 13.02.5.5 and 13.02.5.5.1.

Division II has one dead period for soccer. It is the 48 hours before 7 a.m. on signing day. The words "dead period" do not appear in the Division III recruiting rules at all. Division II Manual, Bylaw 13.02.10. Division III Manual, Bylaw 13.

Two visit numbers the websites get wrong

Tickets. One common recruiting site prints three tickets for an official visit. The bylaw says five, and the bylaw wins. Three is the number for an unofficial visit.

The number of visits. Families still hear "five official visits." The 2026-27 manual prints no cap on the number of official visits a player may take. The limit is one paid visit per school. A school may pay for one more after it hires a new head coach. Division I Manual, Bylaws 13.6.2.2 and 13.6.2.2.1.

Visits at the other levels

  • Division II. A paid visit from 15 June before junior year. One per school, up to 48 hours. On an unofficial visit a Division II school may pay for meals. Division II Manual, Bylaws 13.3.1.1, 13.3.1.2, 13.3.3 and 13.4.2.1.1 to 13.4.2.1.3.
  • Division III. A paid visit from 1 January of junior year. One per school, up to 48 hours. On an unofficial visit the school may give one meal in its dining hall. Division III Manual, Bylaws 13.6.1.1.1, 13.6.2 and 13.7.2.1 to 13.7.2.1.2.
  • NAIA. Tryouts are allowed, and the school may give lodging and meals. Two NAIA pages do not agree on the limit. One says two tryout days at each school. The other, from May 2026, says two days in her whole career. Ask the school which it follows.
  • Junior college. One paid visit per college each recruiting year, up to 48 hours, for her and two guests. NJCAA Bylaws 2026-27, Article VI, Section 4.B.1.

Three more lines worth knowing

  • She may tour any campus at any age on her own. In Division I the athletics staff may not take part before 1 August of junior year. Division I Manual, Bylaw 13.7.1.1.
  • A booster is a fan or donor who backs the team. A Division II booster may not contact her before 15 June before junior year. Division II Manual, Bylaw 13.1.3.1.1.
  • Once she has joined one NAIA school, coaches at other NAIA schools may not contact her first. This holds even before her classes start. NAIA guide for the college-bound student.
