1. Read this first

Read Chapter 2 before you spend any money. Then put the plan in Chapter 3 on one page and cross items off. The rest is for looking things up: what a coach may do, what to send, what the papers mean, and what it all costs.

The main idea

Three things shape this sport, and all three are free to know. The team limit changed on 1 July 2025. The NCAA prints four dead periods, and they set when a Division I coach may watch your daughter. And the rule book sets the first day a coach may write to her, meet her, or host her. A family that knows these dates buys less and wastes less.

Every rule in this book says where it is written. Every number names its document. All of it was read on 11 November 2026, unless a line gives another date. Where this book could not settle a point, Chapter 11 says so.

Four rules that surprise families

  • A Division I coach may not take her call yet. Before 15 June after her sophomore year, a Division I coach may not call, text or email her. The coach may not pick up when she calls, either. The one thing allowed is a short reply that does not recruit her. For the class of 2029 that day is 15 June 2027. This book worked that date out from the rule. Division I Manual, Bylaws 13.1.3.1, 13.1.3.2.5, 13.4.1 and 13.4.1.13.
  • "28 scholarships" is not the rule. Since 1 July 2025 the number 28 is a roster limit at Division I schools that are part of a court settlement. It caps players. It does not promise money to any of them. The old limit was 14 scholarship equivalencies. Chapter 2 tells both stories. The 28 is in Division I Manual, Bylaw 17.2. The old 14 is in the College Sports Commission table.
  • The first visit day can land in a dead period. The rule opens Division I visits on 1 August of junior year. In 2026 that day sat inside a dead period for women's soccer. The first day a coach could meet a girl in person was 12 August. Phone and text stayed open. Division I Manual, Bylaw 13.6.2.1.1, and the printed 2026-27 calendar.
  • There is no National Letter of Intent now. The paper is the school's own offer of aid, in writing. Soccer signs from the second Wednesday in November of senior year, and there is no February date. For her that day works out to 8 November 2028. Division I Manual, Bylaws 13.02.13 and 13.9.3.

If you also own the men's soccer book

Four things are the same in both books: the contact dates, the visit rules, the 28-player limit and the signing date. Six things differ. The calendars differ: women have four dead periods, and men have two dead periods and one quiet period. The old Division I limit was 14 for women and 9.9 for men. Division II is 9.9 for women and 9.0 for men. The odds differ: 7.9 percent of high school girls reach an NCAA team, and 5.9 percent of boys do. The women's first game date is earlier: 12 August 2026, against 27 August 2026. And after college, the top women's league has no draft.

2. How many players a team can take

This chapter sets how long her list should be and what an offer can mean. It starts with one date, 1 July 2025. Before that date the Division I rule was about money. After it, at settlement schools, the rule is about people.

The Division I team limit, told twice

The old rule, before 1 July 2025. A Division I women's soccer team could give 14 scholarship equivalencies. An equivalency is one full scholarship's worth of money. A coach could cut the 14 into pieces and spread them across the team.

The new rule, from 1 July 2025. At a settlement school the limit is 28 players on the roster. The NCAA sets no aid cap for that team. The school decides how much of the roster gets money. It may give full or part scholarships to all 28. It does not have to pay for any spot.

When the limit starts. The 28 limit starts the day after the roster is due. Before that day a team may carry more players in preseason. Ask how many names will be on the roster the school turns in. Division I Manual, Bylaws 17.2.1 and 17.2.2.

What nobody has printed. A settlement school is one in a defendant conference, or one that opted in. There is no published list of the schools outside those conferences that opted in. A school that did not opt in does not have to follow the 28 limit. The school or its conference may set its own roster size. If it gives more aid than the old 14, the settlement rules apply, and so does the 28 limit.

The warning. One recruiting site prints that Division I programs may now "offer 28 scholarships." That site is describing a roster limit, not a budget. Ask each school two things. Did it opt in for the year she would enroll? Schools choose each year. And how many of its players get athletics aid?

Read 11 November 2026. The College Sports Commission table of old scholarship limits and new roster limits lists women's soccer at 14 and 28. Division I Manual 2026-27, Bylaw 17.2 and Bylaw 15. The NCAA settlement question-and-answer, updated 29 May 2026. That last paper was read for this publisher's volleyball book and checked again on 11 November 2026.

Two official counts, and why both are printed

The NCAA projects 349 teams in Division I, 261 in Division II and 416 in Division III for 2026-27. That is 1,026 together. Its report for 2024-25 counted 1,029: 341, then 268, then 420. Both are official. One is a projection and one is a year-end count, for two different years. So this book prints both, with their dates.

The same report gives the average squad. In 2024-25 it was 32.1 players in Division I, 32.2 in Division II and 27.7 in Division III. Look at the Division I figure. It is above the new limit of 28. That report year was the last one before the limit.

How a part scholarship counts

  • Division I, settlement school. A part scholarship counts against no NCAA team total. What counts is the roster spot. Any player who gets athletics aid must be on the roster for the whole school year. The roster is due the day before the first game that counts, or 1 December, whichever comes first. College Sports Commission table.
  • Division II. The school divides her athletics aid by the value of a full grant, which is a full scholarship. So half a scholarship counts as 0.5 of the 9.9. The rule caps the sum, not the number of players who get aid. Division II Manual, Bylaws 15.4.1 and 15.4.2.2.
  • NAIA. Two kinds of aid sit outside the 12. Aid to a student the NAIA calls "academically gifted" can be left out, if she meets the NAIA's academic criteria. The NAIA page read for this book prints no grade or score for that, so ask the school what counts. Aid to a player who does not play in varsity games does not count. NAIA financial aid page.
  • Junior college. In NJCAA Division I the aid may cover tuition, books, fees, room and board, up to $250 in course supplies, and one trip each school year. In NJCAA Division II it stops at tuition, books, fees and the $250. NJCAA Division III gives no athletics aid. NJCAA chart and its page on divisions.
  • One player's ceiling. No Division I player may get more than the school's cost of attendance. Her aid may go up for any reason at any time. Division I Manual, Bylaws 15.1 and 15.3.5.

What the odds really say

In 2024-25, 393,048 girls played high school soccer. That year 31,200 women played NCAA soccer. The NCAA puts the share who reach any NCAA team at 7.9 percent. It puts Division I at 2.8 percent, Division II at 2.2 percent and Division III at 3.0 percent. Each share is rounded, so the three add up to 8.0, not 7.9. NCAA Research table, last updated March 2026.

Read those as a guide, not a promise. The NCAA calls them rough figures, not an exact count. Girls who play only club are not in the high school number. NAIA and junior college spots are not in the college number. Each gap pushes the figure a different way.

Who else wants those spots

College players may give notice that they are leaving in two windows. For Division I women's soccer in 2026-27 they are 23 November to 22 December 2026, and 1 May to 15 May 2027. Spots open and close in those weeks. Ask a coach how many are open after each one. Division I Manual, Figure 13-1.

What is not counted here

This book found no official count of NAIA women's soccer programs. One recruiting service prints 195 SERVICE, and that is a company's number, not the NAIA's. The NAIA's 2026 championship roster listed 40 teams. That is the field for one tournament, not a count of programs. The junior college count has no split by division. No official average squad was found for NAIA or junior college. And no club fee or team tournament price is printed anywhere in this book. Chapter 8 says why.

So how long should her list be?

This is this book's view, not a rule. Build the list across all five levels. Division III has more teams than any other NCAA division. A list made only of Division I names skips most of the map.

Two things these dates do to your money

The August trip. Many guides say visits open on 1 August of junior year. For women's soccer in 2026 that day was inside the 1 to 11 August dead period. The first day a Division I coach could meet a girl in person was 12 August. Phone and text stayed open the whole time. Do not pay for flights until the new calendar is out.

The holiday showcase. From 16 December to 6 January a Division I coach cannot watch or host. So holiday showcases in that window go largely unwatched by Division I staff. If the goal is Division I eyes, that entry fee and that hotel bill buy very little.

Division I: seven chances a year

Each Division I school gets seven recruiting chances with her each year. A chance is a contact or an evaluation. A contact is a face-to-face talk. An evaluation is a coach watching her play or checking her grades. From 1 August of junior year, no more than three off-campus contacts may be among the seven. A talk with a parent counts as a contact. Talks during a campus visit do not count. Division I Manual, Bylaw 13.1.5.1.

At a tournament

A Division I coach may not talk with her in person before she plays on a game day. The coach may talk with her once her games are done for the day and her own coach lets her go. Division I Manual, Bylaw 13.1.6.2.1. Division III works the same way, and its coaches may not message her at the field. Division III Manual, Bylaw 13.1.3.1.

What a dead period stops, and what it does not

In a dead period a Division I coach may not meet her or watch her, on campus or off. No visit of either kind may happen. The coach may still call, text and email. The four dead periods for women's soccer in 2026-27 are 1 to 11 August, 9 to 12 November, 16 December to 6 January, and 28 to 31 July. A dead period stops for her at one school in two cases. She has signed that school's offer of a place or of aid. Or she has paid her deposit there. Division I Manual, Bylaws 13.02.5.5 and 13.02.5.5.1.

Division II has one dead period for soccer. It is the 48 hours before 7 a.m. on signing day. The words "dead period" do not appear in the Division III recruiting rules at all. Division II Manual, Bylaw 13.02.10. Division III Manual, Bylaw 13.

Two visit numbers the websites get wrong

Tickets. One common recruiting site prints three tickets for an official visit. The bylaw says five, and the bylaw wins. Three is the number for an unofficial visit.

The number of visits. Families still hear "five official visits." The 2026-27 manual prints no cap on the number of official visits a player may take. The limit is one paid visit per school. A school may pay for one more after it hires a new head coach. Division I Manual, Bylaws 13.6.2.2 and 13.6.2.2.1.

Visits at the other levels

  • Division II. A paid visit from 15 June before junior year. One per school, up to 48 hours. On an unofficial visit a Division II school may pay for meals. Division II Manual, Bylaws 13.3.1.1, 13.3.1.2, 13.3.3 and 13.4.2.1.1 to 13.4.2.1.3.
  • Division III. A paid visit from 1 January of junior year. One per school, up to 48 hours. On an unofficial visit the school may give one meal in its dining hall. Division III Manual, Bylaws 13.6.1.1.1, 13.6.2 and 13.7.2.1 to 13.7.2.1.2.
  • NAIA. Tryouts are allowed, and the school may give lodging and meals. Two NAIA pages do not agree on the limit. One says two tryout days at each school. The other, from May 2026, says two days in her whole career. Ask the school which it follows.
  • Junior college. One paid visit per college each recruiting year, up to 48 hours, for her and two guests. NJCAA Bylaws 2026-27, Article VI, Section 4.B.1.

Three more lines worth knowing

  • She may tour any campus at any age on her own. In Division I the athletics staff may not take part before 1 August of junior year. Division I Manual, Bylaw 13.7.1.1.
  • A booster is a fan or donor who backs the team. A Division II booster may not contact her before 15 June before junior year. Division II Manual, Bylaw 13.1.3.1.1.
  • Once she has joined one NAIA school, coaches at other NAIA schools may not contact her first. This holds even before her classes start. NAIA guide for the college-bound student.

The classes

Division I and Division II each ask for 16 core courses. Division I adds two timing rules. She must finish all 16 in eight semesters, counted from the start of ninth grade. And ten of them must be done before her seventh semester, with seven of the ten in English, math or science. Division I also asks for a core-course grade average of at least 2.3. NCAA initial-eligibility brochure, and the NCAA slide deck of March 2026.

Division II asks for a core-course grade average of at least 2.2. NCAA Guide for the College-Bound Student-Athlete 2026-27, page 16. Ask her counselor to check her classes against the NCAA's list each spring.

The offer, and the day she signs

  • The paper. The National Letter of Intent is gone from the NCAA rules. What she signs is the school's written offer of athletics aid. The NCAA also calls it an athletics aid agreement. A school may not use any other letter or signing date. Division I Manual, Bylaws 13.02.13 and 13.9.3.
  • The first day. Soccer signs from 7 a.m. on the second Wednesday in November. This year the NCAA printed 11 November 2026. There is one signing period, and no February date for soccer. Each school sets its own last day. NCAA signing dates sheet for 2026-27.
  • The offer cannot come early. Before 1 August of senior year a Division I school may not send the offer. It may not even say in writing that one is coming. Division I Manual, Bylaw 13.9.3.1.
  • Division III. There is no aid agreement. A school may give her a signing form to celebrate once she is accepted. It does not bind her, and staff may not be there when she signs. Division III Manual, Bylaw 13.9.1.1.
  • NAIA. There is no national letter and no signing date. She may sign a letter with one school, but it does not make her attend. Some NAIA conferences honor letters signed inside the conference. NAIA parents page.
  • Junior college. The letter of intent may be signed from 1 November. It lasts one school year, 1 August to 31 July. From the 2027-28 signing date, paper letters are no longer taken. NJCAA chart and NJCAA compliance page.

All read 11 November 2026. Signing dates do not apply to players who transfer from another four-year school.

What the offer can hold

In Division I her aid may not go above the school's cost of attendance. It may go up for any reason at any time. At a settlement school the offer may come with a second paper about settlement benefits. In Division II her aid is one slice of the team's 9.9. In the NAIA one player's aid stops at cost of attendance too. Division I Manual, Bylaws 15.1, 15.3.5 and 13.02.13.

The line she must not cross with a club

A professional team is one that pays any player more than actual and necessary costs, or that calls itself professional. Before college she may play on such a team and try out for it. She must take no more than those costs. Division I Manual, Bylaws 12.02.7, 12.2.2.2.1 and 12.2.1.1.

What ends it is a deal to play for pay. That holds if it is only spoken. It holds if she signs and the club never does. Division I Manual, Bylaws 12.2.4 and 12.2.4.2.

Two official texts differ on tryouts. The bylaw sets no time limit. The NCAA's own recruiting page says a team may pay for one tryout, and that it cannot run past 48 hours. Both are printed here. Ask the school's compliance office, the staff who check the rules, which one it applies.

Three newer rules

  • The clock. Division I gives five years to play. The clock starts when she first enrolls full time at any college, here or abroad. If she waits, it starts with the school year after her 19th birthday. A gap year spent playing does not stop it. Division I Manual, Bylaws 12.6 and 12.6.1.
  • Agents. New in 2026: a recruit may have an agent to market her to professional teams. This covers players who first enroll on or after 1 August 2026. Division I Manual, Bylaw 12.3.1.
  • Prize money. Before college she may take prize money based on how she placed. Only the event's sponsor may pay it. Division I Manual, Bylaw 12.1.2.4.1.

Ask these in writing

  • Did your school opt in to the settlement for the year I would enroll? Schools choose each year.
  • Is the team under the 28-player limit? How many names will be on the roster you turn in?
  • How much athletics aid is in the offer, in dollars, and for which years?
  • Am I on your school's request list?
  • What is the last day your school will take a signed offer?

10. Ten ways families get soccer wrong

Each line below is something families repeat. Each answer names the rule or the document that says otherwise. The last one has a real answer that most other sports do not have.

  1. "She can call the coach any time, and the coach can pick up." Not in Division I. Staff may not take her call before 15 June after sophomore year. Before 15 June a Division I coach may only send a short reply that does not recruit her. It can explain the rules or point her to admissions. Division I Manual, Bylaws 13.1.3.2.5 and 13.4.1.13.
  2. "She gets five official visits, with three tickets each." Both numbers are off. The 2026-27 manual has no cap on her total visits, only one paid visit per school. And an official visit comes with up to five tickets. Division I Manual, Bylaws 13.6.2.2 and 13.6.7.2.
  3. "Visits open on 1 August before junior year." By rule, yes. But in 2026 that day sat in a dead period for women's soccer. The first open day was 12 August. Division I recruiting calendar for 2026-27.
  4. "The holiday showcase is where Division I coaches shop." From 16 December to 6 January they may not watch or host at all. The same calendar.
  5. "Division I has 28 scholarships now." It has a roster limit of 28 at settlement schools, with no aid cap and no duty to fund a spot. Before 1 July 2025 the limit was 14 equivalencies. The 28 is in Division I Manual, Bylaw 17.2. The old 14 is in the College Sports Commission table.
  6. "Soccer signs the National Letter of Intent in February." That letter is gone. Soccer signs an aid agreement from the second Wednesday in November. NCAA signing dates sheet for 2026-27.
  7. "Division II coaches can start calling on 15 June." They may call, text or email at any time, and have since 1 August 2024. The 15 June date is for meeting in person, paid visits and tryouts. Division II Manual, Bylaws 13.1.3.1 and 13.1.1.1.
  8. "She can tour campus at any age." Only without the athletics staff. In Division I a visit with the athletics staff may not happen before 1 August of junior year. Division I Manual, Bylaw 13.7.1.1.
  9. "Playing with pros ends her eligibility." Not by itself. Before college she may play on a professional team if she takes no more than actual and necessary costs. Signing to play for pay is what ends it. Division I Manual, Bylaws 12.2.2.2.1 and 12.2.4.
  10. "There is no real job in soccer after college." There is one, and its pay is in print. See below.
