1. Read this first

Read Chapter 2 before you spend any money. Then put the plan in Chapter 3 on one page and cross items off. The rest is for looking things up: what the rules allow, what to send, what the paper means, and what it costs.

The main idea

Softball does not run on the general recruiting dates. Division I softball has its own contact dates, and they are later than the general rule. It also has its own calendar, and the June part of that calendar moves every year. A family that knows the real dates does not waste a call, a camp fee or a trip.

This book is for the parent of a girl in the class of 2029. She is in 10th grade in 2026-27. Every rule here was read in the rule books on 11 November 2026. Each one carries its bylaw number or its document, so you can check it. Chapter 11 lists every document and its web address.

What makes softball different

  • Its own start date. A Division I softball coach may not call your daughter, send her recruiting email or meet her before 1 p.m. Central time on 1 September of junior year. For her, that day is 1 September 2027. One recruiting site this book read prints 15 June. That is the general rule for other sports. It is not the softball rule.
  • A calendar that moves. The NCAA prints one softball calendar a year. The June dates on it are set from the Women's College World Series. When that event moves, the dates move.
  • A roster number, not a scholarship number. At a Division I school under the House settlement, the limit is now 25 players on the team. The old limit was 12 scholarships. Chapter 2 covers the 25-player limit, and why it is not 25 scholarships.

Proof: Division I Manual 2026-27, Bylaws 13.1.3.1.4, 13.4.1.4, 13.1.1.1.2 and 17.2, and the printed 2026-27 Division I softball recruiting calendar, updated 30 July 2026. Read 11 November 2026.

Three rules that surprise families

  • The coach may not take her call. Before 1 September of junior year, a Division I softball coach may not pick up when she or a parent calls. Silence in 9th or 10th grade is the rule. It is not a no. This is Bylaw 13.1.3.2.8.
  • The first day a coach may call is a day the coach may not meet her. On the printed 2026-27 calendar, 28 August to 3 September is a dead period. A dead period means no meeting in person and no visits. So on 1 September the phone opens and the door stays shut. This is our reading of the rule and the calendar side by side.
  • There is no National Letter of Intent now. Division I ended it on 9 October 2024, and Division II followed that month. The paper is now a written offer of athletics aid. Chapter 7 covers it.

The 25-player limit, and why it is not 25 scholarships

One recruiting site says Division I softball teams are allowed "up to 25 scholarships per year." The rule does not say that. The 25 is a cap on people. The cap on scholarships was taken away at settlement schools. The College Sports Commission prints the softball row of its table like this:

"old scholarship limit 12, new roster limit 25, potential increase in scholarships 13."

The key word is potential. A school may fund 25 players. It may fund far fewer.

Which schools are settlement schools? Every school in the five defendant conferences is one. Any other Division I school may opt in. This book found no public list of the schools that did. So ask each school in writing: "Are you under the 25-player roster limit, or the old 12 scholarships?"

How the 25-player limit works

  • The school turns in its roster by the day before its first game that counts toward the championship, or by 1 December. The earlier date wins.
  • After that, only listed players may take part in team activities.
  • Anyone who gets an athletics scholarship or settlement payments must be on the list.

How many times a Division I coach may see her

  • Each school gets seven recruiting chances per player per year. A meeting counts as one. A trip to watch her counts as one. The rule's word is "opportunities."
  • From 1 September of junior year, the seven may hold no more than three off-campus meetings a year. Meeting her family counts too.
  • A meeting during an official or unofficial visit does not count.
  • Trips to watch her from 1 June through 31 August do not count against the seven. Trips from 1 September through 31 May do, unless a meeting happens the same day.
  • Any number of meetings in one day counts as one.
  • A school may send only four off-campus recruiters out on any one day.

Before a Division I paid visit

Three things must be done first. This is Bylaw 13.6.3.

  1. She sends the school a transcript.
  2. She is registered with the NCAA Eligibility Center.
  3. She is on the school's request list.

The rule book calls it the institutional request list. It is the school's own list of the players it is recruiting. Ask the coach to add her. Being on the list is not an offer, and it does not commit her to that school.

Four more rules about visits
