## B. What parents WANT (outcomes, not problems)
1. A step-by-step plan for their kid's grade and sport, in plain words (top ask across TikTok).
2. Money spent only where it moves the needle (camps, club, services).
3. Their athlete to drive outreach, with the parent as a quiet project manager (TikTok tip praised: keep a to-do list, do periodic check-ins, stop nagging).
4. Proof a coach's interest is real; a realistic level (D1/D2/D3/NAIA/JUCO).
5. Free/low-cost: many ask "are these tips being sold?" and "what's free?"; trust is lost the moment a pitch appears.
6. Scholarship to make college affordable (parents with scholarship kids say it made the choice easier).

## C. What competitors promise parents (Facebook Ad Library, US, read 2026-11-06)
- **NCSA** ran ~31 of ~70 ads in 2 searches: "largest recruiting service", "300,000 athletes helped", free profile, free Camp Search Tool by sport, "Attention parents" guidance ads. Their FB parent group is dormant (0 posts/month).
- **Richie Contartesi** (football): ~19 ads, "Earn a College Football Scholarship" / "Get a Football Scholarship Offer".
- A football service page: "the window is smaller than you think... our team sends the emails and the X DMs to coaches" (done-for-you outreach).
- Mendoza Baseball Academy: free "How to get recruited" workshop. Lead-magnet pattern everywhere: "comment PARENT for the survival guide", "comment HELP for my 30-page playbook".
- Implication: nobody advertises a bylaw-cited calendar or "no-pitch" DIY. Our differentiator is honesty + rules, and the anti-service sentiment (problem 3) is the wedge.

## D. Comparison with W1 DEMAND.md (2026-11-05, Reddit-heavy)
- **Confirmed**: service-worth-it/scam (DEMAND #1) is top-3 here too, now from TikTok/X/YouTube, with new detail (aggressive calls, pitching the 8th grader, cancel requests). Camps worth it (#2), too early/too late (#8), coach email (#7), video how-to (#6), parent role (#9) all reappear.
- **New**: (a) "where do I start / give me steps" is the #1 TikTok signal, barely present on Reddit; TikTok parents are earlier and less informed than Reddit parents. (b) NCAA ID / Eligibility Center registration confusion. (c) HS/club coach won't help. (d) Single-parent/first-gen framing. (e) Emerging sports (women's wrestling, girls flag). (f) Facebook groups' own rules reveal needs: swim group bans "what schools for these times" and Renee Lopez's 270K group closes comments and bans school talk, so parents can't get the specific help they want there.
- **Contradicted / shifted**: DEMAND ranked "what level can my kid play" #3; in this sample it is lower (~5) and replaced by "too late?". DEMAND found little women's basketball; still thin here. One counter-signal: a minority of parents say "if your kid is good, coaches will find you" and a few still recommend NCSA for finding D2/D3/NAIA lists, so not every parent sees services as a scam.
