1. Read this first

Read Chapter 2 before you spend any money. Then put the plan in Chapter 3 on one page and cross items off. The rest is for reference: what the rules allow, what to send, what good video looks like, and what it costs.

The main idea

A family that knows the roster rules, the calendar, and the video standard competes with families spending three times as much. Most of what a $2,000 service sells is phone calls and follow-ups. The rules, the dates, and the right questions are free, and almost nobody writes them down.

What makes volleyball different from basketball and football

Three things move the money and the calendar. First, club is the showcase. In the NCAA's own 2025 survey, 94 percent of college volleyball players played both high school and club, 3 percent played high school only, and 2 percent played club only. High school ball in Wisconsin is a fall sport, and college coaches are playing their own season in the fall. Second, the number a family needs is a roster number, not a scholarship number. Third, the measurable that matters most is an approach touch, and no college publishes a standard for it. Every jump chart a family buys is one company's target.

A Texas State assistant coach wrote in the AVCA's own blog that her staff faced "over 150 club tournaments between Jan. 1 and Aug. 1 alone." A former Division I head coach, writing on his own site, put it harder: "club volleyball is 99% of our recruiting." He said his only thoughts about his players during the high school season were free touches on the ball, and please do not get hurt.

Three rules that surprise families

  • She may write today; the coach may not answer until 15 June after her sophomore year. For the class of 2029 that date is 15 June 2027. USA Volleyball states both halves plainly: no rule stops a recruit from emailing, texting, messaging or calling, and Division I and II coaches cannot reply directly until that day.
  • The old 12-scholarship rule is gone at most big schools, and it did not become "unlimited." Since 1 July 2025, schools in the five defendant conferences and any school that opted in carry an 18-player roster limit instead of a scholarship limit, and aid may be any size on any of those 18. Schools that did not opt in work under the old aid value. There is no printed list of who opted in.
  • There is no National Letter of Intent any more. The NCAA ended it in October 2024. The paper is now a written offer of athletics aid, signed from the second Wednesday in November of 12th grade. For her, that lands on Wednesday 8 November 2028, worked out from the rule. USA Volleyball's own recruiting page still says "National Letter of Intent." The rule book moved and the guides did not.

Words this book uses

WordWhat it means here
Head countThe old Division I rule: 12 scholarships, and each one took one whole spot no matter how small the aid. Women's volleyball used it until 1 July 2025.
EquivalencyA money ceiling the staff may cut into pieces. Division II is 8.0 and NAIA is 8. A "quarter" is one athlete's worth of money, not one athlete.
Roster limitA cap on people, not money. Division I is 18 for women's volleyball at a school that opted in. A conference may set a lower number, but not below 12.
Opted-in schoolA Division I school that chose the settlement rules. The choice covers every sport at that school and is redone each year for ten years. This book says "opted in" instead of the NCAA's phrase "Participating Institution."
Standing reachHow high her hand gets with her feet flat on the floor, shoes on. It is the starting line for every jump number.
Approach touchHow high her hitting hand touches after a real approach. Coaches say "touch" or "attack jump." It is not the same as a vertical.
VerticalTouch minus reach: how high she got off the floor. Two players can touch the same height with very different verticals. Ask which number a chart shows.
Hitting percentage(kills minus errors) divided by swings. A middle who only gets easy quick sets can post a big one. A .250 or better is the line one service uses for a good Division I season.
Reception errorA serve she did not handle. It is the number a libero is really judged on, and the NCAA does not rank it nationally. Ask a coach for the count by match.
Perfect passA pass that reaches the setter's target. Some coaches score 0 to 3, some 0 to 4, so "2.5" means nothing without the scale. A USC coach on the record looked for 62 to 66 percent perfect.
BidA place at a national qualifier. Some bids come only from a national qualifier, and National, American and Freedom bids can come from her own region's qualifier.
Aid agreementThe written offer that replaced the letter of intent. One school, and the others must stop recruiting her once she signs.
Core-course GPAThe Grade Point Average the NCAA builds from its own 16 approved subjects, always on a 4.0 scale. Floors: 2.3 for Division I, 2.2 for Division II. It can differ from her school's GPA.
PROJECTEDA date this book worked out from rule text the NCAA has not printed yet for the 2028-29 year. Chapter 3 marks every one.
