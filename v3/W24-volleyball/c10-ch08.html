The money line for this sport

The gap between a $4,350 national-circuit fee and a $1,450 local-league fee is $2,900. That is about 14 satellite camps at $200, or about nine of the same school's prep camp, or roughly 50 one-evening skills camps at $60.

A camp plus an edit costs $255 at the low end and $760 at the high, which is 6 to 17 percent of one club fee. A top-tier service at $1,320 to $4,200 costs more than the camp and the edit combined, several times over, and in this research not one college coach said on the record that a paid tier moved a volleyball recruit. What a coach did say on the record is that film, a schedule and a free profile are what get used.

Name, image and likeness: what the money really is

Do not plan a budget around this. A 2026 industry report puts the average women's volleyball deal at $663, next to $2,500 for women's basketball and $5,900 for football. A 2024 report from the same company estimated a Division I women's volleyball player's expected annual value at $5,868 and found the sport took 2.9 percent of commercial deal dollars and 0.8 percent of collective dollars. The NCAA's own NIL database for 2023-24, reported through a national outlet, put volleyball third among Power Four women's sports in reported deals at 15.2 percent, behind basketball and softball, with Power Four women's athletes averaging $7,947 in reported earnings.

Two rules matter for a Wisconsin 10th grader. The state high school association lets a student athlete do endorsements, but not using her school's name, uniform or logo, not paid for performance, and not through an agent. On the NCAA side, deals of $600 or more must be reported, counted from the start of 11th grade. So a local car-dealer deal is fine and a school-Logo deal is not. Ask before she signs anything with a school's colors on it, and keep the receipts; the eligibility review asks about teams, events, expenses, awards and anyone who marketed her.

The free things, in the order they pay off

  1. Her own email, written by her, with a link that needs no login. Free, and it is the only thing a coach is required to read.
  2. A free profile on the platform staffs use at tournaments, so a coach can find her schedule and her grades without asking.
  3. The school's own recruit questionnaire. Both Wisconsin and Marquette post one, and it puts her on a list a staff actually maintains.
  4. The free Eligibility Center profile page, which she can upgrade later.
  5. A parent with a tripod, high and wide, at the end line, and a folder for every email a coach sends back.
