4. What a coach may and may not do

Checked 11 November 2026 against USA Volleyball's recruiting timeline and the printed 2026-27 Division I calendar for this sport.

In this sport the rule is not "no contact." It is "she may talk, and the coach may not answer for two more years." Almost every confused email a staff gets comes from a family that learned the rule backwards.

Contact periods and dead periods

The printed 2026-27 Division I calendar for this sport shows a contact period from 1 September to 30 November and a dead period from 9 to 12 November 2026, which is the week of this year's signing date. In a contact period a staff may talk to her face to face on campus. In a dead period it may not talk to her at all, in person or on the phone. The calendar for her own signing week is not printed yet. The rule text is stable; the dates move a day or two a year.

The camp rule, with a flag on it

The coaches' association said in April 2022 that a Division I volleyball camp may only run between Memorial Day and 31 July, and that "recruiting conversations during an institutional camp or clinic are not permitted between an institution's coach and a participating individual before August 1 at the beginning of the individual's junior year." A campus tour for all campers is allowed. This book could not re-check that against the 2026-27 rule book. Assume the talk is off limits at a summer 2027 camp until 1 August 2027, even though phone and email open on 15 June 2027, and ask the director at the front desk.

Three limits on how hard a staff may push

  • A signed aid agreement with one school makes every other school that offers athletics aid stop recruiting her and her family. To talk to another school she must first get a release from the one she signed.
  • Once a school's roster is submitted, its limit applies. A staff that promises a spot past its own limit is promising something it may not be able to deliver that fall.
  • Anything a school gives her above tuition and fees is now a separate contract in most programs. Read it on its own, and ask what happens to it if she is hurt or if the coach leaves.

What your family must not do

  • Do not sign two junior college letters of intent. It costs a year of ineligibility in that association.
  • Do not hire an agent for a Wisconsin high school athlete. The state association lets her do endorsements, but not with the school's name, uniform or logo, not paid for performance, and not through an agent.
  • Do not let a parent send the email and stay silent. Coaches say they want the player's own words, and one Division III coach wrote that "the VAST majority of recruits emailing are not recruits we'd pursue."
  • Do not treat the division's rule as the school's rule. A conference may set a lower roster limit than 18, and each school decides its own aid.
