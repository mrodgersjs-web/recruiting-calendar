Rule: three kinds of number are kept apart: roster data [DATA], published service target [SERVICE], coach quote [COACH].


## 0. How to read this file (grade 8 plain words)
- [DATA] = real numbers off a 2026 college roster page. Rosters are listed by the school itself, so heights are "as listed."
- [SERVICE] = a recruiting company or club page saying "this is what a player needs." These are marketing guides, not rules.
- [COACH] = a named coach saying it on the record (see section 8).
- [OFFICIAL] = a league, hospital, journal, or government page.
- No college has a published height or jump cutoff. Any "requirement" below is a service target or a roster pattern, not a rule.

## 3. Do roster heights look generous?
- [UNCONFIRMED] No school I read says how it measures. I found no official source that says shoes on or off, or measured versus self-reported. So "listed height" is a claim by the school. Checked 2026-11-11
- [DATA] Pile-up test: I counted every listed height in the 11 rosters above (212 players with a height). Counts: 5'8" 13, 5'9" 14, 5'10" 21, 5'11" 23, 6'0" 21, 6'1" 28, 6'2" 17, 6'3" 16. No obvious pile-up at 6'0" in that small sample. Source: roster links in section 2. Checked 2026-11-11

Source: https://ncaa-api.henrygd.me/stats/volleyball-women/d1/current/individual/2 (and /d2/, /d3/, ids 1, 3, 42, 44; a mirror of ncaa.com/stats) Checked 2026-11-11

D2 and D3 show a smaller dip at 5'11" and no clear bump. Treat D1 listings as "about", maybe an inch generous at the 5'11"/6'0" line. This is my read of the counts. Checked 2026-11-11

- [FOLKLORE] A 2010 fan-forum post says D1 programs round 5'10"-5'11" players up to 6'0" and another poster says heights are "in shoes" and "depend on the coach." Anonymous, no data. Source: https://volleytalk.proboards.com/thread/31295/height-measurements-division-1-teams Checked 2026-11-11

- Plain advice for the reader: measure barefoot, to the nearest half inch, with a parent writing it down. If a coach's roster says 6'0" and you are 5'11" in socks, you are not "short." Keep your own number honest.

### 5d. What each stat cannot tell a coach (my plain-words read, grounded in the sources above)
- Kills per set: depends on how many sets she plays, how many balls her setter gives her, and the level of the opponent. A 4.0 in a weak league is not a 4.0 in the Big Ten.
- Hitting percentage: (kills minus errors) divided by attempts. A middle who only gets easy quick sets looks great (see the middle-heavy lists above). It says nothing about hitting a bad pass out of system.
- Assists per set: tracks the offense, not the setter alone. A setter on a team that runs a lot of fast offense can post big numbers with weak ball location.
- Digs per set: a libero on a team that gets hit at a lot digs more. Haley and Rose both say digs alone are weak; Haley wants dig-to-kill, Rose wants to know why you missed the other digs. (Same Coaches Insider URL.)
- Aces per set: high aces usually come with high serve errors. Wisconsin's team has 1.20 aces and 2.37 errors per set. A coach wants the ace-to-error balance, not aces alone.
- Reception errors: the stat a libero is judged on, yet no national page ranks it. Ask the high school or club for a count by match.
- All of these: a stat guide shows what happened, not what she can become. Dana Hallisey (Montana associate head coach) writes that coaches are "watching how you move" more than counting kills. See section 8.

## 6. How a vertical is measured, and why two numbers may not match
### 6a. Words first
- [SERVICE] "Vertical" and "touch" are not the same. Vertical = how high you get off the floor. Touch = how high your hand reaches at the top. Example from the source: a 7'0" reach touching 9'0" has a 24" vertical; a 7'6" reach touching 9'0" has an 18" vertical. Same touch, different vertical. Source: https://jvavolleyball.org/3-methods-to-track-the-vertical-jump (written by a sports-performance trainer, David Hardy) Checked 2026-11-11
- [OFFICIAL] Standing reach is measured with shoes on and feet flat; on a Vertec it is recorded to the nearest half inch. Source (Volleyball Alberta testing manual, Oct 2025): https://www.volleyballalberta.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Volleyball-Alberta-Physical-Testing-Protocols-Manual-October-2025.pdf Checked 2026-11-11
- [OFFICIAL] Spike touch (approach jump): full spike approach, athlete takes off under the Vertec, taps the highest vane with the hitting hand; at least 3 tries, testing ends after 2 misses in a row; vertical = touch minus standing reach. Source (Team Ontario Volleyball): https://teamontariovolleyball.org/physical-testing Checked 2026-11-11

### 6b. Tools, from cheapest to most exact
- Chalk or tape on a wall: cheap, standing jump mostly; for an approach you must be careful not to hit the wall, which changes form. [SERVICE] Source: https://jvavolleyball.org/3-methods-to-track-the-vertical-jump Checked 2026-11-11
- Gym jump tester (Vertec / "slap sticks"): the most common field tool; same source says $350 to over $1,000, and markings can be off so check with a tape. Checked 2026-11-11
- Wearable jump monitor (Vert): easy, but the same trainer warns form and swing timing "could fool the device." Checked 2026-11-11
- Lab force plate: some researchers call force-plate flight time or take-off velocity the gold standard (others say 3D motion capture); the 2025 study below says no system is perfectly accurate. In a study of 13 female varsity volleyball players, jump height from force-plate velocity had the best precision (r=0.90 to 3D video, standard error 0.02 m). Source: https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/5922 Checked 2026-11-11
- [DATA] Systems do not match each other. A 2025 study of 16 elite boys (16-18) compared 7 systems and says "care should be taken when different measurement systems are used alongside one another." Source: https://journal.iusca.org/index.php/Journal/article/download/359/478 Checked 2026-11-11
- [DATA] A Vert wearable read about 5.5 cm (about 2.2 inches) higher than reference tests across volleyball jumps in pro men. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29315832 Checked 2026-11-11
- [DATA] In 128 Brazilian U18 players, attack jump averaged 70.9 cm on a Vertec and 76.3 cm on a Vert (a gap of about 2.1 inches); block jump 53.7 vs 58.5 cm. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29158616 Checked 2026-11-11

### 6c. What makes two numbers NOT comparable (checklist)
- Touch vs vertical (see 6a). Standing vs approach vs block jump. Shoes on vs off. Reach measured the same way both times. Two-hand touch (block) vs one-hand touch (attack). 3-step vs 4-step approach. Device type (chalk, Vertec, wearable, force plate). Number of tries and who watched. Time of day and warm-up. These all follow from the protocols above (my synthesis). Checked 2026-11-11
- [UNCONFIRMED] The NCSA table does not add up by itself: for outside hitters it lists reach 7'9" plus vertical 19.9" (about 9'4.9") but attack jump 9'1" and block jump 9'1". So the columns come from different samples or test types. Treat the NCSA numbers as rough. Source: https://www.ncsasports.org/womens-volleyball/recruiting-guidelines Checked 2026-11-11

## 7. Growth: how much taller will she get?
Assumption: class of 2029 means she is a sophomore this school year (about age 15-16). I did not find her age or period date, so I give ranges only.
- [OFFICIAL] Fastest growth in girls is usually between when breast buds start and about 6 months before the first period. After the first period, most girls grow another 1-2 inches; more than that is less common. Source (American Academy of Pediatrics): https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/gradeschool/puberty/Pages/Physical-Development-Girls-What-to-Expect.aspx Checked 2026-11-11
- [OFFICIAL] Nemours KidsHealth: once girls start to menstruate they usually grow about 1 or 2 more inches, reaching final adult height by about age 14 or 15 (younger or older depending on when puberty began). Source: https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/growth-13-to-18.html Checked 2026-11-11
- [OFFICIAL] Hospital for Special Surgery (sports-medicine hospital): girls generally stop growing and reach maximum height between ages 14 and 16 (boys 16-18). - [OFFICIAL] Johns Hopkins Medicine: girls grow an average of about 2-3 inches after the first period; peak growth is typically 6-12 months before it. Source: https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/health/wellness-and-prevention/what-is-a-growth-spurt-during-puberty Checked 2026-11-11
- [OFFICIAL] Merck Manual (pediatrics): girls' growth spurt runs about age 9.5 to 13.5, peak usually 11 to 12.5, gaining up to 9 cm (about 3.5 inches) in the peak year. Source: https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/growth-and-development/physical-growth-and-sexual-maturation-of-adolescents Checked 2026-11-11
- [OFFICIAL] Duke Health pediatric orthopaedic surgeon: females stop growing around 13 to 15; growth plates close near the end of puberty. Source: https://www.dukehealth.org/blog/growth-plates-what-you-need-know Checked 2026-11-11
