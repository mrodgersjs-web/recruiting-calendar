# 06. Paperwork and the boys lane (research notes)

Lane: NCAA/NAIA/NJCAA paperwork, Division 3 offers, what the House settlement means for the paper a 2029 recruit signs, and men's volleyball as its own road.
Reader: a Wisconsin girl in the class of 2029 (grade 10 now, Nov 2026) and her parent. Written for a grade-8 reading level.
Research date: 2026-11-11. Every claim below carries a source URL, the line "Checked 2026-11-11", and one tag: [OFFICIAL] [COACH] [SERVICE] [DATA] [FOLKLORE] [UNCONFIRMED].
Timeline note (my arithmetic, not a source claim): class of 2029 started 9th grade in fall 2025, finishes 8th semester in spring 2029, and would enroll full time in fall 2029. Her 7th semester starts fall 2028.

## 1. NCAA Eligibility Center (Division 1 and Division 2)

Source for items 1.1 to 1.14 unless noted: NCAA "Guide for the College-Bound Student-Athlete 2026-27" (August 2026), https://s3.amazonaws.com/fs.ncaa.org/Docs/eligibility_center/Student_Resources/CBSA.pdf

1.1 [OFFICIAL] The NCAA says to register with the Eligibility Center "before ninth grade." Our reader is already past that point, so the plain advice is: register now. Source: CBSA.pdf (above), "Registration Checklist." Checked 2026-11-11
1.2 [OFFICIAL] Three account types: Academic and Athletics Certification (for Division 1 or 2), Athletics Certification (mainly international Division 3 or some transfers), and a free Profile Page (not yet recruited, undecided, or a domestic Division 3 plan). Source: CBSA.pdf. Checked 2026-11-11
1.3 [OFFICIAL] The Certification account, with payment or a processed fee waiver, is needed to take Division 1 official visits, sign an athletics aid agreement, and compete at a Division 1 or 2 school. A Profile Page is free and you move it up to Certification once a Division 1 or 2 school recruits you. Source: CBSA.pdf; also https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/register . Checked 2026-11-11
1.4 [OFFICIAL] Walk-ons and "preferred walk-ons" at Division 1 and 2 must also register and meet the academic rules. Source: CBSA.pdf; https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/register . Checked 2026-11-11
1.5 [OFFICIAL] Fee on NCAA's live sign-up page: $120 for a U.S. student and $180 for an international student (Certification account). Fees are not refundable after 30 days. Source: https://web3.ncaa.org/ecwr3/register/CERTIFICATION . Checked 2026-11-11  (See "Could not settle": older NCAA PDFs still say $110.)
1.6 [OFFICIAL] The Athletics Certification account is $75 for all students, per the Eligibility Center home page. Source: http://eligibilitycenter.org/ . Checked 2026-11-11
1.7 [OFFICIAL] Fee waivers exist for families who qualify. Source: https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/register (page says "payment or fee waiver"). Checked 2026-11-11
1.8 [OFFICIAL] Division 1 core courses: 16 total. English 4, math 3 (Algebra I or higher), science 2, one extra year of English, math or science 1, social science 2, "additional" 4. Source: CBSA.pdf p.14 and https://counselors.collegeboard.org/counseling/prepare/athletes/ncaa-course-work [SERVICE for College Board copy]. Checked 2026-11-11
1.9 [OFFICIAL] Division 2 core courses: 16 total. English 3, math 2, science 2, extra English/math/science 3, social science 2, additional 4. Source: CBSA.pdf p.16. Checked 2026-11-11
1.10 [OFFICIAL] The 10-and-7 rule (Division 1 only): finish 10 of the 16 core courses, with 7 of them in English, math or science, before the start of the seventh semester (fall of senior year). Before the seventh semester starts, a needed course can be repeated for a higher grade; after it starts, it cannot be replaced. Source: CBSA.pdf p.14; http://fs.ncaa.org/Docs/eligibility_center/Student_Resources/DI_ReqsFactSheet.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11
1.11 [OFFICIAL] The 16 Division 1 core courses must be done in eight semesters (four straight years) from the start of ninth grade. One extra core unit taken after on-time graduation (within a year, before full-time college) may count for Division 1, but it cannot replace a course used for 10-and-7. Source: CBSA.pdf pp.13-14. Checked 2026-11-11
1.12 [OFFICIAL] Minimum core-course GPA: 2.3 for Division 1, 2.2 for Division 2. Only NCAA-approved core courses count, plus and minus grades are not used (B+, B and B- are all 3 points), and the core GPA can differ from the school transcript GPA. Source: CBSA.pdf pp.12, 14, 16; also https://ncaa.egain.cloud/kb/EligibilityHelp/content/KB-4137/Do-I-need-to-submit-a-standardized-test-score-to-the-NCAA-Eligibility-Center?query=test%20score . Checked 2026-11-11
1.13 [OFFICIAL] Only courses on the high school's own NCAA-approved core-course list count. The list is on the Eligibility Center site and the counselor manages it. Source: CBSA.pdf p.9-10. Checked 2026-11-11
1.14 [OFFICIAL] Test scores: Divisions 1 and 2 removed SAT/ACT from initial eligibility in January 2023. It applies to athletes who first enroll full time on or after 1 August 2023, so it covers the class of 2029. A college can still ask for scores for admission or scholarships. Source: https://ncaa.egain.cloud/kb/EligibilityHelp/content/KB-4137/Do-I-need-to-submit-a-standardized-test-score-to-the-NCAA-Eligibility-Center?query=test%20score ; legislation record https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/search/proposalView?id=106367 ; CBSA.pdf p.12. Checked 2026-11-11

## 2. What the counselor uploads, when, and the amateurism (athletics) check

Source for 2.1 to 2.9 unless noted: CBSA.pdf, https://s3.amazonaws.com/fs.ncaa.org/Docs/eligibility_center/Student_Resources/CBSA.pdf

2.1 [OFFICIAL] The counselor uploads the official transcript after 2 and after 3 school years (4 and 6 semesters), then the final transcript with proof of graduation after she finishes. For our reader that is about June 2027, June 2028, and after graduation in 2029. (Dates are my arithmetic from the NCAA rule.) Source: CBSA.pdf p.11. Checked 2026-11-11
2.2 [OFFICIAL] If she attends more than one high school or program, each one must send its own official transcript. Her school must be added in the Education section of her account before the counselor can upload. Source: CBSA.pdf p.11. Checked 2026-11-11
2.3 [OFFICIAL] The NCAA's own timeline says "ask your high school counselor to upload your official high school transcript at the end of the school year" in 9th and 10th grade, and the final one after graduation. Source: CBSA.pdf p.5. Checked 2026-11-11
2.4 [OFFICIAL] Counselor's job in the NCAA's words: advise on course choices, share the rules, and submit transcripts. "Meeting initial-eligibility requirements is the student's responsibility." Source: http://fs.ncaa.org/Docs/eligibility_center/HS/HS_Counselor_Guide.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11
2.5 [OFFICIAL] Amateurism is now called "athletics certification." Sign-up asks about teams, events, expenses, awards, and anyone who advised her or marketed her skills. The review looks at things like playing with pros, pro contracts, pay or perks for playing, prize money, and written agent agreements. Source: CBSA.pdf pp.7, 19. Checked 2026-11-11
2.6 [OFFICIAL] She must press "request final athletics certification" herself, for each sport. Fall enrollees can ask from 1 April before enrolling. For our reader that is 1 April 2029 (my arithmetic). The NCAA says to ask early and keep finishing other tasks. Source: CBSA.pdf p.19. Checked 2026-11-11
2.7 [OFFICIAL] Certification is not admission. She must also apply to and be admitted by the school. Source: CBSA.pdf p.23. Checked 2026-11-11
2.8 [OFFICIAL] Her NCAA ID (ten digits, top right of the account) goes in emails to coaches so each school can add her to its "institutional request list." Source: CBSA.pdf pp.5, 22, 26. Checked 2026-11-11
2.9 [OFFICIAL] Division 1 results: Qualifier (can practice, play and get aid), Academic Redshirt (aid and practice but no games in year one), Nonqualifier (none of those). Division 2: Qualifier or Partial Qualifier (aid and practice but no games in year one). Source: CBSA.pdf pp.15-16. Checked 2026-11-11
2.10 [UNCONFIRMED] A recruiting service says that from 2026-27 every Division 3 athlete must get an NCAA ID number (https://help.sportsrecruits.com/hc/en-us/articles/360025480672-NCAA-Eligibility-Number). I could not confirm it on NCAA pages. What NCAA does say [OFFICIAL]: Division 3 schools certify domestic athletes on campus; only international Division 3 athletes go through the NCAA. See "Could not settle." Source: CBSA.pdf p.3 and p.8. Checked 2026-11-11

## 3. New 5-year eligibility clock (affects the junior-college question)

3.1 [OFFICIAL] Division 1 (June 2026) and Division 2 (August 2026) adopted an age-based model: up to five years of eligibility if the athlete enrolls full time no later than the academic year after her 19th birthday. Source: https://www.ncaa.org/news/division-i-adopts-age-based-eligibility-model and https://www.ncaa.org/division-ii-adopts-age-based-eligibility-model . Checked 2026-11-11
3.2 [OFFICIAL] The five-year clock starts at the earlier of first full-time enrollment at any college, "including ... two-year college[s]," or the school year after the 19th birthday. It runs without pause: it does not stop if she transfers or sits out. Source: https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/division-i-and-division-ii-age-based-eligibility-rules/ . Checked 2026-11-11
3.3 [OFFICIAL] Students who first enroll full time in fall 2027 or later fall under the age-based rule only. Our reader (fall 2029) is in that group. (Group match is my reading of the NCAA table.) Source: same NCAA page as 3.2. Checked 2026-11-11
3.4 [OFFICIAL] The NCAA says the rule is not "5 for 5" and "does not guarantee five years for all." Redshirt rules, seasons of competition and most waivers are eliminated. Source: same NCAA page as 3.2. Checked 2026-11-11
3.5 [OFFICIAL] The Division 2 10-semester/15-quarter limit no longer applies. Source: same NCAA page as 3.2. Checked 2026-11-11


## 4. NAIA and NJCAA

4.1 [OFFICIAL] NAIA has its own Eligibility Center at PlayNAIA.org. Fee: $110 for a U.S. high-school graduate, $145 freshman after a break, $160 college/transfer, $170 international. One-time and not refundable. Waived for U.S. students with demonstrated need. Source: https://www.naia.org/why-naia/eligibility-center and https://www.naia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/naia_guide_college_bound_student-1.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11
4.2 [OFFICIAL] NAIA minimum: graduate with a 2.3+ GPA, or meet two of three: ACT 18 or SAT 970, overall GPA 2.0, or top half of the class. So unlike the NCAA, a test score can still help at NAIA. Source: https://www.naia.org/why-naia/eligibility-center ; the 2026 guide above. Checked 2026-11-11
4.3 [OFFICIAL] Scores go straight from the testing company to NAIA (code 9876 for SAT and ACT). The high school sends transcript, rank and GPA through the NAIA High School Portal. Source: https://www.naia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/naia_eligibilitycenter_membershipguide.pdf ; the 2026 guide above. Checked 2026-11-11
4.4 [OFFICIAL] NAIA has no letter-of-intent program. A student may sign one with a single school but is "not obligated to attend." Some NAIA conferences make their schools honor each other's letters. Source: https://www.naia.org/student-athletes/prospective/high-school-students/ . Checked 2026-11-11
4.5 [OFFICIAL] NAIA's national office says it "will not force a school to honor a financial aid agreement" and will not make a student stay if she signed an intent letter. It "strongly" recommends reading those papers closely. This is the key NAIA warning on lost aid. Source: https://interpretations.naia.org/implications-of-letters-of-intent . Checked 2026-11-11
4.6 [OFFICIAL] NAIA athletes must be admitted under standards equal to or higher than the general student body. Source: https://www.naia.org/student-athletes/prospective/parents/ . Checked 2026-11-11
4.7 [OFFICIAL] NJCAA entrance rule: high school diploma, state-approved GED, or state equivalency test; full-time means 12 or more credit hours. Source: NJCAA Bylaws (5-4-26), https://media.ardorsportshub.com/media/404933495157163011/imported/njcaa/governance/handbook/2025-26_Documents/NJCAA_Handbook_-_Bylaws_-_5-4-26.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11
4.8 [OFFICIAL] The NJCAA Letter of Intent and Scholarship Agreement is binding and lasts one academic year (1 August to 31 July). It cannot be awarded until the start of senior year. The signer may not play for another school while it runs, with exceptions (not admitted, completed at least one academic year, or a signed release). Source: https://media.ardorsportshub.com/_assets/site-documents/186e6234b27d1be2-186246398_diii_njcaa_loi_26-27.pdf ; https://media.ardorsportshub.com/media/404933495157163011/imported/njcaa/governance/handbook/2025-26_Documents/2026-2027_Sports_Procedures_Chart_-_4-6-26.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11
4.9 [OFFICIAL] NJCAA Division 1 colleges may give full scholarships (tuition, books, fees, room and board, up to $250 course supplies, one trip's travel). Division 2 colleges: tuition, books, fees, up to $250 supplies. Division 3: no athletic scholarships. Source: https://www.njcaa.org/member_colleges/Divisional_Structure . Checked 2026-11-11
4.10 [OFFICIAL] Signing an NJCAA letter with two NJCAA colleges makes a student ineligible in the NJCAA for the next academic year. Source: NJCAA prospective-student brochure (2016, may be dated), https://static.gochapsgo.com/custompages/NJCAA_Prospective_Student_Brochure.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11
4.11 [OFFICIAL] What one junior-college year does to the clock: under the NCAA's new rule, full-time enrollment at a two-year college starts the five-year eligibility period. My arithmetic: one JUCO year leaves four years inside the window. Source: https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/division-i-and-division-ii-age-based-eligibility-rules/ . Checked 2026-11-11
4.12 [OFFICIAL] JUCO aid is only year-to-year in effect, because the NJCAA letter runs one academic year. A four-year school's aid is a new paper with new terms. Source: NJCAA chart above (4.8). Checked 2026-11-11
4.13 [SERVICE] A recruiting service says a JUCO-to-Division 1 transfer needs a 2.0 transferable GPA, "lowered from 2.5 in June 2026." I could not confirm this on NCAA pages. Source: https://play2college.com/junior-colleges . Checked 2026-11-11

## 5. Division 3: admission plus aid, no certificate

5.1 [OFFICIAL] Division 3 schools set their own academic standards on campus. The Eligibility Center certifies only international Division 3 athletes. All other Division 3 athletes are certified by the school. Source: CBSA.pdf p.3, p.8, p.20, https://s3.amazonaws.com/fs.ncaa.org/Docs/eligibility_center/Student_Resources/CBSA.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11
5.2 [OFFICIAL] A domestic Division 3 recruit uses the free Profile Page account. Source: https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/register . Checked 2026-11-11
5.3 [OFFICIAL] Division 3 offers no athletics scholarships. About 80% of Division 3 athletes get non-athletics aid. Source: CBSA.pdf p.3. Checked 2026-11-11
5.4 [OFFICIAL] Division 3 aid cannot be based on athletics leadership, ability, participation or performance, and schools report freshman aid packages to the NCAA so athletes are treated like other students. Source: https://www.ncaa.org/division-iii/governance/membership-and-institutional-resources/financial-aid-reporting-program . Checked 2026-11-11
5.5 [OFFICIAL] What a Division 3 "offer" is, from a Wisconsin athletics department: no athletic scholarships; athletes compete for the same merit, need-based and departmental money as any student. Source: UW-Oshkosh Recruit FAQ, https://uwoshkoshtitans.com/sports/2024/4/18/recruits-FAQ-recruit.aspx . Checked 2026-11-11
5.6 [COACH] UW-Oshkosh: there are no National Letters of Intent or "Signing Day" in Division 3. The commitment is verbal; you "commit" by finishing admission, paying a deposit and registering. A standard, non-binding celebratory form may be signed after admission. Source: https://uwoshkoshtitans.com/sports/2024/4/18/recruits-FAQ-recruit.aspx . Checked 2026-11-11
5.7 [OFFICIAL] The NCAA's celebratory signing form is "nonbinding." Division 3 schools may use it after the athlete is accepted. Source: CBSA.pdf p.26 ("Celebratory Signing Form"). Checked 2026-11-11
5.8 [SERVICE] A D3 "offer" usually means the coach will support her application with admissions. A "support letter" is a strong sign of interest, but "not a guarantee of admission." Coaches may point to scholarships she could qualify for but cannot promise any. Source: https://www.d3-direct.com/newsletter/d3-sports-recruiting-key-terms and https://www.d3-direct.com/newsletter/d3-offers-explained . Checked 2026-11-11
5.9 [FOLKLORE] A Wisconsin club page says "Division III does have a National Letter of Intent." The NCAA and a Wisconsin D3 school say the opposite (5.6, 5.7). Example of wrong paper talk. Source: https://www.wisconsinblaze.com/vbrecruiting . Checked 2026-11-11
5.10 [UNCONFIRMED] "Letter of assurance": I found no NCAA, NAIA or NJCAA definition of this term. See "Could not settle."  Searches run 2026-11-11. Checked 2026-11-11

## 6. What she signs, school type by school type (2026-27)

6.1 [OFFICIAL] The National Letter of Intent in Division 1 ended in 2024. A written offer of athletics aid took its place, with the same signing-date formula. Source: https://www.ncaa.org/news/media-center-di-council-approves-changes-to-notification-of-transfer-windows-in-basketball-football/ . Checked 2026-11-11
6.2 [OFFICIAL] Division 2 council made the same change (written offers replace the NLI). Source: https://www.ncaa.org/news/media-center-dii-management-council-supports-automatic-qualification-proposal-for-football . Checked 2026-11-11
6.3 [OFFICIAL] NCAA's scholarships page says "Division II schools may still use" the NLI. This conflicts with 6.2. Ask each Division 2 school which paper it uses. Source: https://www.ncaa.org/student-athletes/scholarships-and-financial-aid . Checked 2026-11-11
6.4 [OFFICIAL] When she signs a Division 1 or 2 athletics aid agreement, the school promises aid "if you are admitted ... remain eligible and meet the terms." Other schools that give athletics aid must stop recruiting her and her family. She cannot sign with more than one school; to talk to another school she must be released first. Source: https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/recruiting ; CBSA.pdf p.24. Checked 2026-11-11
6.5 [OFFICIAL] A verbal commitment is "not binding on you or the school." A preferred walk-on has a roster spot but no scholarship. Source: CBSA.pdf p.26. Checked 2026-11-11
6.6 [OFFICIAL] Coaches decide who gets aid and whether it renews. A school that will reduce, not renew or cancel aid must give written notice by 1 July and say how to appeal. Source: CBSA.pdf p.24. Checked 2026-11-11
6.7 [SERVICE] Summary table by a recruiting site: D1 and D2 sign a written offer of aid (binding); D3 an optional celebratory form (not binding); NAIA the school's own paper (NAIA does not enforce it); NJCAA the NJCAA Letter of Intent (binding). Source: https://reachrecruit.app/guides/national-letter-of-intent . Checked 2026-11-11 (matches 4.4, 4.8, 5.6, 6.1)
6.8 [SERVICE] First signing date in most non-football sports in 2026-27 is reported as 11 November 2026 for Division 1 (the excerpt I read cut off before other divisions; this date is for the class of 2027, not 2029). Confirm with the NCAA recruiting page. Source: https://play2college.com/blog/post/early-signing-period-athletics-aid-agreement . Checked 2026-11-11


## 7. House settlement and roster limits: the paper in 2029

7.1 [OFFICIAL] From 1 July 2025, NCAA Division 1 rules have no sport-by-sport scholarship limits. Schools that opt in to the settlement have roster limits instead and may offer scholarships to any or all of those players. Source: https://www.ncaa.org/news/2025/6/23/media-center-di-board-of-directors-formally-adopts-changes-to-roster-limits.aspx . Checked 2026-11-11
7.2 [SERVICE] The Division 1 roster limit is 18 for women's volleyball and 18 for men's volleyball in the 2026-27 manual (Bylaw 17.2, as read by a recruiting site from the NCAA manual). Before, women's volleyball had 12 head-count scholarships. Source: https://rosterwise.app/guide/house-settlement [SERVICE copy of NCAA data]; same numbers at https://www.wrhi.com/2025/06/ncaa-eliminates-sport-specific-scholarship-caps-introduces-roster-limits-under-house-settlement-201334 . Checked 2026-11-11
7.3 [SERVICE] The limit counts every player on the submitted roster, with or without aid. A coach decides who is on the roster; a roster spot is not guaranteed. Source: https://rosterwise.app/guide/house-settlement [SERVICE]; Virginia Athletics Q&A, https://storage.googleapis.com/virginiasports-com-prod/2025/06/House_Settlement_FAQ.pdf [COACH/school]. Checked 2026-11-11
7.4 [OFFICIAL] "Designated Student-Athletes" (athletes who would have been cut by the new limit) do not count against the roster limit for as long as they have eligibility. They were named in 2025 for people already on rosters or promised spots, so they are not a factor for a 2029 recruit. (Last half is my reading.) Source: https://www.ncaa.org/news/2025/6/23/media-center-di-board-of-directors-formally-adopts-changes-to-roster-limits.aspx . Checked 2026-11-11
7.5 [OFFICIAL] Not every Division 1 school opts in. If a school does not opt in, the settlement does not require roster limits. Source: NCAA Q&A (Feb 2026), https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/Feb2026D1Gov_PhaseSevenSetQuestionandAnswer.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11
7.6 [OFFICIAL] Revenue sharing: opted-in schools may pay athletes directly. Each school chooses whether and how much. This is a separate deal from an athletics scholarship. Source: NCAA Q&A (Feb 2026), same PDF as 7.5. Checked 2026-11-11
7.7 [COACH] School Q&A: each school has discretion to share up to $20.5 million with athletes in 2025-26, rising each year for 10 years. Source: https://storage.googleapis.com/virginiasports-com-prod/2025/06/House_Settlement_FAQ.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11
7.8 [OFFICIAL] Protection: at Division 1 schools, if an athlete received aid in her first year and still has eligibility, aid "cannot be reduced, canceled or not renewed" for athletics reasons. The list includes injury, illness, mental health, performance, contribution to team success and roster management. Schools must give written notice and a chance to appeal. Source: https://www.ncaa.org/student-athletes/student-athlete-experience/core-guarantees/ . Checked 2026-11-11
7.9 [OFFICIAL] Same page: Division 1 schools must cover medical care for athletics injuries and eligible out-of-pocket medical costs, and give continued care for qualifying injuries for at least two years after graduation or leaving. Source: https://www.ncaa.org/student-athletes/student-athlete-experience/core-guarantees/ . Checked 2026-11-11
7.10 [OFFICIAL] NCAA's wording on roster cuts: if a player on aid loses her spot for roster management, athletics performance or injury, "that student-athlete's scholarship cannot be revoked unless and until the student chooses to transfer." Source: https://www.ncaa.org/news/2025/6/23/media-center-di-board-of-directors-formally-adopts-changes-to-roster-limits.aspx . Checked 2026-11-11
7.11 [OFFICIAL] The NCAA also provides, at no cost, catastrophic insurance for athletics injuries and post-eligibility insurance after leaving school. These sit on top of the athlete's and school's own coverage. Source: CBSA.pdf p.30, https://s3.amazonaws.com/fs.ncaa.org/Docs/eligibility_center/Student_Resources/CBSA.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11
7.12 [OFFICIAL] Length of award: the NCAA says Division 1 offers "multiyear" athletics scholarships; Division 2 uses a partial-scholarship model. Source: CBSA.pdf p.3. Checked 2026-11-11 (I did not verify a rule that says every Division 1 award must be four years; see "Could not settle.")
7.13 [OFFICIAL] Transfer: a Division 1 athlete told she will lose her roster spot cannot have aid cut for entering the portal. Source: NCAA Q&A (Sept 2025), https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/2024-25/Sep2025D1Gov_PhaseSixSetQuestionandAnswer.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11
7.14 [OFFICIAL] Transfer data: of Division 1 athletes who asked to enter the portal in 2024-25, 68% were reported to have transferred to a new NCAA school. Source: CBSA.pdf p.32. Checked 2026-11-11
7.15 [UNCONFIRMED] I found no NCAA page that sets transfer-window dates for volleyball in 2029. A recruiting site says windows change every year and posts 2026-27 tables. Source: https://www.ncsasports.org/recruiting/ncaa-transfer-rules . Checked 2026-11-11
7.16 [SERVICE] Practical advice sites give for a 2026-27 offer: ask for the exact dollars, what it covers, whether health insurance is included, how renewal works, and whether the roster spot is guaranteed; read any separate revenue-share or NIL deal on its own. Source: https://play2college.com/blog/post/early-signing-period-athletics-aid-agreement . Checked 2026-11-11


## 8. Men's volleyball: a different road (for a family with a son)

8.1 [OFFICIAL] NCAA membership data (as of 14 Sept 2026) shows men's volleyball teams: 31 in Division 1, 41 in Division 2, 141 in Division 3 (213 total). Women's: 348, 288, 417 (1,053 total). Source: https://www.ncaa.org/about-us/membership-directory/membership-composition-and-sport-sponsorship/ . Checked 2026-11-11 (My reading of the table columns.)
