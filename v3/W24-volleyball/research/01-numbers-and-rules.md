## 1. NCAA Division I: scholarships and roster limits (the part families get wrong)

- [OFFICIAL] Before 1 July 2025, Division I women's volleyball was a HEAD-COUNT sport with a team limit of 12 (the College Sports Commission table lists "Volleyball (W): old scholarship limit 12"). Source: https://www.collegesportscommission.org/roster-limits . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Since 1 July 2025, schools that are in a "defendant conference" (ACC, Big Ten, Big 12, Pac-12, SEC) or that opt in to the House settlement ("Participating Institutions") have NO NCAA scholarship limit in any sport. Instead each sport has a roster limit; women's volleyball roster limit = 18. Source: https://www.collegesportscommission.org/roster-limits and https://www.ncaa.org/news/media-center-di-board-of-directors-formally-adopts-changes-to-roster-limits . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] NCAA wording: "schools in the defendant conferences and others that opt in ... will have roster limits, and schools will have the option to offer scholarships to any and all of those student-athletes" (DI Board of Directors, 23 June 2025, effective 1 July 2025). Source: https://www.ncaa.org/news/media-center-di-board-of-directors-formally-adopts-changes-to-roster-limits . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] The Division I Manual "no longer includes institutional financial aid limits for any sport" (Q&A B2). Aid may be given "in any amount (full or partial)" (Q&A B3). So at a Participating school a coach may split money across all 18 roster spots; the NCAA does not require a full scholarship for anyone. Source: NCAA Q&A, Implementation of the House Settlement, updated 29 May 2026, https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/May2026D1Gov_PhaseEightSetQuestionandAnswer.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] The old rule "head count" (each of 12 counted scholarships = one athlete, any amount of aid) is gone at Participating schools; in practice every aid recipient now counts against the 18 roster spots because anyone who gets a scholarship "must be included on a sport's submitted roster" (Q&A C10). Source: same May 2026 Q&A PDF above. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] NON-participating Division I schools: roster limits do NOT apply (Q&A C3); "roster sizes may be governed by the conference or institution and not legislated at the national level" (A16). Source: same May 2026 Q&A PDF. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Non-participating schools stay under the 2024-25 aid ceiling or they become subject to the settlement incl. roster limits (A11, A16). Per Q&A B4 they may spread "the value of" the old limit "(as measured in equivalencies)" across more athletes in former head-count sports (the NCAA's worked example is women's gymnastics). Source: same May 2026 Q&A PDF. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] An opt-in covers every NCAA sport at that school; a school cannot opt in team by team (A4). Opt-in decisions are made year by year during the 10-year settlement term (A9). Source: same May 2026 Q&A PDF. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] A conference may set a roster limit LOWER than 18 but not lower than the sport's 2024-25 scholarship limit (for women's volleyball that floor is 12) (A15). Source: same May 2026 Q&A PDF. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Timing: fall-sport roster must be at or below the limit by the end of the calendar day before the first competition that counts for championship selection (Q&A C1, C6). Roster limits "do not apply until the day after a team's deadline for roster submission" (C51), so preseason camps can be larger. After the deadline, only players on the submitted roster may take part in practice or team activities (C10, C27). Source: same May 2026 Q&A PDF. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] "Designated Student-Athletes" (players who would have been cut because of the limits and were on a 2024-25 squad list or were recruited/assured a spot before 7 April 2025) do NOT count against the limit for the rest of their eligibility, even if they transfer (C11-C14). So a Participating school can list more than 18 on its roster by the number of designated athletes still on it (C1, C12). Source: same May 2026 Q&A PDF. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Athletes who were on aid before 2025-26 cannot lose that aid because of the roster-limit change (C4, B5). Source: same May 2026 Q&A PDF. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Only the Division I Board changes the numbers: in Aug 2026 it added a 25-player limit for women's flag football (effective 2027-28). The women's volleyball number (18) was unchanged in that proposal's table. Source: https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/search/proposalView?id=109568 . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [SERVICE] Secondary summaries (NCSA, RosterWise, NextCommit) all state the same 12 -> 18 change for women's volleyball. Source: https://www.ncsasports.org/womens-volleyball/scholarships ; https://rosterwise.app/volleyball/womens/guide/scholarships ; https://www.nextcommit.ai/blog/athletic-scholarships-roster-limits-2026 . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [SERVICE] Warning on errors in the wild: some secondary tables list the OLD women's volleyball limit as 4.5 (that is the MEN'S volleyball limit) - e.g. https://athleteboosthq.com/blog/ncaa-roster-limits-by-sport . The NCAA/CSC table shows women's 12, men's 4.5. Source for correct figures: https://www.collegesportscommission.org/roster-limits . Checked 2026-11-11.

## 2. Division II, Division III, NAIA, NJCAA: scholarship limits

- [OFFICIAL] NCAA Division II women's volleyball: 8.0 scholarship EQUIVALENCIES per team. D2 explains that each sport has a team-wide maximum that coaches "often divide ... among multiple players on the roster" (equivalency). Men's volleyball is 4.5 (different sport). Source: https://www.ncaa.org/division-ii/governance/membership-and-institutional-resources/athletics-scholarships . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] NCAA Division III: "Division III schools do not offer athletics scholarships" (merit- and need-based aid exist). Source: https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/initial-eligibility-requirements/division-iii . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] NAIA: volleyball limit is 8 (full-grant equivalents); the school may divide it among athletes; total money = average cost of attendance x 8. NAIA is equivalency. Source: https://www.naia.org/why-naia/naia-financial-aid . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [SERVICE] NJCAA women's volleyball: 14 scholarships (letters of intent) per team, per the NJCAA sport procedures chart as cited by a secondary guide; NJCAA Division I may cover tuition, fees, room and board, books; Division II tuition, fees, books; Division III no athletic aid. Source: https://playwithnewvision.com/en/blog/national-signing-day-2026-international-athletes and https://www.nextcommit.ai/blog/d1-vs-d2-vs-d3-vs-naia-vs-juco . Primary chart is behind the NJCAA admin portal (https://www.njcaa.org/compliance). Checked 2026-11-11.
- [SERVICE] Only Division I has an NCAA roster limit; D2/D3 have none at national level; NAIA sets no roster-size limit. Source: https://www.pathley.ai/blog-posts/ncaa-roster-limits-by-sport-guide ; https://rosterwise.app/volleyball/womens/guide/scholarships . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [SERVICE] Equivalency vs head-count summary: D1 (old) head count 12; D1 now roster limit 18 with no cap on aid at Participating schools; D2 equivalency 8.0; NAIA equivalency 8; NJCAA 14; D3 none. Source: https://www.ncsasports.org/womens-volleyball/scholarships . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] The NCAA does not require any Participating Division I program to give a full scholarship (Q&A B3). So "equivalency" at D1 now effectively means: aid in any amount to anyone on the roster, with no NCAA total cap at Participating schools. Source: https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/May2026D1Gov_PhaseEightSetQuestionandAnswer.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.

## 5. National Letter of Intent, signing dates

- [OFFICIAL] The NCAA Division I Council approved "the immediate elimination of the national letter of intent program" on 9 Oct 2024; the NLI is replaced by a written offer of athletics aid (athletics aid agreement) in the NCAA rules. So women's volleyball is NOT part of an NLI program at the NCAA D1 level now. Source: https://www.espn.com/college-sports/story/_/id/41702974/ncaa-approves-elimination-national-letter-intent-program ; rule text: NCAA Division I Manual 2026-27 Bylaw 13.02.13, https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008 . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [SERVICE] Division II eliminated the NLI on 23 Oct 2024 (reported by a secondary source; the NCAA D2 Official Notice shows D2 "institutional athletics aid agreement" signing dates, Bylaw 13.02.10). Source: https://playwithnewvision.com/en/blog/national-signing-day-2026-international-athletes ; https://ncaa.soutronglobal.net/Public/Default/en-US/DownloadImageFile.ashx?objectId=53556&ownerId=58441&ownerType=0 . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] 2026-27 NCAA signing dates (published 28 July 2026): "All Other Division I and II Sports - initial signing date November 11, 2026; final date based on institutional policy." Volleyball is in "all other sports"; it has no separate early/regular periods. Source: https://athleticsaid.s3.amazonaws.com/2026-27DI_SigningDates.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.

Source: https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008 ; D2 Official Notice at the soutronglobal.net URL above. Checked 2026-11-11.

## 6. Walk-ons, preferred walk-ons, "no scholarship offered"

- [OFFICIAL] At a Participating D1 school, once the roster deadline passes, anyone who practices or takes part in team activities must be on the submitted roster, and the roster cannot exceed the limit (plus designated athletes). A walk-on who is on the team therefore occupies one of the roster spots (Q&A C10, C27). Source: https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/May2026D1Gov_PhaseEightSetQuestionandAnswer.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Roster limits "do not apply until the day after a team's deadline for roster submission" (C51), so a program can carry extra players in preseason before cutting to the limit. Source: same Q&A PDF. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [SERVICE] Definition: a walk-on joins the team without an athletic scholarship; a "preferred walk-on" is invited by the coaching staff before the season with a roster spot identified and may earn aid later. Source: https://rosterwise.app/volleyball/guide/walk-on-pathways . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [SERVICE] "A program that funds 16-17 players might carry 1-2 walk-ons. Some may carry none" (RosterWise estimate, not NCAA data). Source: https://rosterwise.app/volleyball/guide/walk-on-pathways . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [SERVICE] A walk-on invited with no scholarship does not sign an athletics aid agreement. Source: https://www.ncsasports.org/recruiting/managing-recruiting-process/national-signing-day . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] In D2 and NAIA (equivalency, 8.0 and 8), the team total is a ceiling, not a requirement; D2 says coaches "often divide" the money among players. "No scholarship offered" can therefore mean 0% of an equivalency while the athlete is still on the roster. D3 has no athletic scholarships at all. Source: https://www.ncaa.org/division-ii/governance/membership-and-institutional-resources/athletics-scholarships ; https://www.naia.org/why-naia/naia-financial-aid ; https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/initial-eligibility-requirements/division-iii . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Athletes who were on aid before 2025-26 may not lose aid because of roster limits (Q&A C4); the NCAA states "roster spots are not guaranteed." Source: same Q&A PDF. Checked 2026-11-11.

## 7. Transfer rules and portal windows

- [OFFICIAL] Division I four-year transfers: an undergraduate transfer is eligible to compete immediately at a new D1 school "regardless of whether they have transferred before" if they left the prior school academically eligible, in good standing (no disciplinary suspension) and meet progress-toward-degree requirements at the new school. No D1 "one-time transfer" limit appears in the current NCAA guidance. Source: https://fs.ncaa.org.s3.amazonaws.com/Docs/eligibility_center/Transfer/DITransferBasics.pdf (Spring 2024); https://www.ncaa.org/media-center-division-i-board-of-directors-ratifies-transfer-nil-rule-changes . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Entering the portal does not make a player eligible; a D1 athlete may generally be entered "ONLY ... during the specified window(s) in that student-athlete's sport." Source: https://fs.ncaa.org.s3.amazonaws.com/Docs/eligibility_center/Transfer/DITransferBasics.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] D1 women's volleyball notification-of-transfer windows for 2026-27 (Figure 13-1, updated 8 Oct 2026): fall window December 6, 2026 to January 4, 2027; spring window for fall sports May 1 to May 15, 2027. Source: https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/compliance/d1/D1Comp_UndergraduateTransferWindows.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] On 1 Oct 2026 the Women's Volleyball Oversight Committee approved an accelerated proposal for a 15-consecutive-day fall window opening after the first and second rounds of the championship. The 7 Oct 2026 Cabinet announcement listed football, cross country/track and gymnastics changes but not volleyball. Outcome for volleyball not confirmed. Source: https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/championships/sports/volleyball/d1/women/WVBOC/October2026D1WVBOC_Oct1Report.pdf ; https://www.ncaa.org/news/division-i-cabinet-approves-new-transfer-windows-in-several-sports . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Division III four-year transfer: eligible in the first full-time term if (1) never practiced/competed in intercollegiate athletics, or (2) would have been academically eligible at the previous school, or another listed exception. Source: https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/compliance/forms/d3/2026-27/2026-27D3Comp_SummaryofNCAARegulations.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Settlement note: a D1 athlete on aid before 2025-26 who is told they will lose a roster spot cannot have aid cut for entering the portal (Q&A B5). Source: https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/governance/d1/legislation/May2026D1Gov_PhaseEightSetQuestionandAnswer.pdf . Checked 2026-11-11.

## 8. Eligibility clock and redshirts (as of today)

- [OFFICIAL] On 23 June 2026 the Division I Cabinet unanimously adopted an age-based model: up to five years of eligibility if the athlete enrolls full time no later than the academic year after their 19th birthday; it eliminates "season-of-competition limits, sport-specific eligibility and redshirt rules, and eligibility extension waivers." Source: https://www.ncaa.org/news/division-i-adopts-age-based-eligibility-model . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Effective for prospects initially enrolling full time in fall 2027 or later (the age-based rule applies alone). Fall 2026 enrollees get the better of old or new rules. A class-of-2029 player normally first enrolls in fall 2029, which falls in the "fall 2027 or later" group. Source: https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/division-i-and-division-ii-age-based-eligibility-rules . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] The five-year clock starts at the earlier of first full-time enrollment (incl. a two-year college) or the start of the academic year after the 19th birthday; it "runs continuously" and does not pause if the athlete sits out, transfers or does not compete, so athletic "redshirts" no longer need tracking. Limited exceptions: military/religious-mission service and pregnancy. Source: same NCAA Eligibility 101 page. Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] The same page says the age-based rules apply across Divisions I and II. Waivers eliminated include medical hardship, extension-of-eligibility, season-of-competition and delayed-enrollment waivers. Source: https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/division-i-and-division-ii-age-based-eligibility-rules . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] NAIA: four "seasons of competition" during the first 10 semesters (or equivalent); 12 hours per term. Source: https://www.naia.org/student-athletes/prospective/parents . Checked 2026-11-11.
- [OFFICIAL] Division I Manual 2026-27 Bylaw 12.6: five-year period of eligibility, revised 6/24/26 effective 8/1/26. Source: https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/reports/getReport/90008 . Checked 2026-11-11.
