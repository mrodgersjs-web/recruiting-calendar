6. What to send, part 1: the email

One email, from her, with two links. It is short because a staff gets hundreds of them a week. Everything a coach skims for sits in the first four lines, and every number says how it was measured.

Subject line, @@SUBJ@@ letters

2029 outside hitter / DS at Anytown South HS · 5'11", 9'4" approach · 3.62 GPA · Wisconsin

Body, @@WORDS@@ words, from her own mailbox

Coach Rivera, My name is Madison Weber. I play outside hitter and defensive specialist at Anytown South High School in Anytown, Wisconsin. I graduate in 2029. My club team is Impact Athletic Development 16 Orange, and my schedule is in the link below. Club numbers, 71 sets: 3.08 kills a set, .271 hitting on 640 swings, 3.48 digs a set, 18 aces, 41 serve errors. In serve receive, 268 passes, 104 perfect on a 3-point scale. Measures, 22 May 2027, by my trainer at Impact Athletic Development: 5'11" barefoot, 7'6" standing reach in shoes with feet flat, 9'4" approach touch on a Vertec, best of four. Unweighted GPA 3.62 on a 4.0 scale. NCAA ID 7419286. I want to study nursing. I watched your matches on the stream this fall. Your April update said one outside spot is open for 2029. May I send a full match, and how many outsides are you signing that year? Reel, 2:40, no login: youtu.be/xxxxxxx Free profile: ottoprep.com/xxxxxxx Thank you for reading this. Madison Weber madison.weber@example.com · (555) 555-0148

Invented player, invented school, invented club, invented coach, per this book's rule: sample emails name no real high school, club, camp or service. The numbers are the kind a 16s outside hitter really posts, and the links are placeholders.

Why it is written this way

  • She writes it. A Division III head coach who won a national title said he can "tell the email that dad sent with your name on it." A club coach says the same: "we can tell if a parent wrote them."
  • Position, class and height in the subject. A club coach's rule is to put name, position and grade year in the subject line and to spell the position out, because "coaches will not know" what OH/RS means at a glance. Balltime's guide says the same, and adds that coaches skim for the video link, the class year, the height and approach touch, the grades, and one real reason she likes that school.
  • Stats with denominators. "3.08 kills a set" over 71 sets is a claim a coach can weigh. The same number from six sets is noise.
  • Numbers with a method and a date. See the box below. This is where most families lose credit.
  • One question a coach can answer in five words. A recruiting-coordinator note in the sample assumes the staff has posted a needs list. If it has not, ask for it.
  • No attachments, and no login walls. The Junior Volleyball Association's coach-sourced list says do not send video that needs a sign-in. Unlisted YouTube, or a public Balltime or Hudl page, and test the link in a signed-out browser on a phone first.

Label every number, or a coach discounts all of them

Two studies found that a wearable jump device reads about two inches higher than a Vertec for the same athlete, one of them on 128 Brazilian youth national players at 70.9 centimetres against 76.3. So "vertical 24 inches" means nothing without the tool. Say the tool, the date, who measured, and whether the shoes were on. A bare-height claim should be measured barefoot to the nearest half inch, with a parent writing it down.

One more reason to be honest: this book counted 212 listed heights across 11 nearby college rosters, then counted the NCAA's own national leader lists. Division I shows a bump at exactly 6'0" over 5'11" (143 players against 93), which fits rounding up and does not prove it. If a roster says 6'0" and she is 5'11" in socks, she is not short. Her number, honestly measured, is worth more than a hopeful one.

6. What to send, part 2: what good video looks like

Coaches say two to five minutes, and no two sources agree on the number. The shortest honest number in this research is two minutes, from the company that has the most profiles. Build two to three minutes, best clips first, and keep the full match ready to send when a coach asks.

PositionWhat must be in the reelWhy
Outside hitterAttack from both pins, block, serve, and serve receive and defense. Variety of shots.A coach who recruits outsides said: "include variety in your videos. I once got a video where the recruit literally hit the same shot in every clip."
SetterSetting from both front and back row, out of system, to every antenna, plus defense, blocking and any serving.In and out of system is the whole question at this position.
MiddleQuick attack, block footwork, transition, handling a free ball, and the serve.The club-coach checklist for middles is exactly these five things.
OppositeRight-pin attack against a full block, block, serve.Same list as an outside, minus the passing.
Libero and DSServe receive from every zone, defense from deep and shallow, vision, and the serve if the staff wants it. Passing and defense above everything."If you are a Libero, please include passing and defense." A Division I head coach of a national champion said of liberos: "we don't care if they can jump."

The rules nearly every coach stated

  • Match play, not drills. Controlled-drill clips "do not give them a good sense of your overall athletic ability, decision making and reactions to errors." Reps from a live practice game are fine and preferred.
  • Show a few seconds before and after the ball. One coach: "I want to see your body language, communication with teammates and reactions." A Division III national-champion coach wants an entire point, not a highlight, plus her jersey number, jersey color and side of the court.
  • No slow motion, no music, no big arrows. Several coaches say they mute the audio anyway.
  • Put her name, position, class and jersey number on the first frame. Otto Prep's own advice: a cover card "to remind them who they are watching before the clips start," because "coaches are not going to sit there and fast forward through full matches trying to find your five touches."
  • Skip the flashy passing clips. A coach on the record: "Highlight clips of passing and digging aren't really of all that much value, especially the flashy ones. For all I know, you shanked all your other opportunities."
  • Expect to be judged in seconds. One service measured that coaches watch "the first 10-25 seconds" of a reel; a club coach says 10 to 15. The fix is the same: the best clip goes first.

Two coaches disagree about kills. Both are on the record.

One recruiting site tells players that every hit shown should be a kill. A coach who has scouted for years says kills and aces alone do not show skill, and wants mechanics, movement and what happens after the error. This book prints the second advice, because the first comes from a company selling a service and the second comes from a coach. Send the reel to a school you like and ask what it wants. The answer costs a coach ten seconds and is worth more than any guide.
