# W7 Personalized local curation for athlete parents (2026-11-06)

Author: Aside agent (research only, not a lawyer). Pilot market: Denver, CO, as ONE illustration. Nothing here was sent to anyone; no provider was contacted; no minors were researched.
Evidence rule: every provider/event fact below was read from a public page on 2026-11-06 (URL given). If I did not read it, it says UNVERIFIED. Prices and dates change; each row carries a "read on" date and must be re-checked before any parent sees it.

## 0. Bottom line
- The product is a **calendar-plus-shortlist**, not a marketplace. It answers "what fits MY athlete, MY grade, MY budget, MY drive time, and what can I verify about who runs it?"
- The defensible edge is the **evidence label on every row**: Listed (provider says so), Verified (a third-party registry confirms it), Observed (we read a dated source), Vetted (a human on our side checked it against a written rubric), Endorsed (a named third party recommends it). Today the pilot can honestly claim only the first three. "Vetted" and "Endorsed" need an operator and a process that do not exist yet.
- Do NOT rank on outcomes ("got kids recruited") and do NOT make health claims ("prevents injury"). Rank on fit, verifiability, safeguarding posture, cost clarity and freshness.
- Biggest legal fact found: Colorado law (SB24-113, "Safer Youth Sports") already requires youth sports organizations to run coach background checks and annual mandatory-reporter training. That gives us a concrete, checkable safeguarding question to ask every provider. A 2026 follow-up bill (SB26-100: first-aid/CPR/AED on site, chaperone checks) was **postponed indefinitely on 2026-04-30**, so it is NOT law. Do not cite it as law.

## 2. Taxonomy and the five evidence labels
Provider types: (a) strength and conditioning / sports performance; (b) skill coaches (sport-specific); (c) mental performance; (d) camps and clinics (youth development); (e) college prospect/ID camps; (f) showcases/tournaments; (g) recreational leagues (feeder, mostly younger).

Evidence labels (a row must show exactly one "highest" label plus the underlying facts):
1. **Listed**: the provider's own page says it. Weakest. Marketing copy.
2. **Observed**: we read a dated public page ourselves (event date, price, location). Proves the page said it on that date.
3. **Verified**: an independent registry confirms a specific claim (e.g., a named person holds a named credential). Narrow: proves the credential, not quality or safety.
4. **Vetted**: a human reviewer applied our written rubric and kept the evidence. Needs an operator, a rubric, and a re-review date. NOT available in the pilot.
5. **Endorsed**: a named third party (school, club, coach, former client) recommends, with consent and disclosure of any relationship. NOT available in the pilot; never fabricate.

Availability is separate from all five: "has openings" is perishable and only true on the date read. Never imply a spot exists.

### 3c. Strength credential registries (what can and cannot be checked)
- NSCA: certification verification is by **written request form** (type, certificate number, name, address; "allow 1-2 business days"). So a parent or we cannot instantly confirm CSCS from a name alone. NSCA also runs a public Certified Professional Directory (79,747 listings on the page read 2026-11-06) and digital credentials via Accredible with a verification link; individuals can make credentials private, so absence from the directory does not mean uncertified.
- NASM: public lookup by name or certificate ID, page stamped "Current as of 11/03/2026".
- USA Weightlifting: Coach Directory lists coaches with a valid certification who passed a criminal background screening and SafeSport training (per the page). This is a stronger safeguarding signal than most, but it covers weightlifting coaches only.
- NSCA RSCC (a higher designation) requires CPR/AED, SafeSport and USADA coach course per the NSCA info sheet (2021 PDF; re-check current).
Rule: a credential is Verified only when we or the parent can reach the issuer's own record for that person. "Our trainers are certified" is Listed.

3. Personal contact details from credential directories (emails, phones) stay in the source; we link to the profile.
4. Re-check cadence: events weekly in the 60 days before the date, monthly otherwise; provider prices quarterly; credentials at each recertification year shown. Any row not re-read inside its window shows "not recently checked" and drops from default results.
5. Aggregators are leads, not evidence.

## 5. Safeguarding, minor privacy, and no-health-claims rules
### 5a. Colorado safeguarding facts we can use (public record, read 2026-11-06)
- SB24-113 "Safer Youth Sports" (signed 2024-05-17): from 2025-07-01 each youth sports organization must require every coach to complete **annual mandatory-reporter training**; it must **encourage** annual abuse-prevention training; it must require **pre-hire criminal history record checks by a consumer reporting agency** for coaches; it must not hire a coach with disqualifying convictions; and it must adopt a prohibited-conduct policy and post a notice for parents. "Youth sports organization" is defined as a private for-profit or not-for-profit organization whose core function includes youth athletic activities (read the exact definition and exclusions in the signed bill before applying it to any specific business; one-time or walk-in events are carved out).
- SB26-100 (first aid/CPR/AED adult on site; chaperone checks; checks every 3 years) was **postponed indefinitely 2026-04-30 (5-0)**. Not law. Providers may still do it voluntarily.
- So the parent-facing checklist asks, and records the provider's answer with a date, WITHOUT claiming we verified it: (1) Do all coaches get a background check from a consumer reporting agency? (2) Mandatory-reporter training completed this year? (3) Written prohibited-conduct / one-on-one interaction policy available? (4) Is a first-aid/CPR/AED-certified adult present? (5) Which named coach will work with my child, and what credential number can I check? These are **self-reported (Listed)** until a document is seen.
- The product does NOT run or store background checks on individuals, and does NOT say a provider is "safe". It says what the provider reported and when.

### 5b. Minor privacy design (the parent is the user; the athlete is the data subject)
- Account holder is the parent/guardian (adult). No accounts, profiles, messaging or tracking for under-18s. Under COPPA (amended rule, effective 2025-06-23, compliance by 2026-04-22) collecting personal information from children under 13 triggers notice and verifiable parental consent, and third-party ad disclosure needs separate opt-in consent. Design to avoid collecting any child personal information.
- Collect the minimum to personalize: sport, graduation/class year (not birth date), home ZIP3 or a coarse radius, budget band, and level goals. No child names, photos, school names, stats, or health information.
- Colorado's amended Privacy Act adds duties where a service knows a user is a minor (bill summary: effective 2025-10-01). Because the parent is the user and we collect no child identifiers, exposure is reduced, but counsel must confirm.
- No sale or sharing of parent data with providers. No "lead handoff" to a provider without a separate, explicit, per-provider opt-in by the parent, and none in the pilot.
- No public reviews by or about minors. No testimonials from minors. No location of a child's practice schedule.
- Retention: delete profile data on request; no logs of searches beyond what is needed to run the product.
- Already-open items from W3-legal (auto-renewal wording, real-athlete names) apply to any paid launch and are not re-litigated here.

### 5c. No health claims
- Allowed: "Provider says it offers speed and strength classes for ages 8-16" (quoted as Listed).
- Banned in our own voice: injury prevention, rehab, "safe", "medically supervised", "improves mental health", "reduces anxiety", "fixes", "treats", "prevents burnout", any outcome promise. Provider health claims (e.g., "overseen by experts in sports medicine") are quoted only inside the Listed field, in quotes, with the source, never promoted into a badge or rank.
- Mental performance: show CMPC and any state licence as separate facts. Include a standing note: "Mental performance consulting is not a substitute for medical or mental-health care; if you are worried about your child's wellbeing, talk to their doctor." The product does not triage.
- Fitness for a specific child (load, age-appropriateness) is a decision for the parent and the child's clinician, not a ranking input.

## 6. Sourcing and ranking workflow
### 6a. Pipeline (each step has a real input, no fake waiting)
1. **Discover** (input: seeds + parent request): from official/open sources, provider sites, and aggregators as leads only. Output: candidate rows with source URL.
2. **Read** (input: candidate URL): a person or a permitted tool reads the page; extract only the fields in the schema; stamp read-on date and reader. Capture "unknown" explicitly instead of inferring.
3. **Classify** (input: extracted fields): provider type, sport, ages/grades, location (coarse), price components, who runs it (college / school / nonprofit / government / private company), event past/future.
4. **Check credentials** (input: named individual + credential number if given): go to the issuer's record; store "Verified: <issuer>, <credential>, <year/expiry>, <read-on>" or "Not found / not checkable (NSCA needs a written request)". Never mark Verified from a badge image.
5. **Safeguarding intake** (input: provider's written answers to the five questions): store as Listed with date. Upgrade only if a document is seen. No outreach in the pilot; the sample rows leave these blank.
6. **Score** (input: row + parent profile): see 6b. Show the reasons.
7. **Freshness gate** (input: read-on date + event date): auto-hide past events and rows past their re-check window.
8. **Publish with labels** (output): every row shows its highest evidence label, what each field's label is, and the read-on date.
9. **Feedback loop** (input: parent reports "wrong/closed/dead link"): correct and re-stamp; no public reviews.

### 6b. Ranking: transparent, rule-based, no paid placement
Score only on fit and verifiability, shown to the parent:
- Fit (0-40): sport match; grade/class-year match; level match (e.g., college prospect camp vs rec camp); schedule fits the parent's season window.
- Geography (0-20): drive time bands from the parent's coarse origin; suburban reality matters (the pilot's own examples span Denver, Centennial, Lakewood, Parker and Colorado Springs).
- Cost clarity (0-15): all price parts shown and sourced; unknown price scores low, not zero, and is flagged.
- Verifiability (0-15): named coach with a checkable credential > unnamed "certified staff".
- Freshness (0-10): read recently, date confirmed on the organizer's own page.
Tie-breaker: official/primary source over aggregator.
Hard filters, not scores: past events; sports/grades that don't match; unknown dates shown separately as "Not yet announced".
Prohibited in ranking: sponsorship/paid placement affecting rank; outcome claims; review stars; popularity from clicks. Paid listings, if ever added, are labeled "Sponsored" and sit outside the ranked list.
Disclosure line on every result set: "Ranked by fit, location, cost clarity, checkability and freshness. Not by who pays us."

### 6c. Data schema (minimum)
entity_id, name, type, sport, ages_or_grades, location_coarse, who_runs, price_items[], dates[], url, read_on, reader, field_labels{listed|observed|verified|vetted|endorsed}, credential_checks[{person, issuer, credential, id_present, status, read_on}], safeguarding_answers[{q, answer, source, date, document_seen}], status{active|past|tbd|stale}.

## 8. MVP (smallest thing that tests the demand claim)
Goal: learn whether parents act on a labeled, personalized shortlist, using Denver only as the test bed. No outreach, no providers contacted, no paid placement.
Scope (4 weeks of effort estimate, to be checked by Mike):
1. **Intake**: 5 fields (sport, class year, coarse area, budget band, goal level). No child identifiers.
2. **Dataset**: ~30 hand-read Denver-area rows across the four categories, each with source URL, read-on date and evidence label (the 3 sections above are the seed). Mark every safeguarding field "not collected" (no outreach).
3. **Personal calendar**: only future, relevant events, with "Not yet announced" separated; ICS export.
4. **Row detail page**: labels, reason for rank, credential-check links to issuers, the five safeguarding questions for the parent to ask, source links.
5. **Standing disclosures**: ranking method, "not medical advice", "we have not verified safety", read-on dates.
6. **Report-a-problem** link per row.
Out of scope: accounts for minors, reviews, messaging, lead handoff, payments for local data (reuse existing checkout test-mode), scraping.
Success measures (set before launch, not after): share of rows re-read on time; share of parent sessions that open a credential-check link; share that copy a safeguarding question; parent-reported wrong rows per 100 views; willingness to pay asked as a question, not assumed. Do not report vanity counts.
Kill criteria: if fewer than ~1 in 4 sessions use a credential or safeguarding link, the "verification" thesis is weak and the product should be calendar-only. If upkeep of 30 rows exceeds a few hours a week, the model needs a data feed or smaller scope.
Staffing: one researcher reading sources; one reviewer for the future Vetted tier; counsel for the privacy and vetting-fee questions.

## 9. Risks and open questions
- **Defamation/false endorsement**: naming private businesses and credentialed individuals. Keep to quoted facts with sources and dates; allow correction requests; counsel before launch.
- **"Verified" overreach**: a registry hit proves a credential only. Always say so.
- **Survivorship and class bias**: premium facilities dominate search; rec and school programs are underrepresented in this pilot (I found only one government source). Seek school and nonprofit programs deliberately.
- **Geography**: the pilot's examples are Denver-centric; the same method for rural parents (a W1 theme) depends on fewer local providers and more travel events.
