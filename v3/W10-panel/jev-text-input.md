## Method and limits

Ten fictional parent scenarios were written as isolated first-pass perspectives before synthesis. No persona sees or agrees with another response. These are author-generated hypotheses, not transcripts, quotes from parents, preference counts, market validation or accessibility certification. Sport and association rules must be verified with current authoritative sources before implementation.

## Synthesis: hypotheses to test, not findings

- First-time families need a credible next step, not a promise of recruitment.
- Multi-athlete and multi-household coordination must not compromise privacy.
- Team, individual and adaptive sports need explicit scope rather than one shared timeline.
- Accessible agenda, input and recovery flows are release requirements, not cosmetic extras.

## Conflicts to resolve through actual research

- Household coordination versus athlete privacy: default to private and let the family preview field-level sharing.
- Rich sport context versus cognitive load: show the next action first, expand source and scope on request.
- Cached convenience versus date accuracy: show freshness and withhold unsupported current-rule claims.
- Fast sign-up versus safeguarding: do not require medical disclosure or public exposure to finish onboarding.

## Real validation plan

Recruit consenting parents across team, individual and adaptive sports, and include disabled participants using their own access tools. Explain compensation and consent separately from product access. Use fictional athlete tasks; do not collect children’s private data. Record task outcomes and accessibility barriers with consent, preserving disagreement rather than claiming consensus. Run moderated tasks for profile switching, source checking, rescheduling, sharing preview and account recovery. Do not present this panel as an actual research sample.
