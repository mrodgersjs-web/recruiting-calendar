# The Commit Book: Football Parent Playbook
### Class of 2029 · built from rules read 2026-11-05 · written 2026-11-06 · playbook id `w10-football`

> Education, not legal advice. Nothing here promises an offer, aid, a roster spot or playing time. We do not contact coaches or your athlete. Dates tagged **RULE** come from a bylaw we cite. **PROJECTED** means calculated from current bylaws and not yet published. **RECOMMENDED** and **YOUR PLAN** are our suggestions, not NCAA deadlines. Confirm every date with each school's compliance office.

**Who runs this:** the athlete sends the messages and takes the calls. The parent is project manager: owns the calendar, the budget, the folder and the Sunday check-in. Coaches and families both describe a parent speaking for the athlete as a warning sign [S35; S40].

**How to use it:** do one Stage per sitting. Each Stage ends with a **Done when** line you can check. If you cannot check it, you are not done. Worksheets are in Part C; the Completeness Test is in Part D.

**What the family may do before any date opens.** Nothing in these rules stops an athlete from writing a coach, filling in a questionnaire or going to a camp; the dates limit what the coach may send or say back [S26; S19]. So early outreach is allowed, early *replies* may not come. Silence before the date is the rule working, not a "no."

**New for FBS (effective 2027-01-01).** Bylaw 13.1.5.3 gives a school four recruiting opportunities per prospect from Jan 1 to May 31, at most one a week, two of them only at the athlete's high school; a school visit counts for every prospect there [S1 13.1.5.3, 13.1.5.3.2, 13.1.4.3]. FCS stays on the older rule: two opportunities per contact period after Jan 1 of junior year [S1]. The limit is per program, not per coach. Plan for a spring visit being scarce.

**Two academic lines nothing late can repair.**
1. *The 10/7 lock:* D1 needs 10 of the 16 core courses finished before the seventh semester starts, 7 of them English, math or science. Those grades lock [S1 14.3.1.1(b)].
2. *The finish:* 16 core courses, 2.300 core GPA, graduate, final transcript sent [S1 14.3.1.1(a); S6]. Core GPA is not transcript GPA.
D2 asks for a 2.2 core GPA [S7]. D3 has no NCAA minimum; the school sets it [S7].

**Roster reality.** D1 schools in the settlement are capped at 105 football players [S9]. A roster limit is a ceiling on team size. It is not a count of open spots and says nothing about how many freshmen a school signs [W7-recruitiq 1A; S8]. Designated student-athletes do not count toward the 105 [S10].

**Honest context, not odds.** About 8.1% of high school football players appear on an NCAA roster in the same year (all divisions; 3.4% D1, 2.1% D2, 2.6% D3). That is a ratio of two headcounts for 2024-25. It leaves out NAIA and JUCO and it is not the chance for your athlete [S17]. About 2% of high school athletes receive athletics scholarships across sports [S18].

## Part B. The Stages

### Stage 0. Set up the home base (one evening, any time before 2027-06-15)
**Why:** parents' top complaint is "I don't know where to start" [S40]. A shared folder and a fixed weekly slot remove most of that.
**Do:**
1. Make one shared folder with: film links, transcript, schedule, contact log (Worksheet C3), target list (C2).
2. Pick a fixed 20-minute Sunday slot. Athlete reads the log aloud; parent asks "what is due this week?"
3. Write the spend ceiling for the whole process (camps, travel, any service) in Worksheet C1 before anyone sells you anything. Parents most often report regret over services and showcases that cost hundreds to thousands [S40].
4. Agree who sends: the athlete. Parent may proofread, never sign.
**Done when:** the folder exists, the Sunday slot is on both calendars, and C1 has a dollar figure written in.

### Stage 1. Lock the academics (start now; recheck each semester)
**Why:** the 10/7 and the 16 cores are the only parts of this plan that cannot be fixed late [S1 14.3.1.1].
**Do:**
1. Ask the counselor for the school's NCAA-approved core course list. Check every completed and planned course against it. RECOMMENDED date: 2026-11-15 for the first check.
2. Fill Worksheet C4 (core-course grid). Count courses done, and count how many are English/math/science.
3. Ask the counselor for the date of the first day of the seventh semester. Write it on the calendar. The school sets it; the bylaw only says "before."
4. Open the Eligibility Center certification account. RECOMMENDED date 2027-08-15; 110 USD domestic fee, waivers possible; needed before any D1 official visit [S4; S1 13.6.3].
**Done when:** C4 shows 10 of 16 reachable before the seventh-semester date, 7 of them in English/math/science, and the account number is in the folder.
**If C4 does not reach 10/7:** stop other work, book a counselor meeting this week, and ask in writing what course plan reaches it. Do not assume a senior-year class repairs it [S1 14.3.1.1(b)].

### Stage 2. Build film and a one-page profile (before 2027-06-15)
**Why:** coaches search recruits online [S28; S29]. In one study of 2,644 football recruits, posting highlight film was associated with more offers; this is an association, not proof that posting causes offers [S28]. The same sources say negative online content can hurt, and 17% of staffs in a 2015 survey had rescinded an offer over it [S29].
**Do:**
1. Cut a reel of about 3 to 5 minutes, best plays first, athlete marked clearly in every clip [S31]. Add an info card first: name, class, position, height/weight, school, contact, GPA/test if strong, head coach contact [S32]. Make a 30 to 60 second cut for social.
2. Host it on a public link. Test it in a private browser window; YouTube "unlisted" works with the link, "private" does not [S36; S37].
3. Fill Worksheet C5 (the one-page profile). Use verified numbers: camp-timed 40 or shuttle, not self-timed. Write date and who timed it.
4. Run the social audit (C6) on every account the athlete uses. Remove what you would not want a coach to read.
**Done when:** a stranger can open the film link on a phone in under 10 seconds, C5 fits on one page, and C6 has no open items.

### Stage 3. Build the target list (before 2027-06-15)
**Why:** the question parents ask most is "what level can my kid realistically play?" [S40]. No governing body publishes football measurable standards, and vendors disagree [S27; W7-recruitiq 1C]. So the list is built from rosters and coach feedback, not a universal number.
**Do:**
1. Use Worksheet C2. Aim for 30 to 50 programs across D1, D2, D3, NAIA and JUCO. Every level is a destination.
2. For each: roster page position count, how many seniors at the athlete's position, position coach name and staff email from the official staff page, cost after likely aid (D3 has no athletic aid [S7]), academic fit.
3. Tier them: Reach, Fit, Sure. Roughly 8-12 reach, 15-25 fit, 8-12 sure.
4. Ask the high school coach for an honest read on level and for a reference. If the coach will not help, do the roster work yourself and ask a position coach at a camp.
**Done when:** C2 has 30 rows with every column filled or marked "unknown", and the high school coach has been asked in writing for a level read.

### Stage 4. First contact (2027-06-15 opens coach replies)
**Why:** the first message is the athlete's, in the athlete's voice. After June 15 a D1 coach may reply [S1 13.1.3.1, 13.4.1].
**Do:**
1. Send 5 messages the first week, then 5 more each week. Use Script E1 below. Put the film link in the body.
2. Log every send in C3 the same day.
3. Follow up once after 14 days with new film or news (Script E2). After two unanswered messages move to the next school; coaches report dropping athletes who go quiet or answer slowly [S35].
4. Answer any coach reply within 48 hours (RECOMMENDED). The athlete writes it.
5. Read replies for what they actually say (C7 below). A questionnaire or a camp invite is interest data going to the program, not an offer [S38; W7-access 2d].
**Done when:** 20 messages are logged, every reply has a logged answer, and no follow-up is more than 3 days overdue.

### Stage 5. Camps and junior days: spend by score (Fall 2027 to Spring 2028)
**Why:** camps are the second-biggest worry after services [S40].
**Do:**
1. Score each camp before paying with Worksheet C8 (below). Skip anything under the threshold you set in C1.
2. Prefer camps run by a school on the Fit or Sure list.
3. Afterward, send a thank-you and one new fact within 48 hours.
**Done when:** every camp has a C8 row, the total spend is under the C1 ceiling, and every attended camp has a follow-up logged.

### Stage 6. The visit window (off-campus contact 2028-01-01; official visits from 2028-04-01)
**Do:**
1. Check the NCAA sport calendar for dead and quiet periods before booking any trip. No lane has read the football calendar for you; confirm it on the NCAA recruiting calendar page [S19].
2. Before an official visit: certification account open, transcript sent to the school, athlete on the school's visit list [S1 13.6.3].
3. Use the visit questions (C9). Score the visit that night (C10).
4. August of senior year: FBS/FCS schools may host a paid visit within 48 hours either side of a home game [S1 13.6.2.1.7.1].
**Done when:** every visit has a C10 score dated within 24 hours.

### Stage 7. Offers, comparison, signing (2028-11-15 onward)
**Do:**
1. A verbal offer binds nobody. A written aid offer cannot be made before 2028-11-15 [S1 13.9.3.3].
2. Put every offer in Worksheet C11 on the same terms: dollar amount, years, what happens if the head coach leaves, roster status.
3. Ask each school's compliance office, in writing: is the aid for one year or multiple years, and what is in writing about roster status. Scholarship protection language for opted-in D1 schools says aid cannot be revoked for roster management, performance or injury unless the athlete chooses to transfer [S8]; ask what applies to *this* school.
4. Signing ends other schools' recruiting of the athlete. Sign only when C11 is complete.
5. Request final amateurism certification from 2029-04-01 [S5].
**Done when:** C11 has no blank cell for the offer being accepted and the athlete has chosen with the parent at the table.

## Part E. Scripts (athlete's voice, parent proofreads)

**E1. First email.** Subject: `2029 [Position] | [Ht/Wt] | [School], [City, ST] | Film`
> Coach [Last name], I'm [Name], a Class of 2029 [position] at [School] in [City, ST]. [Height], [weight]. [One verified number, who timed it, date]. My film: [public link]. GPA [x.x]; core courses on track. I would like to know what you look for at [position] and whether I should come to your camp. Schedule: [link]. My head coach: [name, phone, email]. Thank you, [Name], [phone].

**E2. Follow-up (14 days, no reply).**
> Coach [Last name], new film from [game, date]: [link]. I remain interested in [School]. [Name]

**E3. After a camp (within 48 hours).**
> Coach [Last name], thank you for [camp, date]. One thing I took from it was [specific]. My updated film is [link]. [Name]

**E4. Asking the high school coach.**
> Coach [Last name], I'm building a list of colleges. Would you give me an honest read on the level you see for me, and would you be willing to speak to a college coach if one calls? Here is my list: [link]. Thank you, [Name]
