# 02 - Football defense and specialists: stats, sizes, kicking, and the model email

Compiled 11 Nov 2026. Every URL below was accessed 11 Nov 2026. Reader: class of 2029 (10th grade now; junior season is fall 2027, about 9 months away).
Scope: DL (nose, end, edge), LB (inside, outside, WILL, MIKE), DB (corner, safety, field safety), K, P.

## Source labels used in this file
- [COACH] college coaches said it publicly (named, or quoted as a group by a trade outlet).
- [OFFICIAL] official roster, association or event page.
- [SERVICE] a recruiting service, camp company or private kicking coach publishes it. They sell to families. Useful, not neutral.
- [MEDIA] journalist, columnist or trade magazine. [STUDY] peer reviewed.
- [FOLKLORE] repeated on boards and in parent groups; no primary source found.
- UNCONFIRMED = not sourced; the source type that would settle it is named.

Known limits: (a) I found no named, on-record position coach at each of the five levels giving a tackle-count rule, so the level-by-level "weight" is partly inference and is flagged. (b) Roster pulls for UW-Whitewater and Minnesota State returned the first 106 listed players each and Wisconsin only 36, so treat them as samples. (c) cdc.gov growth charts returned HTTP 403 here.

|---|---|---|---|

| FCS | Low. Same order as FBS with slightly lower size bars. | Same, plus all-conference level honors | Same |

### 1.3 What to give instead of a bare tackle number, by position
| Position | Use | Discount or drop |
|---|---|---|
| DL nose / tackle | TFL, sacks, games started, whether he plays both ways, weight and how he carries it, get-off on film | Total tackles; bench max; sacks with no opponent level |
| DE / edge | Sacks, TFL, QB hurries, forced fumbles, height, measured arm length, verified 40 and shuttle | Sacks piled up against small-division tackles |
| MIKE / inside LB | Solo and assisted shown separately, TFL, games played, signal caller or captain, special teams units | One combined tackle total; "led team in tackles" with no games count |
| WILL / outside LB | TFL, sacks, PBU, INT, coverage clips, verified speed | Same |
| CB | PBU, INT, verified 40, track times, man-coverage clips | Tackle total (a corner with many tackles is being thrown at) |
| S / field safety | PBU, INT, forced fumbles, open-field tackles on film, verified speed, special teams | Combined tackle total |

Three things the brief asked about that I could not source:
- Missed-tackle rate and run-stop rate: these are charting stats from college and pro film services. I found no high-school stat service that publishes them and no coach asking a recruit for them. UNCONFIRMED. Do not print one in an email; a staff derives it from full-game film. Settled by: Hudl and MaxPreps stat definitions, and program questionnaires.
- "Counted on both sides of a blowout": no source found. UNCONFIRMED. What NCSA does support is that school size and conference strength change how every number is read. Practical substitute: name the conference, the WIAA division, the team record and the two or three best opponents, and link one full game against the best opponent.
- Competition level as a formal adjustment: the [SERVICE] and [MEDIA] sources all say it matters; none gives a formula.

### 2.3 What elite linebackers looked like in high school
[MEDIA] 247Sports study of 27 top linebacker NFL draft picks as high-school prospects: height 5'11" to 6'4", weight 190 to 260, average 6'1.9" and 216 lb. Verified HS testing existed for 14 of the 27: 40 from 4.39 to 4.80, average 4.61; shuttle 4.09 to 4.68, average 4.36; vertical 30.8 to 39.7 in, average 35.1. https://247sports.com/article/college-football-linebackers-in-the-nfl-draft-trends-147143058
So even future top-of-draft linebackers ran as slow as 4.80 verified in high school, and none weighed under 190.

### 2.6 What "verified" and "one-day" mean in practice
- Hand versus electronic: [STUDY, reported by NASE] 81 Division I players, hand-timed 4.90 versus electronic 5.12, a 0.22 s gap; novice timers were fastest at 4.86. https://www.naseinc.com/blog/hand-timed-vs-electronic-timed-40-yard-dash/
- [SERVICE, timing vendor] Zybek: a hand start is 0.10 to 0.26 s fast, average 0.18. https://zybeksports.com/the-time-difference-between-hand-and-electronic-timing/
- [MEDIA] coachad: stopwatches run 0.20-0.24 fast and "college coaches know this and they discount hand-timed results accordingly."
- [SERVICE] NCSA: coaches want third-party verified height and weight; the free Rivals Camp Series is named as the easy way to get it (URL in 1.1).
- So "verified" covers two different things: (1) a neutral third party measured it (the WFCA combine qualifies for height, weight and jumps), and (2) the time came from laser or fully automatic gates (the 2026 WFCA 40 does not; it was hand-timed by professionals). Say which.
- "One-day" time: I found no published definition. UNCONFIRMED. In camp usage it means every number came from the same event on the same day and surface, not a best-of across months. Settled by: testing protocol pages of camp operators and college prospect camps.
- Working conversion: a 4.9 hand time is roughly a 5.1 electronic time.

### 3.2 How many reps
- Self-made charted video, [SERVICE] National Kicking Service: field goals 3 to 5 each from 35, 45 and 55-60 yd (9 to 15 kicks; shorter distances for younger kickers); 5 consecutive kickoffs; 10 consecutive punts; then 3 punts filmed for hand-to-foot time, minimum 35 yd. Camera never stops; "a best-of set of kicks is far less impressive." Most kickers need 4 to 5 sets to get a keeper. A 1 or 2 inch block is acceptable until after senior season. Film from the press box, calm dry day, 65 F or warmer. (kickpunt URL above.)
- [SERVICE] Chris Sailer Kicking: one-day camps run 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.; national Vegas events are Saturday instruction and Sunday charting and competition; open to 8th grade and up; not invitation-only. One published field-goal chart: 8 kicks with live snap and hold, 2 points dead center, 1 point for other makes, 1 bonus for 8 of 8, maximum 17. A published punt chart leader (class of 2017): average 48.6 yd and 4.45 s, best ball 60 yd / 5.03 s. https://www.chrissailerkicking.com/faq , https://www.chrissailerkicking.com/camps , https://sailersblog.com/tag/camden-wise/
- [SERVICE] Kohl's: rankings use charting numbers, drill work and camp competitions; updated three times a year (January, May, July); only athletes seen in person, and a Showcase or the Scholarship Camp is required to be ranked. Profile pages show 3 to 5 charted balls per kickoff or punt set. Field-goal chart scores are written as "13 of 15" at showcases and "30/36" at the Scholarship Camp, so roughly 15 and 36 points are available; exact kick counts are UNCONFIRMED (settled by Kohl's camp-results pages). https://www.kohlskicking.com/national-player-ratings and https://www.kohlskicking.com/national-player-ratings/kickers/2029

### 3.3 Does a kicking video beat a stat line? Yes, and the data shows why
- [SERVICE] NKS: charted video is "especially important if a player does not have sufficient opportunities on the field." Its sample cover letter leads with the chart ("average 70 yards and 3.75 seconds on 5 consecutive kick offs"), then two game-winning kicks, then awards.
- Evidence from [SERVICE] Kohl's profiles: the class-of-2029 Wisconsin kicker ranked No. 32 in the country attempted only 5 field goals in 13 listed games in fall 2026 (made 3: 25, 37 and 48 yd) and had 28 touchbacks on 56 kickoffs. A second Wisconsin 2029 kicker went 4 for 4 with a long of 38. Four or five attempts cannot rank anyone; the chart and the continuous video do. https://www.kohlskicking.com/player-profiles/ronan-byrnes and https://www.kohlskicking.com/player-profiles/sawyer-uttech (public pages about minors; do not name them in the playbook).
- A named special teams coordinator saying this on the record: UNCONFIRMED.
- Useful game numbers that do carry weight: touchbacks over kickoffs, field goals made over attempted with the long, PAT made over attempted, punts inside the 20.

## 4. A realistic class-of-2029 defender now, and what he must add by junior season

### 4.1 What is wrong with "6 foot 1, 148 pounds, middle linebacker, 4.9 forty"
1. The weight does not exist at the position. Against the [SERVICE] Go Big linebacker targets he is 72 lb under Power 4 (220), 62 under Group of 6 / high FCS (210), 52 under low FCS / high D2 / high NAIA (200) and 42 under low D2 / NAIA / D3 (190). Against [OFFICIAL] rosters, the lightest listed linebacker is 214 at Wisconsin (partial capture), 210 at North Dakota State, 195 at Minnesota State (D2) and 188 at UW-Whitewater (D3). He is 40 lb under the lightest D3 freshman linebacker.
2. He is lighter than college defensive backs too. Every listed DB at North Dakota State and Minnesota State is 170 or more; at UW-Whitewater only one listed DB (143) is lighter than 148.
3. The speed does not rescue the frame. An unlabeled 4.9 reads as a hand time, about 5.1 electronic (section 2.6). That is slower than every Go Big linebacker tier (4.6 to 4.75) and every DB tier (4.5 to 4.7). A 148-lb athlete is expected to be the fast one.
4. The combination contradicts itself. 6'1" matches the Group of 6 linebacker height target, 148 is below a D3 corner, and a MIKE lives in the box taking on guards. A coach reads it as an inflated height, a mislabeled position (he is a safety or receiver), or a template nobody checked.
