# 05 - Grades and academic eligibility

Research for the W22 email playbook. Every source accessed 11 Nov 2026.
Audience: US class of 2029 (10th graders in 2026-27), NCAA DI, DII, DIII, NAIA, junior college.

Evidence labels used below:
- [FULL] I loaded and read the page or PDF itself.
- [EXCERPT] search-index excerpt only. The page was not read in full. Re-fetch before printing a number from it.
- [NOT LOADED] page failed or was not reached. Nothing below rests on it.

Exact quotes sit in the fenced block at the end as Q1 to Q15. Typographic quotes are replaced with straight quotes and PDF line-break hyphens are closed up. Wording is otherwise unchanged.

## Corrections the copy needs

1. "14.2 GPA": delete. No US scale found in this pass produces it (section 1).
2. "5.2 GPA": only readable with the scale named. It is impossible on a 4.0 or 5.0 scale and ordinary on a 6.0 scale.
3. NCAA DI and DII require no SAT or ACT for initial eligibility. There is no sliding scale and no "exemption with conditions" any more. The test was removed outright, effective 1 Aug 2023 (Q2, Q8, Q9).
4. NCAA uses its own core-course GPA on a 4.0 scale, so the transcript GPA is not the NCAA number (Q1).
5. A sophomore cannot claim to be "NCAA eligible" or "cleared". The Eligibility Center decides after graduation, or after six semesters for an early academic qualifier (Q3, Q4, Q8).
6. NAIA: a 2.3 final high school GPA is enough by itself. No test needed on that path (Q11, Q12).

## 1. GPA scale reality

What the sources show:
- Unweighted 4.0. College Board calls the 4.0 scale "just one common example" and says colleges may recalculate (Q14) [FULL]. California's Cal Grant GPA must be on an unweighted 4.00 scale (Q15) [EXCERPT].
- Weighted 5.0. Florida state rule: standard A = 4.0, honors A = 4.5, accelerated A = 5.0. The excerpt applies this statewide scale to students entering 9th grade in 2026-27 and later, and leaves the 2025-26 cohort (the class of 2029) on existing policy, so check the district. North Carolina state policy: honors +0.5, AP/IB/CIE +1.0 for classes entering 9th grade from 2015-16. DC Public Schools: A = 4.0, honors 4.5, AP/IB/dual enrollment 5.0 (Q15) [EXCERPT].
- Weighted 6.0. Broward County FL: AP, AICE, IB and dual enrollment A = 6.0 quality points per credit, honors A = 5. Hays CISD TX: AP tier 6.0. Duncanville ISD TX: 6.0 weighted scale from the class of 2028 (Q15) [EXCERPT].
- 100-point. Duncanville ISD reported weighted averages on a 100-point scale with up to 12 points added for advanced courses, so a weighted 100-point average can pass 100 (Q15) [EXCERPT].
- 4.3 scale (A+ = 4.3): not checked against a primary source in this pass. Cite one before printing it.
- AP and dual enrollment: the bump depends on the district. The same A in AP is 5.0 in DC and 6.0 in Broward's district GPA. Dual enrollment gets the top weight in both. For NCAA, a dual-enrollment course counts only if it is on the high school transcript with grade, credit and a dual-enrollment label (Q6) [FULL].
- NCAA core-course GPA: 4.0 scale, A=4 B=3 C=2 D=1, no plus or minus, weighting only if the school's weighting scale is approved by the Eligibility Center (Q1) [FULL].
- NAIA: takes the GPA the high school reports, with minimums stated "on a 4.0 scale"; if the transcript carries several GPAs, the one used for class rank (Q11, Q12) [FULL].

Can any common US scale give 14.2? Not one I found. The scales above top out at 4.0, 5.0, 6.0 or about 100 (112 with Duncanville's old add-on). 14.2 is above every point scale and would be a failing mark on a 100-point scale. Limit of this finding: I checked a handful of state and district policies, not every US school, and some schools use unusual scales. A 14.2 average does exist on 20-point scales used outside the US (not sourced here). Most likely it is a typo for 4.2. Treat it as an error.

Why "5.2" needs a scale: on a 5.0 scale it cannot exist; on a 6.0 scale it is a mid-range weighted record. A coach in another state cannot tell which. The number means nothing without its maximum.

Recommended print format (my recommendation, not a rule from any association):

    GPA: 3.62 unweighted (4.0 scale) | 4.31 weighted (5.0 scale) | through 9th grade, Jun 2026 | unofficial transcript attached

Optional NCAA line for DI/DII targets:

    NCAA core courses: 5 of 16 done | core GPA 3.4 (my own count on the NCAA worksheet, not certified)

100-point school:

    Average: 91.4 out of 100, unweighted (my school does not report a 4.0 GPA)

Rules for the family: copy the numbers exactly as the transcript prints them, two decimals, no rounding up. Always print the scale maximum. Say which term the grades run through. Never convert to 4.0 yourself without saying "my own conversion". Never print a bare number.

## 2. NCAA Division I

Sources: NCAA 2026-27 Guide for the College-Bound Student-Athlete, dated August 2026 [FULL]; NCAA web page "NCAA Division I Initial Eligibility Requirements" [FULL]; Initial-Eligibility Standards brochure, August 2026 [FULL].

- 16 NCAA-approved core-course credits: English 4, math (Algebra I or higher) 3, science 2, extra English/math/science 1, social science 2, additional 4 (any of those areas or world language, comparative religion, philosophy) (Q3, Q7).
- Finish the 16 in eight semesters from the start of 9th grade (Q3).
- 10/7 rule: 10 of the 16, including 7 in English, math or science, before the seventh semester starts. For the class of 2029 that is before 12th grade starts in fall 2028 (Q3).
- Minimum core-course GPA 2.3 (Q3).
- Sliding scale, current form: none. The GPA-plus-test index went with the test requirement. A flat 2.3 applies.
- SAT/ACT: not part of NCAA initial eligibility. DI Proposal 2022-34 was adopted by the Council on 11 Jan 2023 and final on 12 Jan 2023, effective 1 Aug 2023 for students first enrolling full time on or after that date (Q9) [EXCERPT for the proposal; the Guide statement in Q2 is FULL]. Classes of 2027, 2028 and 2029 all enroll after that date, so one rule covers all three: no test for NCAA eligibility. I did not load the current text of DI Bylaw 14.3.1.1 from LSDBi.
- Early academic qualifier: 14 core credits and a 3.0 core GPA on the record before the seventh semester (Q10) [EXCERPT].
- Not met: academic redshirt (aid and first-term practice, no competition) or nonqualifier.

What a class-of-2029 athlete plans for today: four core courses a year from the school's NCAA-approved list; 10/7 locked by the end of 11th grade (June 2028); core GPA above 2.3, and 3.0 if they want early qualifier status; no test for the NCAA, but check each college's admission and scholarship rules, which the Guide says may still want scores (Q2). The Guide says it is updated yearly and newer rule changes appear on ncaa.org, so re-check each August.

## 3. NCAA Division II

Sources: same Guide [FULL]; LSDBi Division II Proposal 2023-4 [FULL].

- 16 core courses: English 3, math 2, science 2 (one lab if offered), extra English/math/science 3, social science 2, additional 4 (Q4, Q8).
- Minimum core-course GPA 2.2 on a 4.0 maximum (Q4, Q8).
- Test: none. Proposal 2023-4 was adopted 14 Jan 2023, effective 1 Aug 2023 for students first enrolling full time on or after that date (Q8).
- "Exemption conditions": there are none to list. It is not an exemption. The COVID-era waiver was replaced by permanent removal. What remains: proof of graduation, 16 core courses, 2.200 core GPA.
- Early academic qualifier: 2.500 core GPA in 14 core courses after six semesters (3 English, 3 math, 2 science, 6 other core) (Q8).
- Not met: partial qualifier, who may practice and receive aid but not compete in year one (Q4).

## 4. NAIA

Sources: NAIA Guide for the College-Bound Student-Athlete, PDF on play.mynaia.org [FULL]; NAIA interpretations page "Basics of Freshman Eligibility" [FULL]; naia.org "High School Students" page [FULL].

- Standard: graduate of an accredited high school with a 2.3 or higher final GPA, or meet two of three: 18 ACT or 970 SAT; 2.0 GPA on a 4.0 scale; top half of the class (Q11, Q12).
- Test-optional path: the 2.3 GPA alone, or 2.0 GPA plus top-half rank. No core-course list ("no core courses necessary").
- Early decision before graduation: the two NAIA sources disagree. The Guide PDF says 3.0 after junior year (2.5 midway through senior year) "and the minimum test scores required" (Q11). A naia.org Eligibility Center page excerpt says 3.3 without a test score or 3.0 with one [EXCERPT; the live page redirected to naia.org/explore on 11 Nov 2026]. Do not print early-decision numbers until PlayNAIA is checked.
- Cost: US or Canadian high school student $110, one time, nonrefundable; international $170; fee waiver through the counselor for students with demonstrated need (Q11).
- Processing time: not found. No page I could load states one. Do not print a number of days.
- Amateurism: I found no separate amateurism certificate or form in the NAIA Guide. The student lists sports history in the PlayNAIA profile and the Eligibility Center issues one eligibility decision. The Guide lists what ends amateur standing (Q11).
- News to check: naia.org says the membership approved a fifth year of eligibility on 4 Nov 2026, while the Guide PDF still says four seasons in 10 semesters (Q12).

## 5. Division III and junior college

- DIII: "Division III schools set their own admissions and academic standards." No NCAA GPA floor, no Eligibility Center academic certification for domestic students, no athletics scholarships. The student registers a free Profile Page to get an NCAA ID (Q13) [FULL]. The number that matters is the college's own admission bar. Send what admissions reads: transcript, school profile, test scores only if that college asks.
- NJCAA: entrance eligibility is proof of high school graduation, by final high school transcript after graduation, equivalency exam, or a letter on school letterhead. No high school GPA or test minimum appears in the Article V sections I read. Then full-time enrollment (12 credits). With zero prior full-time semesters the academic grid requires 0 credits (Q13) [FULL for the sections quoted, NJCAA Bylaws 2026-27, file dated 27 Oct 2026]. Send the transcript and expected graduation date.
- Not covered: California (CCCAA) and Northwest (NWAC) junior colleges run under their own rules. Not researched here.

## 6. Documents and who sends them

| Document | Who holds or sends it | Timing for the class of 2029 |

| Official transcript, NCAA | The counselor uploads it to the student's Eligibility Center account. One from every high school attended. The counselor cannot upload until the student has an account and has added the school (Q5). | After four semesters (June 2027) and six semesters (June 2028). |

| Counselor or school letter | Real uses found: NAIA class-rank letter, signed, on letterhead with the school seal, when rank is not on the transcript (Q11). NJCAA graduation letter on letterhead, signed by an administrator, stating the graduation date, when the transcript cannot show it (Q13). | After graduation. |

NCAA final athletics certification can be requested from 1 April 2029 for fall enrollees (Q5). |

What a family can honestly write in a first email:
- "Unofficial transcript attached." True whenever they attach it.
- "GPA 3.62 unweighted on a 4.0 scale through June 2026, as printed on my transcript." True if copied exactly.
- "I have an NCAA Profile Page. My NCAA ID is ..." True once registered. The Guide tells students to share the ID with recruiting schools.
- "My counselor, [name, email], will send an official transcript to your admissions office on request." True only after the family has asked the counselor.

What is not true or not theirs to promise:
- "My counselor can send a transcript today." The family does not control the counselor's day, school offices close, and the NCAA upload only works once the account lists the school. Write "on request", not "today".
- "NCAA eligible", "cleared", "qualifier". The Guide says the Eligibility Center determines academic status after graduation (Q3). The only earlier status is early academic qualifier, after six semesters.
- "Certified core GPA". A family count on the NCAA worksheet is an estimate. Label it "my own count".
- "NAIA eligible". The NAIA Eligibility Center decides, and not before the end of junior year.

## 7. Why an emailed GPA is unreadable across states, and the fix

- Grade cutoffs differ. North Carolina: 90-100 = A. DC Public Schools: 93-100 = A and 90-92 = A-, worth 3.7 (Q15). The same 91 is a 4.0 in one and a 3.7 in the other. College Board: "others have different grade cutoffs" (Q14).
- Plus and minus. NCAA ignores them: B+, B and B- are all 3 points (Q1). Many schools count them.
- Pass/fail. NCAA assigns the school's lowest passing grade, so a "pass" can count as a D (Q1).
- Numeric grades. NCAA converts them to letters using the grading scale the high school has on file (Q1).
- Several GPAs on one transcript. NAIA uses the one that sets class rank (Q12).
- Class rank. NAIA uses top-half rank as one of its three tests. If the transcript shows no rank, it needs a signed school letter or nine college credit hours at C or better (Q11, Q12). Rank without class size tells a coach nothing.
- Narrative or mastery transcripts. I found no NCAA or NAIA page on these in this pass, so I state no rule. What Q1 does establish is that the Eligibility Center works from letter grades and the school's scale on file. A family at such a school should ask the counselor how the school reports grades to the Eligibility Center and say so in one line.
- Share of schools that no longer rank: not sourced here. Do not print a percentage.

The fix: name the scale and its maximum, name the term the grades run through, attach the unofficial transcript and the school profile, list letter grades in English, math, science and social science if the school reports no GPA, and give rank only as printed on the transcript with class size ("41 of 412").
