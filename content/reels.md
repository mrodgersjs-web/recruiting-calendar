# Commit Book Highlight Reel Lane (Film and Video Guidance)

Source: five Commit Book highlight-reel research reports and playbooks prepared by James Loehr, November 12 2026, revised November 17 2026. All five are review drafts. Nothing in this lane is published, no price is set, and the website is unchanged. Website status was checked November 17 2026.

## The core principle across every sport

A recruiting reel is evaluation footage, not a cinematic montage. Its only job is to let a coach identify the athlete and judge real, position-relevant play. A reel introduces the player. It cannot promise coach attention, an offer, a roster spot, or a scholarship.

The rules every sport agrees on:
- Lead with the strongest, clearest, most position-relevant clips. Put them in the first 30 seconds.
- Use live competition footage at real speed. No slow motion, no speed ramps, no zooming, no whip transitions.
- No music. Natural sound or mute. Coaches mute the tape if they have to.
- Identify the athlete once, before the play: name, jersey number and color, graduation year, primary position, school or club. Use a quick circle, arrow, or spot shadow pre-snap or pre-rally, then remove it so it never covers the play.
- Keep the camera steady, wide, and elevated so the whole play or rally can be read.
- Keep the full game or full match ready as a second link, and test that link logged out on a phone.
- Refresh with recent film as the season progresses. Do not add weak clips just to lengthen the reel.
- Two cuts, not one: an evaluation cut for coaches, and a separate social cut if a program wants to post. Never ship the evaluation cut with a soundtrack.

What stays variable and must never be sold as a rule: exact reel length, exact clip counts, how much academic or contact detail goes on screen, and how many game situations are enough.

## Football highlight reel

- Length: sources split between 3 to 5 minutes and 2 to 3 minutes. Neither is a rule. Use only strong clips and stop when the reel has shown the relevant skills.
- Front-load: one source says put the 4 to 5 most explosive plays inside the first 30 seconds. That is one source's rule of thumb, not a governing-body standard. Another guide says 15 to 25 clips. Another wants the best play inside the first 2 to 3 snaps.
- Camera: elevated and whole-play. Good views are the end zone / press box angle (shows line, backfield, and secondary at speed, closest to an all-22 cut) and a side elevated wide view. Poor views are ground-level sideline close to one player and a tight sideline that follows the ball, because both hide spacing, releases, run fits, and the blocks and routes a coach is grading.
- Do not shoot from the track. Ground-level sideline film loses spacing and alignment.
- Group clips by position. Keep the primary position's clips together before any secondary position.
- Title cards at both ends: name, grad year, position, height and weight, GPA, coach contact. Add measurements or grades only when true, current, and clearly marked. No home address, no minor's private contact details.
- Offense position evidence: quarterback (variety of throws, placement, timing, footwork, pressure response, throws on the run, runs in the offense; not only deep balls, screens, or clean-pocket throws); running back (reads, patience at the line, burst, cutbacks, inside runs, contact balance, ball security, open-field decisions, receiving, pass protection); wide receiver (releases, route variety, separation, tracking, contested catches, sideline awareness, yards after catch, blocking); tight end and H-back (routes and catches, inline or slot alignment, run blocking, pass protection, pulls, finishing); fullback (lead blocks, awareness, toughness, hands, role-specific receiving); offensive line (stance, first step, leverage, hands, balance, pass sets, run blocks, pulls, second-level blocks, finish, preserving the full rep).
- Defense position evidence: edge and defensive end (get-off, first steps, hand use, leverage, rush moves, setting the edge, run defense, pursuit, effort; do not make sacks the only evidence); defensive tackle and nose (reading blocks, gap discipline, strength at the point of attack, shedding, lateral movement, pocket pressure); inside linebacker (reading keys, run fits, gap discipline, block defeat, tackling, coverage, pursuit; a former Division I linebackers coach stresses instincts and scheme understanding over tackle totals); outside linebacker and hybrid (space coverage, run support, block defeat, blitz, change of direction); corner, nickel, safety (coverage from more than one look, stance and leverage, backpedal and transition, route recognition, ball skills, recovery, tackling in space, special teams; a no-target rep belongs only when the coverage is clear on screen); returner and two-way (return decisions, vision, ball security, in a clearly marked second section or second reel).
- Specialists need a different film plan. NCSA recommends mostly skills footage for kickers and punters; Go Big Recruiting recommends game footage as well. Kicker: field goals from varied spots, PATs, kickoffs, and the result, with a steady camera showing ball flight and landing. Punter: the full punt and its result (flight, landing, direction, game context), representative punts rather than a highlight-only set that hides consistency; one NCSA guide suggests 15 to 20 punts, labeled as its recommendation only. Long snapper: show the punter catching the snap plus a side view of technique; Go Big suggests filming a few yards in front and to the side.
- Editing test: can a coach see the athlete's assignment, technique, and result without guessing where to look? If not, fix the framing or leave the clip out.
- Do not overclaim. These are evidence checklists, not proof of a player's level or fit. Evaluation depends on scheme, competition, role, and the full film.

## Volleyball highlight reel (indoor; beach is out of scope)

- Length: sources give 3 to 5 minutes and 2 to 4 minutes. Length is a recommendation, not a rule.
- One service claims coaches decide in 10 to 25 seconds but publishes no method or sample size. Do not sell a guaranteed attention window.
- Camera: stable, wide, elevated endline or corner showing court coverage, movement, and the ball. Keep both sides of the rally visible. Do not pan wildly, zoom in and out, or lose the ball. Do not shoot tight from floor level where near bodies block the far side.
- Start the clip early enough to show the approach, read, or move into position, and let the rally finish. Do not cut in at contact or trim the ball out of frame.
- Build the core from live match rallies. Several coach sources favor live play over coach-fed drills because a drill cannot show reads, reactions, or transition. Use skills reps only as a separate supplement.
- Link: coach responses collected by the JVA specifically warn against video links that require a sign-in. Test the link on a phone where the viewer is not signed in.
- Two-video model: a short position-specific reel as the introduction, plus a full match or full set for deeper evaluation. Sources disagree on an edited full set versus an unedited match, so keep the original and offer it on request.
- Position evidence: setter (setting from live passes and digs not only perfect tosses, sets to both pins and the middle, in-system and out-of-system decisions, movement to a tight pass, transition from block or defense, plus front-row blocking, defense, serve, and attack when part of the role); outside hitter (attacks from the primary pin with varied shots and tempo, transition from defense or block to attack, and for a six-rotation role serve receive from different back-row zones, pass-to-attack, back-row attack, blocking, defense, including difficult first contacts and range); opposite or right side (attacks off a live setter from the right side, shot choices, block-to-attack transition, blocking the opposing outside, plus back-row defense and passing only if the athlete plays that role); middle blocker (lateral footwork, closing to both pins, reading and blocking, quick attacks off a live setter, transition, varied tempos and routes, plus serve or free-ball handling when it reflects the assignment; avoid a reel showing only height or open-net swings); libero (serve receive from different back-row zones, platform control and direction to the setter, reading the server and hitter, defensive range, movement from base, coverage, communication, longer rallies or multiple contacts; do not make attack clips the core of a libero reel); defensive specialist (back-row role through serve receive, live defense, range, communication, serve, out-of-system second contact); serving specialist (a short separate serving section only if serving is the main role, showing motion, placement or pressure, and the next play); utility and two-way (primary position first, then a clearly labeled secondary section).
- Six-rotation label matters. An outside or opposite seeking a six-rotation role should make back-row skills visible. A front-row-only hitter should label that honestly.
- Open conflict, points versus process: one article says show only successful outcomes such as kills, good passes, and points from serves or blocks. A coach writing on a coaching blog counters that a kill, ace, or stuff block is not automatically a good rep because movement and decision-making matter. Commit Book's recommendation: pick clean clips that match the position, then let the rally show the result. Do not cut out the process just to make every clip end in a point.
- Rep counts conflict: one position guide suggests 5 to 7 swings, another service guide says 10 to 15. Treat counts as samples, then choose for quality and variety.

## Basketball highlight reel

- Basketball is the loudest demand and the thinnest evidence in the family. One college head coach reports receiving 200 to 300 highlight videos a day, and NCSA says it edits more than 40,000 recruiting videos a year. A coach does not review a reel, he triages it: the first four clips form the impression and the rest confirms them.
- Length: published guidance runs 90 seconds, 2 minutes, 3 to 4, 3 to 5, 4 to 6, and "maybe 7", with a 5-minute ceiling. Commit Book would teach 3 to 4 minutes as the highest published floor, not a coach-published standard.
- Clips: 20 to 30 clips at 5 to 15 seconds each, best five or six first, non-chronological.
- Basketball's all-22 is the wide elevated mid-court shot. NCSA: focus the camera from mid-court while making sure the student-athlete is easily recognizable. Both three-point lines visible, all ten players, jersey number readable. A vendor guide states elevated midcourt beats baseline because a baseline angle shows post moves beautifully but obscures perimeter spacing. Zoom only at the free-throw line.
- Free throws are a negative, not filler. Coaches do not want to see free throws, warm-ups, or dunk-contest clips. One published exception exists from a single independent source: free throws are allowed only if you have no jump-shot footage.
- Include at least two defensive clips and one screen-the-screener clip. Coaches grade cutting and screening action. A reel that shows only offense is a warning sign.
- Do not lead with a layup line. Uncontested breakaway finishes read as padding.
- Deliver by Hudl link or unlisted YouTube. Unlisted YouTube lets you share directly with coaches without making it broadly searchable.
- Attach one or two full, unedited games as a second link, labeled clearly so nobody watches the wrong one. Some coaches only evaluate game film, and a highlight-only tape invites the question of what was left out.
- Women's coaches ask for full game film more insistently than men's sources do. For a girls' reel, treat the unedited game as a second required link, not a nice-to-have.
- Position evidence: point guard (shoot and score, penetrate and finish, ball-handling under pressure plus passing, transition reads, pick-and-roll reads including reject, roll, and pop, the pass to the roller, the pull-up, evidence of guarding a bigger guard; the read matters more than the result); shooting guard (catch-and-shoot from corner, wing, and top of the key, three each with identical form, off-ball movement into shots, drive-and-kick, stopping penetration on the other end, active hands, help defense, no free throws); small forward (15 to 25 clips at 5 to 15 seconds from competitive games, shooting plus off-ball movement, guarding 2 through 4, rebounding from the perimeter, transition both ends, post-up if it exists); power forward (mid-range shooting emphasized over threes at this level, finishing at the rim through contact, offensive and defensive rebounding, rim protection even when not blocking shots, post footwork, screening that frees a guard); center (rim finishing, scoring against traditional post defense, rebounding, shot-blocking, guarding multiple positions, post footwork, pick-and-roll execution and the defensive stop that follows, height and wingspan on the card).
- Framing ruling: shoot the evaluation reel from elevated mid-court whatever the position. A baseline or corner angle can sit on the profile as extra footage but never replaces the wide shot.
- Discard list: free throws, warm-ups, dunk-contest footage, uncontested breakaways, the same shot repeated, 100-plus clips, anything over the length taught. Fix before they cost a look: shaky handheld, zoomed framing, player unidentifiable in a wide shot or matching uniform, graphics on every clip, music with lyrics, one-sided footage that reads as hiding something.
- Front card: five fields, five seconds, no animation: name, grade, position, height, school and club, contact.
- Share of high school players who reach Division I basketball: 1.1 to 1.4 percent.
- Court dimensions for the drawn shot plan: a high-school half court is 50 ft wide and 47 ft deep, the paint is 16 ft wide, and the arc is drawn at the boys' high-school radius while girls' and women's lines are shorter, so print the number for the rule set the family actually plays under.

## Men's baseball highlight reel

- Baseball is the most rule-bound film problem in the family, and the only sport where sources agree on the shape of the deliverable: two videos, not one. A skills cut under three minutes plus a separate uncut game (full at-bats, full innings, labeled separately).
- Published skills-cut lengths run 1 to 2, 2 to 5, 3 to 5, and "five or less". Commit Book would teach under three minutes.
- Numbers must be visible in the frame. Include a radar gun reading in the frame for each pitch if possible. A claimed 90 mph with no gun on screen is worth less than no claim at all. Pop time must be timed on screen with the second baseman in frame, and a home-to-first must be visibly a hard 90. Coaches discount what they cannot watch.
- A coach's decision is fast: Driveline reports most evaluators settle within three to five pitches, and a travel organization's video guide says the first fifteen seconds carry the verdict.
- Camera: three angles per position in a fixed order at a fixed height. Pitchers: open side, then behind the mound on the arm side, then from behind the catcher. Tripod at five feet, same spot every pitch, no zoom, never behind a fence or a cage frame.
- Equipment rules can invalidate a clip. Film hitting with an aluminum bat, never wood. BBCOR and USABat mark requirements change on January 1 2028. The national showcase circuit runs wood-bat-only events. A reel full of illegal-bat swings is a reel a coach cannot use at a showcase.
- The scholarship number everyone quotes is stale. 11.7 remains the ceiling only at non-opt-in programs; opt-in schools operate under a 34-player roster limit from July 1 2025.
- Include consecutive reps with the misses in. A reel of nothing but perfect reps reads as selective.
- Hitters and pitchers ship separate videos. Multi-position players lead with their best position.
- Clean audio: the ball off the bat and into the glove, no parent chatter.
- Measurables slate on the front: height, weight, position, grade, bat and throw, then the pitches.
- Market: 467,530 boys play high-school baseball across 16,257 schools.
- The live baseball playbook already exists at /playbooks/mens-baseball-road-to-college, 27 pages at $29, twelve chapters, film at chapter 6, eleven dated primary sources. So this research becomes a revision, not a new page.
- Do not state as fact: the 20-pitch bullpen norm, exact rep counts per pitch type, and the "three to five pitches decides it" figure. Each comes from one kind of source, a service or a vendor blog with no stated method.

## Fastpitch softball highlight reel

- Softball has the most rule-driven filming constraints and the least consistent advice about game footage. A former Division I head coach quoted by a retailer wants the unedited version ("I don't want you to edit it. I want to see it all. I wanna see you go hard. I wanna see you get dirty") while the most detailed published softball guidelines instruct athletes to send skills footage only and not game footage. Both sit on page one of the research and a family cannot tell them apart.
- Skills-reel length: Commit Book would teach 2 to 3 minutes. Published guidance runs 2 to 3, 3 to 5, and 3 to 4 plus a position deep dive.
- Geometry is different and the playbook must say so: 60-ft base paths, a 43-ft pitching distance, and a flat 16-ft circle. Never 90 ft, never a mound. A 90-ft base path printed in a softball playbook is an instant credibility loss.
- A 2026 rule change makes the athlete the camera's problem: under NFHS rules no player shall transmit or record audio or video from the playing surface, so the parent on the dugout steps is now a rules question, not just a bad angle. Media areas are dead-ball territory. USA Softball rule 1-1-7 applies.
- Camera: film from behind the pitcher. A behind-the-plate angle is worse for pitchers because the catcher gets in the way (one named former Division I head coach against two service guidelines that ask for a behind-the-plate set).
- Benchmarks: a pop time of 1.8 seconds and a home-to-first of 2.9 seconds are the published benchmarks. Every number needs a named source, paired with the clip. Metrics from validated sources such as Blast Motion or Diamond Kinetics.
- Do not ship a hitting-only reel. Show the athlete is more than one-dimensional. Wrong-possession film, such as a pitcher's tape judged as a shortstop's, is a stated reason coaches throw a reel out.
- Label pitch type on screen, group by pitch, and film after a full warm-up. One service's pitching spec is three reps per pitch type then 20 varied pitches with at least five fastballs; print it as that service's spec.
- Music loses a coach's attention; the sound of contact beats a song.
- The calendar is a wall. Division I scholastic evaluation runs August 10 to November 22, and non-scholastic travel tournaments are evaluable only on six specific weekends in autumn 2026: October 17 to 18, October 24 to 25, October 31 to November 1, November 7 to 8, November 14 to 15, and November 21 to 22. Film is what works on the other 359 days.
- Softball has one signing date. There is no spring signing period, and the National Letter of Intent program ended in October 2024.
- Market: 331,162 girls play fastpitch in high school across 15,712 schools.
- The live softball playbook exists at /playbooks/womens-softball-road-to-college, 34 pages at $29, twelve chapters, twelve dated sources.
- Not established, so do not claim: circle-work or pickoff-at-first clip counts, gap awareness or cutoff-relay specs, and opposite-field or inside-out hitting clips, despite appearing in circulated checklists. No source requires a set number of full innings; one vendor argues two complete innings from the backstop and that is a sales position.

## How the chatbot should answer film and reel questions

- Name the sport. Film rules differ sharply by sport, and the reel lane is sport-specific by design.
- Give the exact figure and label it honestly. Lengths and clip counts are source recommendations, never NCAA rules. Say which kind of source said it (coach, service, vendor, association) when the report labels it.
- Always mention the second link: keep the full game or full match ready, and test the link logged out.
- Never promise coach views, replies, offers, roster spots, or scholarships.
- Where sources conflict, name the conflict and give Commit Book's recommendation. Do not average two numbers into a fake consensus.
- Point to the film chapter of the relevant sport playbook at /playbooks and the sport pages at /sports, and to /start for the free class-year plan.
