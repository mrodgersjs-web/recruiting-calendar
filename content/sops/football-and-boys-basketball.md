# Football and boys' basketball: athlete SOP

Source: The Commit Book playbooks, edition 2026-27. Football rules and both printed calendars checked 11 November 2026. Boys' basketball rules and the current calendar checked 10 November 2026. Sample players: Marcus, men's football, class of 2029. Derrick, men's basketball, class of 2029.

This is education. It is not legal advice or school advice. It promises no result, no playing time, no acceptance, and no money. The NCAA Eligibility Center makes the final call on school rules. Each school decides its own aid. Dates marked PROJECTED are worked out from the rule text, not printed by the NCAA yet.

Do not mix the two sports. A football date is not a basketball date. A boys' basketball date is not a girls' basketball date. The boys' book prints the girls' dates so a family does not use the wrong one.

Structured files: `football.json` and `boys-basketball.json`.

## What both books agree on

- Read the roster limit before spending money.
- A working list is 40 to 55 schools across five levels, with the top division as the bonus. A shorter list is a wish.
- The player sends the email, from his own address. The parent owns paperwork and dates.
- The National Letter of Intent is gone. Division I ended it in October 2024. What gets signed now is a written offer of aid, or the division's own paper.
- Once he signs, other schools must stop contacting him. Do not sign early just to feel settled.
- A verbal yes binds nobody.
- Film the season yourself. Mark him in every clip. Keep a full game behind the reel.
- A $5,000 service is mostly appointments and follow-ups. The rules, the dates, and the questions are free.
- The NCAA has not required an SAT or ACT to clear a student starting college from 1 August 2023. Some colleges, the NAIA, and many junior college and Division II offices still use a score. Tests in these books: SAT $68, ACT $70, waivers available.
- PROJECTED dates can land a week early or a week late. Print the NCAA calendar when it is published. Do not trust a 2024 handout for a specific week.

## Football: Marcus, class of 2029

### The plain truth

Football has 670 NCAA teams and rosters of 105 at the top level, but 1.03 million boys play high-school football. Seats exist. They are chased harder than in any other sport. A coach may call, text, or email from 15 June after 10th grade (13.1.3.1). He may meet him off campus from 1 January of 11th grade (13.1.1.1.6). He may not put money in writing before 15 November of 12th grade (13.9.3.3). Talk is early. Paper is late.

There is no limit on how many players a school may sign in a period. The only real limit is the roster cap. A staff can sign 30 in December and still owe none of them a spot in August.

NCAA math, published March 2026: 1,029,588 boys played high-school football in 2024-25 and 83,794 played in the NCAA. That is 8.1% overall: 3.4% to Division 1, 2.1% to Division 2, 2.6% to Division 3. Those figures leave out the NAIA and junior college. On aid: 70% of Division 1 athletes and 77% of Division 2 athletes receive some athletic aid. 80% of Division 3 athletes receive help from the aid office. "Some aid" often means part of tuition, not a free ride.

In January 2026 more than 10,500 college players entered the transfer portal in one 15-day stretch. No NCAA rule limits how much of a football roster can arrive that way.

### Academics

- Unweighted GPA, plain 4.0, printed first. Weighted GPA is useless unless the scale is named.
- Core-course GPA is only the 16 NCAA subjects, computed by the NCAA on a 4.0 scale. It is not his overall GPA.
- Division 1 floor: 2.3. Division 2 floor: 2.2. A 3.0 keeps the early-qualifier option.
- Four approved core courses a year.
- SAT and ACT are not required to clear him. Take one only if the college needs it for admission or aid.

### Video

Three to five minutes. About 25 plays. Best plays first. The first 30 to 60 seconds decide whether the rest is played. A staff decides in about two minutes. Only about one in three Division 1 coaches watches all of the film sent.

High and wide, all 22 players. Arrow or shadow on every play. Start each clip a couple of seconds before the snap. No music, no sound effects, no slow motion, no sped-up clips. Do not edit mistakes out. Full games stay ready.

Quarterback skills tape: 2 to 4 minutes, 2 or 3 reps per drill. A back who never blocks looks like a high-school player. Line: show the kick slide. Linebacker: one drop into a zone.

Email: subject near 70 letters, body near 180 words. The printed sample is 182. From the player. One-page sheet with who measured height and weight, when, and how. A hand-timed 40 runs about 0.2 seconds fast. Two studies put hand times 0.18 to 0.31 seconds fast. Trust order: the college's own camp, then an electronic combine, then a documented laser session. A missing number is read as a bad number.

No association publishes a size or speed standard. Service charts are targets, not rules. School-listed heights run about an inch generous.

Follow up in one to three business days. Ask where he stands and what they still need to see. Do not ask about a scholarship on the first call. Text only after a coach gives a cell number, between 8 in the morning and 9 at night in the coach's time zone, full name signed. An update every six to eight weeks, with one new fact.

### Camps, 7-on-7, combines, visits

Pay for a camp only after a written yes. One combine, not four ($49 to $299). Showcase only if the staff list is published ($96 to $399; about $1,950 for a full package). All-star game if invited and free or nearly free. NFL scouts may not attend a game where players paid to play.

7-on-7 is for reps, not recruiting credit. Named coaches, including a former Stanford head coach, say the film and the stats mean nothing. Entry: $200 to $530 per team, or $20 to $34 per player.

One-day college camps checked for the book: $49 to $125. Multi-day with room and board: $400 to $800 before travel.

Paid visit: one per school before 15 October after graduation and one after (13.6.2.2). A new head coach may add one (13.6.2.2.1). The old yearly cap on how many paid visits a program could hand out was removed in April 2026. Family travel is never free: about $500 to $1,500 for two nights. One target school per trip. Stack a second school in the same state. Free tickets: up to three, inside 30 miles, general seating, none in a dead period (13.7.3.1).

On a visit, ask for the position coach, academic support, year-one role and depth chart, and what happens to aid if he is hurt.

### Money

Plan $3,000 to $6,000 a year and skip the packages. A vendor figure of $3,000 to $10,000 a year, and $15,000 to $50,000 across high school, is the top of the range. One quoted case was $40,000. A national survey puts average spend on one child at about $1,500 a year, and $634 for tackle football. Travel is the big line, not the camp fee. Budget four trips. Skip the $2,000 packages.

Video: $0, or about $20 a month to edit it yourself. One freelance reel: $150 to $538, most jobs $150 to $250. Full-service company: $250 to $2,459. Play-picking: $450 to $700 per game. A $50 to $95 per-game breakdown does the same job.

Recruiting services: big names do not publish prices. Third-party reports say $250 to $2,100 a year, others up to $4,200. Named coaches say those packets go in the trash.

Eligibility Center: $120 on the live page checked 11 November 2026. The printed checklist still says $110. Trust the page where you pay. Free with a test-fee waiver or free school lunch. The profile page is free until a school asks for certification.

### Paper

| Paper | When | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verbal commitment | Any time | Nothing. It only tells other coaches to stop calling. |
| Written offer | On or after 15 November of 12th grade | States the number. He cannot accept it until the window opens. |
| Athletics aid agreement | Early window, then the first Wednesday in February | The real deal. Other schools must stop. |
| Division 2 award letter | From the sport's signing date. No early football window. | One year. Renewal is not automatic. |
| Division 3 package | Ordinary admissions calendar | No sports aid paper. Letters of intent are banned. |
| NAIA school letter | School policy | The NAIA will not enforce a national letter. |
| NJCAA letter | Recent fall date 3 February. Confirm it. | One school year. A parent signs if he is under 18. Leaving takes a written release. |

Aid cannot be cut during the year of the award for a coach's opinion of his play, or for injury or illness. Six listed reasons, and he gets a hearing first (15.3.4.1, 15.3.4.2). A player who loses a roster spot for roster management, performance, or injury keeps his aid unless he chooses to leave.

Preferred walk-on means a roster place and no scholarship. The phrase appears zero times in the 412-page Division 1 rule book. Ask what it becomes in year two. He still registers with the Eligibility Center.

Four questions on any paper: who must sign for the school, what it covers, how long it lasts and what renews it, and what happens to the money if he transfers or is hurt.

### Ten ways families get football wrong

1. They think 85 is the number. At a settlement school it is 105 seats. The scarce thing is a seat, not a scholarship.
2. They read one guide and stop. The calendar left the rule book in October 2026 and is re-ratified each July. FBS and FCS calendars differ. A July evaluation period no longer exists.
3. They pay for 7-on-7 and expect credit.
4. They pay for a camp before anyone has answered an email.
5. They build a list of twelve schools.
6. They let the parent email be the plan.
7. They skip Division 3 and NAIA. Division 3 alone is 243 programs.
8. They sign a junior-college letter to hold a place. Getting out takes that school's release.
9. They ask about academic aid at the visit instead of on the phone. The answer changes the number on the paper.
10. They plan the money backwards. Travel is the big line.

### Grade checklist

**9th.** Get on the field, any unit. Lift with a plan you can write down. Keep every grade. Three folders: film, stats, coach email. Spend nothing on recruiting.

**10th.** Film the whole season from a high wide angle. List of 40 to 55. Learn the calendar. On 15 June after this year, send the first email round.

**11th.** Four approved core courses. Counselor check. Free Eligibility Center page. Paid visit inside the April window. One camp at a school that has answered. Grades in the fall.

**12th, before 1 September.** One update with new film. Expect summer silence. Full games behind the reel. One combine for measured numbers.

**12th, September to November.** Play. Send the schedule. On 15 November, ask for the number and the date in the same email.

**12th, December.** Settle every question before early signing week. Sign only if the paper is right.

**After signing.** Do not let grades fall. Finish the 16 subjects. Graduate. Send the final transcript the week the school asks.

## Boys' basketball: Derrick, class of 2029

The boys' book covers NCAA Division I, Division II, Division III, NAIA, and junior college. It does not use FBS or FCS. Girls' dates in the same PDF are not his dates.

### The plain truth

The Division I limit is fifteen players on the team, paid or unpaid. The old limit was 13 for men and 15 for women. There is no cap on the amount of aid. A coach can carry fifteen and pay only six. No one is promised more than one year at a time.

540,704 boys play high school basketball. 3.6% reach any NCAA school: 1.1% Division I, 1.1% Division II, 1.5% Division III. Across all high school sports, about 2% of athletes get any money for sports. Last year: 1,187 first-year players at Division I, 988 at Division II, 897 at Division III. 61% of those open spots were at Division II and Division III.

A Division I men's coach may contact him from 15 June after 10th grade. Before that, the coach may watch and rate him. The rule is on the coach, not on the family.

Summer basketball is watched. A Division I coach may only see players at events the NCAA has approved. Ask before you pay.

### Video

Exact band: 3:00 to 3:45, never over 4:00. The 10th-grade first reel is 2 to 3 minutes. That is the first cut, not the senior standard.

15 to 25 clips, 5 to 7 seconds each, plus two or three full plays. Best three plays in the first 30 seconds, before any title screen. An intro longer than three seconds gets skipped. Sections: shooting, passing, defense, full possessions. Arrow or spotlight in every clip. Game sound or silence. No music. Camera high at mid-court, tripod, no zoom, scoreboard in frame, clean jersey. Unlisted YouTube link, no password, plus a separate full game. New reel every 6 to 8 weeks, finished by the end of junior year.

Email under 180 words. Subject shape in the book: class year, position, height, unweighted GPA, one number, school. One honest reason. One question. No attachments. Fill out the school form the same day. If no answer, one follow-up 12 to 14 days later with one new fact. Do not write "just checking in." Do not send two emails in one day. After a coach gives a number, a short text every four to six weeks is normal.

One-page sheet: name, position, off-season measured height, weight, class year, Eligibility Center ID, high school, summer team, jersey number and color, town where the video was shot, GPA, test scores if he has them, where he stands on required classes, this season's stats with the source, two or three role numbers, both links, player phone and email, high school coach, parent contact, one line on what he wants to study. No star ranking.

### Mistakes

The boys' book does not print a chapter of ten mistakes. It prints six ways families waste money. Four more wrong moves are stated in the same book. All ten are in `boys-basketball.json`.

1. Showcase before a named coach says he will be there.
2. Top summer circuit when he is not that kind of player. Regional events are where Division II, Division III, and NAIA coaches are.
3. Paying a service for a ranking.
4. Music, free throws, warm-up dunks, offense-only video.
5. Parent-written emails.
6. Waiting for a camp invite from a school that is already texting.
7. Asking how many scholarships they have, instead of how many of the fifteen will be paid.
8. Signing in November to please a friendly coach, or deciding during the window.
9. A list shorter than 40 to 55, or skipping the 40 to 60 done line and the school forms.
10. Advice that still says sign an NLI, including from a high school coach.
