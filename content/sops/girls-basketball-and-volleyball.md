# Commit Book athlete SOPs: Girls' basketball and women's volleyball

Education, not legal advice. No result, no playing time, no acceptance, and no money is promised. Every number below is taken from The Commit Book playbooks checked 11 November 2026. Dates marked PROJECTED were worked out from rule text the NCAA had not printed yet for 2028-29. Structured copies: `girls-basketball.json` and `volleyball.json`.

Sample athletes in these files: Madison Weber (girls' basketball, class of 2029, invented in the playbook email) and Ava (women's volleyball, class of 2029 sample athlete).

## Girls' basketball: the plain truth

Girls' basketball does not run on the same clock as boys' basketball. Among NCAA schools there are 1,077 women's programs against 1,070 men's. The NCAA's own figure is 4.7% of high-school girls playing in the NCAA, against 3.6% of boys. A girls' coach may email or call from 1 June after 10th grade, and may not meet her off campus until 1 September of 12th grade. From 1 April to the end of July a girls' coach may not call, text, or email her at all.

NCAA math, published March 2026: 356,240 girls played high-school basketball in 2024-25, and 16,823 played in the NCAA. That is 4.7% overall: 1.4% Division 1, 1.4% Division 2, 1.9% Division 3. NAIA and junior college are left out of that figure, so the chance of playing somewhere after high school is better than 4.7%.

A family that knows the roster limit, the signing dates, and the video standard competes with families spending three times as much. Most of what a $5,000 service sells is phone calls and follow-ups.

Division 1 seats already held by others: 1,042 players from outside the United States on 2025-26 women's rosters (up from 951), close to one seat in five against about 5,000 players. Of players on 2026-27 Division 1 women's rosters, 20.8% were new transfers. Together, roughly four of every ten Division 1 seats are filled by someone who was not a high-school senior that year.

List length: 40 to 55 schools. Starting split: 8 to 12 Division 1, 10 to 15 Division 2, 10 to 15 Division 3, 5 to 10 NAIA, 3 to 5 junior college schools she would truly attend.

### Contact, visits, signing (class of 2029)

- June 2027: coaches may email, text, and call (13.1.3.1.5 and 13.4.1.6). Send the first round that week.
- January 2028: paid visits may begin (13.6.2.1.6). Each school may pay for one visit before 15 October of the year after she graduates, and one more on or after that date (13.6.2.2). She needs a transcript and Eligibility Center registration first.
- 1 April through 31 July 2028: no calls, no texts, no email. In July, no visit at all unless she has already signed or put down a deposit. Send the last update in March.
- August 2028: written aid offers may go out (13.9.3.1). She cannot accept until the signing window.
- September 2028: first off-campus meeting (13.1.1.1.5).
- 8 to 15 November 2028 (PROJECTED): early signing, the second Wednesday in November through the following Wednesday. No late period in basketball.
- 11 April 2029 (PROJECTED): regular signing opens. The school sets the closing date.
- Junior college letters of intent: from 1 November. Binds one school year. A parent signs if she is under 18. Leaving takes a written release.
- The National Letter of Intent ended in October 2024.

Other limits: seven recruiting opportunities per player per year (13.1.5.5). Women's staff capped at 65 recruiting days a year (13.1.7.6). A Division 1 coach may watch club basketball only at NCAA-approved events (13.18). High-school games always count. Women's June evaluation window: noon Thursday to 6 p.m. Saturday. Spring and July approved events may run 8 a.m. to 10 p.m.

Core GPA floors: 2.3 Division 1, 2.2 Division 2, on the NCAA's 16 subjects, 4.0 scale. A 3.0 keeps the early-qualifier option. NCAA no longer requires an SAT or ACT score to clear her. SAT $68, ACT $70, only if a college needs the score. Eligibility Center: $120 on the live page; the printed checklist still says $110. Trust the page where you pay.

### Video

Two minutes. No slow motion, no music, no free throws. Mark her in the first three plays. If he cannot find her in three seconds, he stops. One full game, wide, from a high angle. Show defense and rebounding. Do not cut every miss. Only one in three Division 1 coaches watches all the video sent. Most decide in about two minutes.

Email from her, subject near 70 letters, body near 180 words. Label who measured every number, when, and how. Height barefoot. Attempts next to every percentage. No money ask on the first contact.

### Money (published prices, checked 11 November 2026)

In 10th and 11th grade a family can do this for $3,000 to $6,000 if it skips the expensive packages. A seller's range is $3,000 to $10,000 a year and $15,000 to $50,000 across high school. Treat that as the high end.

- Club or AAU: $700 to $4,400, plus $100 to $430 uniforms. National circuits start near $4,000. Buy once a year, only if events are NCAA-certified.
- AAU membership: $22 to $24 (1 September to 31 August).
- Tournament entry: $185 to $700 per team. National championship entry $545, or $445 before the early date.
- Day or team camp: $125 to $425, at a school that already wrote. Boarding camps $1,500 to $5,400. A $5,000 boarding camp is a vacation, not recruiting.
- Video: $0 or about $20 a month to cut it yourself. One freelance edit $150 to $250, or $50 to $95 for a game breakdown. Full-service company $250 to $2,459.
- Recruiting service: prices not published. Third-party reports $250 to $2,100 a year, others up to $4,200.
- Unofficial visit weekend: $500 to $1,500 for two nights. One target school per summer. The trip costs more than the camp.

### Ten ways families get girls' basketball wrong

1. They read "15 scholarships" and stop. 15 is a roster of people, not 15 free rides.
2. They build a list of twelve schools. Aim for 40 to 55.
3. They read April-to-July silence as rejection. That silence is the rule.
4. They pay a club without asking which events are NCAA-certified.
5. They send 500-word emails written by a parent.
6. They send highlights with no full game and no defense.
7. They ask about money on the first call.
8. They skip Division 3. There are 416 programs.
9. They sign a junior-college letter to keep a spot warm. It binds a school year.
10. They plan the money backwards. Budget travel first, then cut the $2,000 packages.

## Women's volleyball: the plain truth

Women's volleyball fields 1,053 NCAA teams. A girl can be seen from a free profile page and one email. Club is the showcase. In the NCAA's 2025 survey, 94 percent of college players played both high school and club, 3 percent high school only, and 2 percent club only. USA Volleyball repeats this as "more than 90%." High school ball in nearly every state is a fall sport, and college coaches are playing their own season then.

492,799 girls played high-school volleyball in 2024-25. The NCAA counted 19,810 volleyball players across three divisions in 2023-24. That is about 4.0 percent reaching an NCAA team: about 1.3 percent Division I, 1.2 percent Division II, 1.6 percent Division III. The coaches' association, counting 2019, put college-team odds at 4.6 percent and Division I at 0.6 percent, because their denominator also counted club players. Read those as directions, not promises.

A family that knows the roster rules, the calendar, and the video standard competes with families spending three times as much. Most of what a $2,000 service sells is phone calls and follow-ups.

### How many seats

The number that used to matter was money. For most big schools it is now people. The old Division I limit was 12 scholarships. The figure 4.5 is men's volleyball's old limit. If a guide says women's volleyball was a 4.5 sport, the rest of that guide is not worth the paper.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Division I, opted in | No aid cap. Roster limit of 18 players on aid in any amount, since 1 July 2025. A conference may set a lower number, not below 12. | 348 | The scarce thing is a seat. A small aid offer still uses one of the 18. |
| Division I, not opted in | No roster limit. School must stay under the old aid value. About 17 schools were named as objectors. No printed list of who chose which. | Inside the 348 | Ask. The answer changes the offer. |
| Division II | 8.0 aid equivalents, cut into pieces. No roster limit. | 288 | A staff can spread 8.0 over 19 players. |
| Division III | No athletic aid. No roster limit. | 417 | The biggest door, and the one families walk past. |
| NAIA | 8 aid equivalents. No roster limit. | 226 schools (one recruiting service) | Small staffs. A coach often answers directly. |
| Junior college | 14, and exempt athletes do not count. The published table is inside a login. | About 300 to 545 | Two counts are in circulation. |

Live roster pages counted 11 November 2026: Division I 18, 18, 16, 16, 15, 15. Division II 19. Division III 19 and 25. NAIA 20 and 31. Service averages, not rules: 18.1 Division I, 19.3 Division II, 18.2 Division III, 18 NAIA, 13 junior college.

List length, this book's guidance, not a rule: 40 to 60 schools across all five levels. Writing to twelve schools and calling it a plan is the single most common mistake in this sport.

Before a coach spends one of 18 spots on a 12th grader: returning players are counted first, and transfers have no one-time limit. 2026-27 Division I portal windows: 6 December 2026 to 4 January 2027, and 1 to 15 May 2027. Division I roster submission was 2 October 2026. Division II submission is the fifth Sunday in March.

### Contact, visits, signing (class of 2029)

She may write today. USA Volleyball prints NO RESTRICTIONS on her side. A Division I or II coach may not answer until 15 June 2027. Division III, NAIA, and junior college coaches may answer any time.

- Division I visits, official and unofficial: 1 August 2027. Five official visits in a lifetime. School pays official visits. She needs a paid certification account for an official visit.
- Division II official visits: after 15 June of 10th grade, so 15 June 2027.
- Division III official visits: after 1 January of 11th grade, so 1 January 2027.
- Junior college official visits: after the first day of classes of 11th grade. Ask the college what the trip covers.
- NAIA: no visit rule printed.
- Signing: there is no National Letter of Intent. The NCAA ended it in October 2024. She may sign a written offer of athletics aid from the second Wednesday in November of 12th grade. The book projects Wednesday 8 November 2028. The NCAA printed 11 November 2026 for the current year. The 2028-29 sheet is not out. Once she signs, other schools that offer athletics aid must stop recruiting her until she gets a release.
- Final athletics certification: she may ask from 1 April 2029. Ask before the deposit.
- Do not sign two junior college letters of intent. That costs a year of ineligibility in that association. A junior-college year now starts the NCAA five-year clock, so one year there leaves four.

Core GPA: 2.3 Division I, 2.2 Division II, on 16 approved subjects, 4.0 scale. No SAT or ACT required for anyone first enrolling on or after 1 August 2023. Eligibility Center live page: $120 combined, $75 athletics certification only. Printed guide still says $110. NAIA $110. Confirm the number on the day she pays. Up to two fee waivers. Registration must be open at least 60 days before a transcript upload is due.

Printed 2026-27 Division I calendar: contact period 1 September to 30 November. Dead period 9 to 12 November 2026, the week of that year's signing date. In a dead period a staff may not talk to her at all, in person or on the phone. Her own signing-week dates are not printed yet.

Camp talk: the coaches' association said in April 2022 that a Division I volleyball camp may only run between Memorial Day and 31 July, and that recruiting conversations at a camp are not permitted before August 1 at the beginning of her junior year. This book could not re-check that against the 2026-27 rule book. Assume talk is off limits at a summer 2027 camp until 1 August 2027, even though phone and email open on 15 June 2027.

A camp invite is not interest. USA Volleyball: an invitation to a camp may or may not indicate interest. Real activity is a coach watching her profile, replying, or asking for film or her schedule.

### Video

Build two to three minutes. Coaches say two to five minutes. The shortest honest number in this research is two minutes. Best live-play clip first. Coaches watch the first 10 to 25 seconds. A club coach says 10 to 15. Cover card on the first frame: name, position, class, jersey number. Match play, not drills. High and wide from the end line. No slow motion, no music, no big arrows. No login wall. Keep the full match ready. Show serve receive and defense, not only kills.

Label every jump: tool, date, who measured, shoes on or off. A wearable reads about two inches higher than a Vertec for the same athlete (one study: 128 Brazilian youth national players, 70.9 centimetres against 76.3). No college in this research publishes an approach, block, or reach standard. Service height charts are one company's targets, not rules.

Hitting percentage: (kills minus errors) divided by swings. A .250 or better is the line one service uses for a good Division I season. That is a service line, not an NCAA rule.

- Local or regional club: $1,350 to $2,600. One example: $1,450 for eight league dates, no required out-of-state travel. Best value per touch.
- Mid-travel club: $3,100 to $4,350, if the qualifier is one staffs attend.
- National circuit: one club prints $4,350 for 18s and $4,950 for 15s to 17s. Another: $3,500 to $5,800. A top 14s team: $7,380. Pay for the schedule, not the label.

- Tryout: $25 to $70. Uniform if separate: $240 to $374. Membership: $90 in one region.
- Qualifier entry: $925 per team, about $78 to $93 a player on a 10 to 12 player roster.
- Family hotel and gas: one marker is $4,000 to $5,000 for six overnight tournaments, about $670 to $830 a trip. Plan four trips, not fourteen camp weekends.
- Camps: $310 commuting and $380 resident prep camp; satellite overnight $200; one-evening passers camp $60; prospect camp $160 for 40 spots; Division I position camp $375 to $485; Division III $154 to $318; invite-only campus camp $342 to $447. 2027 prices were not posted when the book was checked.
- Film: $20 to $60 freelance. $450 to $1,350 at a volleyball-specific shop. Full-service $500 to $1,000 or more. A $55 edit of live play beats a $1,350 reel of drills.

- NIL is not a budget. A 2026 industry report puts the average women's volleyball deal at $663, next to $2,500 for women's basketball and $5,900 for football. Deals of $600 or more must be reported, counted from the start of 11th grade. No agent. No school name, uniform, or logo.

### Ten ways families get volleyball wrong

1. They plan the year around high school ball. Club is where staffs watch. 94 percent of college players came through club as well.
2. They wait for a coach to write first. She may write today. A Division I or II coach may not answer until 15 June 2027.
3. They count 12 scholarships. At an opted-in school the number is an 18-player roster. 4.5 is the men's line.
4. They pay $2,000 for a service and stop emailing.
5. They take a camp invite as interest.
6. They print a bare height and a bare vertical.
7. They send a nine-minute reel of drills. The first 10 to 25 seconds do the work.
8. They skip Division III and the NAIA. Division III alone is 417 teams.
9. They sign a junior-college letter to hold a place. Two of them cost a year, and one year there starts the five-year clock.
10. They plan the money backwards. Budget four trips, ask what the fee excludes, and skip the packages.

## What a chatbot should not say

- Do not promise a roster spot, a scholarship, playing time, or a signing date.
- Do not treat a PROJECTED date as a printed NCAA calendar date.
- Do not quote 4.5 as the women's volleyball scholarship number.
- Do not quote 15 girls' basketball seats as 15 full rides.
- Do not tell a girls' basketball family that silence from 1 April through 31 July is rejection.
- Do not tell a volleyball family to wait for the coach to write first.
- Do not invent a jump standard, a wingspan floor, or a sprint floor. Neither playbook found an association rule for those.
- Division 3 has no athletic aid in either sport. Help paying, if any, comes from the school, not the coach.
